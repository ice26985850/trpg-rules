Scenario S1: Wilderness Birth

Suggested Era Range: Tribal Age → City-State Age
Suggested Level: Beginner (newbie-friendly)
Estimated Turns: ~40 turns (10 years in-game)
Difficulty: ★☆☆☆☆
Core Themes: Survival, foundation, the first choices
Player Count: 1 player + 1 GM

I. Scenario Overview

Synopsis

A company of fewer than a hundred souls arrives at the edge of the continent of Elden. They are survivors who fled the old continent because of war, persecution, or dreams. The land before them — beautiful yet dangerous — will become their new home.

But the wilderness shows no mercy to anyone. Before the first winter comes, they must raise a camp, stockpile food, and fend off wild beasts. And when the chimney first gives off the smoke of cooking, they will discover they are not alone — this land already has a master.

Scenario Objectives

  1. Establish the first settlement
  2. Survive the first winter
  3. Grow the population past 250 and advance into the City-State Age
  4. Establish formal relations with the initial neighboring state (whether friendly or hostile)
  5. Plant firm roots in the wilderness

II. Scenario Structure

Phase One: Landing (Turns 1–5, Spring of Year 1 → Spring of Year 2)

Opening Narration (read aloud by the GM):

After a long journey, the ship (or caravan) finally reaches this unknown coast. The hull is battered and broken, and supplies are nearly spent. Before you lies a dense forest — or open plains, or rugged mountains — depending on the starting point you choose.

Everyone steps wearily onto the land. A young man runs to the river and scoops up water to drink; an old veteran silently tightens his grip on the hilt of his sword; a child cries and asks her mother, "Are we home?"

You — the leader they have chosen — watch all this. More than fifty people are counting on you to keep them alive.

You turn to your advisor: "Find water first, then cut timber. Before nightfall, we need a shelter against the wind and rain."

Key Events:

Turn Event Type
Turn 1 Choose a camp location — by the river (resources + flood risk) or on high ground (defense + fewer resources) Exploration
Turn 2 Discover an abandoned hunter's cabin — a few supplies inside, but it seems to have been abandoned in haste Exploration
Turn 3 First contact — a native hunter observes the camp from afar. Do you make contact? Diplomacy
Turn 4 Strange sounds from the forest — a large beast, or something else? Threat
Turn 5 Completion ceremony of the first permanent building — Morale (MOR) rises, but food reserves begin to run low Milestone

GM Notes: - The first 5 turns are a tutorial — lower the difficulty (DC −2) so players learn the system - Encourage players to try different kinds of actions - If a check fails, offer an alternative rather than a direct penalty

Phase Two: The First Winter (Turns 6–10, Autumn–Winter of Year 2)

Key Events:

Turn Event Type
Turn 6 The autumn harvest falls short of expectations — food reserves only enough for 3 turns Resource Pressure
Turn 7 A group of refugees from farther north asks to join the camp (+10 population, but increased food pressure) Moral Choice
Turn 8 A blizzard strikes — construction efficiency drops to 0; everyone must shelter Crisis
Turn 9 Food runs out — do you dip into the seed reserves? (doing so halves next year's yield) Moral Choice
Turn 10 Spring arrives — survivors count the living. If anyone died, Morale drops Milestone

GM Notes: - This is the players' first brush with real survival pressure - "Whether to accept the refugees" is the first major moral choice — there is no correct answer - "Whether to eat the seed" is a classic survival dilemma - If anyone dies, describe specific individuals to add emotional weight

Phase Three: Expansion (Turns 11–25, Years 3–6)

Key Events:

Turn Event Type
Turn 11 Spring — discover a rich mineral vein (or fishing ground / hunting grounds, depending on terrain) Resource Discovery
Turn 13 A stranger calling himself a "merchant" appears with goods — is he a real merchant or a spy? Diplomacy / Recon
Turn 15 The camp's first newborns arrive — natural population growth begins Milestone
Turn 17 An ancient temple ruin is found in a nearby forest — offer a sacrifice for a blessing Exploration
Turn 20 The first real conflict — a neighboring patrol crosses the border; do you strike back? Diplomatic Crisis
Turn 23 Two factions in the camp clash over resource distribution — mediation needed Internal Event
Turn 25 A charismatic young person challenges your leadership — how do you respond? Internal Crisis

Phase Four: The Shadow of Neighbors (Turns 26–35, Years 7–9)

In this phase, the initial neighboring state shifts from onlooker to active participant.

Turn Event Type
Turn 26 The neighbor formally sends an ambassador — what do they want? Trade? Submission? Or intelligence? Diplomacy
Turn 28 Neighbor's markers found on the border — they are drawing boundaries that include land you consider yours Territorial Dispute
Turn 30 A defector from the neighbor asks for asylum with intelligence — take them in? (doing so: relation −30) Moral Choice
Turn 32 A camp-wide outbreak — quarantine, treatment, and reassurance required Crisis
Turn 35 The first formal diplomatic summit — the neighbor proposes terms of alliance or vassalage Diplomacy

Phase Five: Becoming a City-State (Turns 36–40, Year 10)

Turn Event Type
Turn 36 Population breaks 200 — the camp has become a true settlement Milestone
Turn 37 Caravans from two different directions arrive at once — choose which to trade with first Diplomacy / Economy
Turn 38 The first external threat — a bandit gang appears nearby, attacking caravans Military
Turn 39 Era-upgrade preparation — the last condition must be met (population past 250) Construction
Turn 40 City-State Founding Ceremony — your settlement officially becomes a city-state. Surrounding powers all send envoys Ending

III. Ending Conditions

Success Ending

  • Population > 250, advancing into the City-State Age
  • Formal relations established with at least 1 neighbor
  • No famine occurred (food never hit zero)
  • Reward: All attributes +5; unlock the legendary deed "The First Cornerstone"

Partial Success

  • Population > 300 but not yet 500
  • Survived but failed to enter the City-State Age
  • Reward: Population (POP) +10; may continue in free mode

Failure Conditions

  • Population drops to 0 (total wipeout)
  • Morale drops to 0, triggering a full revolt
  • Remedy: Use the "inheritance mechanic" — play as survivors founding a new camp (retaining 50% of attributes)

IV. Key NPCs

Name Role Relationship
Old Karl The camp's eldest, a former farmer, offers agricultural advice Loyal Mentor
Martha A young hunter, the first to spot the neighbor's markers Brave but impulsive
Merchant Victor A mysterious traveling merchant, knows much but never reveals the source Pragmatic Ally
Neighbor Ambassador (varies by region) Representative of the initial neighbor Depends on player actions

VI. GM Tips

  1. Pacing: The first 5 turns are tutorial mode — slow down and explain each mechanic
  2. Emotion: Give NPCs names and stories — let them be more than just numbers
  3. Weight of Choices: Every major decision should let the players feel its consequences
  4. No Mercy: The first winter should genuinely be tense — this is the moment players learn to respect the rules
  5. Foreshadowing: NPCs and powers introduced in S1 return in S2 and later scenarios
