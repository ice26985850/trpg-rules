Sample Freeze Zone Expansion — Frozen Wasteland TRPG

This document contains 12 complete Freeze Zone samples, covering the full difficulty gradient from novice to legendary. Each Freeze Zone can be used independently, or strung together within a campaign. The first 3 (the supermarket, the hospital, and the military checkpoint) are the basic samples; the remaining 9 are newly added expansions.

I. Novice Freeze Zones (×3)

Sample 01: Supermarket "Frostfall Supermarket" (Novice, Slumber Type)

Overview: A medium-sized chain supermarket, frozen at the height of a weekend shopping rush. Frost slowly settled from the ceiling, wrapping the entire store in tranquil white. The aisles are filled with the Frozen holding their shopping poses — a mother pushing a cart, a child hesitating in the snack aisle, an elder queuing at the register. Thanks to its structural stability and gentle thaw rhythm, this is the ideal introductory venue for novice Cold Walkers.

The freeze here came especially gently — as if time had decided to let this community sleep peacefully. Yet, "anomalies" in certain houses hint that the freeze was not entirely random. At the center of the community is a small park whose fountain froze into a perfect ice sculpture.

Sample 04: Hospital "St. Mary's Memorial Hospital" (Intermediate, Pulse Type)

Overview: An eight-floor general hospital, frozen at the height of the ER rush. The freeze here is extremely uneven — the ER is almost fully frozen, while some wards are merely "room-temperature stagnation." Deep in the hospital, in an operating room, a heart surgery was frozen at a critical moment — the surgeon's scalpel hovering half a centimeter from the heart. Most eerie is the morgue: its freeze did not come from outside, but spread outward from within. The hospital is strewn with large amounts of medicine, medical equipment, and patient records — extremely valuable, but equally risky.

Overview: The city's subway system before the freeze, about 40 km long, deepest point 60 m underground. At the moment of freezing, all trains stopped simultaneously — some in tunnels, some beside platforms. Because the underground environment is enclosed, the freeze is preserved unusually well. Passengers in the cars hold their commuting poses: office workers reading newspapers, students with headphones, dozing elders. But what's special about the subway is that time here shows a strange "stratification": different tunnel segments flow at inconsistent rates. The deepest transfer station, "Zero Point Station," is said to hide one of the Frozen Wasteland's oldest secrets.

Sample 06: Power Plant "Everfrost Power Plant" (Intermediate, Breath Type)

Overview: A medium geothermal power plant that powered half the city before the freeze. Because it used geothermal + experimental Time Crystal hybrid generation, the plant did not stop at the freeze — it entered a kind of "time closed loop" state. The turbines still turn, the control panels still run, the whole plant sits in an eternal "operating" state. But the problem is: the plant's core reactor is slowly drawing energy from Time Crystals, and this process is accelerating the freeze in the surrounding area. If the reactor is not shut down or stabilized in time, the freeze will spread outward from the plant, devouring tens of kilometers of area.

Sample 11: Wasteland Abyss "Genesis Corridor" (Legendary, Breath Type)

Overview: If the Clock Tower is the "heart" of the freeze, then the Genesis Corridor is its "memory." This is a cave tunnel system at the wasteland's deepest point, said to have formed at the world's birth. The tunnel walls are covered with unrecognizable ancient writing and patterns — but they were not carved, they "grew": mineral crystals naturally arranged as text. At the corridor's deepest point is a place called the "Genesis Wall" — a giant crystal wall displaying this world's history in moving images, from birth to... the future not yet happened. Those who successfully reach the Genesis Wall are said to be able to "see the full picture of time." But most never return — not because of death, but because they chose to stay there, watching time's flow forever.

Map Description: - Entrance — Throat of Oblivion: A giant cave entrance hidden deep in the wasteland. The rock at the entrance shows unnatural spiral patterns — as if time itself left traces in the rock. Near the entrance is a small camp ruin — a temporary base left by the previous expedition. - Segment 1 — Corridor of History: Tunnel walls display images of the distant past — world birth, life origin, civilization rise. These images move, but silently. Touching the wall lets you hear the era's sounds (Tech (TEC) Difficulty (DC) 16 to record sound samples). - Segment 2 — Beginning of the Freeze: The walls here display the whole process of the freeze occurring — but the viewpoint is not from the ground, but from... above the sky? Images show a giant energy wave spreading outward from the Clock Tower's position, instantly covering the whole world. But the energy wave's "source" seems to be beyond the sky — pointing at a point in the starry sky. - Segment 3 — Sea of Possibilities: The wall here splits into countless branches, each showing a possible future. Some show the freeze permanently continuing, civilization perishing; some show the freeze reversed, the world restored; some show completely incomprehensible scenes. Branches constantly split, merge, vanish — time's countless possibilities exist here simultaneously. - Segment 4 — Void Rift: The corridor is interrupted. Ahead is a giant abyssal cleft — ~50 ft wide, bottomless. The cleft has no ice, no light, not even time — a realm of absolute nothingness. Crossing requires a special "Bridge of Time" — by using the Perfect Crystal or large amounts of Time Crystal Shards at the cleft's edge, you can temporarily create a time bridge in the nothingness (lasts 3 rounds). - Segment 5 — Genesis Wall: The corridor's end. A giant crystal wall ~100 ft high, ~200 ft wide, its surface flowing with gorgeous light. The wall displays not the past, but the "now" — a live panorama of the whole wasteland. Every settlement, every Freeze Zone, every still-active person — all on this wall. At the wall's center is a raised crystal — touching it lets you "enter" any scene on the wall, witnessing what is happening as an observer. But most unsettling is — it shows what is beyond the border. At the wasteland's edge, a giant dark silhouette is slowly approaching...

In-Zone Threats: - Crystal Guardian: Native creature of the Genesis Corridor — made of pure Time Crystal, appearing as polyhedral geometric forms with memory-light flowing inside. They guard the corridor, attacking any existence trying to go deeper. Health (HP) 60, Defense (AC) 19, Speed 25 ft (fly). Attacks are "Memory Projection" (+11 to hit, 3d8+5 mental damage; those hit need a Guts (GUT) Difficulty (DC) 19 or randomly lose proficiency in one skill — until a long rest) and "Time Freeze Ray" (ranged 40 ft, +9 to hit, 2d8+4 cold damage + target time-slowed — Speed −15 ft for 1 round). Special ability "Memory Drain" — each round can steal a memory fragment from a character (GM describes a past scene of the character "played" by the guardian), the character needs a Guts (GUT) Difficulty (DC) 18 or lose the emotional weight of that memory (game effect: Disadvantage on checks related to that memory, until leaving the corridor). Count: 2d2 per segment. - Void Abyss: The void cleft of Segment 4. This is not a threat, but the purest "non-existence." Anything that falls into the void ceases to exist — not death, but erasure from time. No one remembers they existed. Characters can try to jump the cleft (Speed (SPD) Difficulty (DC) 24 — nearly impossible), but once failed, no rescue is possible. The Bridge of Time is the only safe passage. - Future Self: In Segment 3 (Sea of Possibilities), characters may meet a "possible future" version of themselves. This future self may be friendly or hostile — depending on the character's choices in the campaign. Stats: identical to the character, but level +1, possessing gear and abilities the character might gain in the future. This is not a mandatory fight — the future self may offer warnings, advice, or... a plea for help.
