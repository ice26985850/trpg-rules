Campaign Scenario: Road of Ice

Campaign Overview: This is a continuous campaign spanning 8–10 game sessions, telling the story of a group of Cold Walkers who awaken from the wasteland and gradually uncover the truth of the Freeze. From learning to survive in a supermarket to reshaping the world atop the Clock Tower—this is a road from "simply living" to "choosing how to live." The campaign is built around a central secret: the "Genesis Hypothesis"—the origin of the Freeze and the possibility of reversing it. Running throughout is the emotional throughline of "The One You're Still Searching For"—every character is searching for someone important they lost to the Freeze.

Act I: Awakening

Beneath the ice, something is awakening. Not a monster—but a question. Who froze this world? Why? And you—the one you lost to the Freeze—are they still waiting somewhere?

Session 1: Under-Ice Market

Suggested Game Session: 1st
Difficulty: Novice
Freeze Zone: Frostfall Supermarket (Sample 01)
Estimated Time: 2.5–3.5 hours

Briefing: The characters are residents (or new arrivals) of the settlement "Lakeheart City," who for their own reasons have become "Cold Walkers"—adventurers brave enough to enter Freeze Zones to explore. Supplies in Lakeheart City are running low, and the city's Supply Dispatcher has issued a public commission to all Cold Walkers: "Explore Frostfall Supermarket and bring back any usable supplies. Reward: 20% of recovered supplies for personal use, plus 5 Time Crystal Shards per person." This is the characters' first mission—and the starting point of their fate.

Mission Objectives: - Primary Objective: Enter Frostfall Supermarket and collect at least 30 Rations and 10 Medical Supplies (team total) - Optional Objective A: Open the cold storage door and retrieve the high-value goods inside (+5 Time Crystal Shards/person) - Optional Objective B: Find the "Supplier List" in the checkout area to learn the source of supplies (story clue, no direct reward but unlocks later information) - Optional Objective C: Rescue the critically frozen child in the cart (major ethical choice, see below)

Scene Flow:

  1. Lakeheart City—Commission Issued
    Supply Dispatcher Ed gathers the Cold Walkers before the notice board at the city center. He is a pragmatic middle-aged man who speaks quickly, but when he mentions the Frozen in the supermarket, his voice lowers slightly: "Try... not to disturb them. They're just out shopping." This scene establishes the world's tone—the scarcity of supplies, the everydayness of the Freeze.
    Key Encounter: The players meet another group of Cold Walkers—the "Ironfang Brotherhood," three experienced but crude veterans. They mock the newcomers' arrival but offer valuable advice: "There are Crystal Shards in the supermarket's cold storage—but the door is frozen solid. Bring a crowbar."
    Suggested DC: Negotiation (NEG) Difficulty (DC) 12 to get extra intel from the Ironfang Brotherhood (number of Frostmist Globs in cold storage).

  2. Frostfall Supermarket—Entrance Exploration
    The characters arrive outside the supermarket. The automatic doors are half-open, frost forming strange patterns on the floor. The interior is dimly lit; the outlines of shelves stand like silent sentinels.
    Key Encounter: The first Thaw Omen appears (Difficulty (DC) 10 Perception (PER) check to notice slight vibrations). This is the crucial moment for teaching the rules—let the players experience the Thaw mechanic for the first time.
    Suggested DC: Perception (PER) Difficulty (DC) 10 to notice the Omen; Tech (TEC) Difficulty (DC) 12 to read the cash register's transaction records.

  3. Fresh Produce Section—First Threat
    The characters enter the fresh produce section (Zone A) and encounter the first Frostmist Glob. This is a tutorial combat—enemy stats are moderate, letting players learn the basic combat mechanics. The slippery floor adds a tactical dimension.
    Key Encounter: Frostmist Glob ×1; slippery floor requires Speed (SPD) Difficulty (DC) 10 to avoid slipping.
    Suggested DC: Guts (GUT) Difficulty (DC) 11 to avoid Cold Fatigue from the Frostmist Glob.

  4. Dry Goods Section—Supply Collection
    The core supply area. Canned goods and dry rations on the shelves are relatively intact. Characters can conduct systematic supply collection.
    Key Encounter: A hungry Scavenger—a mentally broken survivor. This is not a pure combat encounter: characters may choose to fight, soothe (Negotiation (NEG) Difficulty (DC) 14), or bypass.
    Suggested DC: Negotiation (NEG) Difficulty (DC) 14 to calm the Scavenger; Perception (PER) Difficulty (DC) 13 to find extra supplies hidden behind the shelves.

  5. Cold Storage—High Risk, High Reward
    The frozen cold storage door requires Tech (TEC) Difficulty (DC) 14 or combined effort to push open. Inside are abundant supplies but also Frostmist Glob ×2.
    Key Encounter: Frostmist Glob ×2 (if the characters haven't encountered them before, this is a harder version).
    Suggested DC: Tech (TEC) Difficulty (DC) 14 or three people pushing together (combined Strength check ≥30) to open the frozen door.

  6. Checkout Area—Clues and Departure
    The final exploration area. The transaction records in the cash register and the Supplier List are story clues.
    Key Encounter: Here the characters discover the "Cart Mother"—see the ethical dilemma in Freeze Zone Sample 01.
    Suggested DC: Tech (TEC) Difficulty (DC) 12 to read the cash register records; ethical choice—rescuing the child requires spending a Defrost Agent + Perception (PER) Difficulty (DC) 12 to confirm the child's Thaw Threshold.

Key Ethical Choice:
"The Child in the Cart"—Near the checkout area, the characters find a mother pushing a shopping cart (Fully Frozen) and the child in her cart, about 4 years old (Critically Frozen—pupils still faintly glowing). The child's Thaw Threshold is only 12, and can be attempted without triggering a mass Thaw. But it requires spending the team's only Defrost Agent (from the Supply Dispatcher—"This is precious, use it sparingly").
- Option A—Rescue: Spend 1 Defrost Agent. After thawing, the child is extremely frightened, unable to communicate, and needs the characters to spend time comforting her. She becomes an NPC who needs protection (may become an emotional core in later campaign sessions). But the Supply Dispatcher will express displeasure at "wasting the Defrost Agent." Unlocks later plot: "The child's father works at the harbor—he may be at Frosttide Harbor."
- Option B—Do Not Rescue: Keep the Defrost Agent. The mission completes more smoothly, but each character must make a private Guts (GUT) Difficulty (DC) 13 check that night (the GM privately asks each character: "Can you sleep tonight?"—purely narrative, no mechanical consequence). In later plot, the child's whereabouts become a lingering knot in the characters' hearts.

Loot and Rewards: - Time Crystal: 8 Time Crystal Shards per person + 5 mission reward shards (13 total/person) - Milestone: Completing the first mission—gaining "Cold Walker" identity (narrative growth) - Story Clue: Supplier List (points to the harbor—Session 6), clue about the child's father (if rescued)

Ending Branches: - If the players choose to rescue the child → At the start of Session 2, the child is placed in Lakeheart City's orphanage. The characters gain the "Guardian" reputation tag (NPCs are friendlier toward them). - If the players choose not to rescue → The child reappears later in the campaign as one of the Frozen (Session 4 hospital—she is found by other Cold Walkers and brought to the hospital). The reunion scene is extremely emotionally charged. - If the players discovered Time Crystal Shards in cold storage → Unlock basic knowledge of Crystal use (a brief Crystal mechanic tutorial before Session 2).

Session 2: Voices of the Neighborhood

Suggested Game Session: 2nd
Difficulty: Novice
Freeze Zone: Frostbell Quarter (Sample 02)
Estimated Time: 3–4 hours

Briefing: The success of Session 1's mission earns the characters a certain reputation in Lakeheart City. Several days later, a young acolyte from the "Silent Hand Church"—Lena—approaches the team. She asks the characters to help investigate an anomaly at the Frostbell Quarter fountain: "The community park's fountain froze into a perfect ice sculpture—that's not normal. Ice everywhere else is slowly changing, but the ice there has never changed shape. The Church believes... that may be a 'Time Stable Point'—a place where the Freeze isn't so complete." Lena will pay a reward (10 Time Crystal Shards per person), but her true purpose is to verify some theory of the Church. Meanwhile, someone on the team receives a private message—someone has seen "a figure who looks like the one you're searching for" in Frostbell Quarter.

Mission Objectives: - Primary Objective: Reach the community park, investigate the fountain anomaly, collect 3 fountain ice crystal samples - Optional Objective A: Explore Residence B-8, discover the old man's notes (first fragment clue of the Genesis Hypothesis) - Optional Objective B: Find clues about the character's "One You're Still Searching For" (at Residence C-35) - Optional Objective C: Obtain the community directory at the community center—learn the full list of residents (useful for finding specific people later)

Scene Flow:

  1. Lakeheart City—Lena's Commission
    Lena receives the characters in the church's small chapel. She is young (about 20), idealistic, devoted to the church's doctrine but not fanatical. "The Silent Hand Church believes the Freeze is time's 'rest'—but rest should not be eternal. We are searching... for a way to awaken it." Her candor is surprising.
    Key Encounter: Lena shows a blurry photo—the Frostbell Quarter fountain. On the back is written a line: "Time rests in the water." This is a phrase from the Church's ancient texts.
    Suggested DC: Perception (PER) Difficulty (DC) 14 to notice Lena avoiding certain questions (she is hiding the Church's true goal—the Church leadership's interest in the fountain goes far beyond "investigation"). Negotiation (NEG) Difficulty (DC) 13 can get Lena to reveal more: "Some elders believe that by finding enough Time Stable Points, the Freeze can be dismantled from within—not by thawing anyone, but by letting the Freeze fail on its own."

  2. Frostbell Quarter—Entrance
    The characters arrive at the quarter's entrance. The "Frostbell Quarter" lettering on the arch is covered in frost. The guard in the guard booth maintains his tea-drinking pose.
    Key Encounter: A Frost Hound prowls the entrance—what is it guarding? Fight or bypass.
    Suggested DC: Perception (PER) Difficulty (DC) 12 to notice the hound's behavior pattern (it only patrols within 20 feet of the entrance—can be bypassed).

  3. Row B Residences—The Old Man's Garden
    Residence B-8 is the key exploration point. The old man ("the Old Man in the Garden"—see Sample 02) and his notes are an important source of main-plot clues.
    Key Encounter: Encounter the partially frozen old man. His notes record strange dreams before the Freeze—"a giant clock, its hands running backward." The notes also contain a hand-drawn star chart draft—the same as the star chart on the blackboard at Dawnlight Elementary.
    Suggested DC: Perception (PER) Difficulty (DC) 14 to find a page the old man deliberately hid in his notes (tucked in the back cover)—it reads "He is watching us. From between the stars." Negotiation (NEG) Difficulty (DC) 16 for limited communication with the old man (he can only move his lips extremely slowly).

  4. Row C Residences—Clues of "The One You're Still Searching For"
    Residence C-35. According to the client's information, someone saw "a figure who looks like the one you're searching for" here. In reality, this house's backyard contains a small pottery studio—filled with many ceramics whose style is strikingly similar to the work of "that person" in the character's memory. On the studio table is an unfinished clay pot—the spinning wheel frozen mid-craft. Carved on the pot's bottom is a date—three days before the Freeze.
    Key Encounter: Time Echo—in the studio, the characters experience a strong Time Echo. They briefly "see" that person—not a physical entity, but a Time Imprint left at the moment of the Thaw. That person seems to be speaking toward some direction, but the voice cannot be heard.
    Suggested DC: Perception (PER) Difficulty (DC) 16 to catch a few words from that person's mouth shape in the Time Echo (depends on GM setup—suggested: words with special meaning to the character). Guts (GUT) Difficulty (DC) 14 to not be overwhelmed by the Time Echo's emotional impact.

  5. Community Park—Fountain Investigation
    The fountain is frozen into a perfect ice sculpture. Lena's theory is correct—this is indeed a Time Stable Point. While collecting ice crystal samples, the characters discover a line carved at the fountain's base: "May time rest here—founded by the Silent Hand, Eileen, the first priest."
    Key Encounter: The Church knew about this place long ago—Lena's investigation is not out of academic curiosity, but some kind of test within the Church (testing the team's capability and trustworthiness).
    Suggested DC: Tech (TEC) Difficulty (DC) 14 to collect complete fountain ice crystal samples (3). Perception (PER) Difficulty (DC) 18 to discover the carving at the fountain's base (covered by ice).

  6. Community Center—Epilogue
    The final exploration. The gym in the community center has a message wall covered with notes residents left before the Freeze. One note stands out—written minutes before the Freeze: "They said no evacuation needed. They said it's just a cold snap. But I saw it—coming from the horizon, it wasn't a cloud. It was time."
    Key Encounter: After the characters finish investigating, Lena makes an additional request: "Keep the carving at the fountain's base secret. Don't tell anyone—not even the Church people. Please." Her expression is no longer idealistic—it is fear.

Key Ethical Choice:
"Lena's Secret"—Lena asks the characters to keep the fountain-base carving secret. She hints at division within the Church—"Some want to exploit the Stable Points, not to end the Freeze, but to control it."
- Option A—Keep Secret: Respect Lena's request. Gain Lena's trust (she becomes a reliable insider in later campaign sessions, providing key intel in Sessions 5 and 8). But hiding the information means missing the chance to understand the Church's true goal.
- Option B—Report to the Church: Report the carving to the Church leadership. Gain extra Church reward (15 Time Crystal Shards per person + large boost to Church favor) and unlock Church-related plot lines. But Lena will be punished for it (she has been reassigned by Session 5—losing an important ally).

Loot and Rewards: - Time Crystal: 10 Time Crystal Shards per person (Lena's reward) + exploration gains (varies by supply distribution) - Milestone: Completing the first Church-related mission—unlock "Silent Hand Church" faction relationship - Story Clues: Old man's star chart draft (Genesis Hypothesis fragment 1/3—points to the school, Session 3), fountain-base carving (clue to Church secret), Time Echo at C-35 pottery studio (trace of "The One You're Still Searching For")

Ending Branches: - If the fountain carving is kept secret → Lena becomes an ally (later gains secret intel). Lena will proactively contact the characters at the start of Session 5. - If reported to the Church → Church favor rises to "Friendly." Unlock Church-exclusive mission line (later campaign may gain unique Church support—such as free Defrost Agents and Time Crystals). But in Session 5, Lena is no longer at her post. - If the characters successfully captured info in the C-35 Time Echo → Unlock the character's personal mission line (gain clues to find "that person" in later campaign).

Session 3: The Star Chart on the Blackboard

Suggested Game Session: 3rd
Difficulty: Novice (leaning hard)
Freeze Zone: Dawnlight Elementary School (Sample 03)
Estimated Time: 3.5–4.5 hours

Briefing: The old man's star chart draft from Session 2 matches none of the characters' existing astronomical knowledge—the constellation arrangement shown doesn't correspond to any known star chart. The only clue is a faint seal in the bottom-right of the chart—"Dawnlight Elementary Astronomy Club." With help from Lakeheart City's Archivist, the characters confirm the location of Dawnlight Elementary. Meanwhile, an unsettling rumor begins circulating in Lakeheart City: a Scavenger heard "the school bell" near the school—in a completely frozen world.

Mission Objectives: - Primary Objective: Find the school library, collect at least 5 "Time Memory" text fragments (to assemble the first complete Genesis Hypothesis info) - Optional Objective A: Enter the third-grade classroom, record the complete star chart on the blackboard (Genesis Hypothesis fragment 2/3) - Optional Objective B: Enter the boiler room, obtain the custodian's maintenance log (records of an "anomalous heat source" underground at the school) - Optional Objective C: Find why the scoreboard in the gymnasium is flickering (reveals time can "run backward" under specific conditions—core worldview info)

Scene Flow:

  1. Lakeheart City—Rumor and Preparation
    The Archivist shows the characters Dawnlight Elementary's files—the school was holding a "Science Week" before the Freeze, themed "Time and Space." This may be a coincidence—but in the Frozen Wasteland, coincidences are never simple.
    Key Encounter: A mysterious figure calling himself the "Collector's Agent" contacts the team. He offers a deal: "If you find anything in the school that 'doesn't belong to this time'—bring it back to me. I'll pay better than anyone." This is the Collector faction's first appearance.
    Suggested DC: Negotiation (NEG) Difficulty (DC) 15 to pry more info from the agent—the Collector is gathering "Time Anomalies," items that shouldn't exist in the current timeline. Perception (PER) Difficulty (DC) 14 to notice the agent's expression flicker when mentioning the school library.

  2. Dawnlight Elementary—School Gate
    The characters arrive at the school. The guard at the gate is frozen before the surveillance monitor, which still records the moment of the Freeze. If the characters check the monitor, they see the chaos on campus at the Freeze—and one detail: the library emitted a brief blue flash at the moment of the Freeze.
    Key Encounter: The first "school bell" rings—a Time Echo. All characters need Guts (GUT) Difficulty (DC) 13 to avoid trance.
    Suggested DC: Tech (TEC) Difficulty (DC) 12 to start the monitor playback; Guts (GUT) Difficulty (DC) 13 to resist the bell's trance effect.

  3. Classroom Building—Third-Grade Classroom
    This is the core of the main-plot clue. The star chart on the blackboard perfectly matches the old man's draft—but more detailed, more precise. The teacher is partially frozen, his fingers trembling slightly, as if trying to complete a final mark. Words on the blackboard: "He is watching us from there."
    Key Encounter: The characters must record the star chart without thawing the teacher. If they try to thaw (or move) the teacher, the whole classroom of students will begin to thaw—triggering mass panic.
    Suggested DC: Perception (PER) Difficulty (DC) 15 to record the complete star chart (needs drawing tools or memory); Negotiation (NEG) Difficulty (DC) 18 to attempt extremely limited communication with the teacher without thawing (he can only move his eyes—yes/no answers).

  4. Library—Time Memories
    The library is where all "Time Memory" texts are concentrated. The books' contents have all been rewritten into different versions of the same sentence: "Time remembers everything." But reading carefully, the characters find longer information hidden in the texts—when multiple fragments are pieced together, they form a coherent narrative.
    Key Encounter: Reading Time Memory texts for long periods requires Perception (PER) Difficulty (DC) 14 to avoid confusion (distinguishing true info from noise generated by Time Memory).
    Suggested DC: Perception (PER) Difficulty (DC) 14 to filter valuable text fragments; Tech (TEC) Difficulty (DC) 16 to use equipment to magnify the texts' microscopic textures (higher-quality info).

  5. Gymnasium—The Backward Scoreboard
    The scoreboard flickers endlessly between 47:42 and 42:47. This is not a malfunction—the time on the scoreboard is genuinely running backward. By observing the scoreboard's flicker pattern (Tech (TEC) Difficulty (DC) 15), the characters can discover that every 13 flickers, the scoreboard briefly shows a complete image—a panorama of the gymnasium at the Freeze moment, including every face in the stands.
    Key Encounter: If the characters look carefully at the spectator panorama (Perception (PER) Difficulty (DC) 18), they find a discordant detail—a person who doesn't belong to this school sits in the last row of the stands. A figure in a black coat and hat, gazing at the court. This figure is not frozen—in the brief panorama moment, he moves.
    Suggested DC: Tech (TEC) Difficulty (DC) 15 to analyze the scoreboard flicker pattern; Perception (PER) Difficulty (DC) 18 to spot the figure in black.

  6. Boiler Room—Heat Source Underground
    The custodian's maintenance log records a disturbing discovery: "Anomalous heat source detected in underground pipes—not the boiler. Depth about 30 meters. Temperature stable at body heat. Possibly an underground water source—but water isn't that warm."
    Key Encounter: Boiler maintenance challenge—while exploring the boiler room, boiler pressure begins to rise, requiring Tech (TEC) Difficulty (DC) 14 maintenance to avoid overheating explosion.
    Suggested DC: Tech (TEC) Difficulty (DC) 14 to maintain the boiler; Perception (PER) Difficulty (DC) 16 to find extra notes lightly penciled on a blank page of the custodian's log—"Heard the sound again last night. From underground. Not mechanical. Like a heartbeat."

Key Ethical Choice:
"The Choice in the Classroom"—In the third-grade classroom, the star chart on the blackboard is the key clue. But the only way to get the complete star chart is to thaw the teacher (let him complete the final mark), or risk recording it incompletely. Thawing the teacher will trigger the whole classroom of students to thaw—the students may suffer irreversible psychological trauma (they experienced the Freeze while conscious; reawakening may cause extreme panic).
- Option A—Risky Thaw: Use a Defrost Agent to release the teacher. Gain complete star chart info (Genesis Hypothesis fragment 2/3 full version). But the classroom's student group (about 30 children) begins to thaw. The characters must try to soothe them (Negotiation (NEG) Difficulty (DC) 18 group soothing, or soothing one by one takes time but lower DC). Failure means the students fall into mass panic—possibly injured, possibly fleeing the classroom into more dangerous surroundings. Consequence: Lakeheart City learns of this, character reputation damaged.
- Option B—Partial Record: Record the star chart as completely as possible without thawing. Gain partial star chart info (incomplete—about 30% missing). Avoid facing the consequence of the student group thawing. But later need to complete the star chart from other sources (increases difficulty in Session 6 harbor and Session 8 lab—because info is incomplete, characters need extra exploration and reasoning).
- Option C (Hidden)—Use Time Crystals: If the characters accumulated enough Time Crystal Shards from previous adventures (at least 5), they can try to use Crystals to stabilize the classroom's time field—creating a small time buffer zone. Tech (TEC) Difficulty (DC) 16. Success allows thawing only the teacher without thawing other areas of the classroom.

Loot and Rewards: - Time Crystal: 12 Time Crystal Shards per person + 2 Time Crystal Shards found in boiler room (custodian's private stash) - Milestone: Assembling the first Time Memory info—unlock the "Time Memory" mechanic (characters can actively search for Time Memories in later campaign to gain info) - Story Clues: Complete/partial star chart (Genesis Hypothesis fragment 2/3), the figure in black (Silent Hand Church or Collector leadership—revealed in later campaign), the "heartbeat" underground (points to deeper wasteland—Session 8 or 10 clue), custodian's heat source record (points to some life or device underground)

Ending Branches: - If the star chart is complete → Session 6 harbor's observation station data can directly correspond, greatly reducing difficulty. - If the star chart is incomplete → Session 6 needs extra exploration and puzzle-solving to complete the info. - If the characters discovered the figure in black → Unlocks a subplot—a mysterious figure moving between Freeze Zones, observing everything. Characters may rediscover his trail in later sessions (Session 5 power plant, Session 8 lab). - If the characters accepted the Collector's Agent's deal → If they bring out "Time Memory" texts from the school and give them to the Collector, gain extra reward (15 Time Crystal Shards per person), but lose the chance to keep the texts themselves (the text info helps later puzzle-solving). Also opens the Collector faction relationship.

Act II: Going Deeper

The ice grows deeper. You begin to see what lies beneath the Freeze—not treasure, not supplies, but truth. It is not beautiful. But you have gone too far to turn back.

Session 4: A Heartbeat Under the Scalpel

Suggested Game Session: 4th
Difficulty: Intermediate
Freeze Zone: St. Mary's Memorial Hospital (Sample 04)
Estimated Time: 5–7 hours

Briefing: The character team's reputation in Lakeheart City keeps growing. One day, a contact from the Research Society—a middle-aged researcher named Carl—approaches the team. His research team, analyzing Time Memory texts, discovered a recurring reference: "St. Mary's Memorial Hospital—Operating Room 3—the Seed of Time." Carl believes the "Seed of Time" may be an extremely rare Time Crystal form, crucial to understanding the Freeze. He is willing to pay a high reward (20 Time Crystal Shards per person + Research Society favor) and provides a hospital security pass (bypasses some administrative security). Meanwhile, if the characters rescued the child in Session 1, they receive news from the orphanage: new leads on the child's father—a harbor worker—whose medical records are at St. Mary's Hospital.

Mission Objectives: - Primary Objective: Reach Operating Room 3, investigate the "Seed of Time," and go to the morgue to confirm its existence and state - Optional Objective A: Obtain rare drugs from the pharmacy (can craft special medical supplies, permanently increasing the team's medical capability) - Optional Objective B: Search the administrative office for the "Anomalous Cases List"—the Research Society will pay extra - Optional Objective C: Resolve the "Boy on the Operating Table" dilemma in the operating room (see the ethical choice in Sample 04) - Optional Objective D (if child not rescued in Session 1): In the inpatient ward, find the "Child in the Cart" brought by other Cold Walkers—she has been transferred to the hospital for observation. A chance to make amends.

Scene Flow:

  1. Research Society Base—Carl's Commission
    Carl is an overly enthusiastic researcher, his obsession with Time Crystals nearly pathological. "You know, Time Crystals aren't minerals—they're solidified memory. Every Crystal stores a fragment of someone's consciousness from the Freeze moment." His words are both information and foreshadowing.
    Key Encounter: Carl shows the characters a Time Crystal he privately holds—through special equipment, a blurry human face can be seen inside the Crystal. Carl: "This Crystal came from the supermarket you visited before. This person—this person is still inside the Crystal."
    Suggested DC: Perception (PER) Difficulty (DC) 16 to identify the face in the Crystal (possibly a Frozen the characters saw in the supermarket). Negotiation (NEG) Difficulty (DC) 14 to get Carl to reveal more about the "Time Memory" theory.

  2. St. Mary's Hospital—Lobby and ER
    The characters arrive at the hospital. The frozen scene in the ER is unsettling—chaotic waiting patients, patients on stretchers, a nurse frozen mid-run.
    Key Encounter: Thawing patients—3 patients partially thaw as the characters approach, repeating pre-Freeze actions. This is a non-combat encounter—characters need to bypass or soothe them.
    Suggested DC: Negotiation (NEG) Difficulty (DC) 16 to soothe a single patient; Speed (SPD) Difficulty (DC) 14 to bypass patients without triggering more thaws.

  3. Inpatient Ward—Reunion or Discovery
    Depends on the Session 1 choice.
    If the child was rescued: The characters find a medical record in the inpatient ward—the child's father was treated here (work injury). The record has a contact address—pointing to the harbor (Session 6).
    If the child was not rescued: The characters find the child transferred here (she was rescued from the supermarket by other Cold Walkers). She is now cared for by a volunteer at the hospital—this volunteer is a member of the "Antiquities Authority" (new faction appears). This is an emotionally charged scene.

  4. Operating Room—Seed of Time and the Boy
    Operating Room 3. A heart surgery frozen at the critical moment. Beneath the shadowless lamp over the operating table, the characters find the first clue to the "Seed of Time"—the surgery record mentions an "anomalous crystal" removed from near the boy's heart. The crystal was sent to the morgue for analysis.
    Key Encounter: The Boy on the Operating Table—see the ethical choice in Sample 04.
    Suggested DC: Tech (TEC) Difficulty (DC) 18 (use anesthetic + careful scalpel removal); Perception (PER) Difficulty (DC) 16 to find key info in the surgery record.

  5. Morgue—The Seed of Time
    The morgue is the most dangerous area in the whole hospital. Anti-thaw effect, Time Parasites—and the metal cabinet at the deepest point.
    Key Encounter: Time Parasite ×2. The metal cabinet needs Tech (TEC) Difficulty (DC) 18 or finding the morgue administrator's key (in the administrative office) to open. Inside is a sealed glass container, suspended within which is a grain-of-rice-sized black crystal—the "Seed of Time." Label on the container: "Sample #0001—Source: human cardiac tissue—Note: self-growing—indestructible."
    Suggested DC: Tech (TEC) Difficulty (DC) 18 to open the metal cabinet; Guts (GUT) Difficulty (DC) 15 to resist the anti-thaw effect; after obtaining the Seed of Time, must evacuate immediately—holding it accelerates the characters' Freeze progression (each turn Guts (GUT) Difficulty (DC) 15 or gain 1 level of Freeze).

Key Ethical Choice:
"The Boy on the Operating Table"—see Sample 04 description. More details provided here: - Option A—Anesthesia Plan: Use anesthetic obtained from the pharmacy, anesthetize the boy first, then carefully remove the scalpel. Tech (TEC) Difficulty (DC) 18. Success means the boy is saved, can become an NPC in later campaign (he has unique perception of the "crystal in the heart"—can help the characters in Session 8 lab). Failure means the scalpel moves, the boy dies—the characters must bear great emotional consequence. - Option B—Give Up Rescue: Acknowledge insufficient ability to safely rescue the boy. Leave the operating room, let the boy remain frozen. This is a rational choice, but affects team morale. Meanwhile, if Lena is an ally (Session 2 Option A), she warns the characters: the Silent Hand Church already knows the characters are collecting "seeds," and they've sent their highest-level agents to the lab—"If you meet someone in red robes there—don't hesitate. They're not there to negotiate."

Mission Objectives: - Primary Objective: Enter the B5 Synthesis Level, obtain the Perfect Crystal (the Third Seed) - Optional Objective A: Reach B7 Eternal Moment, witness the moment the Freeze occurred (major plot reveal) - Optional Objective B: Obtain Dr. Vahl's black security card (for entering B7) and her research notes (containing the "Time Unification Field Theory") - Optional Objective C: Collect research data from each level to fully understand the theoretical basis of the "Five Thaw Rhythms" (crucial for the Session 9 Clock Tower challenges) - Optional Objective D (if Lena is an ally): Stop the Church agents from obtaining the Perfect Crystal before the characters

Scene Flow:

    Key Encounter: The characters can examine these relics for intel. The sole survivor of the third team carved a line on the entrance wall: "Don't look at the clock—the clock is also looking at you." His equipment is still there—including a weapon enhanced by Time Crystal (special item).
    Suggested DC: Perception (PER) Difficulty (DC) 15 to organize predecessors' notes (gain intel fragments about B2–B4 levels); Tech (TEC) Difficulty (DC) 14 to repair aleftover radio (can monitor the lab's still-operating internal comm channel).

B3's feed shows a sealed room (Project Stillwater)'s door is... slowly opening. B4's feed shows a flickering humanoid (Specimen-7) moving in the corridor. B7's feed is pure white—nothing visible.
    Suggested DC: Tech (TEC) Difficulty (DC) 16 to activate the surveillance system; combat or stealth to bypass Guard Automatons.

Attack is "Time Tentacle" (+7 to hit, can attack targets within 15 ft, 2d8+4 Mental Damage + paralysis 1 turn). Special ability "Time Refraction"—each turn can create two illusions of itself (false targets with Defense (AC) 18, disappear when hit). The lab's notes record Project Stillwater's goal: "Study Time Crystal's ability to preserve biological consciousness—preparation for long-term space travel." (This corresponds to the artificial structure in the star chart—is the Freeze related to space travel?)

  4. B4—Human Experiment Level
    Ethics Warning: The GM should explain to the players before entering this level that the scene contains potentially uncomfortable themes (non-consensual human experimentation). Players may choose to skip detailed descriptions, keeping only core info.
    Human experiments were conducted here. Specimen-7 operates on this level. The characters' goal is to obtain the blue security card (in the lab director's office).

If the characters try to communicate with him (Negotiation (NEG) Difficulty (DC) 22), he briefly regains sanity, speaking some info about the experiments. The priest uses Time Crystal-enhanced miracle abilities (spell-like).
    Suggested DC: Negotiation (NEG) Difficulty (DC) 20 to negotiate with the priest (if the characters can prove they're collecting seeds to enter the Clock Tower to "resolve" the Freeze, the priest may—small chance—agree to cooperate. DC drops to 16 if the characters show the other two seeds.); Tech (TEC) Difficulty (DC) 18 to safely remove the Perfect Crystal from the magnetic field (higher DC without special equipment).

    Dr. Vahl is in loop-freeze before her desk. The characters need to obtain the black security card within her 5-minute window of lucidity.
    Key Encounter: See the ethical choice in Sample 09—Dr. Vahl's dilemma. If the characters already have the Perfect Crystal, they can use it to stabilize Dr. Vahl's time state (similar to the Lighthouse Keeper's Option C), giving her more lucid time.
    Suggested DC: Negotiation (NEG) Difficulty (DC) 22 to persuade Dr. Vahl within 5 minutes (if using Perfect Crystal, drops to DC 16 and time extends to 30 minutes); Tech (TEC) Difficulty (DC) 19 to crack the time-locked safe for the "Time Unification Field Theory" document.

    If the characters obtained the black security card, they can enter B7—the lab 30 seconds before the Freeze.
    Key Encounter: This is the most revealing scene in the whole campaign. The characters witness firsthand the moment the Freeze occurred—the research team's experiment equipment suddenly lost control, an energy wave released from the equipment, passing through walls, spreading outward. But the key detail is: the energy wave is a "response" to an external signal—the equipment's screen shows "Detected external time field resonance—source: extraterrestrial coordinate—synchronizing—" then the Freeze occurs.
    This means the Freeze was not an experiment accident—the experiment equipment was merely "responding" to some signal from beyond the sky. The true source of the Freeze is in the stars. In the loop's next reset, the characters can see more detail—the chief researcher's expression at the Freeze moment is not fear, but... sudden realization. She seems to understand something.
    Suggested DC: Need to activate the experiment equipment after realizing the loop (Tech (TEC) Difficulty (DC) 20) to safely exit the loop—otherwise the characters may be trapped.

Key Ethical Choice:
In B7's eternal loop, the characters face a unique choice:
- Option A—Terminate the Loop: Activate the experiment equipment, permanently end B7's time loop. This releases the research team trapped in the loop—they will immediately Fully Freeze after the loop ends (their bodies can no longer sustain outside the loop). But this also means the experiment equipment is shut down—cannot be used again.
- Option B—Maintain the Loop: Leave B7, let the loop continue. The research team continues their eternal 30 seconds. But the experiment equipment's data may be crucial to understanding the Freeze—the characters can record the equipment's parameters and on-screen info, bring back to the Research Society for analysis.
- Option C (Hidden)—Modify the Loop: Use the Perfect Crystal to recalibrate the experiment equipment (Tech (TEC) Difficulty (DC) 24—extremely difficult). On success, the loop is no longer 30 seconds before the Freeze, but 30 years before the Freeze? Or 30 years after? The characters can choose to set the loop to point to some past or future time point. This is the most radical choice—the GM needs to determine specific consequences based on the time point the characters choose.

The characters are now ready to enter the Clock Tower - Story Clues: Project Stillwater's space travel goal (the Freeze may be a "preservation" mechanism—for some interstellar journey?), B7's revelation—the Freeze is a response to an extraterrestrial signal (ultimate plot reveal), Dr. Vahl's "Time Unification Field Theory" (full understanding of the Five Thaw Rhythms' physical principles—can apply this knowledge in Session 9 Clock Tower)

Ending Branches: - If the Perfect Crystal is obtained and the three seeds complete → Unlock Session 9 Clock Tower. The three seeds respectively represent: Seed of Time (time in life—from human body), Purified Core (time in energy—from geothermal and Crystal mixture), Perfect Crystal (pure time—artificially synthesized perfect form). - If the characters fully understand the Five Thaw Rhythms theory → The DC of each level's challenges in Session 9 Clock Tower drops by 2 (characters can predict and adapt to different thaw patterns). - If the characters terminated or modified the loop in B7 → Triggers a major world event—all time loops in the Frozen Wasteland begin to destabilize. Thaw behavior in other Freeze Zones may change anomalously. This gives the GM a chance to introduce new variables. - If the characters chose to maintain the loop in B7 → Keep B7 as a potential future resource point (if the characters need to use the experiment equipment again).

Session 9: For Whom the Bell Tolls

Suggested Game Session: 9th
Difficulty: Legendary
Freeze Zone: Eternal Spire (Sample 10)
Estimated Time: 10–14 hours (strongly recommended in 2–3 sessions)

Briefing: The three seeds are complete. The three Genesis Hypothesis fragments have converged into a complete truth—or at least part of it. The Clock Tower's location is confirmed—at the exact center of the wasteland, a place called the "Absolute Freeze Zone." All previous clues point here: the old man's dream, the school's star chart, the harbor's observation data, the lab's research—all converge atop the Clock Tower's spire.

But the characters are not the only ones who know the Clock Tower's location. The Silent Hand Church, the Collector, the Research Society—all forces are heading to the Clock Tower. Each force has its own view of "how the Freeze should end." When the characters arrive at the Clock Tower, they find a multi-sided standoff unfolding in the plaza outside the tower. The characters must decide who—if anyone—can be trusted.

Meanwhile, the clue of "The One You're Still Searching For" points in the same direction. Cross-referencing the nautical chart from the harbor and the lab's spacetime data, that person was employed by the Church before the Freeze, transporting something to the "Spire." He/she may be inside the Clock Tower—in some form.

Mission Objectives: - Primary Objective: Use the three seeds to enter the Clock Tower, climb to the Seventh Floor (top of the clock face), make the final choice - Optional Objective A: Resolve the multi-sided standoff in the plaza outside the tower—decide who can enter the tower - Optional Objective B: On the Third Floor (Time Heart) communicate with the "First Pilgrim"—gain a shortcut bypassing the Fourth and Fifth Floors (but at a cost—see Sample 10 ethical choice) - Optional Objective C: Find "The One You're Still Searching For"—he/she may be on some time level inside the tower - Optional Objective D: Collect "Timeline Fragments" in the Fourth Floor (Chain Gallery)—learn the futures different choices may lead to

Scene Flow:

  1. Plaza Outside the Tower—Multi-Sided Standoff
    The characters arrive at the edge of the Absolute Freeze Zone. In the plaza outside the tower, three forces have assembled:
  2. Silent Hand Church: Led by high priest Marcus, about 10 armed Church personnel. Their stance: "The Freeze is time's gift—should not be reversed. But the internal 'wrong Freeze' can be adjusted. We enter the tower not to end the Freeze, but to perfect it."
  3. The Collector: Led by the Collector himself (first appearance—a masked figure in ornate robes), about 6 mercenaries. Their stance: "What's inside the tower—whatever it is—belongs to collection. The Freeze's fate is irrelevant. What matters is the ownership of knowledge and items."
  4. Research Society: A small research team (4 people) led by Carl, equipped with scientific devices. Their stance: "We need to understand the tower's nature. Entering the tower is for research, not to change anything—unless we fully understand the consequences."

  5. Antiquities Authority: If the characters have good relations with Vera, she leads a small team to "ensure any change serves the settlements' interests."
    Key Encounter: The characters must mediate between these forces. Anyone entering the tower alone triggers other forces' displeasure—even violence. The characters must decide: ally with some force? Persuade everyone to wait? Or force a breakthrough?
    Suggested DC: Negotiation (NEG) Difficulty (DC) 24 to persuade all forces to a temporary agreement (extremely hard—but if a character has good reputation with all sides, DC can drop); combat plan—confront one or more forces (not recommended—the Church and Collector both have strong combat personnel).

  6. First Floor—Time Gate
    The characters use the three seeds to open the Time Gate. After the seeds embed into the three grooves on the door, the inscription lights up: "Time is not a river—time is an ocean. You swim in it, but the ocean is also within you." The door opens, the characters enter.
    Key Encounter: The door does not close after the characters pass—this means other forces may follow. The characters can choose to destroy the seeds (closing the door, but meaning they themselves cannot return through the door—unless they find another way out), or leave the seeds (keeping the door open).
    Suggested DC: Guts (GUT) Difficulty (DC) 18 to decide to destroy the hard-won three seeds (even though they've fulfilled their mission).

  7. Second Floor—Hall of Memories
    The characters' personal memories are displayed here one by one. Each character sees the memory most important to them—perhaps beautiful, perhaps painful. The GM should discuss with each player beforehand the character's most crucial memory. The Hall of Memories' challenge is not combat, but the characters must pass through their own memories without being trapped by them.
    Key Encounter: The memory of "The One You're Still Searching For"—the person the character seeks appears in the memory, vividly alive. That person in the memory may speak to the character—but are these words the true words from the memory, or the Clock Tower's deception?
    Suggested DC: Guts (GUT) Difficulty (DC) 18 (each memory level deeper +2) to not be trapped by memories; characters who successfully pass all memories gain the "Memory Walker" ability—gain Advantage when resisting Mental effects in the future.

  8. Third Floor—Time Heart and the First Pilgrim
    A giant Time Heart beats at the center. The First Pilgrim (see Sample 10) offers a shortcut here.
    Key Encounter: The First Pilgrim's offer—"Give me a part of your time, and I'll tell you how to bypass the two levels below, directly reaching the Sixth Floor." His true identity is—the Silent Hand Church's founding priest (corresponds to the fountain carving—"founding priest Eileen"—but actually the founding priest is male? Or is this name misunderstood?). He entered the tower seeking eternity, but was trapped beside the Time Heart, becoming the Heart's "Guardian."
    Suggested DC: Negotiation (NEG) Difficulty (DC) 22 to deeply communicate with the First Pilgrim, understand his true motive; accepting the shortcut means each character sacrifices 1d10 years of lifespan (actual game effect: the character's maximum Health permanently drops by 1d4, but gains the ability to directly reach the Sixth Floor).

  9. Fourth Floor—Chain Gallery (if no shortcut used)
    Countless possible futures unfold here. The characters need to find the correct path through the maze.
    Key Encounter: In one room, the characters see a possible future—themselves, standing atop the Clock Tower, making a choice. The choice's content varies by character, but the future scene's character expression—satisfied? despairing? fanatical?—will influence the character's judgment at the final choice.
    Suggested DC: Perception (PER) Difficulty (DC) 20 to locate the correct path in the maze (using the Five Rhythms knowledge from the lab lowers to DC 16); Guts (GUT) Difficulty (DC) 18 to not be distracted by possible futures.

  10. Fifth Floor—Abyss of Spasm (if no shortcut used)
    The most dangerous floor. Time fragments replay threats from past adventures.
    Key Encounter: The characters may encounter a "Time Shadow" here—themselves from a wrong timeline. This is a battle against the "self you might have become." The GM should shape the Time Shadow's image and abilities based on the character's choices in the campaign—if the character made dark choices, the Time Shadow is stronger; if the character remained morally consistent, the Time Shadow may instead become an ally.
    Suggested DC: Combat—the Time Shadow's stats match the character's but possess abilities the character didn't choose. Guts (GUT) Difficulty (DC) 20 to face the Time Shadow without wavering.

  11. Sixth Floor—Peak of Breath
    An hourglass keeps flipping here. This floor's challenge is time pressure—the characters must activate a device within the limited time of the hourglass flipping (5 turns) to open the passage to the Seventh Floor.
    Key Encounter: The Time Guardian's first form appears here—blocking the passage to the Seventh Floor. The Health (HP) 80 version (see Sample 10).
    Suggested DC: Tech (TEC) Difficulty (DC) 18 to activate the device (one attempt per turn); combat—Time Guardian ×1.

  12. Seventh Floor—Top of the Clock Face—Final Choice
    The characters finally reach the top of the Clock Tower. A giant clock face moves slowly overhead. At the center of the clock face is a groove—shaped for the three seeds. Here, the characters learn the full truth of the Freeze:

Truth Revealed: The Freeze was not an accident, not an attack, but a kind of "preservation." Before the Freeze, some extraterrestrial existence (the "Observer"—the existence at the celestial coordinate the school star chart points to) detected this world was about to face an unavoidable disaster (what exactly the disaster is is for the GM to decide—possibly an asteroid impact, possibly solar activity, possibly civilization's self-destruction in nuclear war). The Observer could not prevent the disaster, but it could "preserve" this world—freezing the whole world's time at the last moment before the disaster. The Clock Tower is the core of this preservation mechanism—it is the anchor of time.

Now the characters face the ultimate choice—the clock face's groove is prepared for the three seeds. Placing the seeds into the groove, the characters can:

  • Option A—Release Time (Reverse the Freeze): Place the seeds into the groove and turn the clock face clockwise. This reverses the Freeze—the whole world awakens from the Freeze, time resumes normal flow. But this means the disaster will happen along its original trajectory—the Observer's "preservation" will be lifted. The world gains time, but may lose the future.
  • Option B—Deepen the Freeze (Permanent Preservation): Place the seeds into the groove and turn the clock face counterclockwise. This makes the Freeze permanent—time will stay frozen at this moment forever. No one will wake again, no one will die again. The world becomes an eternal museum.
  • Option C—Reset (Return to Before the Freeze): Place the seeds into the groove and press the clock face's center. This resets time—the whole world returns to the moment before the Freeze. Everyone's memories will be lost. The characters won't remember each other, won't remember their adventures. But this also means the world has a chance to avoid the disaster—if the characters can find and warn people before the Freeze.
  • Option D—Replace (Become the New Observer): Instead of placing the seeds, inject the character's own time energy (life) into the clock face. This makes the character the new "Observer"—replacing the existence beyond the sky. The character gains the ability to manipulate time, but will be forever trapped in the Clock Tower, becoming the new Time Guardian. This is the most extreme choice—one character's sacrifice in exchange for complete control over the Freeze.
  • Option E—Destroy (End Everything): Use the three seeds' energy to counter-impact the clock face, destroying the Clock Tower and the entire time preservation system. The Freeze moment is lifted, but the time structure also collapses—no one knows what happens next. This is the most unpredictable choice.

Key Encounter: The Time Guardian's final form (Health (HP) 120 version—see Sample 10) conducts the final battle here. But combat is not mandatory—the Time Guardian gives the characters a chance to state their choice. If the characters' choice is persuasive (the character must truly believe in their choice—Negotiation (NEG) Difficulty (DC) determined by the GM based on roleplay quality), the Time Guardian may allow them to make the choice without combat. If the characters cannot reach consensus (internal team division), the Time Guardian will require them to "prove" their resolve through combat.

Loot and Rewards: - Time Crystal: immeasurable—atop the clock face the characters can sense all the Time Crystal energy inside the tower - Milestone: Complete the Clock Tower—this is a character-defining moment. Whatever ending chosen, the characters enter the Legendary - Story Clues: the full truth of the Freeze (or partial truth—the GM can keep some mysteries), the Observer's nature (extraterrestrial existence—its true form and motive can be set by the GM)

Ending Branches (affecting the whole world):
- Option A (Release Time) → The world begins to thaw. The wasteland begins to recover. But the disaster's threat reappears—the characters and all survivors must face the coming disaster. This creates a brand-new threat for later campaign (if players wish to continue). - Option B (Deepen the Freeze) → The Freeze is permanent. The characters become among the last few able to move freely. They can choose to roam the eternal wasteland—or find a way to enter the Freeze themselves. - Option C (Reset) → Time returns to before the Freeze. The characters can try to warn the world—but no one will believe them. They live in the past with memories of the future—blessing or curse? - Option D (Replace) → One character becomes the new Observer. The Freeze can be finely controlled—specific areas thaw, specific people thaw. The world gains a new "god of time." - Option E (Destroy) → The time structure collapses. The GM decides what happens next based on their own design—this can be anything, from "apocalypse" to "brand-new beginning."

Final Act: The Choice

Some choices are bigger than the world. Some answers heavier than the questions. At the end of time, what you save is not the world—but yourself.

Session 10: The Full Picture of Time

Suggested Game Session: 10th
Difficulty: Legendary
Freeze Zone: Genesis Corridor (Sample 11)
Estimated Time: 8–12 hours (recommended in two sessions)

Briefing: Session 9's choice changed the world—but left many mysteries unanswered. Whatever ending the characters chose, one question remained unanswered: what exactly is the "Observer"? Why did it choose this world? The Genesis Corridor—the ancient tunnel system at the deepest point of the wasteland—is said to preserve all memories since the world's birth. Here, the characters can find the ultimate answer about the Observer, and—perhaps—a chance to converse with the Observer.

This session's timing depends on Session 9's ending: - If Release Time (A) was chosen → Session 10 happens days after thawing begins, the characters need to understand the Observer's nature to face the coming disaster. - If Deepen the Freeze (B) was chosen → Session 10 happens within the eternal Freeze, the characters search for meaning in endless time. - If Reset (C) was chosen → Session 10 happens in the post-reset world, the characters retain past-life memories, searching the Genesis Corridor to confirm if everything is real. - If Replace (D) was chosen → In Session 10, the new Observer (one of the characters) guides the others to the Genesis Corridor, to understand the full meaning of their new role. - If Destroy (E) was chosen → Session 10 happens in the chaos of time collapse, the Genesis Corridor possibly the only stable place.

Mission Objectives: - Primary Objective: Reach the Genesis Wall, view the full picture of time, understand the Observer's nature - Optional Objective A: Converse with the "Future Self" in the Sea of Possibilities—gain revelation about the character's personal fate - Optional Objective B: Cross the Void Rift—tests the character's resolve and teamwork - Optional Objective C: Awaken a predecessor before the Genesis Wall—gain knowledge from a past era - Optional Objective D: Search the Genesis Wall for the "Dark Outline Beyond the Boundary"—understand what is approaching the wasteland

Scene Flow:

  1. Throat of Forgetting—Entrance
    The characters arrive at the Genesis Corridor's entrance. The predecessor camp at the entrance provides the last warning about the corridor's interior.
    Key Encounter: The newest note in the camp was written by a "Collector"—he arrived a few days before the characters. The note reads: "What's inside isn't treasure. It's answers. But the price of answers is—you will never be able to pretend you didn't know." The note's last page is torn off—leaving only half a sentence: "The Genesis Wall told me—it is not the Observer. It is—"
    Suggested DC: Perception (PER) Difficulty (DC) 18 to find the torn half-page in the camp (tucked in a book)—it reads: "—a mirror. The Observer is our own—" (unfinished).

  2. Corridor of History—Memories of Time
    The characters pass through tunnels displaying the world's history. These images provide the characters new understanding of the world's nature.
    Key Encounter: In the Corridor of History, the characters see a key scene before the Freeze—an event they didn't know before. The specific content is for the GM to decide based on unresolved questions in the campaign. Possibly: the Church's founding, the Collector's true identity, the Observer's first contact, or a secret in some character's personal history.
    Suggested DC: Perception (PER) Difficulty (DC) 20 to find a hidden detail in the historical images (requires extremely careful observation).

  3. The Beginning of Freeze—Source of Energy
    The walls here show the moment the Freeze occurred—the energy wave came from beyond the sky, struck the Clock Tower, then spread outward. But the key detail is: before striking the Clock Tower, the energy wave was "guided" by the Clock Tower—the Clock Tower is not a passive receiver, but an active receiving device. This means the Clock Tower may have been built in an earlier era, specifically to receive the Observer's energy wave.
    Key Encounter: The characters discover the Clock Tower's builders were not human—at least not humans of the current civilization. Images of the builders show them as a highly advanced ancient civilization that foresaw the Observer's arrival and built the Clock Tower as a "gift"—or a "trap."
    Suggested DC: Tech (TEC) Difficulty (DC) 18 to analyze the Clock Tower's construction technology (far beyond current civilization level); Perception (PER) Difficulty (DC) 16 to identify the builders' physiological traits (they differ subtly from modern humans—taller, slimmer, larger eyes).

  4. Sea of Possibilities—Future Self
    The characters meet here their selves from possible futures.
    Key Encounter: Each character's "Future Self" is different—depending on the choice they made in Session 9 and their personal journey in the campaign. The Future Self may offer warning, advice, comfort, or challenge. This is the most personal scene—the GM needs to conduct this conversation individually with each player.
    Suggested DC: Guts (GUT) Difficulty (DC) 18 to face the Future Self without being overwhelmed by "possible futures"; Negotiation (NEG) Difficulty (DC) 20 to persuade the Future Self to provide key info.

  5. Void Rift—Ultimate Test
    A 50-foot-wide Void Rift stretches ahead. No time, no space, no existence—pure void.
    Key Encounter: Crossing the rift needs Time Crystals to create a Time Bridge. This requires teamwork—one person maintains the bridge (each turn consumes 1 Time Crystal Shard + Guts (GUT) Difficulty (DC) 18 concentration), others cross quickly. The rift tries to attract the characters—each person crossing hears the void's whisper, promising "an end to pain," "eternal peace," and "answers to all questions."
    Suggested DC: Guts (GUT) Difficulty (DC) 18 to maintain the Time Bridge; Guts (GUT) Difficulty (DC) 20 to resist the void's temptation; Speed (SPD) Difficulty (DC) 16 to cross the bridge quickly (the bridge shortens 5 ft each turn).

  6. Genesis Wall—The Full Picture of Time
    The characters finally reach the Genesis Wall. The giant crystal wall displays a live panorama of the wasteland world. The protruding crystal at the wall's center lets the characters "enter" any scene to observe. But most important—the Genesis Wall's edge. There, the characters can see a giant dark outline slowly approaching the wasteland's boundary. The outline's shape cannot be determined—it is too large to fully present in one view. But it is moving. It is approaching.
    Key Encounter: The Genesis Wall's whisper asks each character the same question: "Are you willing to see everything?" (see the ethical choice in Sample 11.) Meanwhile, the characters can choose to touch the Genesis Wall's edge area, trying to understand the approaching dark outline's nature—this needs Guts (GUT) Difficulty (DC) 24 (may cause brief mental breakdown, but gains ultimate info).
    Suggested DC: Guts (GUT) Difficulty (DC) 24 to understand the dark outline's nature (on success the character gains a "cognition" that cannot be put into words—they know what it is, but cannot explain it to others); Guts (GUT) Difficulty (DC) 20 to resist the Genesis Wall's whisper.

Key Ethical Choice:
"The Genesis Wall's Whisper"—Each character independently faces the Genesis Wall's question: "Are you willing to see everything?"
- Option A—Accept: Gain the "Genesis Sight" ability (permanent), but each use needs Guts (GUT) Difficulty (DC) 20 or fall into 1 hour of "Time Immersion." The character will be able to sense the flow of time anywhere—including past, present, and possible futures. But the burden of this knowledge is immense—the character will never be able to "not know."
- Option B—Refuse: Unaffected. The character keeps their original state. But they will not gain the ultimate knowledge the Genesis Wall provides—including the dark outline's nature and the Observer's true intent.
- Option C (Hidden)—Ask: Neither accept nor refuse, but ask the Genesis Wall their own question. The Genesis Wall will answer—but the answer may not be what the character wants to hear. Each character can ask one question; the GM is free to decide the Genesis Wall's answer (suggested: reveal truth while keeping mystery).

Loot and Rewards: - Time Crystal: The Crystals in the Genesis Corridor are endless—but should the characters take them? Taking Crystals from the Genesis Corridor damages the corridor's integrity (each character can safely take 1d4 Time Crystals without obvious harm) - Milestone: Reach the Genesis Wall—complete the "Road of Ice" campaign. The characters become Legendary, their story will spread through the wasteland - Story Clues: the dark outline's nature (GM's ultimate reveal—this may be: another approaching Observer? the Freeze's creator? remnants of the ancient civilization? Or something entirely different?), the "Genesis Sight" holder's new understanding of time

Finale—The Character's Legendary Ending:
At Session 10's end, the GM should review each character's journey with each player. The character starts as a Cold Walker searching for canned goods in a supermarket, to standing before the Genesis Wall gazing at the full picture of time—this is a complete arc. The GM should provide each character a personalized ending description, reflecting their choices, growth, and sacrifice in the campaign.
