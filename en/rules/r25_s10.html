Scenario 5: Passing On the Hearthfire

Basic Info

Item Content
Difficulty ★★★★★ Legendary (Solo)
Suggested Reputation 70+
Length 8-12 Turns
Core Theme Legacy, leaving something for the world

Story Summary

Your shop is no longer "that little corner shop." Your name has spread across Ynserghard. Young craftsmen take you as their role model. The Hearthside Circle elects you as an honorary elder.

But you begin to ask yourself a question: on the day you no longer stand before this hearth—what will become of this shop?

The Wanderer God tore the recipe to pieces. He doesn't want someone who retrieves the recipe—He wants someone who passes the recipe on.

Now it's your turn.

Key NPCs

NPC Role
Hearthside Circle delegation Young craftsmen from the Five Major Regions—they are here to learn
Your companion They too have reached their own crossroads
Future apprentice (custom) A new young character—shaped jointly by you and the GM. He/she will inherit your shop

Phase Design

Phase 1: The Legacy Gathering (Turns 1-4)

The Hearthside Circle invites you to host the first "Legacy Gathering." Five young craftsmen from the Five Major Regions will come to your shop to learn from you for a week.

The GM creates five apprentices (using NPC Companion Cards), each representing a Class/Region combination. The player mentors one apprentice per day, one teaching scene per day.

Apprentice From Class Trait
Ada Anvilhearth Smith Too forceful, always breaks things—but she says "Break it ten times and one will be right"
Noah Seabreeze Merchant Too good at calculating, but doesn't know how to talk to people
Luna Verdantvale Alchemist Perfect pharmacological knowledge—but she's afraid of fire
Carl Watchkeep Gatherer Extremely high hunting talent—but he hunts too much, no restraint
Mira Royal Capital Cook Perfect technique—but her dishes have no "heart"

Teaching Mechanics: Each teaching scene = one 3d6 + player's primary attribute Check (DC 12). Success → apprentice learns something (gains +1 future potential). Critical Success → apprentice has a breakthrough realization. At the end of each apprentice, the player gives him/her a line of words—this becomes the guide for that apprentice's future path.

Phase 2: The Masterwork (Turns 5-7)

After the Legacy Gathering, the player decides to craft a "Masterwork"—a work there is only one of in a lifetime, left for the world.

This is not a commercial work. Its price is priceless. Its only quality goal is one: Legendary.

The GM gives the player the greatest narrative space: - What Legendary material to use? (choose from the Material Codex) - What story or memory to imbue it with? - Where will it be placed? (museum? Hearthside Circle headquarters? or just stay in your shop?)

Crafting Difficulty (DC) 18. Requires Legendary (DC+15 and Critical Success). The player may use all accumulated resources, the full assistance of companions, and the inspiration brought by apprentices at the Legacy Gathering (each successfully mentored apprentice → +1 to this crafting).

Phase 3: The Final Decision (Turns 8-10)

After the Masterwork is complete, the player makes the ultimate decision:

Option Content
Take an Apprentice and Teach Choose one of the five apprentices (or find another). Train wholeheartedly. After the character retires, continue the game using the disciple character.
Write a Book Write a lifetime of craft into a book. Centuries later young craftsmen still learn from it.
Pass to Companion Your old companion takes over the shop. You step behind the scenes, occasionally come for a cup of tea.
Carry On No grand ending. Still open the shop tomorrow. The hearth will still burn. This is the best legacy.

The ending is not written by the scenario. The ending is chosen by the player.

Ending Scene: The Wanderer God's Last Line

Whatever the player chooses, in the final scene, the GM describes:

That night, you close the shop door last. The street is very quiet. You look back once at the hearth.

Then you recall the line someone told you long ago—what the Wanderer God said when He tore the recipe to pieces and scattered it across the land.

"The best dish is always the next one."

But that wasn't all the Wanderer God said. He said another line too. That line isn't in any book—

It's in every act of teaching. In the day every apprentice learns their first recipe. In every little shop with its lamp lit.

You smile. Close the door.

The lamp still burns.
