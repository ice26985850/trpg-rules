Scenario 5: Dark Continent — The Other Side of the Gate

Growth Stage: Star Hunter ~ Legendary (151-350+ XP) Suggested Players: 4-5 Estimated Duration: 12-20 hours (can split into 3-4 sessions) Prerequisites: Complete Scenario 4, or the GM arranges another way to obtain the "Gate" coordinates Warning: This is endgame content. The PC's risk of death is extremely high.

Background

Three hundred years ago, the V5 signed the Dark Continent Silence Treaty. Since then, the "Dark Continent" was only a legend — a ghost story to scare young Hunters.

But the words on that slab changed everything.

They point to a coordinate on Lake Mobius — where, it is said, a "Gate" exists.

The Association split over this. The cautious faction demanded the news be sealed; the expansionist faction advocated organizing an expedition immediately. Eventually a compromise was reached: send a small advance team — you.

The mission briefing contains only one sentence: "Find the Gate. Record everything. Return alive."

Scene 1: The Voyage — Lake Mobius

Duration: 120 min Type: Exploration / Survival

Voyage Method (PC choice)

Method Cost Risk Advantage
Association ship Free (provided by Association) Monitored by V5 Highest safety
Kakin Kingdom ship Intel exchange Political entanglement Most familiar route
Hire private ship 500,000 J Variable crew quality Full autonomy
Fly yourself (Nen ability) SP cost No resupply Most covert

Voyage Event Table (d8, roll once per 2 hours)

d8 Event
1-2 Beast attack — flying beast × d3 (CR 3-5) attacks the ship
3 Abnormal weather — dense fog causes loss of direction (Navigation check DC 18)
4 Nen field interference — SP cost doubled within area (lasts 1 hour)
5 Ghost ship — find an empty V5 ship with logs from an expedition lost 20 years ago
6 Calm — no event, may take a long rest
7 Watcher contact — the "Gatekeeper" of Lake Mobius (see below)
8 Two consecutive events — roll d8 once then d8 again

NPC: "Gatekeeper" Ong

An old man suddenly appears on the lake surface. He stands on a piece of driftwood, no sail, no engine, just floating.

He looks at least two hundred years old. But when he speaks, his voice is like a thirty-year-old's — clear, powerful, with overwhelming authority.

"Go back." "There is nothing behind the Gate you want." "I've seen five expedition teams go in. Not one came out — whole, as a person."

He looks into your eyes. Those eyes reflect not threat — but weariness. "But if you insist —"

He offers a scale. Black, cold, metallic to the touch. "Take this. The Gate knows me. Perhaps… it will show you mercy."

Gatekeeper's Scale: The bearer gains one automatic success on Survival checks in the Dark Continent (one-time; the scale shatters after use).

Scene 2: The Gate — Trial at the Entrance

Duration: 90 min Type: Puzzle / Survival

Gate Description

It is not the gate you imagined.

A "water wall" rises from the lake surface — a waterfall flowing upward from Lake Mobius, vanishing into the clouds. At the center of the water wall is a black circular void that reflects no light.

As you approach, you hear whispering. From all directions, from inside your own mind.

It speaks in your ear — in a language you don't understand. But you somehow grasp its meaning:

"Prove."

The Gate's Trials (five "Proofs"; pass any three to enter)

The Gate actively chooses the trial content — the GM need not design it; just execute per the rules below.

# Proof Rule (GM executes directly) Phenomenon after passing
1 Proof of Strength The PC makes a full-power attack on the void in the Gate. Single hit ≥ 30 damage passes (Ten, Ko, Nen ability may be used). If no one meets it after all try, the Gate gives a second chance — but the damage threshold rises to 40. Golden ripples appear around the void
2 Proof of Wisdom Ancient script appears on the Gate's surface. The PC makes an Intelligence (Nen Knowledge) DC 22 check. Can cooperate — one main check, others assist (assistants need Intelligence 12+). If failed, may study 1 hour (re-roll, DC drops to 18). A line of text appears: "Worthy of conversation"
3 Proof of Will The PC stands before the void, enduring the Gate's mental pressure. Make Will save DC 25, must succeed 3 times consecutively. Each failure deals 1d4 mental damage; may choose to withdraw. Different PCs may relay — A succeeds N times then B continues, but DC +1 for the new relay. Whisper turns to ancient song
4 Proof of Connection The Gate projects the PC's memory fragments — roll d8 per the "Memory Fragment Table" below to decide which past appears. The PC must face this memory and respond (no roll needed). If the PC chooses to flee (turns away or silent over 30 seconds), this proof permanently fails for that PC, and a teammate must take over. The void begins to expand, edges no longer waver
5 Proof of Protection The Gate releases "trial phantoms" — CR 6 ×2 phantom creatures (data below), attacking the PC "chosen" by the Gate (the one who faced the memory in Proof of Connection). All may join to protect that ally. Pass by defeating all phantoms before the protected one's HP drops to 0. All whispers vanish, void stabilizes

Design logic: Proof of Protection triggers last — it needs Proof of Connection to first select the "one watched by the Gate." If the PC tries the other four proofs first, Proof of Protection becomes the fifth (and most dramatic) proof. Any three of five lets you enter. Passing all five doesn't affect entry, only adds ritual weight.

Proof of Connection — Memory Fragment Table (GM rolls d8, no design needed)

d8 Memory Fragment Content What the PC must face
1 Moment of Failure: The PC's most painful failure relived — a failed mission, a fallen comrade, an unkept promise. Self-blame and regret
2 Scar of Betrayal: Someone once trusted turning away or a knife in the back, clear as yesterday. Doubt of human nature
3 Lost Beloved: The one you'll never see again, standing in the Gate's illusion, saying to the PC: "Why couldn't you save me?" Sorrow and helplessness
4 Root of Fear: The PC's deepest fear made tangible — perhaps an enemy, a situation, or their own dark side. Fear itself
5 Unspoken Words: Words the PC always wanted to say to someone but never did, thrown back by the other's phantom in the illusion. Regret and unfinished business
6 Past Choice: The biggest crossroads of life relived — the Gate shows the PC "what if you'd chosen the other path." Doubt of one's own choices
7 Abyss of Loneliness: The PC stands alone in the void; all teammates, all connections, all support gone. Loneliness and abandonment
8 Emptiness of the End: The PC sees the scene after achieving all goals — then finds nothing has changed. Existential void

Phantom Guards (for Proof of Protection only; GM uses the data directly)

Attribute Value
Challenge Rating (CR) 6 (two appear at once)
HP 80 each
AC 14 (immune to physical attacks; only Nen attacks can hit)
Attack Phantom impact: d20+7 to hit, 2d8 Nen damage
Tactics The two guards pincer the Gate-chosen PC from both sides. If intercepted by others, one continues attacking the original target, the other turns to the interceptor.
Weakness Only attacks the Gate-chosen one; won't proactively attack other PCs.

Gatekeeper Scale Effect

If the PC carries the Gatekeeper's Scale, the Gate's trial DCs are all −3, and only 2 Proofs are needed to enter.

Scene 3: Beyond the Gate — Crystallized Wasteland

Duration: 180 min Type: Exploration / Survival / Combat

Crystallized Wasteland Environment

Beyond the Gate is not another world — at least not the kind you imagined.

You stand on an endless plain. The ground is translucent crystal; deep below your feet light flows slowly — like a sleeping river. The wind in the air is still, but you see a "storm" in the distance: countless glowing fragments spinning, like a silent fireworks.

Then you notice your left hand — the fingertips have begun to slightly turn transparent.

Crystallization Syndrome Rules

  • After entering the Crystallized Wasteland, roll Fortitude save DC 16 each hour
  • First failure: Dexterity −1
  • Second failure: Dexterity −2, move speed −5
  • Third failure: Dexterity −3, Perception −2, partial finger crystallization
  • Fourth failure: Whole body begins crystallizing; must leave the wasteland within 1 hour or use Nen ability healing
  • Fifth failure: Full crystallization — character petrifies, equivalent to death (recoverable via Legendary-tier Nen ability or the Dark Continent's "Reverse-Crystal Flower")

Healing methods: - In Zetsu state, save DC −2 - Emission healing Nen ability grants one re-roll - The rare "Reverse-Crystal Flower" in the Crystallized Wasteland (find difficulty: Survival DC 20) fully heals and grants 12-hour immunity

Crystallized Wasteland Exploration (optional goals)

The PC need not explore all zones. Optional goals below:

Zone A: Hill of Memory Crystal

A small hill made of pure silver crystal. Each crystal seals a "memory" inside — you see an ancient Hunter's final moment, the birth of a Dark Continent creature, the dusk of a long-extinct civilization.

Obtainable item: Memory Crystal Shard (consumable; after use may ask the GM one question, the GM must give a true answer — but the answer may be incomplete)

Guardian: Crystal Guardian Beast (CR 10) - HP 120, AC 18 - Attack: Crystal Shard Scatter (range 10m, 3d8 piercing, Reflex DC 17 for half) - Special: When taking physical damage, deals 1d6 reflection damage to the attacker (crystal burst)

Zone B: Ancient Civilization Ruins

At the center of the crystal plain lies a buried pyramid. Its architectural style belongs to no known civilization. A steady blue light radiates from its top — some energy source that has never gone out in three hundred years.

Obtainable item: Eternal Crystal (Legendary-tier Nen equipment material)

Internal threats: - Autonomous Guard ×4 (CR 7, ancient-tech construct, no Nen but with energy shield — auto-recovers 5 HP each round) - Central Core (CR 11, Boss tier) — choose between "technical hack" (Intelligence DC 22, need 3 successes) or "forceful destruction"

Zone C: Reverse-Crystal Spring

At the center of a crystallized forest, a small pool of uncrystallized liquid. It reflects a rainbow glow belonging to no light source.

Effect: Fully heals Crystallization Syndrome, grants 12-hour immunity. The pool water is enough for only one use each time (total 3 uses).

Guardian: Crystal Dragon (CR 13) - HP 250, AC 20 - Constitution 20, Willpower 20 - Dragon Breath: Crystallization Ray (line 30m, all targets in range Fortitude DC 20 or gain 2 Crystallization progress steps immediately) - Weakness: no resistance to fire damage, and after using breath needs 2-round cooldown

Scene 4: Tear Sea — Weight of Sorrow

Duration: 150 min Type: Exploration / Psychological

Tear Sea Environment

You thought the Crystallized Wasteland was the most dangerous place in the Dark Continent. Then you saw the Tear Sea.

It is not just a sea. It is a mirror — reflecting not your appearance, but your heart. When you look at the water, you see the saddest moment of your life. Not memory — real, happening images. You see yourself losing that person, that chance, that happiness that should have been yours.

Then you find: your steps grow heavy. Not illusion — gravity increased.

Emotion-Physicalization Rules

Emotion Physical Effect Trigger
Sorrow Weight increases (Dexterity −2, move speed −5) Gazing at the sea over 1 minute
Anger Body temperature rises (1 damage/round, but attack +3) Attacked in the water
Fear Water viscosity increases (move speed −10) Failed Will save
Calm Water surface turns solid — walkable Will DC 20 to control emotion

Tear Sea Encounter

Guardian: Ancient Tear Sea Guard

When you first step into the Tear Sea, the water begins to churn. A giant figure rises from the depths — made of pure liquid, but its eyes are alive.

Ancient Tear Sea Guard (CR 12) - HP 180, AC 15 (liquid form, disadvantage on physical attacks) - Attack: Emotion Shockwave (range 15m, 3d8 Nen damage + Fortitude DC 18 or trigger random emotion-physicalization) - Special: The guard's attributes change with the PC's current emotion — when the PC is sorrowful the guard's attack +2; when the PC is calm the guard's AC −3 - Weakness: The guard cannot move out of the Tear Sea range; once the PC retreats to shore the guard vanishes

Discovery: Ancient Ruins — Library of Emotions

Deep in the Tear Sea is a half-submerged building, preserving the "emotion records" of a super-ancient civilization:

You open one "Emotion Crystal" — instantly, you experience the last day of a civilization extinct for thirty thousand years. You feel their fear, their hope, their love for each other at the final moment.

Then you understand — the Dark Continent is not uninhabited. It is a graveyard. Countless civilizations older than humanity all met their end here.

Obtainable items: - Emotion Crystal ×3 (usable to craft Mental-type Nen equipment) - Ancient Star Chart (marks other regions of the Dark Continent — foreshadows future expansion scenarios)

Scene 5: Truth — Why the Gate Is Here

Duration: 120 min Type: Revelation / Final Choice

Endpoint: First Civilization Ruins

You crossed the wasteland, ferried across the Tear Sea. Finally you reach the deepest part of the Dark Continent — a hall built of "black stone."

There are no guards, no traps, nothing to block your advance. Because those who built it never meant to block anyone — what they wanted was to be found.

At the center of the hall is a black pool. Floating on the surface is the last Memory Crystal — larger than the others, brighter than the others.

When you touch it — you see the truth.

Last Words of the First Civilization

The First Civilization was a sapient species that existed in this world before humanity. Their technology surpassed the understanding of Nen, their lifespan counted in millennia, their civilization lasted hundreds of thousands of years.

But they discovered a terrible thing: the essence of life is consumption. The ultimate fate of every civilization is to consume its world to exhaustion. Humanity will be the same.

So they did what no species would do — they built the "Gate," locked themselves in the Dark Continent, then collectively chose extinction.

Not because they could not survive. But because they chose not to consume the planet's remaining resources — leaving them for the next species.

That next species is humanity.

Final Choice

The last words of the Memory Crystal: "Now, it is your turn to choose."

The final choice the PC faces:

Choice Consequence
Seal the Gate Advise the Association to permanently seal the Dark Continent. Gain Association lifetime achievement award, Three-Star Hunter eligibility. But the truth is sealed in the Association's deepest archive.
Open the Gate Reveal the truth of the Dark Continent to the world. Triggers international upheaval; the V6 system shakes. The PC becomes a pioneer of the new era — or a traitor.
Inherit the Guardianship Stay in the Dark Continent, become the next "Gatekeeper." The PC character retires, becomes an NPC, appearing as a Legendary-tier NPC in future scenarios.
Bring Back the Truth Bring back only part of the truth, let the Association and the world decide for themselves. The safest option — but also means handing the responsibility to others.

Afterword: Words to the GM

These five scenarios form a complete growth arc — from an examinee who doesn't even know what Nen is, to a Hunter who stands at the deepest part of the Dark Continent facing humanity's fate.

But this is only the beginning. The Dark Continent still has dozens of unexplored regions, and factions only mentioned but never shown in the scenarios (V5's secret departments, the Kakin Kingdom's throne succession struggle), and the bonds and hatreds the PCs forged on their journey.

The most interesting stories are always the next one.
