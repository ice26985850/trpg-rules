Galaxy Civilization TRPG — Solo Campaign: Lone Vessel of the Galaxy

For: 1 player + 1 GM, Level 1-20 (covering all Five Eras) Companion: Galaxy Civilization Solo Rules Supplement Reference: All chapter citations in this campaign point to the Galaxy Civilization Player Rulebook and the GM Rulebook

You are a solitary interstellar civilization. The galaxy is vast and dangerous—you have no other players to rely on. But you will not be alone forever. In this century-spanning epic, you will find allies, face betrayal, withstand cataclysms, and make the final choice at the galaxy's end.

Key NPC Companions

This campaign features three recruitable NPC companion civilizations. The GM should ensure these companions' key figures interact with the player across all acts.

Companion A: Verdant Guardian

Info Content
First Appears Act I, Turns 6-8
Personality Archetype Loyal Guardian
Leader Seran, a plant-type species, calm and idealistic
Goal To prove in the galaxy that even a small civilization can exist with dignity
Bond with Player The player sent reinforcements when the Verdant Guardian was attacked by raiders (key event of Act I)

Companion B: Steel-Skull Khanate

Info Content
First Appears Act II, Turns 22-25
Personality Archetype Avenger
Leader Gorak the Great Khan, an insectoid, fierce but honor-bound
Goal To avenge the Interstellar Empire that destroyed its hive
Bond with Player The player chose to intervene when the Steel-Skull Khanate was besieged by the Interstellar Empire (climax of Act II)

Companion C: Silent Observer

Info Content
First Appears Act III, Turns 42-45
Personality Archetype Mentor Civilization
Leader Omega, a machine intelligence, calm to the point of unsettling
Goal To prevent the awakening of a threat older than the galaxy itself
Bond with Player The Silent Observer hold the crucial information about the endgame crisis, but will only share it after the player proves themselves trustworthy

Act I: Stellar Dawn (Level (Lv.) 1-4, ~20 Stellar Turns (ST))

Era I. Your civilization has just stepped into the stars. The galaxy is dark, unknown, and full of possibility. You have only a few ships, one homeworld, and a future full of problems.

Chapter 1: First Jump (Stellar Turn (ST) 1-5)

Core Mechanics: Civilization creation, basic exploration, resource management

Stellar Turn (ST) 1: Departure

The player completes civilization creation (using Chapter 2 of the core rules). The GM describes the player's home system—its stars, planets, history, and culture. Emphasize the fragility and hope of this very moment.

Check: None (pure narrative)

Stellar Turn (ST) 2-3: Perimeter Survey

The player's science vessel begins surveying nearby systems.

Check: Science Power (SCI) Survey Check (see Player Rulebook 4.5.1 Survey Action Procedure)

GM Notes: - The first surveyed system is set as a "promising discovery"—containing a habitable planet (ocean world, Habitability 80%) - The second system is set as "potentially dangerous"—containing an anomaly (geological anomaly, Difficulty Class (DC) 6) - At the end of Stellar Turn (ST) 3, the science vessel receives a faint distress signal from a third, unsurveyed system

Stellar Turn (ST) 4-5: Distress Signal

The player dispatches the science vessel to track the signal.

Check: Science Power (SCI) Survey Check, Difficulty Class (DC)=7 (signal is faint, requires precise positioning)

Discovery: The signal comes from a small plant-type colony—Verdant Colony—currently under threat from Raider Mercenaries (use the NPC data template from Player Rulebook Chapter 3: small raider fleet, Military Power (MP) ~500).

Key Choice: The player may— - A) Immediately dispatch a fleet to protect the colony (costs 2 Action Points (AP) military strike, Military Power (MIL) Check) - B) Attempt a diplomatic solution (costs 1 Action Point (AP) diplomatic proposal, Diplomacy Power (DIP) Check) - C) Stand by and watch (Verdant Colony will be destroyed at Stellar Turn (ST) 8, losing the chance to recruit Companion A)

Result of Choosing A or B: The Verdant Guardian civilization is formally born, and Seran becomes the player's first NPC companion (initial loyalty 8).

Civilization XP (CXP): Complete First Contact +5 / Resolve Event +10

Chapter 2: Neighbors & Borders (Stellar Turn (ST) 6-12)

Stellar Turn (ST) 6-8: Expansion Impulse

The Verdant Guardian shares the star chart of nearby space with the player. The player discovers a resource-rich system (use GM Rulebook star map generation, ensuring it contains a planet with Habitability ≥60% and a mineral vein).

Check: Economy Power (ECO) Colonization Check (Player Rulebook 2.14 Colonization Procedure)

Civilization XP (CXP): Colonize New Planet +15

Stellar Turn (ST) 9-12: A Competitor Appears

A human militarist civilization called the Kronos Syndicate expands near the player's border. They claim sovereignty over the system the player views as a colonization target.

GM Notes: Kronos Syndicate (use GM Rulebook NPC Civilization Generator: human/militarist/oligarchy/xenophobe+1)

Key Choice: - A) Armed confrontation (Military Power (MIL) Check, small fleet battle, Military Power (MP) ratio roughly 1:1) - B) Diplomatic negotiation (Diplomacy Power (DIP) Contest Check; may propose border division or joint development) - C) Back down and retreat (lose the system; Kronos Syndicate's attitude becomes "contempt")

Civilization XP (CXP): Win Fleet Battle +25 / Successful Diplomatic Agreement +10

Chapter 3: Secrets Beneath the Heavens (Stellar Turn (ST) 13-20)

Stellar Turn (ST) 13-16: Anomalous Signal

Scientists of the Verdant Guardian discover an ancient structure (archaeological site Lv2) in an abandoned system.

Check: Science Power (SCI) Archaeological Excavation (see Player Rulebook 4.5.4 Archaeological Site Excavation)

GM Notes: This is the main story foreshadowing running throughout the campaign. Inscriptions found at the site point to an ancient entity called the "Void Lord," and some secret deep in the galactic core.

Civilization XP (CXP): Complete Archaeological Site +20

Stellar Turn (ST) 17-20: Echoes from Afar

As excavation nears completion, the player's scientist receives a psychic echo from deep in the galaxy—an ancient and powerful entity seems to be "watching" them. At the same moment, on the other side of the galaxy, in the capital system of a Fallen Empire (use GM Rulebook Fallen Empire template), an ancient beacon begins to flicker.

Check: Unity (UNI) Check, Difficulty Class (DC)=7 (resist the influence of the psychic echo)

GM Notes: Whether the check succeeds or fails, give the player a sense that "something is awakening in the deep void"—this is a prelude to the Act IV crisis. On success, the player gains a vague prophecy; on failure, one of the player's scientists falls into short-term mental confusion.

Act I ends. The player should reach Level (Lv.) 3-4.

Act II: Stellar Expansion (Level (Lv.) 5-8, ~20 Stellar Turns (ST))

Era II. You are no longer a tiny newborn civilization. You already have a loyal companion, several colonies, and a clear threat. Now is the time to build real power.

Chapter 4: Wrath of Steel Skull (Stellar Turn (ST) 21-30)

Stellar Turn (ST) 21-25: Border War

An insectoid civilization called the Steel-Skull Khanate launches a raid on the player's eastern border. At first hostile, but deeper investigation reveals: the Steel-Skull Khanate's hive was destroyed by a powerful Interstellar Empire (use GM Rulebook NPC template: militarist+authoritarian+xenophobe, Scale 3~4)—they are not attacking indiscriminately, they are fleeing.

Check: Intelligence Power (INT) Intelligence Gathering, Difficulty Class (DC)=7 (discover the truth)

Key Choice: - A) Forcefully expel them (Military Power (MIL) Check, medium fleet battle, Military Power (MP) ratio roughly 1.5:1 player advantage) - B) Offer shelter and alliance (Diplomacy Power (DIP) Check, Difficulty Class (DC)=8; must overcome the insectoids' natural distrust of aliens) - C) Drive them toward the Interstellar Empire (leave them to their fate, but lose the chance to recruit a companion later)

Choosing B and Succeeding: The Steel-Skull Khanate becomes the second NPC companion (initial loyalty 6; Great Khan Gorak's trust in the player was hard-won).

Civilization XP (CXP): Discover Truth +10 / Form Alliance +15

Stellar Turn (ST) 26-30: Counterattack

The player and the Steel-Skull Khanate join forces to launch a counterattack against the Interstellar Empire.

Check: Continuous Military Power (MIL) operations and Diplomacy Power (DIP) negotiations (see Player Rulebook Chapter 3 War Framework)

GM Notes: This is a medium-length war sequence (3-5 turns). Use the War Weariness system from Player Rulebook 3.1. The condition for victory is not to completely annihilate the Interstellar Empire, but to force them to sign a peace agreement and recognize the player's sovereignty over the border.

Civilization XP (CXP): Win the War +25

Chapter 5: Halls of Power (Stellar Turn (ST) 31-40)

Stellar Turn (ST) 31-35: Galactic Community

The player's influence has grown too large to ignore. The Galactic Community (GM Rulebook section 4.3) extends an invitation—but the invitation comes with conditions.

GM Notes: Design an initiation ceremony for the Galactic Community. The player must deliver a speech in the council (Diplomacy Power (DIP) Check, Difficulty Class (DC)=7) to gain a permanent council seat. The representative of the Kronos Syndicate (the competitor from Act I) tries to obstruct.

Check: Diplomacy Power (DIP) Check (speech) + optional Intelligence Power (INT) Check (gather dirt on the Kronos Syndicate as a counterattack)

Civilization XP (CXP): Successfully Join +10

Stellar Turn (ST) 36-40: Power Games

Divisions emerge within the Galactic Community. An anti-player alliance is forming, led by the Kronos Syndicate and a religious civilization called the Void Cult. The player must maneuver politically, building coalitions.

Check: Multiple rounds of Diplomacy Power (DIP) Contest Checks, each round representing one diplomatic lobbying effort or political deal

GM Notes: Introduce initial contact with the Silent Observer—they anonymously provide the player a crucial piece of intelligence that helps dismantle the anti-player alliance. But their identity remains mysterious until Act III.

Civilization XP (CXP): Dismantle Anti-Player Alliance +15

Act II ends. The player should reach Level (Lv.) 7-8.

Act III: Galactic Integration (Level (Lv.) 9-12, ~20 Stellar Turns (ST))

Era III. You are already a major power in the galaxy. But the greater the power, the stronger the enemies. And deep in the galaxy, some ancient threat is awakening.

Chapter 6: Awakening Guardians (Stellar Turn (ST) 41-50)

Stellar Turn (ST) 41-45: Silent Observer Appear

The Silent Observer (machine civilization, early mentor companion) formally make contact with the player. They reveal a startling truth:

"We have observed your civilization for over two hundred turns. You were not born at random—your species' genes carry the mark of an ancient civilization. That civilization foresaw the coming of a certain threat tens of thousands of years ago, and planted 'contingency plans' among multiple races across the galaxy. You are one of them."

Check: Unity (UNI) Check, Difficulty Class (DC)=8 (psychological shock of accepting this truth)

GM Notes: The Silent Observer join as the third NPC companion (initial loyalty 5—they do not yet fully trust the player; the player must prove it through actions). Omega shares an ancient star chart marking a location in the galactic core called the "Sealed Land."

Civilization XP (CXP): Contact Silent Observer +15

Stellar Turn (ST) 46-50: Journey to the Core

To reach the Sealed Land, the player must traverse a sector claimed as holy domain by a Fallen Empire—the Holy Sentinels.

Check: - Attempt stealth passage: Intelligence Power (INT) Check, Difficulty Class (DC)=9 - Attempt diplomatic negotiation: Diplomacy Power (DIP) Check, Difficulty Class (DC)=10 (negotiating with a Fallen Empire is extremely difficult)

GM Notes: Whichever path is chosen, this is the most challenging part of Act III. Failed stealth triggers an encounter battle with the Fallen Empire's border fleet. Successful diplomacy may require paying a price (an advanced technology, a colony, or a promise to protect its holy land).

Civilization XP (CXP): Successfully Cross +20

Chapter 7: Sealed Land (Stellar Turn (ST) 51-60)

Stellar Turn (ST) 51-55: Into the Ruins

The Sealed Land is a massive ancient structure—a ring-shaped fragment of a world orbiting a dormant black hole. There are no living guardians here, but the ancient automated defense systems are still active.

Check: Science Power (SCI) Archaeological Excavation (cumulative excavation progress, Difficulty Class (DC)=9~11)

Ruins Discovery Table (d6): 1. Ancient Database—unlocks a random T3 technology 2. Sealed Specimen—an organic lifeform sealed for tens of thousands of years (possibly friendly, possibly dangerous) 3. Void Rift—an unstable dimensional rift through which one can see the domain of the "Void Lord" 4. Star Chart Room—a complete galactic star map marking all Fallen Empires and crisis trigger points 5. Armory—unlocks "Ancient Energy Weapon" technology (a special weapon upgrade) 6. Last Words of the Precursors—a holographic recording revealing the nature of the Void Lord and the reason it was sealed

Civilization XP (CXP): Each major discovery +10~15

Stellar Turn (ST) 56-60: The Trial Begins to Awaken

The player's activities at the Sealed Land stir the "Void Lord"—a high-dimensional entity sealed by an elder civilization. Signs of its awakening begin to appear across the galaxy: dimensional rifts, anomalous psionic fluctuations, missing ships.

Silent Observer' Omega issues a warning: "Time is short. The Void Lord will fully awaken in about 30 turns. You must prepare—or find a way to permanently seal it."

Act III ends. The player should reach Level (Lv.) 11-12.

Act IV: Galactic Hegemony (Level (Lv.) 13-16, ~15 Stellar Turns (ST))

Era IV. The awakening of the Void Lord is no longer a secret. The entire galaxy can feel its influence. The Fallen Empires begin to act. The omens of cataclysm have arrived.

Chapter 8: Rifts (Stellar Turn (ST) 61-70)

Stellar Turn (ST) 61-65: Full Mobilization

The Void Lord's vanguard force—the Void Devourers—begins pouring in from dimensional rifts at the galactic core.

GM Notes: Use the "High-Dimensional Invader" template from the crisis system in GM Rulebook section 4.6, but adjust the scale per Solo Rules Supplement section 1.2 (the player has 3 NPC companions, counted as 4 participants → use the original 100% scale).

Check: - Galactic Community emergency meeting: Diplomacy Power (DIP) Check, Difficulty Class (DC)=7 to initiate a joint defense proposal - Fleet battle: use the fleet battle rules from Player Rulebook Chapter 3

NPC Companion Reactions: - Verdant Guardian (loyalty 8): fights with full force, provides fleet support - Steel-Skull Khanate (loyalty 6): fights, but demands the player promise to help rebuild their hive after the war - Silent Observer (loyalty 5): provides crucial intelligence and tech support, but their fleet is mainly used to protect ancient installations

Civilization XP (CXP): Each time a Void fleet is repelled +15 / Hold a key system +20

Stellar Turn (ST) 66-70: Sacrifice

In a large-scale Void invasion, the player faces a painful choice:

Key Choice: The Void Devourers attack two targets simultaneously— - A) The player's home system - B) The Verdant Guardian's homeworld

The player's fleet can only reach one in time to rescue it.

Choosing to Defend the Homeworld: The Verdant Guardian's homeworld is destroyed; Seran survives but loyalty drops to 2 ("You chose yourself"), and the Verdant Guardian can only provide limited support for the rest of the campaign.

Choosing to Defend the Verdant Guardian: The player's homeworld suffers heavy damage (population halved, capital buildings destroyed and needing reconstruction), but Seran's loyalty rises to 10, and the Verdant Guardian thereafter devotes all its resources to supporting the player.

This choice has no "correct answer"—it is the ultimate test of the player's values, and will define the emotional tone of the story that follows.

Civilization XP (CXP): Survive the Sacrifice Event +30

Chapter 9: Awakening Ancients (Stellar Turn (ST) 71-75)

Stellar Turn (ST) 71-75: The Fallen Empire's Choice

The threat of the Void Lord has grown too large to ignore. The Fallen Empires slumbering in the galaxy begin to awaken—but not all who awaken will help you.

GM Notes: - Ancient Caretakers: choose to join the player's side (their calling is to protect organic life) - Holy Sentinels: declare neutrality—but their holy land lies directly on the Void Lord's path of awakening - Military Isolationists: consider all this "the trouble of lesser civilizations" and refuse to participate

Check: Diplomacy Power (DIP) Check, Difficulty Class (DC)=9 (persuade the Holy Sentinels to at least allow the player's fleet to pass through their territory)

Civilization XP (CXP): Gain Ancient Caretakers' Support +25

Act IV ends. The player should reach Level (Lv.) 15-16.

Act V: Endgame Era (Level (Lv.) 17-20, ~15 Stellar Turns (ST))

Era V. The Void Lord is about to fully awaken. The fate of the galaxy depends on you and your allies. This is the final campaign.

Chapter 10: Final Seal (Stellar Turn (ST) 76-85)

Stellar Turn (ST) 76-80: Ultimate Preparation

The player must complete final preparations before the Void Lord fully awakens:

  1. Assemble the fleets of all allies (total Military Power (MP) should reach 20000+)
  2. Deploy a "Dimensional Suppressor" at the galactic core (requires completing an Economy Power (ECO) megastructure construction Check)
  3. Decide the final strategy: permanent seal / complete annihilation / attempt communication (directly affects the rules of the endgame Boss battle)

Check: Multiple parallel lines (Economy Power (ECO) construction / Diplomacy Power (DIP) rally / Science Power (SCI) research the suppressor technology)

Stellar Turn (ST) 81-85: Final Decisive Battle

Final Boss: Void Lord

Use the High-Dimensional Invader Boss data template from the GM Rulebook (100% scale, since the player has 3 NPC companions):

Void Lord — Endgame Boss
Hull Points (HP): 25000 | Dimensional Barrier: 8000 | Speed: 1
Attack: Reality Collapse 500-800 (50% double damage)
Traits:
  • Dimensional Devour: every 3 rounds releases a Void Pulse; all fleets in range lose Hull Points (HP) -20%
  • Summon Vanguard: each round summons 3-5 Void Devourer units
  • Endgame Countdown: from round 10, each round a random galaxy system is devoured by the Void
Weaknesses:
  • After Dimensional Suppressor activated: cannot summon reinforcements and barrier halved
  • Psionic attack damage +100%
  • More than 3 civilizations attacking simultaneously → for each extra one, Boss defense -10%

GM Notes: This is the climax of the entire campaign. Use the full fleet battle rules from Player Rulebook Chapter 3 for specific tactical deployment. The GM should give each NPC companion its own "hero moment" in battle.

Civilization XP (CXP): Defeat the Void Lord +50

Epilogue: Afterglow (Stellar Turn (ST) 86-90)

The Void Lord has been defeated. The galaxy welcomes peace—at least for now.

Ending Paths

Depending on the player's choices throughout the campaign, the following endings are possible:

A) Galactic Guardian (the player maintained loyal companion relationships throughout, Verdant Guardian survives) → The player civilization, the Verdant Guardian, and the Steel-Skull Khanate jointly establish a new galactic order. Seran delivers a touching speech, reminiscing about the journey from first meeting to now.

B) Lonely Victor (the player chose power over friendship, perhaps sacrificing some companions) → The player civilization becomes the galaxy's sole superpower, but the victory is bitter. Gorak's soul seems to whisper from the depths of the Void: "You got what you wanted—but lost what you needed."

C) Echoes of the Void (the player chose to communicate/trade with the Void Lord) → The Void Lord is re-sealed, but the player civilization undergoes some changes—part of the population begins to exhibit psionic power, and the rest of the galaxy watches you with suspicion. A brand-new story is brewing.

The Final Roll

Regardless of which ending, at the end the GM has the player make a Unity (UNI) Check—not to determine success or failure, but to set the future tone of the galaxy. The result of this roll should be recorded on the player's civilization character sheet as a permanent mark of this campaign.
