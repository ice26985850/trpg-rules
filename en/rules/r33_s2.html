S2 · Scars in the Iron Valley

This scenario follows the Trials after Macmillan Estate, moving the scene to the Autohaven Wreckers. The player discovers words carved into the wrecks — and the scars differ from Trial to Trial. Someone is using them to leave messages, and the one who left them is gone. This scenario deepens two threads: "memory loss is systematic" and "someone is fighting it."

2. GM Summary

Storyline: At the Campfire, Gilmar Sato (§8-A) brings back a sheet of iron pried off the last Trial, carved with "Do not trust the fifth one." The player enters the Autohaven Wreckers Trial and finds 6–8 scars scattered around, each one chipping away at the player's confidence in "escape," yet also offering rules hints and main-story clues. Core design: the scars are collectible information, but collecting them costs Action Points (AP), and each scar challenges the player's perception. After returning to the Campfire, the Ember Moment reveals: the words were carved by Eloise herself — every time she returns she forgets, so she carves outside, and on her own body.

Truth (GM-Exclusive): Memory loss is systematic, not accidental. Someone is fighting it (Eloise), and leaving messages is possible. This opens S3's exploration of "memory is archived, not destroyed." Wraith (WRT) serves here as the "heartbeat-less Killer" tutorial — it has no Terror Radius (TR); the player hears no heartbeat and must read position from other cues.

How to Run: This scenario introduces the "exploration phase" for the first time — scar collection is an active investigation, not mere reaction. The GM should create resource tension (time / Action Point (AP)) between collecting scars and "repairing to escape." The solo configuration places 8 scars (including 2 extra) to ensure the solo player has enough information density.

3. Opening Text (read aloud)

You return to the Campfire. Eloise is staring at a sheet of rusted iron, like it's a face.

Gilmar holds the iron up to the firelight. A line of crooked characters is cut into it with a knife:

"Do not trust the fifth one."

"What does it mean?" you ask.

Eloise shakes her head. "I don't know. But I've seen this hand before. At least three times. Across several Realms."

She flips the iron over. On the back is half a sentence left unfinished, its edge scored by fingernails.

"Whoever wrote it," she says, "is gone. But the words stayed."

Gilmar shoves the iron into your hands. "Next time you see one like it, stop and look. Maybe… it was left for you."

6. NPC Appearances and Lines

Eloise Finch (§8-A) - Central to this scenario. The closing reveals she is the author of the scars. - Closing line: "These are my words. …I forget every time I come back. So I carve outside, and on my own body. But I never finished that one sentence. Because every time I'm halfway through — I can't remember what I meant to say."

Gilmar Sato (§8-A, Scavenger-type Survivor) - Brought back the iron sheet, provides Autohaven Wreckers terrain knowledge. - Line: "Next time you see one like it, stop and look. Maybe it was left for you."

Echo Companion · the Hollow Hugger (§8-B, solo configuration) - Always embracing something that isn't there. If the player heals it, it passes the "thing in its arms" to the player — it's empty, but the player gains 1 Hope Point (HOP).

Whisperer · Iron-Rust Chorus (RC-02) - The Autohaven Wreckers Whisperer, intelligence-type (Encounter Budget Cost (EBC) 0). Can trade Hope Point (HOP) for scar locations. - Line: "Metal remembers sound. You knock, it answers. Knock a few more times and I'll tell you which sheet of iron carries words."

7. Decision Points and Branch Results

Decision One: Spend Action Point (AP) to read the scars? - Reading a scar = giving up time to repair / flee, but gaining main-story info and rules hints. - Branch: if the player gives up an action favorable to themselves in order to read a scar (e.g. skipping a repair, detouring, glancing while rescuing), it satisfies the Ember Moment trigger condition.

Decision Two: The fifth Generator - The required count usually does not need the fifth (see player-count table: required 3–5, total 4–7). But Scar #1 warns "Do not trust the fifth one." - Branch: if the player actively repairs the fifth, it triggers the E6 trap (free position reveal for the Killer); if the player heeds the scar and meets the quota with four, the trap is avoided and escape is still completed.

Decision Three: Treatment of the Hollow Hugger (solo) - Player heals Echo · Hollow Hugger → gains 1 Hope Point (HOP). This is a key source of Hope Point (HOP) in the solo game.

Eloise looks at those words for a long time without speaking. Then she says, "These are my words." She reaches out and pulls up her sleeve. 10. Connections to Adjacent Scenarios

  • Forward (S1): ah_opt_locker Scar #8 "Stop looking for me" echoes S1's mm_opt_locker; S1's contradiction of Eloise saying "don't rescue people" is paid off here — the player learns she herself is the author of the scars.
  • Backward (S3): Scar #5 "The fire remembers" is the lead-in to S3 "Roots That Eat No Soil" — Eloise reasons that "if memory is taken, it must be put somewhere." The iron sheet and nail the player gains in S2 become a campaign-spanning "message" mechanic.
  • Hook payoff points: ① "Do not trust the fifth one" hints that Trial rules can be interfered with by messages; ② the scars on Eloise's body → S4 afterimage "the one who writes"; ③ Gilmar (if the solo player's favorite Echo to rescue) builds an emotional bond here → rescued from the Void in S5.
