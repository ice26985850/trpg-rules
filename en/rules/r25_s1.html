Scenario 1: This Month's Rent

Basic Info

Item Content
Difficulty ★ Intro (suitable for new GMs and new players)
Suggested Reputation 0-9 (Unknown Shop)
Suggested Party 2-4 players, any class combination
Length 1-3 turns (about 1-2 sessions)
Core Themes Survival pressure, resource management, team cohesion
Prerequisites Players just finished character creation; shop just opened

Synopsis

The tax-free period of the Settlement Decree has ended. A letter from the Royal Capital tax office sits quietly on the counter-three months ago, when you opened the shop, a small line on the form you filled out read: "From the date of opening, the first quarter's business tax is exempt." Today is exactly day 91.

Tax owed: 150G. Deadline: three weeks.

How much is in your wallet? About 80G. Some inventory, but not much. The shop just opened; your regulars are only the baker next door and the stray cat that always comes begging for food.

Three weeks. 150G. A small shop that hasn't yet found its footing.

Key NPCs

NPC Role Key Line
Tax Officer Harlo Stern but negotiable "I'm just doing my job by the rules. However-if you can pay in full before the deadline, I can waive the late penalty."
Baker Betty Friendly neighbor "If you need help, I know a few clients who need catering."
Stray Cat Soot Mascot / plot catalyst (Doesn't speak, but always shows up at the crucial moment)

Turn Design

Turn 1: Shock and Inventory

Business Prep: Roll Market Fluctuation (1d6). GM hands the tax letter to the players.

GM Guidance: - Have players calculate the gap: 150G − current Funds (G) = amount to earn - Take inventory: what materials are available? Which recipes earn the most in the short term? - Betty knocks on the door-she heard about the tax bill. "My sister is getting married next week. We'd booked the city restaurant, but they raised their price at the last minute. Can you take it?"

Material Acquisition: Proceeds normally. Betty's wedding banquet needs specific materials-guide players to make trade-offs.

Crafting & Production: The core turn-Betty's order vs. daily business. Which to do first?

Business & Sales: First day of business. Foot Traffic 1d6. Let players feel normal income (about 30-60G/week).

Event: Roll Event Trigger (1d6). Warm-hearted events preferred.

Growth Settlement: End of week one. Let players see the gap between income and 150G.

Turn 2: Sprint

Business Prep: Market Fluctuation. The wedding banquet is this weekend.

GM Guidance: - If week 1 income was poor, Betty will proactively introduce more clients (Hearthside Circle connections) - If week 1 went smoothly, Tax Officer Harlo will return: "I was passing by. Just wanted to remind you-two weeks left." - Option: Normal business + wedding catering (Action Points (AP) stretched thin)? Or abandon one side and sprint with full force?

Optional Side Quests: - "Emergency Commission": An adventurer needs a Stamina (STA) potion/boxed lunch delivered the next day (quick cash but high pressure) - "Discount for Cash": The Material Merchant Guild offers an advance-cash scheme, but material purchase prices +20% for the next three months

Crafting & Business: On the day of the banquet-Crafting Check (DC 12); quality affects income (Fine +30G, Superior +60G).

Turn 3: Ending

Branching Endings:

Result Condition Narrative
Paid in Full Funds (G) ≥ 150G Harlo takes the tax, smiling rare: "Not bad. You're stronger than most first-year shops." Reputation +2.
Barely Made It 100-149G + Social Check (DC) 12 Harlo sighs: "I'll give you a one-week extension. But don't let it happen again."
Hearthside Circle's Aid Failure + has Hearthside Circle ties Betty brings the Hearthside Circle's emergency mutual-aid fund-you owe the Circle a favor (Mutual Aid Points −3, to be repaid later)
Worst Case All failed Harlo seizes part of the inventory to cover the tax. Shop Funds (G) drop to zero, Decor −1. But the tax bill is cleared-you can start over

Rewards

Achievement Reward
Paid in Full Reputation +2, retain remaining Funds (G), unlock Betty's long-term partnership
Banquet Critical Success (Superior) Reputation +1d4, bride's family introduces noble clients
First Successful Cooperation Each participating player gains a "Team Rapport" mark (future Cooperative Crafting Check +1, one-time)
Any Ending The shop survives-that alone is the greatest reward

GM Notes

  • This is a tutorial scenario: The focus is to let players get familiar with the game loop and money management, not to crush them
  • Give newbies a break: The first customer is always a good customer. The first transaction should succeed
  • Soot's uses: The stray cat can appear anywhere-it's the GM's all-purpose plot catalyst. It can knock things over to trigger events, lead the way to lost materials, or simply rub against players when they're down
  • Pacing control: Turn 1 makes players anxious, Turn 2 gives hope (the wedding order), Turn 3 releases the pressure
