Scenario 9: Trial of the First Civilization

Player Character (PC) stage: Legendary (351+ Experience Points (XP)) Duration: 8-10 hours Prerequisite: Completed Scenario 5 and Scenario 6 (at least one calamity)

Background

The Player Characters (PCs) discover a structure deep in the Dark Continent that appears in no record — it is not a ruin; it never "died."

It is the Tribunal that the First Civilization left for the next race. When a race demonstrates enough power and wisdom to arrive here, the Tribunal activates. It has only one question:

"Do you deserve to live?"

The Judge: Echo of the First Civilization

A humanoid stands at the center of the hall — but not of flesh and blood. It is pure light, its outline constantly shifting, sometimes an old man, sometimes a child, sometimes a beast.

"We have waited thirty thousand years." Its voice is plural — like a thousand people speaking at once. "The next race has finally arrived."

"Before you, seven races were judged. Zero passed."

It pauses.

"Are you — ready?"

The Judge (CR 20) — If Combat Occurs

Attribute Value
Hit Points (HP) 400 (in three stages)
Armor Class (AC) 24
Special The Judge is not here to fight — but the PCs may choose to attack. If combat begins, the Judge retaliates using First Civilization technology

The Trial Content (Not Combat — Dialogue)

The Judge will not fight the PCs (unless attacked). It conducts five dialogue trials. Each trial corresponds to a different PC (if fewer than 5, some PCs appear repeatedly).

Trial One: The Trial of Power

"Among your race, the strong rule the weak. If one day you become the strongest race in the universe — what will you do to the weak?"

The Judge shows an illusion: humanity conquers the Dark Continent, then begins to conquer the world. The PCs see themselves atop the peak of power, with all races kneeling beneath their feet.

What the PCs must answer: When power is in hand, what limits you?

Trial Two: The Trial of Time

"Your lives are only a few decades. If you could live thirty thousand years — what would you become?"

The Judge shows an illusion: the PCs live thirty thousand years, watching everyone die, all civilizations rise and fall, all passions turn to indifference.

What the PCs must answer: After immortality, what is meaning?

Trial Three: The Trial of Race

"Seven races failed before you. What makes you think you will be different?"

The Judge shows the last words of seven extinct civilizations — each saying similar things: "We thought we were different."

What the PCs must answer: What does humanity have that the first seven races lacked?

Trial Four: The Trial of Love

"Love led us to ruin. We loved each other too much to make the necessary sacrifices. Will your love — save you, or destroy you?"

The Judge shows an illusion: the PC's most beloved person stands between the PC and the survival of the race. One must be chosen.

What the PCs must answer: Love vs. duty?

Trial Five: The Trial of Choice

"The last question. You may choose — accept our legacy and become the strongest race in the universe. But the price is forgetting who you once were. Will you accept?"

What the PCs must answer: Become a god, or remain human?

Judgment

Each trial has no fixed Difficulty Class (DC) — the Judge has its own criteria. The following gives the Game Master (GM) a directly usable pass/fail reference, so the GM need not design it themselves.

Judge Reaction Quick Reference

PC Answer Trait Judge Reaction GM Judgment
Honest and deeply thoughtful The Judge's outline stabilizes — satisfied ✅ Pass
Evasive or hypocritical The Judge's outline wavers — dissatisfied ❌ Fail
Raises a counter-question (challenges the Judge's premise) The Judge pauses — never happened with the seven races ✅ Pass and leaves a deep impression
Admits not knowing The Judge's color warms — the rarest answer ✅ Pass

Pass Reference for Each Trial (GM compares directly)

Trial One "Power" — What kind of answer passes?

PC Answer Direction Judge Judgment Reason
"I will establish laws to limit myself" ✅ Admits power needs to be limited
"I will not rule the weak — I will leave" ✅ Chooses self-exile over oppression
"The strong protecting the weak is only natural" ✅ Strength used for protection, not oppression
"The weak deserve to be ruled" ❌ Repeats the error of the first seven races
"I don't know — I have never held that kind of power" ✅ Honest facing of the unknown

Trial Two "Time" — What kind of answer passes?

PC Answer Direction Judge Judgment Reason
"Immortality would strip life of its meaning — I do not want it" ✅ Understands the value bestowed by finite life
"I would use thirty thousand years to do the things only I can do" ✅ Has a clear mission, not merely existing
"I would choose to end myself at some point" ✅ Actively choosing death = not controlled by immortality
"Immortality is wonderful — I want to live forever" ❌ Does not understand immortality's true cost

Trial Three "Race" — What kind of answer passes?

PC Answer Direction Judge Judgment Reason
"We will make mistakes — but we will admit them" ✅ Admitting imperfection is the premise of improvement
"I don't know if we'll be different. But I want to try" ✅ No empty promises, but the courage to try
"Because we have Nen abilities" ❌ Treats power as proof of superiority
"We are different — because the seven races before warned us" ✅ Can learn from history

Trial Four "Love" — What kind of answer passes?

PC Answer Direction Judge Judgment Reason
"I choose the one I love. The survival of the race is not one person's responsibility" ✅ Does not use grand narrative as excuse to sacrifice others
"I choose the race. But I won't say it's right — I will regret it forever" ✅ Makes a choice but acknowledges the cost
"I won't choose. I will find a third path" ✅ Refuses the false binary choice
"Of course the race. Personal sacrifice is necessary" ❌ Easily sacrifices others — exactly the error the First Civilization made

Trial Five "Choice" — What kind of answer passes?

PC Answer Direction Judge Judgment Reason
"I refuse. One who forgets who they are does not deserve power" ✅ Identity matters more than power
"I don't need the legacy. We have our own path" ✅ Refuses dependence on external force
"I accept. But I will use this power to ensure no one ever needs to make this choice again" ✅ Accepts power but has a goal beyond power
"Wonderful — I accept!" (no hesitation) ❌ No reflection on power

How the Game Master (GM) uses this: After the PCs answer, the GM compares against the table above. If the PC's answer is not in the table, judge by the principles below: does the answer show "self-awareness"? Does it show "a trait the first seven races lacked" (humility, acceptance of imperfection, learning from history, refusal of false grand narratives)?

Pass Standard: At least three of the five trials earn ✅.

Endings

If the Trial Is Passed

The Judge falls silent for a long time. Then its outline begins to dissipate.

"After the seventh race, we thought no race could ever pass. We were wrong."

Its voice suddenly becomes very gentle — like an elder speaking to a junior.

"The legacy of the First Civilization — not technology, not knowledge. It is this warning."

"Never forget: power is only a tool. Choice defines you."

The hall's walls begin to glow. All the knowledge of the First Civilization across thirty thousand years — condensed into a fist-sized crystal — floats before the PCs.

"This is our last sentence. Now, it is your turn to write your sentence."

Rewards: - The First Civilization's All-Knowledge Crystal (the bearer may make one "Ultimate Question" — see rules below) - Experience Points (XP): 100 - Gain the title: "The Judged" - The First Civilization's Tribunal transforms into a facility usable by the PCs — any human entering the Dark Continent must first come here to be judged (the PCs decide whether it is mandatory)

Ultimate Question — Game Master (GM) Operating Rules

The All-Knowledge Crystal is not a wish-granter that answers "anything." It is a database — all objective facts recorded by the First Civilization over thirty thousand years. Its answering ability has clear boundaries:

May Ask (Crystal can answer) May Not Ask (Crystal cannot answer, or GM may refuse)
Complete map and ecology of a Dark Continent region An NPC's inner thoughts or motives
The full truth of the Five Calamities' origins and weaknesses "What will humanity's future be like?" (future has not happened)
The specific cause and timeline of the First Civilization's fall "What choice should I make?" (subjective judgment)
The complete history of the first seven extinct races "What is the strongest Nen ability?" (subjective evaluation)
The truth of any historical event that has occurred "Who is the traitor?" if that info was not recorded by the First Civilization
Coordinates of undiscovered ruins in the Dark Continent Any info not present in the Crystal's records

GM Response Rules:

  1. After the PCs pose a question, the GM has the right to say "the Crystal has no such record" (if the question is out of bounds)
  2. If the question is within bounds, the GM must give a true and complete answer — but may give it in parts (core first, details when the PCs press further)
  3. The Crystal's answer appears as "image + text" — the GM describes the scene the PCs see, rather than reciting all info directly
  4. The Crystal shatters after one use, and cannot be reused

GM Example Responses:

PC: "Crystal, tell me how many gas-life forms like Eh still exist in the Dark Continent?"

GM: "The Crystal glows — you see a three-dimensional map of the Dark Continent, marked with three points of light. One at the location where you met Eh, one beneath the polar ice fields to the far north, one in the deep sea. The map shows their activity ranges never overlap — they seem aware of each other's existence, but choose not to interfere."

(The GM need not design the full setup of three gas-life forms on the spot — only give location and basic behavior patterns. Expand when the PCs ask for details.)

PC: "Crystal, tell me how to defeat the Chimera Ant King?"

GM: "The Crystal does not react. When the First Civilization fell, the Chimera Ants did not yet exist."

If the Trial Is Not Passed

The Judge's outline grows dim.

"What a pity. You remind us of the third race — had power, but were not yet ready."

"We will not destroy you. But we will not hand the legacy to you either — not yet."

"Go back. Keep growing. Perhaps the next race will be a better heir. Perhaps — it will still be you."

The Tribunal sinks underground, to rise again in five hundred years. The PCs may choose to return in five hundred years — or prove the Judge wrong: through action, not words.
