Scenario S2: Three-Way Standoff

Suggested Era Range: City-State Age → Kingdom Age
Suggested Level: Intermediate
Estimated Turns: ~60 turns (15 years in-game)
Difficulty: ★★☆☆☆
Core Themes: Competition, diplomacy, war, regional dominance
Player Count: 1 player + 1 GM (multiple players may play the other two city-states)

I. Scenario Overview

Synopsis

Your city-state is not the only power in this region. When the population breaks 250 and city walls rise from the ground, you find a similarly young city-state rising on each of your flanks. A three-way standoff forms — the ownership of resources, trade routes, and ancient ruins will be decided by diplomacy, war, and stratagem. Whoever steps into the Kingdom Age first will dominate this region.

Scenario Objectives

  1. Establish your standing in the region (cannot be clearly the weakest side)
  2. Control at least one key resource node or trade route
  3. Build a stable relationship (alliance or vassalage) with at least one neighbor
  4. Break the population and National Power (NP) thresholds and step into the Kingdom Age
  5. Decide the region's final shape (hegemon / tri-state council / two-power partition)

II. Scenario Structure

Phase One: Rise (Turns 1–12, early city-state period)

Opening Narration (read aloud by the GM):

When your city wall first casts its long shadow in the sunset, scouts bring word: in the river valley to the east, another city-state is being built; on the western hills, a third is sending up smoke.

Three young nations open their eyes on the same land. You look to the map — that fertile mining district in the middle, and the ancient trade road connecting the three, whoever controls it first holds the throat of the future.

Your advisor whispers, "What we can extend is more than a hand of friendship."

Key Events:

Turn Event Type
Turn 1 Set the nation's identity — military power / trade hub / magic realm, deciding the early focus Internal Affairs
Turn 3 First contact with the eastern city-state "Emerald Vale's" ambassador, who probes your strength and intentions Diplomacy
Turn 5 The western city-state "Ironpeak" sends a merchant who is actually gathering intelligence (you may send a Spy (SPY) for counter-recon) Diplomacy / Recon
Turn 7 Discover the neutral mining district "Silvervein" — all three may claim it, first come first served Resource Contest
Turn 9 A tribe of refugees floods in; choose to accept (population +, food pressure) or drive away (Morale −) Moral Choice
Turn 12 Representatives of the three nations meet informally for the first time at the trade-road market town — the mood is delicate Diplomatic Milestone

GM Notes: - This phase is mostly exploration and diplomacy, with low war threat; let players get used to interacting with "neighbors with their own will" (see Chapter 16 of the GM Guide, Neighbor AI). - Encourage players to send Hero Units (General / Envoy) on concrete tasks, experiencing hero personal combat and diplomatic specialization.

Phase Two: Resource Contest (Turns 13–30)

Key Events:

Turn Event Type
Turn 13 Silvervein clash — whoever stations troops triggers tension; you may preemptively build a "mining outpost" Military / Construction
Turn 16 Bandits appear on the trade road, threatening the three-way trade; you may jointly clear them or cut off a rival's supply in the chaos Military / Stratagem
Turn 19 Emerald Vale proposes "resource division of labor": you supply food, they supply ore, Ironpeak supplies weapons Diplomatic Proposal
Turn 22 A plague breaks out in Ironpeak and spreads outward — do you seal the border? (sealing harms neighbor relations) Crisis / Moral
Turn 25 An ancient trade-road ruin surfaces, containing a repairable market structure (after repair, regional trade +) Exploration
Turn 28 Ironpeak secretly expands its army; scouts discover an abnormal number of barracks Intelligence
Turn 30 First border friction — Ironpeak patrol and your scouts exchange fire, 1 wounded Military Crisis

GM Notes: - Introduce the "Random Wilderness Encounter Table" (GM Guide Chapter 25): when players send heroes to Silvervein / the ruin, roll on the table for extra variables. - Neighbor behavior is driven by the GM based on their personality (expansionist / defensive / cunning); need not be perfectly symmetric.

Phase Three: Diplomatic Maneuvering (Turns 31–45)

Key Events:

Turn Event Type
Turn 31 Emerald Vale proposes a "two-nation alliance" against Ironpeak — accept and Ironpeak becomes an enemy, refuse and you lose a strong ally Major Diplomatic Choice
Turn 34 You may send an Envoy (ENV) to use the "Treaty" skill (Opposed +3) to persuade a neutral tribe to join Hero Diplomacy
Turn 37 Ironpeak dangles a marriage alliance to win you over — marriage raises relations but may hijack policy Diplomacy / Internal
Turn 40 The trade-road ruin market is repaired; regional trade flourishes, taxes (TAX) + Economic Milestone
Turn 43 A Spy (SPY) reveals: Ironpeak plans a surprise strike on Silvervein — you may warn or frame them Intelligence / Stratagem
Turn 45 A regional assembly convenes, the three sides state their positions; the situation splits in two (alliance vs. isolation) Diplomatic Climax

Phase Four: On the Brink of War (Turns 46–55)

Key Events:

Turn Event Type
Turn 46 If you refused the alliance, Ironpeak joins another power to declare war on you (see GM Guide Chapter 14, War System) War Trigger
Turn 48 Border battle — the General (GEN) may enter the field for "Tactical Command" +2 attack, or a hero duel to behead a general and break enemy morale War / Hero
Turn 51 Silvervein garrison mutinies; needs internal pacification (Morale check) or you lose the resource node Internal Crisis
Turn 53 A third party (Emerald Vale) may reap the spoils while both sides are wounded — do you sue for peace? Strategic Choice
Turn 55 The war reaches a stalemate or one side collapses; sign a truce or victory treaty War Ends

GM Notes: - War uses multi-turn military opposition; see GM Guide Chapter 14; hero personal combat see Chapters 11 / 24. - In single-player, the GM also controls the hostile city-state and should give it reasonable strategy rather than mechanically piling on troops.

Phase Five: Birth of a Kingdom (Turns 56–60)

Key Events:

Turn Event Type
Turn 56 National Power (NP) breaks the threshold, era-upgrade preparation — the final condition must be met (castle + city walls) Construction
Turn 58 The regional shape is set: you become hegemon / a member of the tri-state council / one of two powers Milestone
Turn 60 Kingdom Founding Ceremony — neighbor envoys gather, a new regional order is born Ending

III. Ending Conditions

Success Ending (Regional Hegemon)

  • Defeat or suppress at least one neighbor, control Silvervein and the trade road
  • Build a stable alliance or vassalage with another neighbor
  • Step into the Kingdom Age
  • Reward: All attributes +8; unlock the legendary deed "Regional Arbiter"

Partial Success (Tri-State Balance)

  • Survive and not become a vassal; region forms a tri-state council
  • Reward: Economy (ECO) +10, all diplomatic relations +20

Failure Conditions

  • Conquered by a neighbor (Military (MIL) collapses and the capital falls)
  • Remedy: Play as a government-in-exile, rebuilding under a protectorate's shelter (retain 60% of attributes)

IV. Key NPCs

Name Role Relationship
Lordess "Leah" of Emerald Vale Ruler of the eastern city-state, a pragmatic diplomat Can ally / can oppose
Archduke "Gor" of Ironpeak Ruler of the western city-state, martial and expansionist Primary military threat
Spy "Nightcrow" A Spy (SPY) you recruit, infiltrating enemy lands Loyalty to be cultivated
Old One of the Trade Road Guardian of the ancient trade-road ruin, holds the knowledge to repair the market Neutral intelligence source

VI. GM Tips

  1. Neighbor AI: The two hostile city-states should have different personalities, not all alike (see GM Guide Chapter 16).
  2. Hero Spotlight: S2 is the best stage to showcase hero personal combat and diplomatic specialization — proactively give heroes tasks.
  3. Weight of Choices: Alliance / marriage / declaration of war should all bring perceptible follow-on consequences.
  4. Balance: Ensure a single player can also maneuver — let the two hostile city-states check each other, avoiding a joint siege.
