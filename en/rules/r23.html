Introduction

Yonghe Martial Codex — A Wuxia Life Story, from Greenhorn to Grandmaster

Have you ever imagined what it would be like to roam the Jianghu yourself? Yonghe Martial Codex is a tabletop role-playing (TRPG) system rooted in Chinese martial-arts culture. Here, you are not a battle-machine on a stat card — you are a Jianghu person who takes a master, trains in the martial arts, makes enemies, and builds a reputation. Beginning as a raw beginner who has only just peeked through the gate in the Yonghe era, you cultivate your way up to a return-to-simplicity Grandmaster — and this journey is yours to write.

Worldview

The story unfolds in the fictional "Yonghe era," a time that resembles the Song yet is not the Song — a age of cultural brilliance where border troubles are quietly rising. The martial arts of the world bloom in a hundred flowers, and eight great powers each hold their own corner: the orthodox and renowned such as the Tianjian Alliance, Wanfo Temple, and Taixu Palace; the half-righteous, half-evil such as Yaowang Valley, the Beggar Sect, Tang Sect, and Qianji Pavilion; and the Demon Cult — the Blood Moon Cult — lurking in the Western Regions. The Jianghu now finds itself woven through by the "Four Clouds of the Age": a leadership succession, unrest in the Demon Cult, a legend of a hidden treasury, and war on the Northern Frontier — countless grudges and adventures await to draw you in.

Who do you play? What do you play?

You play a son or daughter of the Jianghu. The core loop is: join a sect, learn martial arts, undergo adventures, and break through to new heights — until at last you create your own unique style. Beyond fists, blades, and swords, the Jianghu is also about human relationships — whether your renown is high or low, the tangle of gratitude and enmity, and the trade-off between chivalry and infamy often shape your fate more than any single victory.

System Highlights

First, the dice use a bell-curve design of 2d10 against a Difficulty Class (DC). The stronger the character, the more success is decided by skill rather than dice luck; and that mere 1% "natural double-ten" critical success is the true, heart-pounding highlight moment.

Second, Qi is the soul resource of combat. It serves as both attack amplification and defense, as mobility and as a last-resort lifeline — how you spend your Qi is itself a tactical contest. Combat is not a collision of numbers, but a martial exchange of technique-countering-technique, of reading and unravelling the opponent's moves.

Third, martial arts drive growth. Over thirty named techniques, a six-tier realm system, paired with eight great sects and the twin tracks of Chivalry and Infamy, let you forge a unique path through the martial world.

Who is it for?

Players who enjoy tactical combat as well as story immersion and resource management will feel it most. You need a little TRPG experience, but the core rules are clear and not cumbersome — read them through once and you can start.

Game Style Tags

  • Story-driven: Story-supported
  • Resource management: Moderate management
  • Character customization: Highly customizable
  • Character-creation complexity: Complex
  • Rule density: Medium rules
  • GM requirement: GM required (a two-person group is 1 player + 1 GM)

Estimated Play Time

  • Single scenario: 3–5 hours (one session, including character creation)
  • Full campaign: 60–100 hours (the six-chapter "One-Dragon" connected campaign, about 22–34 sessions)

Basic Information

  • Players: 1 player + 1 GM (two-person group) up to 4–6 players + 1 GM (team group)
  • Core dice: 2d10
