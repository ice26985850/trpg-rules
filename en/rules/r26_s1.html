Scenario 1: Hunter Exam — 288th Exam

Growth Stage: Rookie (0-15 Experience Points (XP)) Suggested Players: 3-5 Estimated Duration: 4-6 hours Prerequisites: Characters created; Hunter License not yet obtained

Background

The 288th Hunter Exam is about to be held in "Saba City." This year, Cheadle, one of the Zodiacs, serves as the Chief Examiner, and she designed the exam around the principle of "testing true wisdom and adaptability."

Tens of thousands of examinees flood into Saba City — some for money, some for power, some to find a missing loved one, some purely to prove themselves. And you, each with your own reasons, have come here too.

But you will soon discover: fewer than one in a hundred survive the exam.

Scene 1: Saba City Gathering — First Round Screening

Duration: 45 min Scene Type: Social / Exploration

Opening Description

Saba City's central plaza is packed with people. All manner of faces — burly martial artists, scholars in lab coats, dangerous figures with dark eyes, and teenagers who look barely past ten.

A giant screen at the center of the plaza lights up, and a woman in glasses appears on it. Her tone is gentle, but every word carries immovable authority:

"Welcome, examinees. I am Cheadle, the Chief Examiner of the 288th Hunter Exam." "The first-round screening begins now — within two hours, find the five hidden registration points around the plaza. Those who fail to find them, go home."

The screen goes dark. The crowd scatters in panic.

Five Registration Point Locations and Clues

# Location Clue Check
1 Top of the clock tower "Look up; time waits for no one" Perception DC 10
2 Sewer entrance "The dirtiest place hides the cleanest answer" Investigation DC 12
3 Café second-floor private room "Order a latte, say the passphrase 'Prey'" Persuasion DC 12
4 Behind a hidden door in an abandoned warehouse "A building with no number plate" Perception DC 14
5 Bottom of the fountain in the park "The flowing water washes away your hesitation" Athletics DC 10 (diving)

Non-Player Characters (NPCs): Other Examinees

Examinee Role Interaction with Player Character (PC)
Tonpa (Rookie Killer) Veteran examinee, failed 16 times Pretends to be friendly, gives false info (Deception DC 15 to see through)
Hanzo (Ninja examinee) Capable competitor Competitive but reliable; can cooperate briefly
Ponzu (Fighter examinee) Hot-blooded competitor Directly challenges PC to a physical contest

Game Master (GM) Notes

  • This is the first scene of the system; use it to teach non-combat checks like Perception, Investigation, Persuasion
  • Each registration point examiner has a different attitude — some serious, some cold, some give hints
  • The first examinee to find all 5 registration points gains "Lead Intel" (gains advantage in Scene 3)

Scene 2: Death Marsh — Endurance and Survival

Duration: 60 min Scene Type: Exploration / Combat

Opening Description

After finding the five registration points, you are loaded onto a truck and transported to the outskirts with about fifty other surviving examinees. The truck stops — before you is a mist-shrouded marsh.

"Cross this marsh and reach the assembly point on the far side." Cheadle's voice comes from a drone. "No time limit. But note — every six hours, the marsh's water level rises by one meter. Those who hesitate too long will sink."

You look at the muddy water and faintly see something moving within.

Marsh Threats

Threat Challenge Rating (CR) Encounter Chance Response
Corrosive mud — Fixed (route required) Athletics DC 12
Crocodile-type magic beast 1/4 30% / movement Combat or Stealth
Poison frog swarm 1/4 × 3 20% / area Reflex save DC 12
Man-eating vines 1/2 20% / fixed point Combat or Acrobatics DC 14

Corrosive Mud Rules

  • 1d4 acid damage per round after stepping in
  • Escape requires Athletics DC 12 or ally assistance (group check)
  • After water rises, more areas become mud

Key Choice Point (Midway)

The PC discovers another group of examinees stuck in the mud: - Choose to help: Costs 15 min + may trigger an encounter, but gains 1 "Favor token" (can gain one advantage at the final interview) - Choose not to help: No cost, but characters who witness this must make a Willpower DC 12 save to avoid psychological burden

Endpoint

At the far-side assembly point only about twenty remain — of fifty, nearly half failed to cross the marsh.

Cheadle stands at the assembly point, checking her watch. "Not bad. Now, divide into groups of four within five minutes." "Next stage — teamwork. Those who can't work with others need not participate."

Scene 3: Mirror Maze — Teamwork

Duration: 60 min Scene Type: Puzzle / Team

Rules

  • PC is forced to team up with random examinees (may include prior NPC examinees)
  • Each room is a mirror maze; entering alone, you see your "mirror double" — with abilities identical to your own
  • Core mechanic: Must cooperate to pass — Room A's switch is in Room B, and vice versa

Maze Structure (Seven Rooms)

Room Content Pass Condition
A Mirror double combat (CR = own CR) Defeat or make the double "acknowledge itself"
B Pressure plate puzzle Two people must stand at specific positions simultaneously (coordinate via comm device)
C Logic gate puzzle AND/OR/XOR three gates; must exchange opening order with teammate
D Trust trial One blindfolded, the other guides via voice through traps
E Memory challenge Memorize a pattern sequence to pass; teammate may hint beside
F Role swap A and C temporarily swap bodies; must adapt to each other's abilities
G Shared exit Door opens only when everyone arrives

Mirror Double Combat Rules

  • The double has attributes, skills, and Nen abilities identical to the PC
  • The double will not use Binding Pledge points (since the double has no "real risk")
  • Victory condition is not necessarily knockdown — can "persuade" the double (Persuasion DC 18) or "surpass yourself" (use a Nen ability with Binding Pledge points (BP) ≥ 3)

Game Master (GM) Notes

  • This scene is designed to let players experience the necessity of teamwork
  • Watch the time — if a puzzle stalls over 15 min, provide a hint
  • The mirror double combat is the players' first fight against an "equal-level opponent," ideal for teaching the combat system

Scene 4: Live Elimination — 1v1 Matches

Duration: 90 min Scene Type: Combat (consecutive)

Rules

  • Remaining examinees hold a 1v1 elimination tournament
  • Nen abilities may be used
  • Rules: surrender is valid; lethal attacks forbidden (violators are immediately disqualified)
  • No weapon restrictions

Opponent Rotation

Round Opponent CR Opponent Type Special
First round 1/2 Mook tier (d6: 1=Strength fighter, 2=Agility assassin, 3=Tactical soldier, 4=Wild beast tamer, 5=Trickster liar, 6=Nen ability novice) Basic match
Second round 1 Mid-tier (has 1 Nen ability prototype) Begin observing opponent's Nen
Semifinal 1-2 Mid-tier (full Nen ability) Tough enemy, may need to spend Spirit Points (SP)
Final 2 High-tier mid (Nen ability with restriction & pledge) Final opponent

Opponent Quick-Generation Table

First-round opponent attribute templates (d6):

d6 Type Main Attribute Attack Method Weakness
1 Strength fighter Constitution 15 Melee heavy strike (1d10+3) Low AC (10)
2 Agility assassin Dexterity 15 Twin daggers (1d6+2, 2×/round) Low HP (35)
3 Tactical soldier Intelligence 14 Pistol (2d6) + cover tactics Weak in melee
4 Wild beast tamer Perception 14 Tamed beast assist (CR 1/4 beast ×1) Weak body
5 Trickster liar Charisma 15 Throws lime (Blinded DC 12) + sneak attack Weak in frontal combat
6 Nen ability novice Willpower 14 Nen bullet (1d6, SP 1) Never learned Ten (Nen Defense 0)

Semifinal Opponent Example

"Shadow Step" Elwin (CR 1-2) - HP 50, SP 30, AC 13 - Dexterity 16 (+3), Willpower 12 (+1) - Nen ability: Shadow Step (Emission, SP 2) — spend a move action to teleport 5m instantly; next attack +2 to hit - Tactics: hit-and-run, use obstacles, becomes conservative when SP drops below 10

Final Opponent Example

"Oath Fist" Carlo (CR 2) - HP 70, SP 40, AC 12 - Constitution 17 (+3), Willpower 14 (+2) - Nen ability: Oath Fist (Enhancement, SP 3, Binding Pledge points (BP) balance 4/4) - Restriction: usable only 3 times/day (+1 BP), cannot move for 1 round after use (+1 BP), if opponent surrenders must stop immediately or lose Nen ability (+2 BP) - Enhancement: damage +2d6 (2 BP), ignore half Nen Defense (2 BP) - Effect: 3d6+3 damage, ignores half of target's Nen Defense

GM Note: This is the opponent best suited to demonstrate the "restriction & pledge system." Let the PC see "how self-limitation trades for power."

Scene 5: Final Interview — Cheadle's Trial

Duration: 30 min Scene Type: Social / Roleplay

Background

After the elimination tournament, fewer than ten examinees remain.

Cheadle sits behind a long table, calling examinees into the interview room one by one. When it's your turn, she flips through your file, then looks up at you.

"Sit." Her glasses reflect the light; you can't read her expression. "Now, answer me one question —" "A truly difficult question."

Cheadle's Questions (chosen by character motivation)

If the PC's motivation is… Cheadle's question
Searching for someone "After you find that person, what meaning is left in your Hunter identity?"
Pursuing power "Have you seen someone truly powerful? Are you sure you want to become that kind of person?"
Protecting someone important "If becoming a Hunter means putting those people in greater danger, would you still want it?"
Money and fame "A rich person can hire a Hunter with ten million Jenny to do anything. So what's the difference between you and a mercenary?"
Revenge "After your revenge succeeds, every one of your days will be defined by that day. Are you ready?"
Freedom "The Hunter License is indeed a pass. But have you considered — what is the price of true freedom?"

Judgment

  • No right or wrong: Cheadle doesn't care about the content of the answer; she cares about the process — honesty, depth of thought, self-awareness
  • If the PC earned "Favor tokens" or "Lead Intel" in the first four scenes, they may spend them here for one Insight check advantage
  • Any sincere examinee passes (GM discretion)

Ending

Cheadle is silent for a long time. Then she nods slightly.

"Congratulations. You are already a Hunter."

She hands you a card — on a black background, the golden Hunter Association emblem glimmers faintly. "This license opens many doors. But remember — it also closes the door to the ordinary life you might otherwise have lived."

"Go. The world is waiting for you."
