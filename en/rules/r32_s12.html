Scenario 2: "The Windowless Room"

Item Content
Type Seal Investigation
Recommended Marks 2-4 (basic experience)
Players 2-4
Sessions 2-3
Core Attributes Awareness, Negotiation, Anchor
Difficulty ★★☆☆☆

Commission

A partner at a well-known architecture firm—Mr. Lin—privately contacted a Gap Walker. Their firm is renovating a century-old mansion. The construction crew discovered a "non-existent room" on the third floor—it appears on the blueprints, but all its windows are sealed with bricks, and the only door is welded shut from the inside. Stranger still: any worker who tries to open this room feels "unable to breathe" before they even start, and is forced to leave.

The only annotation on the blueprint reads: "September 1923—Sealed."

Mr. Lin wants to know: what is in the room? Why was it sealed? Is it safe to open?

Surface Map

  • The mansion: A three-story brick building, built in the 1910s. Once a private residence, converted into a hospital in the 1940s, abandoned since the 1980s.
  • Third-floor hallway: At the end is the last door—slightly recessed from the surrounding wall, with no light leaking from the door seam.
  • First-floor archive room: Holds part of the hospital-era medical records.

Gap Layer Map

The mansion's Gap Layer is a "time compressor"—different eras stack layer upon layer in the same space. Players will see a 1940s nurse's Residual pushing a wheelchair on the second floor, and a 1910s butler tidying the hall on the first floor.

The third-floor Gap Layer: At the end of the hallway is a pitch-black Crack, with the "healed scar" of surrounding space clearly visible—like skin after a burn. A Seal Watcher stands beside the Crack (Negotiation DC 18).

Key NPCs

NPC Info
Mr. Lin (Client) An architect in his 40s. A pragmatist, but "spooked" by this room. "I've been in this business twenty years and never encountered anything like this."
Old Physician A retired physician in his 80s who interned at this hospital in the 1940s. Still remembers "that unopened room on the third floor."
Seal Watcher Stands beside the Crack. Not hostile—it is waiting for a "worthy person."

Sealed Space Information

Item Content
Reason Deliberately sealed—regular artificial marks at the edge of the Crack
Inside A private study from 1923. A man (the mansion's original owner) signed a document here and then took his own life. The document's contents: he transferred all his property to a "person who does not exist"—his wife, who suffered from mental illness. He realized only after signing that his wife's guardian would not acknowledge it, but he could no longer revoke it. He chose to seal the room—letting the evidence and himself disappear together
Key The Old Physician remembers: "That room's key… seemed to be a small silver box. The director always carried it with him."—This box is now a Lost Item, dropped in the Gap Layer of the first-floor consultation room of the hospital

Key Scenes

  1. Investigate the mansion: Players examine the third-floor sealed room on the Surface. Awareness DC 15 reveals the "spatial pressure" around the room—as if the entire building's Gap is avoiding this room.

  2. Explore the Gap Layer: Enter the Gap Layer through the Natural Threshold at the mansion's main gate (DC 8). Move through the time-compressed hallway. Traverse DC 12 to avoid "temporal dislocation"—on failure, briefly slip into the 1940s and see nurses and patients.

  3. Find the key: The Old Physician provided a clue—the small silver box. Retrieval DC 12 to find the box in the first-floor Gap. Inside is an exquisite brass key, and an engraved line: "Forgive me."

  4. The Watcher's trial: Return to the third-floor Crack. The Seal Watcher (Negotiation DC 18) gazes at the players. The players must present an item related to the Sealed Space—the small silver box (the key). The Watcher steps back.

  5. Enter the Sealed Space: Time inside the room is frozen at 1923. On the desk lie the transfer document and a fountain pen. A man's Residual sits in the chair, frozen in the posture of the moment of signing. He will not move—because his "hesitation" was completed the instant he signed his name. He does not need liberation—he only wants to be "seen." After the players take the document, the room begins to unseal—the bricks outside the window shatter on their own, and sunlight pours in.

  6. Return: Inform Mr. Lin of the document and the truth. Choose: publicize this history as the building's "story," or keep it secret. Return Mark +1.

Ending Options

  • Publicize the history: Mr. Lin's firm preserves the room as a "time capsule," making it a building feature.
  • Keep secret: After unsealing, the room becomes an ordinary room. Mr. Lin privately thanks the players.
  • Free the Watcher: If you summon the Watcher by its True Name—you can set it free. Gain Return Mark +1.
