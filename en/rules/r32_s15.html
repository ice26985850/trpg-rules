Scenario Five: "The Other Me"

Item Content
Type Mirror Chase
Recommended Marks 7-10+ (high tier)
Player Count 2-4
Sessions 3-4
Core Attributes Anchor, Negotiation, Awareness
Difficulty ★★★★★

Commission

This is not a commission—it is personal.

One player's Mirror begins to make "movements it shouldn't." At first just tiny deviations—when you stop, it pauses half a second late. But the situation is worsening. Last time you saw it in the Gap, it spoke to you. In your own voice, but saying things you didn't know.

It said: "Do you still remember that summer, years ago? Under the bridge—what was it you cast away?"

You never cast anything away under the bridge. But you do have a gap in your memory—some summer in childhood, you forgot something.

Your Mirror seems to know more than you do.

Core Design

This scenario is highly personal. The GM must privately communicate with the target player before starting, to determine: 1. A memory the player character forgot in childhood (could be real trauma, or a blank left in the character background) 2. The secret the Mirror "knows"—related to that memory 3. The Mirror's purpose—does it want to replace you? Or is it warning you about something?

Surface Map (Example—adjustable to player background)

  • Childhood Home: Demolished, now a park.
  • That Bridge: An old bridge on the outskirts of the city. Closed for years.
  • Old School: The elementary alma mater, about to be demolished and rebuilt.

Gap Layer Map

The childhood home still exists in the Gap Layer—complete, even clearer than in memory. Because "the forgotten home" is the Gap's favorite material.

The bridge in the Gap Layer is cracked open—beneath the bridge deck is unfathomable darkness. It is not water—it is "repressed memory."

The Mirror's Three Stages

Stage Behavior Trigger
Stage One: Hinting The Mirror reacts with delay, makes tiny different movements At scenario start
Stage Two: Dialogue The Mirror begins to speak, using the player's voice to say things the player doesn't know When the player first attempts to negotiate with the Mirror
Stage Three: Confrontation The Mirror waits under the bridge—or at the core location of childhood memory—for you. It wants your "that one memory." Or it wants to give you that memory When the player arrives at the memory core

Key NPCs

NPC Info
The Player's Mirror Negotiation DC 16. Uses the same attribute values as the player. It is not an enemy—it is "the repressed part."
Childhood Acquaintance A childhood friend, neighbor, or family member who may still be on the Surface. Can provide clues about "that summer."
Lost Item Collector A Gap Creature who once witnessed the Mirror leave the player and "act independently." Can trade for information.
Memory Guardian (Special) Guards the location of the core memory—the Path Guardian under the bridge. Requires "a name" or "a genuine memory" to pass.

Key Scenes

  1. First Anomaly: The player sees their own Mirror during a routine crossing of the Gap. It stops mimicking, stands still, and says one sentence. The player must make an Anchor Check (DC 14) to confirm "is this real or my hallucination."

  2. Track the Mirror: The Mirror moves through the Gap, leaving a traceable "Residual-echo path." Awareness DC 12 to track. The path leads the player through their personal history—the childhood home, the old school, finally to the bridge.

  3. Dialogue with the Mirror: In the Gap Layer of the childhood home. The Mirror stands there, looking at the player's childhood diary (Memory Crystal). Negotiation DC 16. The Mirror reveals: "What you forgot was not a summer. You forgot a person."

  4. Dig Up the Memory: The player needs to assemble the truth through Lost Items and NPCs. The diary mentions a childhood friend—who drowned under the bridge one summer. The player was there, but out of fear did not ask for help. Afterward, they "forgot" this memory.

  5. Confrontation Under the Bridge: Arrive at the bridge's Gap Layer. The Mirror stands under the bridge. It is not "replacing you"—it is the embodiment of that repressed memory. Choices:

  6. You accept this memory: The Mirror "merges" with you. You gain the complete memory, losing the "Mirror's Sacrifice" ability (since the Mirror is now part of you). Any Stage of Gap Sickness reverses by one stage.
  7. You reject the memory: The Mirror becomes an independent Gap Creature—a new Mirror, no longer belonging to you. You may encounter it again. Anchor Check DC 18 to resist the worsening of Gap Sickness.

  8. The Mirror's Sacrifice: If you are in any stage of Gap Sickness (including Terminal Stage), the Mirror can choose to "take your place." It bears your Gap Sickness; you recover. But you lose the Mirror forever.

  9. Wrap-Up: Whatever the choice, the player is permanently changed. If the memory is accepted: Return Mark +1 (this is a unique kind of "return"—what you return is a memory, to yourself).

Ending Options

  • Merge: The player character becomes more whole. All future Anchor Checks gain permanent Advantage (because you faced your greatest fear).
  • Reject: The Mirror stays in the Gap. May appear again in future scenarios.
  • Sacrifice: The player character recovers. The Mirror disappears forever—but every time the player sees a mirror surface in the Gap, they will remember it.
