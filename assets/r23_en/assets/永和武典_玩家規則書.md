## I. Attribute System

### 1.1 Six-Attribute Overview

A martial artist's cultivation is measured by six core attributes. The six attributes span the three dimensions of "body — mind — Qi," depicting both a warrior's combat ability and their behavioral style in the Jianghu.

| Attribute | English | Core Meaning | Affected Areas |
|------|------|---------|--------|
| **Strength (STR)** | Strength | Muscle power, burst, physical toughness | Unarmed/heavy-weapon damage, carrying, destructive actions |
| **Agility (AGI)** | Agility | Speed, flexibility, lightfoot foundation | Evasion, initiative, lightfoot distance, light-weapon hit |
| **Constitution (CON)** | Constitution | Physique, meridian capacity, vitality | Health Points (HP), Qi cap, poison resist, wound recovery |
| **Comprehension (CMP)** | Comprehension | Martial comprehension, learning efficiency, move analysis | Martial-learning speed, move-breaking, self-created art threshold |
| **Willpower (WIL)** | Willpower | Will, mental stability, spirit resist | Resist inner demons/pressure, Qi-Deviation resist, meditation efficiency |
| **Charisma (CHA)** | Charisma | Personal charm, social influence, Jianghu renown | Social/persuasion, Jianghu renown bonus, commanding crowds, pressure opposed |

### 1.2 Attribute Value and Modifier Mapping

The core in-game meaning of an attribute value lies in its **modifier** (the check bonus). The mapping is:

```
Modifier = Attribute Value − 4
```

| Attribute Value | Modifier | Description |
|--------|--------|------|
| 1 | −3 | Decrepit body |
| 2 | −2 | Below ordinary |
| 3 | −1 | Common folk |
| 4 | 0 | Foundation of martial training (ordinary-human limit) |
| 5 | +1 | Initial grounding |
| 6 | +2 | Jianghu adept |
| 7 | +3 | Regional power |
| 8 | +4 | First-Rate level |
| 9 | +5 | Peerless bearing |
| 10 | +6 | Grandmaster physique |
| 11 | +7 | Transcendent |
| 12 | +8 | Saintly (human limit) |

### 1.3 Attribute Range and Growth Path

| Stage | Attribute Value Range | Corresponding Realm |
|------|-----------|---------|
| Char creation initial | 1–4 (primary attributes), 1–2 (secondary attributes) | First Realm · First Glimpse |
| Late First Realm | 3–5 | Peak of First Glimpse |
| Second Realm | 4–6 | Crossing the Threshold |
| Third Realm | 5–7 | Complete Mastery |
| Fourth Realm | 6–8 | Perfecting the Craft |
| Fifth Realm | 7–10 | Peak of Perfection |
| Sixth Realm | 8–12 | Return to Simplicity |

The attribute cap is set by realm (see Section IV, Realm System). Each realm breakthrough grants **3 attribute points**, at most +2 to a single attribute.

### 1.4 Attribute Interrelation Logic

The six attributes have an organic interlinking, encouraging players to consider pairing rather than stacking a single attribute to the limit:

```
Strength + Agility → Combat mobility (initiative modifier, combo judgment)
Constitution + Willpower → Inner-art stability (core combo against Qi Deviation)
Comprehension + Willpower → Cultivation efficiency (martial-learning speed, meditation recovery multiplier)
Constitution + Comprehension → Qi control (high-level art execution precision)
Charisma + Strength → Pressure effect (aura judgment)
Agility + Comprehension → Move analysis (check to break opponent's martial art)
```

**Design logic**: No attribute is a "useless attribute." A pure physical warrior (high STR/AGI/CON) lacks resistance to spirit-type arts; a pure inner-art cultivator (high WIL/CMP) may have a fragile body; a high-Charisma character may thrive in Jianghu politics yet not win every fight.

## II. Core Dice System

### 2.1 Scheme: 2d10 + Modifier vs Difficulty Class (DC)

**Core check formula**:
```
Check Result = 2d10 + Attribute Modifier + Skill/Martial-Art Modifier
Success condition: Check Result ≥ Difficulty Class (DC) (difficulty level)
```

### 2.2 Why 2d10

After evaluating four schemes — D20, 3d6, dice pool, and 2d10 — 2d10 was chosen based on the following analysis:

#### Problems with the D20 Scheme

D20 is a uniform distribution, each face strictly 5%. This means:
- Natural 1 and natural 20 each have 5% probability; extreme results combined total 10%
- In the mortal wuxia stage (low attribute modifiers), check results depend heavily on luck rather than character ability
- A decade-trained master being defeated by a rookie hooligan on luck alone is too probable (about 5%–10%, depending on Difficulty Class (DC))
- **Fundamental contradiction**: D20's "high drama" at the low end manifests as "frequent unjust failures," against players' expectation of character growth

#### Problems with the 3d6 Scheme

3d6 is an over-concentrated bell curve (standard deviation ~2.96):
- 68% of results fall in 8–13; natural 3 and 18 each only ~0.46%
- The "desperate outburst" — the core thrill of wuxia works — is nearly impossible under 3d6
- The marginal gain per +1 modifier is extremely low at the bell-curve ends, making stacking extreme attributes unattractive
- For the "life-and-death" mortal wuxia stage, 3d6 is too safe and conservative

#### Problems with the Dice-Pool Scheme

- Success-degree counting adds per-round computation, exceeding the "medium complexity" design constraint
- Chinese TRPG players have far stronger intuition for value opposition (Difficulty Class (DC) system) than success-degree systems
- The probability distribution of success-degree systems is unintuitive, unhelpful for on-the-fly GM adjudication

#### Advantages of the 2d10 Scheme

| Trait | 2d10 | D20 | 3d6 |
|------|------|-----|-----|
| Mean | 11 | 10.5 | 10.5 |
| Std. Dev. | ~4.06 | ~5.77 | ~2.96 |
| 68% interval | 7–15 | 4–18 | 8–14 |
| Extreme probability (2/20) | 1% each | 5% each | N/A |
| Growth feel | Best (+1 yields highest gain at center) | Uniform | End gain very low |
| Wuxia fit | **Optimal** | Too random | Too conservative |

**Core advantages of 2d10**:
1. **Bell-shaped but not conservative**: 44% of results concentrate in 9–13; character ability decides success, not dice luck. But the distribution is wide enough (std. dev 4+) to preserve "outburst" space.
2. **1% extreme probability**: Natural 2 (double 1) and natural 20 (double 10) each only 1%; true critical failure/success are rare highlight moments, not a daily occurrence every battle.
3. **Strongest growth feel**: Each +1 attribute modifier raises the success rate on a Difficulty Class (DC) 15 check by about 8–12% (depending on current value); in the key interval (modifier +2 to +6) the growth experience is smoothest.
4. **Realm modification embeds naturally**: A high-realm character's "Advantage Die" does not change the die-face distribution but changes the probability density — perfectly matching the wuxia logic that "those pure in skill output steadily."

### 2.3 Realm Dice Modification

As realm rises, a character not only has higher attributes but stronger "command" over the dice. Realm dice modification realizes the wuxia growth arc of **"from relying on luck to relying on skill"**:

| Realm | Dice Modification | Design Intent |
|------|---------|---------|
| First Realm · First Glimpse | No modification (base 2d10) | Mortal wuxia, life and death by skill and luck alike |
| Second Realm · Crossing the Threshold | vs lower-realm opponents, **Advantage Die** (2×2d10 take higher) | Third-Rate masters already crush mortals |
| Third Realm · Complete Mastery | vs same-realm-or-below, key checks **Advantage Die** (2 per scene) | Jianghu adepts more reliable at key moments |
| Fourth Realm · Perfecting the Craft | All non-combat checks get **Steady Die** (3d10 take middle two) | First-Rate masters rarely fumble any skill |
| Fifth Realm · Peak of Perfection | Once per battle, may take check directly as **18** (Inner Strength Infusion) | The "I'm serious now" of a Peerless grandmaster |
| Sixth Realm · Return to Simplicity | Three Inner Strength Infusions per battle; Advantage Die range expanded to all checks | The composure of a generation's grandmaster |

**Design notes**:
- "Advantage Die" = roll two sets of 2d10, take the higher set's result.
- "Steady Die" = roll 3d10, drop the lowest, sum the remaining two (mean ~12.5, std. dev ~3.5).
- "Inner Strength Infusion" = no roll, take 18 directly (≈ a modifier +7 character's 2d10 mean 11 + 7 ≈ 18). This is a top master's "stable floor."

### 2.4 Difficulty Class (DC)

| Difficulty Class (DC) | Difficulty | Wuxia Example |
|-----|------|---------|
| 5 | Effortless | Leap a low wall, identify common poison, sprint on flat ground |
| 8 | Easy | Climb a tree and scale a wall, hear a hidden weapon's whoosh, trot on tiles |
| 10 | Minor Challenge | Run silently on tiles, break a Third-Rate move, recognize footprints in night rain |
| 12 | Requires Effort | Borrow force on a bamboo branch, see through disguise, sword true in rain |
| 15 | Quite Tricky | Train sword under a waterfall, see through a First-Rate master's feint, hidden weapon true in gale |
| 18 | Jianghu Hard | Unerring arrow in a blizzard, comprehend a fragmented secret manual, deflect a thousand jin with softness |
| 20 | Wall of Masters | Pierce a falling leaf's vein with one sword, hear a whisper ten zhang away in noise |
| 22 | Realm of Grandmasters | Create a unique martial art, flick aside a blade with a finger, break all methods with one sword |
| 26 | Legendary Feat | Leave words on a stone stele with finger force, hold breath underwater one incense stick, wind bends grass |
| 30 | Martial Myth | Flick-finger thunder, grab objects at a distance, face a thousand alone unharmed |

**Reference success rates** (no modifier, pure 2d10):

| Difficulty Class (DC) | Success Rate | Feel |
|----|--------|------|
| 5 | 99% | Will not fail |
| 10 | 64% | Likely success |
| 15 | 15% | Quite difficult |
| 18 | 3% | Very difficult |
| 20 | 1% | Nearly impossible (only natural 20 passes) |

A Jianghu adept with modifier +3 (mid Second Realm) facing a Difficulty Class (DC) 15 check rises from 15% to 45% success — this is the felt reality of growth.

### 2.5 Critical Success / Critical Failure Rules

**Critical Success**: 2d10 natural sum is **18–20** (three tiers, combined ~6%)
- Check auto-succeeds
- Extra benefit (choose one or GM adjudicates):
  - Move crit (damage +50% or extra effect)
  - Discover key clue / extra information
  - Aura pressure (opponent next round −2)
  - Extra action chance (combo)

**Critical Failure**: 2d10 natural sum is **2–4** (three tiers, combined ~6%)
- Check auto-fails
- Extra consequence (choose one or GM adjudicates):
  - Weapon drops / off-balance (next round −2)
  - Move over-extended, counterattacked (opponent gets an opportunity attack)
  - Extra Qi spent (this spend +2)
  - Expose opening (opponent Advantage Die next turn)

**Perfect Critical Success**: double 10 (2d10 = 20), 1% probability
- An unexpected positive effect occurs: kill at low HP, stroke of genius, comprehend secret ultimate
- May trigger "Martial Epiphany" — after this check, the related martial art gains one free advancement

**Perfect Critical Failure**: double 1 (2d10 = 2), 1% probability
- A severe accidental consequence occurs: weapon breaks, Qi Deviation, heavy-wound collapse
- Cannot be reversed by any "reroll" ability — fate's joke cannot be withdrawn

**Design logic**: A 6% crit success/failure rate over a 20-session scale means an extreme result every 3–4 sessions. Enough to create drama, without making the character seem incompetent.

### 2.6 Opposed Checks

When two characters (or a character and an NPC) directly oppose:
```
Each side makes a 2d10 + Modifier check
Higher result wins
On a tie, the active side wins (attacker hits, but effect halved)
```

Realm Advantage Die also applies in opposed checks. Moreover, if one side's realm exceeds the other's by two or more tiers, the higher-realm side auto-wins — the embodiment of "power crush" in the wuxia world.

## III. Qi / Inner Strength System

### 3.1 Qi Concept

Qi is the life energy a martial artist accumulates in the meridians through inner-art cultivation. It is not "mana" — Qi originates from the refinement of one's own life essence; over-spending directly damages the body.

At the game level, Qi is the **second resource pool** (the first being Health Points (HP)), driving all high-level martial arts.

### 3.2 Qi Pool Capacity

```
Qi Cap = Constitution × Inner-Art Coefficient + Realm Base Value
```

**Inner-Art Coefficient** (set by the highest inner-art manual tier the character currently has):

| Inner-Art Tier | Coefficient | Inner-Art Example |
|---------|------|---------|
| No inner art | 1 | — (ordinary person has only a breath of dantian qi) |
| Entry manual | 2 | Breathing Art, Mixed-Origin Post, Shaolin basic inner art |
| Orthodox manual | 3 | Huashan Mixed-Origin Art, Quanzhen Mental Method, first layer of Purple Dawn Art |
| Superior manual | 4 | Nine Yang Manual, Nine Yin True Qi, first layer of Tendon-Changing Classic |
| Peerless divine art | 5 | Great Achievement of Nine Yang Divine Art, Northern Nether Art, Grand Mystery Classic |

**Realm base value**:

| Realm | Qi Bonus |
|------|---------|
| First Realm · First Glimpse | +0 |
| Second Realm · Crossing the Threshold | +3 |
| Third Realm · Complete Mastery | +6 |
| Fourth Realm · Perfecting the Craft | +10 |
| Fifth Realm · Peak of Perfection | +15 |
| Sixth Realm · Return to Simplicity | +20 |

**Examples**:
- Constitution 4, entry manual, First Realm → Qi Cap = 4 × 2 + 0 = **8**
- Constitution 6, orthodox manual, Third Realm → Qi Cap = 6 × 3 + 6 = **24**
- Constitution 9, peerless divine art, Fifth Realm → Qi Cap = 9 × 5 + 15 = **60**

### 3.3 Qi Consumption

Qi consumption is set by **martial moves**; reference consumption by type:

| Martial Tier | Qi Cost (per use) | Typical Moves |
|---------|-------------------|---------|
| Basic move | 1–2 | Black Tiger Steals Heart, Swallow Skims Water, basic lightfoot |
| Advanced art | 3–5 | Eighteen Dragon-Subduing Palms · Dragon Shows in the Field, Taiji Sword · Grasping the Sparrow's Tail |
| Absolute art / sect-suppressing divine art | 6–10 | Six Meridians Sword · Shaoshang Sword, Nine Yin White Bone Claw, One-Yang Finger |
| Sustained art (per round) | 1–3 | Golden Bell Cover (1/round), Treading Snow Without Trace (1/round), Northern Dipper Qi (2/round) |

**Special consumption**:
- Qi projection (ranged-attack arts): extra +2 Qi
- Air / water-treading lightfoot: 1–2 Qi per round (basic ground movement costs no Qi)
- Amplified move (e.g. "full-power strike"): pay extra 1–3 Qi for damage/effect bonus
- Protective Qi (passive/active damage reduction): spend corresponding Qi on trigger

### 3.4 Qi Recovery

| Recovery Method | Rate | Condition |
|---------|------|------|
| Natural recovery | Recover **Willpower** points of Qi per 10 min (in-game) | Non-combat, non-strenuous |
| Sit / meditate | Recover **Willpower + Constitution** points of Qi per 10 min | Requires seated focus, Awareness check −4 |
| Short rest (~1 hour) | Recover **Realm Tier × 5** Qi points | Light activity allowed |
| Long rest (one night) | Qi fully restored | Safe environment, at least 6 hours |
| Pills / spirit medicine | Instantly restore X points | Defined by item system; same pill has cooldown |

**Recovery speed examples**:
- First-Realm warrior with Willpower 5: natural recovery 5 points/10 min; full pool 8, basically fine
- Fourth-Realm master with Willpower 7: natural recovery 7 points/10 min; Qi pool 40+, natural recovery no longer enough — must master efficient meditation or rely on pills
- **Design intent**: As realm rises, the Qi pool inflates faster than recovery speed, forcing high-realm players to manage Qi more carefully, rather than "infinite casting"

### 3.5 Qi Overdraft and Qi Deviation

#### Overdraft Mechanism

When a character's current Qi is insufficient to pay a move's cost, they may choose **forced circulation (overdraft)**:
- The shortfall is "borrowed" from the Qi cap
- During the borrow, the Qi cap is temporarily lowered, and the borrowed portion cannot recover naturally
- Borrowed Qi must be returned via special recuperation (meditate 1 hour / per 1 point borrowed) or a long rest
- Each overdraft triggers a **Qi Deviation check**

#### Qi Deviation Trigger Conditions

| Triggering Situation | Check Triggered |
|---------|---------|
| Forced circulation (overdraft Qi) | Each trigger, Difficulty Class (DC) = 15 + overdraft points |
| Cultivating inner art beyond one's Willpower (inner-art tier > Willpower − 3) | Triggered once after daily cultivation |
| Continuing circulation while meridians are damaged | Each round |
| Attacked by special arts (e.g. "Star-Absorbing Great Method," "Skill-Transforming Powder") | Per that art's specific rules |
| Forcibly fighting after severe wound | Each round |

#### Qi Deviation Check

```
Qi Deviation Check = 2d10 + Willpower Modifier + Constitution Modifier (if relevant) vs Difficulty Class (DC)
Difficulty Class (DC) base = 15 + overdraft points (overdraft case)
```

**Failure consequences (by failure margin)**:

| Failure Margin | Effect | Name |
|---------|------|------|
| 1–4 | Qi Backlash: lose 1.5× the spent Qi; next round cannot use Qi | Qi Backlash |
| 5–8 | Meridian Shock: take 1d10 Internal Injury (ignores armor); all checks −1 until short rest | Meridian Shock |
| 9–14 | Qi Deviation: one random attribute temporarily −2, Qi cap temporarily halved; needs long rest to recover | Qi Deviation |
| 15+ | Meridian Severance: one random attribute permanently −1 (recoverable via long recuperation/fortuitous encounter), Qi cap permanently −2, collapse on the spot | Severed Meridians |
| Perfect Critical Failure (double 1) | Inner strength abolished on the spot, Qi cap zeroed, requires special fortuitous encounter or peerless spirit medicine to recover | Loss of All Martial Arts |

**Design intent**:
- Qi Deviation is not "an occasional accident" but "the inevitable price of reckless use of power"
- This forces players to make hard tactical choices in desperation — risk overdraft to open the situation, or play safe but possibly lose the fight
- High-Willpower characters can safely bear more risk, giving the "Willpower" attribute clear tactical value

### 3.6 Strategic Depth of Qi

Qi management forms one of the core tactical decisions of combat:

1. **Spend rhythm**: When to use high-cost absolute arts? When to reserve Qi for defense?
2. **Recovery timing**: Meditate in combat gaps? What if ambushed during meditation?
3. **Overdraft gamble**: Risk overdraft in desperation? Is the gain worth the Qi-Deviation risk?
4. **Art pairing**: How to combine sustained-cost type (Golden Bell Cover) with burst-cost type (Six Meridians Sword)?
5. **Inner-art choice**: High Qi capacity (Constitution build) vs high recovery speed (Willpower build) — two completely different combat styles

## 3.5 Health Points and Damage Basics

### Health Points Formula

```
Health Points Cap = Constitution × 5 + Realm Base Bonus
```

| Realm | Health Points (HP) Bonus | CON 3 HP | CON 6 HP | CON 9 HP |
|------|--------|---------|---------|---------|
| First Glimpse | +0 | 15 | 30 | 45 |
| Crossing the Threshold | +5 | 20 | 35 | 50 |
| Complete Mastery | +10 | 25 | 40 | 55 |
| Perfecting the Craft | +15 | 30 | 45 | 60 |
| Peak of Perfection | +20 | 35 | 50 | 65 |
| Return to Simplicity | +25 | 40 | 55 | 70 |

### Damage Types

| Type | Description | Recovery Method |
|------|------|---------|
| External Injury | Physical damage from blades, fists, feet | Natural recovery / Medicine / pills |
| Internal Injury | Inner-strength impact shaking meridians and organs | Needs inner-art regulation / special pills |
| Poison | Toxin continuous erosion | Detox pill / force out toxin with Qi |
| Sealed Meridian | Acupoint sealed / meridian locked | Self-break acupoint (Willpower check) / external acupoint release |

### Dying and Death

- Health Points (HP) drops below 0 → Dying, each round needs a Constitution check (Difficulty Class (DC) 15) to avoid worsening
- Health Points (HP) drops below −(Constitution) → Death
- Stabilize wound (ally Medicine check Difficulty Class (DC) 12) can stop dying deterioration

### Health Points (HP) Recovery

| Recovery Method | Rate | Condition |
|---------|------|------|
| Short rest (~1 hour) | Recover **Constitution** points of Health Points (HP) | Non-combat, light activity |
| Bandage (needs Medicine skill) | One bandage restores **1d6 + Medicine level** HP (External Injury only) | Consumes Jin Chuang Medicine or cloth; Medicine check Difficulty Class (DC) 10 |
| Long rest (one night) | Recover **(Constitution × Realm Tier) External** + **Constitution Internal** HP | Safe environment, at least 6 hours |
| Pills | Instantly restore X points (see Equipment & Pills chapter) | Same pill has cooldown |
| Famous-doctor treatment | Double recovery; can clear "Internal Injury" state | Needs Yaowang Valley or town famous doctor, costs time and silver |

> **External vs Internal recovery**: External Injury can be quickly recovered by bandage and pills; Internal Injury (inner-strength impact, Qi Deviation) only responds to long rest and special pills.

**Recovery examples**:
- Constitution 4 First Realm → short rest 4 HP, long rest 8 HP (External 4×1=4 + Internal 4)
- Constitution 8 Fourth Realm → short rest 8 HP, long rest 40 HP (External 8×4=32 + Internal 8)
- High-realm warriors are not only thick-blooded but astonishingly recoverative — "those deep in inner art never tire in a hundred battles"

## IV. Realm and Growth Framework

### 4.1 Six-Realm System

Realm is the core measure of a martial artist's cultivation tier; each great-realm breakthrough brings a qualitative leap.

| Realm | Title | Combat Reference | Jianghu Renown | Code |
|------|------|---------|---------|------|
| First Realm | **First Glimpse** | Can beat ordinary fighters, three to five cannot close in | Unknown nobody | Initiate |
| Second Realm | **Crossing the Threshold** | Hundred-men foe, can make a name locally | Slightly known | Third-Rate |
| Third Realm | **Complete Mastery** | Jianghu adept, backbone of every sect | Regional hero | Second-Rate |
| Fourth Realm | **Perfecting the Craft** | First-Rate master, sect-elder level | Renowned in the Jianghu | First-Rate |
| Fifth Realm | **Peak of Perfection** | Peerless master, sect-leader level | Commands the martial world | Peerless |
| Sixth Realm | **Return to Simplicity** | A generation's grandmaster, can found a sect | Martial myth | Grandmaster |

### 4.2 Breakthrough Conditions: Trinity

Each realm breakthrough requires **simultaneously satisfying** three conditions — numeric threshold, martial requirement, narrative unlock. None may be omitted, ensuring growth is both playable and story-driven.

| Breakthrough To | Experience (Martial Truth) | Martial Requirement | Plot / Mindset Lock |
|--------|-------------------|---------|------------|
| Second Realm | Cumulative ≥ 20 XP | Master at least one entry manual, one basic martial art | Complete entry trial (sect coming-of-age mission / first fame in battle / epiphany after a life-and-death moment) |
| Third Realm | Cumulative ≥ 70 XP | Orthodox manual or above, master at least 5 martial arts | Comprehend "Martial Truth" (mindset externalization in a key plot: burst forth protecting someone important, insight after defeat and rebirth, sensing the martial path in nature) |
| Fourth Realm | Cumulative ≥ 170 XP | Superior manual or above | Renowned locally (renown reaches "Regional Hero") + complete at least one Grudge/Favor line (revenge / repay kindness / settle) |
| Fifth Realm | Cumulative ≥ 370 XP | Peerless divine art | Experience a major Jianghu event (orthodox/evil great war / sect survival / All-Under-Heaven Martial Assembly), create your own move (or personally adapt an existing absolute art) |
| Sixth Realm | Cumulative ≥ 870 XP | Unique inner art mastered (self-created or adapted superior manual into a unique one) | Complete a legendary plot line (e.g. unify / destroy a great power, end a century of grudges, find the ultimate answer of the martial path), mind at peace, can found a sect |

**Plot-lock design philosophy**:
- A pure numeric-experience system leads to "grind monsters to level up" — fatal in a story-driven wuxia TRPG
- In wuxia novels, the protagonist never breaks through naturally "after killing enough monsters" — every breakthrough is a mental event tightly bound to the story
- Plot locks ensure the player's growth trajectory resonates with the Jianghu story, avoiding the absurd disconnect of "my combat power is already grandmaster but I'm still fighting mountain bandits"
- The GM holds final adjudication, but is also encouraged to proactively lay breakthrough opportunities for players

**Plot-lock examples (for GM reference)**:

| Realm Breakthrough | Plot-Lock Example |
|---------|-----------|
| First → Second Realm | Complete the first truly meaningful Jianghu mission (not newbie guidance); or survive a life-and-death battle |
| Second → Third Realm | Prove oneself within the sect (e.g. top three in the sect minor tournament, complete a sect trial); or independently resolve a local-level crisis outside |
| Third → Fourth Realm | Renowned locally — your name appears in Jianghu rumors (recorded in Baixiao Sheng's journal). Need to do something affecting the power landscape: e.g. mediate / intensify conflict between two powers, defeat a famed master |
| Fourth → Fifth Realm | Publicly acknowledged by the Jianghu — at least two different powers' sect-leader / elder-level NPCs express recognition. Suggested plot: participate in a world-shaping great event (e.g. Wanxiang Grotto contest, Tianjian Alliance abdication struggle) and play a key role |
| Fifth → Sixth Realm | Legendary — complete a full legendary plot line: unify / destroy a great power, end a century of grudges, find the ultimate answer of the martial path, or pioneer a new martial school. Must have at least one "reason worth dying for" no longer existing (let go of obsession), mind truly at peace |

> **GM pacing suggestion**: Do not let all PCs break through at once. Staggered breakthroughs give each player a "highlight moment." Someone at the Fourth Realm while another just reached the Third — that is not imbalance, that is the Jianghu.

### 4.3 Realm's Effect on Attributes

Each time a great realm is broken:

| Breakthrough Gain | Note |
|---------|------|
| **Attribute +3** | Distributable to any attribute, at most +2 per single attribute |
| **Qi realm bonus** | See Qi system (Section III) |
| **Attribute cap unlock** | See cap table below |
| **Dice-modification upgrade** | See core dice system (Section II) |
| **Martial Proficiency ×1** | Gain a realm-exclusive ability (defined by martial-art / character systems; e.g. Fourth Realm gains "Protective Qi Body" passive, Fifth Realm gains "Mind Moves Body Follows" initiative modifier) |
| **Martial-tier unlock** | Higher realm unlocks qualification to learn higher-tier arts |

**Realm attribute caps**:

| Realm | Attribute Value Cap |
|------|----------|
| First Realm · First Glimpse | 5 |
| Second Realm · Crossing the Threshold | 6 |
| Third Realm · Complete Mastery | 7 |
| Fourth Realm · Perfecting the Craft | 8 |
| Fifth Realm · Peak of Perfection | 10 |
| Sixth Realm · Return to Simplicity | 12 (human limit) |

**Fun of the cap design**:
- First Realm max attribute 5 (modifier +1) — even the gifted cannot surpass ordinary men severalfold without realm support
- Sixth Realm breaks the human limit (attribute can reach 12, modifier +8) — this is the realm of "pluck a leaf, fly a flower, all can wound" and "not a feather can be added"
- The attribute cap prevents "lopsided freaks" — a character with Strength 10 but other attributes only 2 cannot exist in the First Realm

### 4.4 Experience and Long-Term Pacing

#### Experience Gain Reference (per session)

| Source | Experience (XP) Range |
|------|---------|
| Basic participation | 2–3 |
| Combat victory / major tactical decision | 1–3 |
| Key plot advancement | 2–5 |
| Roleplay / character-growth performance | 1–3 |
| Adventure / achievement / puzzle solved | 1–5 |
| **Average per session** | **5–10 XP** |

#### Reference Pacing Standardized on 20 Sessions

| Session | Realm | Core Narrative Node |
|------|------|------------|
| 1st | First Realm | First entering the Jianghu / joining a sect / building the relationship web |
| 2nd–3rd | First Realm → | First battle, learn entry arts, learn basic Jianghu rules |
| 4th | **↗ break to Second Realm** | Entry trial complete, gain sect / Jianghu recognition |
| 5th–8th | Second Realm → | Jianghu tempering, collect arts, build renown |
| 9th | **↗ break to Third Realm** | First comprehension of Martial Truth, key plot event |
| 10th–14th | Third Realm → | Jianghu contest, interwoven grudges, sect missions |
| 15th | **↗ break to Fourth Realm** | Renowned locally, insight after major victory / defeat |
| 16th–20th | Fourth Realm → | Jianghu storms, absolute-art contests, embryo of self-created art |
| 21st+ | **↗ break to Fifth Realm** | Become a Peerless master, join world-shaping events |
| 25th+ | **↗ break to Sixth Realm** | Legendary plot line complete, found a sect or retire |

**Flexible adjustment**:
- **10 short/medium sessions**: break one realm every 2–3 sessions, compress the Jianghu-simulation part, focus on the main line
- **30 long/medium sessions**: insert more side quests, Jianghu events, personal plots, and deep exploration of apprenticeship within the above rhythm
- **GM pacing control**: Realm breakthroughs are regulated by the GM per plot and player state; XP is a necessary condition, not an auto-trigger

## V. Design Trade-offs

### Trade-off One: Dice System — Why We Rejected D20 and Dice Pools

**Dropped D20**: D20's 5% crit success/failure rate produces a negative "frequent failure" experience at the low end. In wuxia context, a decade-trained character being beaten by a hooligan on luck alone should not be as high as 5%. Moreover, D20's uniform distribution means the marginal gain of an attribute modifier is constant — every +1 is +5% success. Mathematically "fair," but experientially "boring": players feel no tension of "about to break through." 2d10's bell curve naturally produces the experience of "the closer to the difficulty line, the more tense."

**Dropped dice pool**: The dice-pool system's advantages (intuitive success degree, visualized skill growth) have not yet formed enough awareness in the Chinese TRPG circle. Moreover, counting success degrees each round significantly slows the pace in combats of 4+ people. The Difficulty Class (DC) system's "call out a number → GM adjudicates" is the smoothest adjudication flow, fitting the "medium complexity" positioning.

### Trade-off Two: Attribute System — Why Not Traditional D&D Six Attributes

The problems with traditional D&D six attributes (Strength/Agility/Constitution/Intelligence/Wisdom/Charisma):
- **Intelligence** lacks distinction in wuxia context — martial artists need no puzzle-solving or academic knowledge. Replaced by **Comprehension** to precisely depict "martial comprehension"
- **Wisdom** is split into **Comprehension** (combat intuition) and **Willpower** (spirit resist, mind stability), which have utterly distinct narrative functions in wuxia
- **Charisma** is remodeled — not only a social attribute, but also the "pressure formed by projected inner strength," directly affecting aura duels in combat

The six-attribute design ensures rich Build space (20–25 points at char creation, each attribute 1–4 start, plus sect/background bonus), while ensuring every attribute has clear in-game utility — no "junk attribute" exists.

### Trade-off Three: Realm System — Why We Need Plot Locks

The biggest problem with a pure-XP realm system: players may become "grandmasters" in numbers but are still "fighting mooks" in narrative. This is acceptable in video games (Zelda's Link at full HP and weapons still cuts grass), but in a TRPG it utterly destroys immersion.

Plot locks liberate "growth" from the numeric table and return it to the story — **a realm breakthrough is not a popup when XP is full, but what your character experienced in the Jianghu, comprehended, and changed.** This technically adds narrative burden on the GM, but this is the core value of a narrative-driven TRPG.

## II. Sect / Background System

In the Jianghu, a sect is not only the source of martial arts, but determines the character's righteous/evil stance, Jianghu connections, and growth path.

### 2.2 Details of Each Sect

#### (1) Sword Sect — Huashan Swordsman / Emei Heroine

> "Sword in hand, the world is yours to walk."

- **Core attribute bonus**: STR+1, AGI+1

  - Huashan Sword Art (entry external art · sword)

- **Sect resources**: May borrow sword manuals from the sect, gain chances at famed-sword inheritance
- **Jianghu standing**: Famous orthodox sect, good relations with officialdom / orthodox figures
- **Stance**: Orthodox (initial Heroism +20 tendency)

#### (2) Qi Sect — Wudang Daoist / Quanzhen Disciple

> "Qi circulates the whole body, all methods return to the source."

- **Core attribute bonus**: CON+1, WIL+1
- **Starting martial arts**:
  - Taiji Fist (entry external art · fist/palm)
  - Pure Yang Mental Method (advanced internal art)
- **Sect specialty**: Deep inner strength, large Qi pool, suits long battles; inner art can feed external-art power
- **Sect resources**: May comprehend Daoist classics, gain inner-art breakthrough chances
- **Jianghu standing**: Daoist orthodoxy, respected by all sides
- **Stance**: Orthodox (initial Heroism +20 tendency)

#### (3) Poison Sect — Tang Sect Disciple

> "Hidden weapons leave no shadow, poison treads the world."

- **Core attribute bonus**: AGI+1, CMP+1
- **Starting martial arts**:
  - Rain of Flowers (entry external art · hidden weapon)
  - Five Poisons Scripture (entry special art · poison)
- **Sect specialty**: Ranged hidden-weapon attack, poison can stack sustained damage and debuff states; excels at stealth and trap-laying
- **Sect resources**: Tang Sect poison vault, hidden-weapon craft inheritance
- **Jianghu standing**: Feared; orthodox unwilling to cooperate but also unwilling to be enemy, underworld often comes for poison
- **Stance**: Evil-leaning Neutral (initial Heroism −10 tendency)

#### (4) Vajra Sect — Shaolin Monk

> "Indestructible golden body, Vajra's furious gaze."

- **Core attribute bonus**: STR+1, CON+1
- **Starting martial arts**:
  - Arhat Fist (entry external art · fist/palm)
  - Vajra Mental Method (entry internal art · hard art)
- **Sect specialty**: High Constitution, strong defense, can be the party front-liner; some arts have taunt / guard effects
- **Sect resources**: Shaolin Scripture Pavilion (may study various arts), Shaolin pills (recovery type)
- **Jianghu standing**: Pillar of the martial world, words carry weight
- **Stance**: Orthodox (initial Heroism +25 tendency)

#### (5) Shadow Sect — Xiaoyao Disciple / Ancient Tomb Heir

> "Treading snow without trace, coming and going without a shadow."

- **Core attribute bonus**: AGI+2
- **Starting martial arts**:
  - Treading Snow Without Trace (entry lightfoot)
  - Xiaoyao Mental Method (entry internal art)
- **Sect specialty**: Excels at lightfoot / agility, extremely high initiative, strong evasion; may learn some strange arts
- **Sect resources**: Freely appear in all terrains, reach places ordinary men cannot (adventure trigger rate +)
- **Jianghu standing**: Mysterious, whereabouts uncertain
- **Stance**: Neutral (initial Heroism tendency: 0)

#### (6) Skill Sect — Gaibang Disciple

> "The world is vast; no beggar knows it not."

- **Core attribute bonus**: CON+1, CHA+1
- **Starting martial arts**:
  - Dog-Beating Staff (entry external art · staff)
  - Beggar's Mental Method (entry internal art)
- **Sect specialty**: Owns the Jianghu's largest information network; Dog-Beating Staff combines attack and control
- **Sect resources**: Gaibang information net (gather-info check Difficulty Class (DC) −2), free food and lodging at branch lodges everywhere
- **Jianghu standing**: The world's largest gang, extremely widespread
- **Stance**: Orthodox (initial Heroism +15 tendency)

#### (7) Medicine Sect — Yaowang Valley Disciple

> "A healer's benevolent heart; medicine cures the disease."

- **Core attribute bonus**: CMP+1, CON+1
- **Starting martial arts**:
  - Medicine King Needle Art (entry special art · medical martial)
  - Medicine King Heart Scripture (entry internal art · healing)
- **Sect specialty**: Healing ability; can refine pills (recovery, buff, antidote); acupuncture can simultaneously deal damage and healing effects
- **Sect resources**: Yaowang Valley Hundred-Herb Garden (rare herbs), Medicine King Canon (recipe inheritance)
- **Jianghu standing**: Every power unwilling to offend, universally respected
- **Stance**: Neutral (initial Heroism tendency: +10)

#### (8) Independent — Self-Taught / Jianghu Wanderer

> "No sect, no school; self-made style."

- **Core attribute bonus**: Freely allocate 3 points to any attribute (may exceed other sects' bonus cap)
- **Starting martial arts**:
  - Basic Fist and Foot (entry external art · unarmed)
  - Choose one entry martial art (coordinated by GM per character background)
- **Sect specialty**: No sect constraints, completely free art acquisition; greatest Build flexibility as the game progresses
- **Sect resources**: No fixed resources, all accumulated through Jianghu experience
- **Jianghu standing**: Initially unknown to all
- **Stance**: Self-chosen (initial Heroism tendency: self-chosen between −20 and +20)

#### (9) Court Sect — Six Doors Probation Constable / Imperial Guard Attendant

> "Eat the ruler's salary, serve the ruler's affairs. Jianghu men say the court's blade has no spine — they do not understand that this official robe weighs more than any sect."

- **Core attribute bonus**: STR+1, WIL+1
- **Starting martial arts**:
  - Six-Door Swift Saber (entry · external) or Imperial Guard Spear Art (entry · external), choose one
  - Official-Pressure Dominance (special) — dominate the opponent with official authority / aura, Willpower opposed
- **Sect specialty** (choose one of three):
  - "Legal Principle": Charisma check Difficulty Class (DC) −2 when negotiating in towns / government premises; may review case files, commandeer post stations
  - "Manhunt": Tracking / Read Intent +1 (no point cost); may access Six Doors wanted archives
  - "Imperial Guard Training": Gain +1 Strength per realm breakthrough (not counting against attribute allocation)
- **Sect resources**: Monthly salary 10 taels (collected at Six Doors branches in each prefecture); may borrow 1–2 constables to assist (requires bureau-chief approval); government documents (pass / message token)
- **Jianghu standing**: Respected by officialdom, feared by the Jianghu — orthodox sects do not reject but keep distance; evil sects see you as a natural enemy; commoners both respect and fear you
- **Stance**: Righteous (initial Heroism: +10)
- **Sect constraint**: "Obey orders but not summons" — the GM may at any time issue a mission via "superior's secret order" (but not abuse it; at most 1 per 3 sessions). Disobedience stops salary, lowers government favor, and in severe cases leads to dismissal and manhunt.
- **Defection consequence**: Dismissed and manhunted — Six Doors issues a wanted poster. All town government premises are unfriendly to you (Difficulty Class (DC) +5). But your learned Court-Sect arts are retained (moves unchanged, Qi/attribute retention).

**Special positioning of Court-Sect players**:
- You are the court's eyes and hands in the Jianghu — but you need not be the court's puppet. You may choose "serve the ruler's affairs" or "a general abroad need not obey every royal command"
- Your official robe is both weapon and shackle — wearing it you may enter many places, but only taking it off can you do some things
- Unlike the Independent: the Independent is unbound but has no one to rely on; the Court Sect has the court as backing but must bear the label of "the government's man"

## III. Attribute System

> **⚠ Coordination point with Core Mechanics**: Attribute checks and the dice system are designed by Bi Douyin in Core Mechanics (01). This chapter defines attribute names, meanings, and ranges; the specific check formula is unified after Core Mechanics is settled.

### 3.1 The Six Attributes

| Attribute | Abbrev | Affected Range |
|------|------|---------|
| **Strength (STR)** | Str | Physical attack damage, carrying, armor-breaking, some external-art power bonus |
| **Agility (AGI)** | Agi | Initiative, evasion rate, lightfoot effect, ranged/hidden-weapon attack precision |
| **Constitution (CON)** | Con | Health Points cap, poison/injury resist, natural recovery speed |
| **Comprehension (CMP)** | Cmp | Martial-learning speed, secret-manual study efficiency, adventure trigger chance |
| **Willpower (WIL)** | Wil | Resist inner demons / pressure, Qi-Deviation resist, meditation efficiency, will opposed |
| **Charisma (CHA)** | Cha | Social checks, trade discount, NPC initial favor, Jianghu-rumor spread |

**Attribute value ranges**:

| Value | Human Correspondence | Martial Meaning |
|------|---------|---------|
| 1–2 | Frail / clumsy | Lacking in this, hard to walk the Jianghu |
| 3–4 | Ordinary person | Average level of a Jianghu man |
| 5–6 | Gifted | Worthy of being a local adept |
| 7–8 | Dragon among men | Enough to serve as sect leader / elder |
| 9–10 | Century genius | Talent foundation of a legendary figure |
| 11+ | Transcendent to saintly | Only reachable by breakthroughs during play (high-power cap) |

### 3.2 Point-Buy Rules

- **Point pool**: 25 points
- **Starting value**: All attributes start at **1**
- **Purchase price**: 1 point = attribute +1 (no tiered pricing, simple and clear)
- **Char-creation cap**: Single attribute max **8** (excluding sect bonus)
- **Sect bonus**: Added after point-buy, **may exceed the cap of 8** (i.e. after sect bonus max can reach 10)
- **Lower bound**: No attribute may drop below 1 (i.e. cannot lower an attribute to gain extra points)

#### Point-Buy Examples

| Plan | STR | AGI | CON | CMP | WIL | CHA | Spent |
|------|------|------|------|------|------|------|------|
| Balanced | 5 | 5 | 5 | 5 | 5 | 5 | 30 (needs sect bonus to fill attributes) |
| STR Focus | 8 | 4 | 4 | 4 | 3 | 2 | 25 |
| WIL Focus | 3 | 3 | 4 | 4 | 8 | 3 | 25 |
| AGI Player | 3 | 8 | 4 | 4 | 3 | 3 | 25 |

> **Design intent**: 25-point buy + 6 attributes at minimum 1 = 6 points already used + 19 points freely allocatable. A typical allocation pushes 3 attributes to 5–6, keeping the rest at 3–4 — guaranteeing clear strengths and weaknesses while leaving room to grow.

### 3.3 Attributes and Sub-Derived Values

The following derived values are set by attributes; specific calculation is settled after Core Mechanics:

| Derived Value | Attributes Involved | Note |
|--------|---------|------|
| **Health Points (HP)** | Constitution × 5 + Realm Bonus | Character's total HP (see Core Mechanics) |
| **Qi Pool** | Constitution × Inner-Art Coefficient + Realm Value | Resource pool consumed by inner arts and moves (see Core Mechanics) |
| **Initiative** | Agility + Willpower | Decides action order in combat |
| **Evasion** | Agility Modifier + Lightfoot Level | Ability to avoid attacks |
| **Physical Defense** | Constitution Modifier + Armor | Reduces physical damage |
| **Will Defense** | Willpower Modifier | Resists inner demons / pressure / spirit-type arts |

## V. Heroism and Infamy

### 5.1 Concept

Heroism and Infamy are not two ends of one dimension, but **two independent dimensions**:

```
                Infamy (high)
                    │
    Both Righteous     │      Evil Grand Demon
    and Evil (dual-high)│      (high Infamy, low Heroism)
                    │
────────────────────┼──────────────────── Heroism (high)
                    │
    Unknown Nobody      │      The Greatest of Xia
    (dual-low)         │      (high Heroism, low Infamy)
                    │
                Infamy (low)
```

- **Heroism**: Righting wrongs, eliminating the wicked, saving the desperate → orthodox NPC favor, righteous-art unlock
- **Infamy**: Killing for spoils, colluding with the underworld, violating prohibition by force → underworld NPC favor, evil-art unlock

> **Design trade-off note**: The problem with the traditional "good-evil single axis" is that a both-righteous-and-evil character (like Huang Yaoshi, or Xie Xun's early phase) cannot be accurately described. The dual-dimension design allows complex characters who are "spat on by orthodoxy yet feared by the underworld" or "given face by both black and white."

### 5.2 Initial Settings

| Sect | Initial Heroism | Initial Infamy |
|------|-----------|-----------|
| Sword Sect | +15 | 0 |
| Qi Sect | +15 | 0 |
| Poison Sect | 0 | +10 |
| Vajra Sect | +20 | 0 |
| Shadow Sect | 0 | 0 |
| Skill Sect | +10 | 0 |
| Medicine Sect | +10 | 0 |
| Independent | Self-chosen (sum of the two between −10 and +10 not exceeding 15) | Same as above |

### 5.3 Gain and Loss

#### Heroism

| Behavior | Heroism Change | Example |
|------|-----------|------|
| Risk life to save others, no reward sought | +5~10 | Jump into a fire to save a child |
| Defeat a local tyrant / demon terrorizing the area | +2~5 | Wipe out bandits entrenched on a hill |
| Help commoners, no reward sought | +1~3 | Escort refugees, fund a bridge |
| Refuse fair payment to help the poor | +1 | Treat the poor for free |
| Stand by while able to save (see death, not save) | −3~5 | Witness evil but stand aside |
| Harm the innocent | −5~10 | Slaughter civilians, wanton killing |
| Break a heroic pledge | −3~5 | Promise protection then abandon it |

#### Infamy

| Behavior | Infamy Change | Example |
|------|-----------|------|
| Kill with cruel means | +3~8 | Exterminate a family, torture opponents |
| Collude with underworld powers | +1~3 | Work for the Demon Cult, sell forbidden medicine |
| Openly challenge orthodox authority | +2~5 | Cause chaos at an orthodox sect, insult its leader |
| Intimidating behavior that inspires fear | +1~3 | Force others to submit with force |
| Right wrongs, save the desperate | −3~5 | The only way to wash off infamy |
| Long retirement from the Jianghu | −2 per year | Infamy fades with time |

### 5.4 Jianghu Influence

| Heroism | Jianghu Title | Actual Effect |
|--------|---------|---------|
| ≥40 | Grand Xia | Orthodox sects offer shelter and resources; may request orthodox NPC help |
| 20~39 | Xia Knight | Orthodox NPC favor +1; may enter orthodox sects |
| 1~19 | Budding Heroism | Loved by commoners; some orthodox NPCs favorable |
| 0 | Unknown Nobody | No special effect |
| Infamy | Jianghu Title | Actual Effect |
| ≥40 | Great Demon | Orthodox manhunt; underworld fears and willing to submit |
| 20~39 | Notorious | Underworld NPC favor +1; orthodoxy refuses cooperation |
| 1~19 | Crooked Deeds | Some orthodox NPCs cold |
| 0 | Nobody | No special effect |

**Dual-high case** (Heroism ≥20 and Infamy ≥20):
- Title: **Both Righteous and Evil**
- Effect: Friends and enemies in both orthodox and evil camps; some neutral powers open only to those who "see through worldly affairs" (e.g. Shadow Sect higher-ups, Ancient Tomb Sect) may accept you

### 5.5 Heroism / Infamy and Martial Arts

- Some arts have **Heroism / Infamy thresholds**:
  - E.g. "Eighteen Dragon-Subduing Palms" needs Heroism ≥30
  - E.g. "Star-Absorbing Great Method" needs Infamy ≥10
  - E.g. "Taiji Fist" (advanced) has no requirement — the great Dao is simple, no distinction of righteous or evil
- The **nature** of self-created arts is influenced by the character's Heroism / Infamy —
  - High Heroism tends toward creating aboveboard, righteous arts
  - High Infamy more likely creates sinister, vicious moves

## VI. Step-by-Step Character-Building Tutorial

> This chapter uses **Liu Rufeng** as a running example, leading new players step by step to create their First-Realm character. Each step has explanation, calculation demonstration, and design rationale. When done, your character sheet should be directly usable in play.

The whole process takes about **15–20 minutes**; tools needed: rulebook, dice (2d10), a blank character sheet (or just paper and pen).

---

### Step One: Define the Character Concept (about 2 min)

Before picking up the pen, think through three questions:

1. **What kind of person do you want to play?** — A sword-fighter quick to repay grudges? A calm, reserved Daoist? A traceless assassin? A healer who saves lives?
2. **What is your personality?** — Passionate and impulsive, or calm and deep? Upright and stern, or both righteous and evil?
3. **What role do you take in the party?** — Front-line tank? Main damage dealer? Back-line support? Control / kite?

The answers guide every later choice. You need no perfect concept — a vague idea is enough. The rest of the details emerge naturally during character building.

He is a young Huashan swordsman from a martial family, trained in the sword since childhood, gifted but slightly impulsive. In battle he wants to **suppress opponents with fast sword, high hit to strike first**, serving as the party's main physical damage dealer.

### Step Three: Allocate Attributes (about 5 min)

This is the most central and most deliberative step of character building.

#### Rules Quick Reference

- **Point pool**: 25 points
- **Starting value**: Each attribute starts at 1
- **Purchase price**: 1 point = attribute value +1 (linear pricing)
- **Char-creation cap**: Single attribute max 8 (excluding sect bonus)
- **Sect bonus**: Added after point-buy, **may exceed the cap of 8**

#### Six Attributes' Combat Influence Quick Reference

| Attribute | Influence | Suited For |
|------|------|---------|
| **Strength (STR)** | Melee damage, heavy weapons, armor-break | Sword Sect, Vajra Sect, external output |
| **Agility (AGI)** | Initiative, evasion, lightfoot effect, hidden-weapon precision | Shadow Sect, Poison Sect, lightfoot flow |
| **Constitution (CON)** | Health Points (HP), Qi cap, poison resist, defense | Vajra Sect, Qi Sect, front-line |
| **Comprehension (CMP)** | Martial-learning speed, move-breaking, skill points | Medicine Sect, Poison Sect, multi-cultivation flow |
| **Willpower (WIL)** | Qi recovery, Qi-Deviation resist, inner-art drive | Qi Sect, inner-art flow |
| **Charisma (CHA)** | Social checks, NPC favor, Jianghu renown | Skill Sect (info / network flow) |

#### Allocation Strategy: Primary — Secondary — Weakness

Suggest allocating attribute points in three steps:

1. **Set primary attribute(s)** (1–2, push to 6–8): the attribute you rely on most in combat
2. **Set secondary attribute(s)** (2–3, keep at 3–5): secondary attributes not easily suppressed by opponents
3. **Accept weakness(es)** (1–2, at 1–3): can be role-played as a character trait

These three steps build a clear attribute portrait, avoiding the mediocre "jack of all trades, master of none" character.

### Step Four: Calculate Derived Values (about 2 min)

With attributes set, calculate your combat base data by the following formulas:

#### Derived Value Formulas

| Derived Value | Formula | Liu Rufeng Calc |
|--------|------|-----------|
| **Health Points (HP)** | Constitution × 5 + Realm Bonus | 4 × 5 + 0 = **20** |
| **Qi Cap** | Constitution × Inner-Art Coefficient + Realm Bonus | 4 × 2 + 0 = **8** |
| **Initiative** | Agility + Willpower | 6 + 3 = **+9** |
| **Evasion** | Agility Modifier + Lightfoot Level | (6−4) + 0 = **+2** |
| **Physical Defense** | Constitution Modifier + Armor Value | (4−4) + 0 = **0** |
| **Will Defense** | Willpower Modifier | 3 − 4 = **−1** |

#### Formula Details

**① Health Points (HP)**

```
Health Points (HP) = Constitution × 5 + Realm Base Bonus
```

The realm base bonus depends on the character's current martial realm:

| Realm | Health Points (HP) Bonus |
|------|--------|
| First Realm · First Glimpse | +0 |
| Second Realm · Crossing the Threshold | +5 |
| Third Realm · Complete Mastery | +10 |
| Fourth Realm · Perfecting the Craft | +15 |
| Fifth Realm · Peak of Perfection | +20 |
| Sixth Realm · Return to Simplicity | +25 |

> Liu Rufeng is First Realm, Health Points (HP) = 4 × 5 + 0 = **20**. This HP cannot last long in a frontal slugfest; he needs to rely on initiative and evasion to reduce damage taken.

**② Qi Cap**

```
Qi Cap = Constitution × Inner-Art Coefficient + Realm Base Value
```

The inner-art coefficient is set by the highest inner art you currently cultivate:

| Inner-Art Tier | Coefficient | Example |
|---------|------|------|
| No inner art | 1 | Ordinary person, some Poison Sect players |
| Entry manual | 2 | Sword Sect Mental Method, Vajra Mental Method, Beggar's Mental Method |
| Orthodox manual | 3 | Pure Yang Mental Method (advanced), Purple Dawn Art |
| Superior manual | 4 | Nine Yang Divine Art, Nine Yin Manual |
| Peerless divine art | 5 | Great Achievement of Tendon-Changing Classic, Grand Mystery Classic |

Realm base value:

| Realm | Qi Bonus |
|------|---------|
| First Realm | +0 |
| Second Realm | +3 |
| Third Realm | +6 |
| Fourth Realm | +10 |
| Fifth Realm | +15 |
| Sixth Realm | +20 |

> Liu Rufeng holds "Sword Sect Mental Method" (entry internal art, coefficient = 2), Qi = 4 × 2 + 0 = **8**. 8 Qi means he can use "White Rainbow Pierces the Sun" (cost 1 Qi) 8 times, but if Qi runs out he must rest — the typical dilemma of a novice swordsman.

**③ Initiative**

```
Initiative = Agility + Willpower (uses the attribute values themselves, not modifiers)
```

Initiative decides action order in combat. Higher initiative acts first. Initiative check = 2d10 + Initiative + Lightfoot / martial-art bonus.

> Liu Rufeng: Agility 6 + Willpower 3 = **+9**. With the initiative-bonus effect of Huashan Sword Art · White Rainbow Pierces the Sun, he can almost always seize first-strike attack in round one.

**④ Evasion**

```
Evasion = Agility Modifier + Lightfoot Level
```

When a character chooses "evade" rather than "block / defend" to respond to an attack, this value is the bonus for the check. Lightfoot level comes from lightfoot arts cultivated (Treading Snow Without Trace, Cloud-Ladder Ascension, etc.).

> Liu Rufeng has not cultivated lightfoot, Evasion = (6−4) + 0 = **+2**. Only by body instinct, better than nothing. To raise survival, he should consider learning a lightfoot art later.

**⑤ Physical Defense**

```
Physical Defense = Constitution Modifier + Armor Value
```

Physical Defense directly reduces physical damage taken. Armor value comes from worn armor (leather, iron, etc.).

> Liu Rufeng wears no armor, Physical Defense = (4−4) + 0 = **0**. This means he takes full damage from every direct hit — the fate of a fast swordsman: either dodge, or tough it out.

**⑥ Will Defense**

```
Will Defense = Willpower Modifier
```

Used to resist inner demons, pressure, spirit-type arts (such as Heavenly Demon Zither, Heart-Devouring Finger, etc.). This is Liu Rufeng's most obvious weakness.

## VII. Design Rationale and Trade-offs

### 7.1 Design Rationale

1. **Why 25-point buy?**
   - 25 points + 6 base points (1 each for 6 attributes) = 31 total attribute points
   - Average ~5.17 per attribute; this number allows a character specialty in 1–2 attributes (7–8) while keeping the rest medium (3–5)
   - A moderate-complexity char-creation experience, no extra computation burden from tiered pricing

2. **Why do sects provide fixed arts rather than choices?**
   - Reduces analysis paralysis at char creation
   - Sect arts are "signature," part of sect identity
   - Variation and personalization come through later in-game art acquisition

3. **Why Heroism / Infamy dual-dimension?**
   - A single "good → evil" axis cannot describe the many gray characters in wuxia literature
   - The dual dimension supports "both righteous and evil," "violence against violence," "reclusive master" and other character images
   - Gives the GM richer plot-trigger conditions

4. **Why does the Independent get "free 3 points" more than other sects?**
   - The Independent has no sect-inherited martial advantage (fewer and weaker starting arts)
   - The extra attribute points compensate for the disadvantages of no sect resources and no connections
   - Embodies "the all-roundness tempered by wandering the Jianghu"

### 7.2 Design Trade-offs

**Trade-off One: General Skills vs Professional Depth**

The current skill system has 19 items, slightly rich for "medium complexity." Considered reducing to 10–12, but the core reason to keep more skills is — the requirement analysis explicitly stated "all trades have their uses." Non-combat professions (e.g. Medicine, Alchemy, Trapcraft, Disguise) need enough skills to support their play style. Of course, this also means the GM must ensure non-combat skills have use scenes during play — this should be emphasized in the GM Guide.

**Trade-off Two: Tiered Attribute Pricing vs Linear Pricing**

Considered tiered pricing (higher attributes cost more), but ultimately chose linear pricing (1 point = 1 level),because:
- Char-creation time target is 15–20 minutes; tiered pricing would significantly increase computation time
- Under linear pricing, the balance of "one specialty, many skills" vs "jack of all trades" is tested by the GM's actual scene checks — some scenes high Strength is useless, needing Comprehension or Charisma
- If playtesting later finds extreme point-buy too OP, a post-build "growth decay" can be added to correct it (i.e. the cost of further raising high attributes grows)

**Trade-off Three: Can Righteous/Evil Stance Change?**

The current design allows Heroism / Infamy to change with behavior — meaning a Vajra Sect monk can fall to the demonic path, and a Tang Sect disciple can be cleansed. Sect identity stays, but the behavior label can change, fitting the core design philosophy that "the Jianghu is alive." But this also means sect NPCs need reaction rules for "black-sheep disciples" and "prodigal returns" — this part is left to the Jianghu Simulation module.

## 〇. Martial Data Format Notes (Must Read)

Each martial art's damage and hit follow a unified format. The following explains why it is designed this way, and how to read the Martial Arts Codex.

### Damage Formula

```
Damage = Base Die + Attribute Modifier
```

**Reading "1d8+STR(+4)"**: 1d8 is the base die, "+STR(+4)" = add the Strength **modifier**. The +4 in parentheses is an example — when the character's Strength is 8, modifier = 8−4 = +4, total damage = 1d8+4. **The same move at Strength 4 only hits 1d8+0.**

> **Key**: What is added is the modifier (attribute − 4), not the attribute itself.

### Relationship Between Weapon and Move

When performing a martial move, the move's damage **already includes the weapon contribution** — no need to stack "weapon 1d8 + move 1d6." "Huashan Sword Art · White Rainbow Pierces the Sun (1d8+STR)" is the complete damage of swinging the sword.

A normal attack (not performing a martial art) uses the weapon's base damage (see equipment table: long sword 1d8 + Strength modifier).

## I. Martial Classification and Qi Mode

### 1.1 Four Great Martial Categories

| Category | Essence | Typical Arts | Qi Role |
|------|------|---------|---------|
| **External** | Attack / defense technique | Sword art, fist art, saber art, staff art | Consume Qi to drive powerful moves |
| **Internal** | Qi-cultivation mental method | Mental method, Qi-circulation art | Provides Qi pool, recovery, passive buffs |
| **Lightfoot** | Agility and movement | Footwork, leaping art | Spend Qi for mobility |
| **Special** | Support / abnormal martial | Poison art, hidden weapon, medical martial, formation | Varied consumption mode |

### 1.2 Detailed Categories

#### (1) External Art — The Study of Moves

External arts are the attack/defense techniques a character uses in combat. Each external art contains a set of **moves** that can be combined into combos.

**External art structure**:

```
External Art Name: Huashan Sword Art
Type: External · Sword
Tier: Entry Art
─────────────────────────────
Move list:
  White Rainbow Pierces the Sun (normal attack)  Qi cost: 0   Effect: basic sword strike, Strength bonus
  Pine Greets Guest (block)            Qi cost: 1   Effect: this round block value +2, next round counter +1 damage
  White Cloud Leaves the Ravine (thrust) Qi cost: 2   Effect: ignore 2 points defense, Agility bonus
  Phoenix Arrives (finisher)          Qi cost: 4   Effect: 3-hit combo, each hit Strength bonus, needs Complete Mastery+ to unlock
```

- **Move count**: Depends on art tier and proficiency
  - Entry art: 3 moves (First Glimpse unlocks 1, Minor Accomplishment +1, Complete Mastery +1)
  - Advanced art: 4 moves
  - Superior art: 5 moves
  - Absolute art: 6 moves

- **Qi cost**: Stronger moves cost more
  - Normal-attack type: 0 Qi (base output)
  - Control / block type: 1–3 Qi
  - Powerful strike: 2–5 Qi
  - Finisher / ultimate: 4–8 Qi

#### (2) Internal Art — The Root of Qi

Internal art is the foundation of a character's Qi cultivation. Internal art does not directly produce moves, but decides:
- **Qi pool size** (max Qi value)
- **Qi recovery speed** (per round / per action recovery)
- **External tier it can support** (low-tier internal cannot drive high-tier external)
- **Passive buffs** (e.g. damage bonus, damage reduction, attribute boost)

**Internal art structure**:

```
Internal Art Name: Pure Yang Mental Method
Type: Internal
Tier: Advanced Art
─────────────────────────────
Qi pool: base 10 + Willpower×3 (e.g. Willpower 6 → 10+18=28 Qi)
Qi recovery: 2/round (base) + Willpower/4 (floor) (e.g. Willpower 6 → 2+1=3/round)
─────────────────────────────
Passive buffs (unlock by proficiency):
  First Glimpse: external damage +1 (fist/palm only)
  Minor Accomplishment: Qi recovery extra +1/round
  Complete Mastery: inner-strength damage taken −2
  Perfecting the Craft: may use "Taiji Force" — convert successful defense into counter bonus
  Transcendent: Qi pool extra +10
─────────────────────────────
Supported external tier:
  First Glimpse: can drive entry external
  Minor Accomplishment: can drive advanced external
  Complete Mastery: can drive superior external
  Perfecting the Craft+: can drive absolute art
```

**Internal art's Qi supply mode**:

The internal art is both the Qi "resource pool" and the "water pump" — it decides both how big the pool is (Qi pool) and how big the faucet is (recovery speed). In combat, when Qi drains fast you must weigh whether to use the big move; after using it you must "regulate breath" (forgo attack to focus on recovery).

This design ensures:
- Combat has tactical depth of resource management
- Internal-art masters (e.g. Qi Sect) have clear advantage in attrition battles
- External-art masters (e.g. Sword Sect) must fight fast and decide fast

#### (3) Lightfoot — The Pinnacle of Agility

Lightfoot affects mobility and survival in combat.

**Lightfoot structure**:

```
Lightfoot Name: Treading Snow Without Trace
Type: Lightfoot
Tier: Entry Art
─────────────────────────────
Effect (varies by proficiency):
  First Glimpse: initiative +2, one extra grid move per battle
  Minor Accomplishment: initiative +3, evade one physical attack (once/battle) Qi cost: 3
  Complete Mastery: initiative +4, movement no longer triggers opportunity attack
  Perfecting the Craft: initiative +5, may evade 2× per battle
  Transcendent: initiative +6, may immediately counter after evasion
─────────────────────────────
Qi cost: base movement free, evade / special movement cost 2–5 Qi
```

Main functions of lightfoot:
- Raise **initiative** (decides action order)
- Provide **evasion** (actively spend Qi to evade attacks)
- Enhance **movement** (change positioning, disengage / close on enemy)
- Some advanced lightfoot give tactical mobility (vault obstacles, walk on water, etc.)

#### (4) Special Martial Art — Strange Arts

Covers martial types not belonging to external / internal / lightfoot.

| Subtype | Representative Arts | Qi Cost Mode | Note |
|--------|---------|-------------|------|
| **Poison Art** | Five Poisons Scripture, Hundred-Poison True Scripture | 1–3 Qi per application | Apply Poisoned state to enemy, sustained HP loss / debuff |
| **Hidden Weapon** | Rain of Flowers, Storm Pear-Blossom Needles | 0–2 Qi per throw | Ranged physical attack, may attach poison / effect |
| **Medical Martial** | Medicine King Needle Art, Nine Needles to Cross Calamity | 1–4 Qi per treatment | Has both attack and healing functions |
| **Music Martial** | Blue Sea Tide Song, Heavenly Demon Zither | 2–5 Qi per use | Area effect, affects multiple targets (control / damage) |
| **Formation** | Zhenwu Seven-Section Formation, Dog-Beating Grand Formation | 1/round to maintain | Multi-person combined strike / defense bonus |

### 1.3 Qi Consumption Model Overview

```
┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                     Qi Flow Model                     │
│                                                      │
│  ┌─────────┐    consume    ┌──────────────────┐       │
│  │  Qi Pool │──────────→   │  External moves   │       │
│  │ (internal)│             │  (attack/defense) │       │
│  │          │──────────→   │  Lightfoot        │       │
│  │    ↕     │             │  (move/evade)     │       │
```
│  │  Recovery  │──────────→│  Special martial    │        │
│  │  (inner)   │           │  (poison/hidden/heal)│       │
│  └─────────┘           └──────────────────┘        │
│                                                      │
│  Qi Pool = Inner Foundation + Willpower bonus        │

└──────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

#### Self-Created Martial Art

The Self-Created Martial Art is the ultimate form of martial cultivation — a technique a character devises based on their own practice and understanding.

**Creation conditions**:
- At least **three martial arts** cultivated to **Perfecting the Craft** or above
- Related attribute ≥ 8
- Inner-art cultivation at **Perfecting the Craft**
- One significant **epiphany scene** (a plot climax arranged by the GM)

**Creation rules**:
- A self-created art's tier is tied to the character's cultivation (cannot exceed the character's current highest martial-art tier)
- A self-created art's special effects are fused from martial arts the character already masters
- Designed jointly by GM and player, ensuring it fits the character's cultivation history

### 3.1.5 Apprenticeship System (full rules)

The most orthodox way to learn martial arts in the Wulin is not picking up manuals — it is taking a master. The complete apprenticeship mechanics follow.

#### I. Finding a Master

| Path | Check | Time | Result |
|------|-------|------|--------|
| Internal sect application | No check (sect reputation ≥ 10) | 0 (comes with sect) | May take a sect elder as master; teaching cap up to "Superior" |
| Jianghu search (ask around) | Jianghu Lore DC 15 | 1 session | Learn a hermit/master's secluded location |
| Introduced by someone | Charisma DC 12 (to ask for introduction) | 0–1 session | Gain an audience card / recommendation letter; apprenticeship DC −3 |
| Chance encounter | GM judgment | Opportunity | Accidentally meet a hermit/master |

#### II. The Master's Trial

Finding a master is only the first step — a master has conditions for taking disciples.

| Master Type | Typical NPC | Disciple Conditions | Apprenticeship DC (Charisma/Willpower, higher used) |
|-------------|-------------|---------------------|------------------------------------------------------|
| Sect elder | Sword Sect elder, Vajra Sect chief | Sect reputation ≥ 20, and complete one sect trial task | DC 12 |
| Jianghu independent | Chu Huaisha, Jianghu Old Eccentric | Help him with a personal matter | DC 14 |
| Righteous grandmaster | Lu Chenyuan, Huijue, Ziyang | Heroism ≥ 30, and pass the "Heart-Nature Trial" | DC 18 |
| Evil-path expert | Li Canyang, Ye Xiuluo | Infamy ≥ 15, and pass the "Loyalty Trial" | DC 16 |
| Hidden grandmaster | Hidden Grandmaster (14_#20) | No fixed conditions — the grandmaster takes disciples by personal preference | DC 22 (one chance only) |
| Court expert | Han Tieyi, Cao Shaoqin | Court Sect background or court recommendation | DC 14 / DC 20 (Cao — he never truly takes disciples) |

> **Heart-Nature Trial example**: Huijue may have the PC seclude themselves in Wanfo Temple for three days, no food, no speech — each midnight they must pass a Willpower DC 15, all three passes to take him as master. Lu Chenyuan may have the PC spar with him using a wooden sword — he uses no inner strength, judging only the PC's sword intent.

#### III. What a Master Can Teach You

| Master Realm | Max Martial Tier Teachable | Max Quantity Teachable | Other |
|--------------|----------------------------|------------------------|-------|
| Second Realm | Initiate | 2 moves | Can guide skills (skill DC −2) |
| Third Realm (Duan Tianya level) | Advanced | 3 moves | Can impart exclusive insight (one art's proficiency DC −3) |
| Fourth Realm (Gongshu Xuan level) | Superior | 4 moves | One guidance session per month (proficiency DC −5) |
| Fifth Realm (Lu Chenyuan level) | Superior + Self-Create entry | Unlimited | Can help the PC self-create an art |
| Sixth Realm (Huijue level) | Supreme | Unlimited | After transmitting the art can accelerate proficiency — training time halved |

**Special cases**:
- The same martial art cannot be taught by two different masters
- A master-taught art counts as "sect teaching" — training time 1–3 sessions (not 2–6 like manual study)
- If the master's and your inner-art attributes conflict (e.g. Pure Yang mental method vs the master's cold/ice martial arts), learning DC +4

#### IV. Master-Disciple Relationship and Its Price

| Obligation | Notes |
|------------|-------|
| Periodic visits | At least return to the sect once every 5 sessions, or master favor −5 (cumulative −15 means no more active guidance) |
| Sect tasks | Master may assign tasks anytime — may refuse but favor −3 |
| Fight in master's stead | When someone challenges the master, "answer in the master's stead" — win gives Heroism/reputation +2; loss shames the master |
| Art not to be passed on | Without permission, may not teach the master's art to others — violators lose 20 master favor (placed on the "sect purge" list) |
| Master-disciple falling out | See the Sect Defection rules — a master-disciple falling out is a form of defection |

#### V. Graduating (Leaving the Master)

When your realm surpasses the master, or you have mastered all the master's taught arts and at least one to "Transcendent", you may choose to "graduate":

- **Glorify the sect**: publicly thank the master, Heroism +5, master favor +5, sect reputation +10
- **Leave quietly**: say nothing, master favor −5 ("not even a word of thanks?")
- **Betray the master**: go through the defection process — betraying a master is worse than betraying a sect: the master may personally hunt you down

### 3.2 Training Progress (Five-Realm Proficiency)

How far each martial art is cultivated is measured by these five realms:

| Realm | Level | Unlocked Content | Proficiency Condition | Typical Sessions Needed |
|-------|-------|------------------|-----------------------|--------------------------|
| **First Glimpse** | Lv.1 | Unlock 1 basic move | Achieved upon learning | Achieved at learning |
| **Minor Accomplishment** | Lv.2 | Unlock 2nd move, attribute bonus +1 | Successfully use the art to attack/defend 10 times | 1–3 sessions |
| **Complete Mastery** | Lv.3 | Unlock 3rd move, move Qi cost −1 | Minor Accomplishment + 2 sessions training + Comprehension check | 3–5 sessions |
| **Perfecting the Craft** | Lv.4 | Unlock 4th move (if any), can use ultimate | Complete Mastery + 3 sessions training + Comprehension check (high DC) | 7–10 sessions |
| **Transcendent** | Lv.5 | Unlock all moves, can teach others | Plot-triggered (this art must play a decisive role in a key plot) | Depends on plot |

**Proficiency condition details**:

1. **Use count**: number of times the art is **successfully used** in combat. Models "practice makes perfect".
   - "Successful use" defined as: attack hits, block succeeds, evasion takes effect, etc.
   - Different arts count differently — e.g. Lightfoot counts "successful evasion", inner arts count "Qi-recovery trigger count"

2. **Training time**: secluded cultivation / leaving the mountain, measured in "sessions".
   - Sect teaching halves training time
   - Comprehension ≥ 7 reduces by 1 session

3. **Comprehension check**:
   - DC = 10 + (martial tier × 3) + (target realm × 2)
     - e.g. Advanced art from Complete Mastery → Perfecting the Craft: DC = 10 + 6 + 8 = 24
   - Failure does not regress, but wastes training time and requires a retry
   - Each failure, next DC −2 (accumulated experience)

4. **Plot condition** (Transcendent only):
   - Must have a **signature battle** with this art — defeat a strong foe with it, or play an irreplaceable role in a key scene
   - This ensures the highest realm isn't reachable by "grinding numbers" — it must come with great plot

### 3.3 Training Synergy

Different martial arts may have **synergy effects** in cultivation:

| Synergy Type | Effect | Example |
|--------------|--------|---------|
| **Same source** | Same-sect martial arts cultivate 20% faster | Huashan Sword Art → Dugu Nine Swords |
| **Mutually generating** | When inner and external arts are compatible, external power +2 | Pure Yang mental method + Taiji Fist |
| **Mutually restraining** | Conflicting arts cannot be cultivated together (or coexist only with great difficulty) | Pure Yang inner art + Frost Palm |
| **Complementary** | Different-type arts fill tactical gaps | Sword Art (melee) + Hidden Weapon (ranged) + Lightfoot (mobility) |

### 3.4 Forgetting and Replacing Martial Arts

- **Forgetting**: a character may choose to "seal" a martial art (no longer use it), freeing a "martial slot"
- **Martial slot cap**: the number of arts a character can **actively use** at once is limited by Comprehension:
  - Comprehension 1–3: at most 4 martial arts
  - Comprehension 4–6: at most 6 martial arts
  - Comprehension 7–9: at most 8 martial arts
  - Comprehension 10+: at most 10 martial arts
- **Note**: a sealed art can be reactivated when needed, but proficiency may drop from long disuse

## 4.5 Meridian and Acupoint System

Inside everyone's body run 12 Regular Meridians and 8 Extraordinary Meridians, forming the network through which Qi flows. Opening the meridians is the core of inner-art cultivation — not only a prerequisite for realm breakthroughs, but a direct path to greater combat power.

### Meridian System

| Category | Count | Description |
|----------|-------|-------------|
| **Twelve Regular Meridians** | 12 | The main highways of Qi flow. Opening each raises Qi cap +2 |
| **Ren and Du Meridians** | 2 | Heads of the 12 regular meridians. Conception Vessel (front midline): Qi recovery +1/round; Governing Vessel (back midline): Willpower +1 (Qi-related) |
| **Eight Extraordinary Meridians** | 8 | Auxiliary meridians. Opening them grants special abilities (one effect unlocked per 2 opened) |

### Conditions for Opening Meridians

| Meridian Type | Required Realm | Experience (XP) Cost | Difficulty |
|---------------|----------------|----------------------|------------|
| One Regular Meridian | From First Realm | 5 XP each | Willpower + Constitution check DC 12 |
| Conception or Governing Vessel | From Third Realm | 20 XP each | Willpower + Constitution check DC 18 |
| One Extraordinary Meridian | From Fourth Realm | 30 XP each | Willpower + Constitution check DC 22 |

> Opening meridians is an **between-sessions action**, taking 1 day each. Failure does not lose XP but can be retried (another day spent).

### Extraordinary-Meridian Unlock Effects

| Extraordinary Meridians Opened | Unlock Effect |
|-------------------------------|---------------|
| 2 | Choose one attribute +1 (within realm cap) |
| 4 | Inner Strength Infusion uses +1 / battle |
| 6 | Qi cost −1 (minimum 1) |
| 8 (all open) | "Unity of Heaven and Man": Qi cap ×1.5; intimidate same-realm and below foes (Willpower check DC = opponent's realm × 3) |

### Acupoint Striking

As a combat application of meridians, a character may attempt acupoint striking (requires a related martial art or skill):

- **Strike Acupoint** (main action in combat, requires a hit): Strength or Agility check DC 15 + target's Willpower. On success the target is Sealed Meridian for 1d3 rounds
- **Release Acupoint** (minor action): an ally's Willpower check DC 12 to release the target, or the target forces it open themselves (Willpower check DC 15, once per round)
- **Sealed-Meridian duration**: target cannot use Qi-consuming martial arts; each round auto-attempts to force open (Willpower DC 15)
- **Learning acupoint striking**: must take a master or chance adventure to learn "Acupoint-Striking Art" (second-tier special martial), or upon reaching Fourth Realm or above may force-strike using base attributes (DC +5)

## V. Design Rationale and Trade-offs

### 5.1 Design Rationale

1. **Why "four-category" (external / inner / lightfoot / special) instead of finer classification?**
   The Jin Yong system often divides into a dozen categories — fist, sword, saber, staff, hidden weapon, poison, inner, lightfoot, etc. The reason for simplifying to four:
   - Fist/sword/saber/staff are essentially all "attack modes" — better to unify as external and distinguish via the specific art's description than to run a dozen parallel systems
   - Four categories are enough to produce meaningful Build differences (you've already chosen which external type to main)
   - Reduces rule redundancy; the GM need not design separate check rules for each category
   - The special category serves as a "catch-all" for non-standard combat like poison craft, hidden weapons, medical-martial arts

2. **Why is the martial-slot count limited by Comprehension?**
   This comes from the internal logic of the wuxia universe — those without enough Comprehension "bite off more than they can chew." Guo Jing had low Comprehension early yet mastered the Eighteen Dragon-Subduing Palms through devotion; Yang Guo had high Comprehension so could fuse many arts to self-create the Gloom-Separation Palm. Limiting martial slots by Comprehension both fits wuxia logic and creates a Build trade-off — high-Comprehension characters can collect more arts but may invest less in any single attribute.

3. **Why must Transcendent be plot-triggered?**
   If every realm could be reached by "grinding numbers," martial proficiency becomes a number game. Binding the highest realm to plot ensures:
   - The peak of character growth occurs at the most narratively meaningful moment
   - The GM can control the pacing of martial pinnacle
   - Players are motivated to pursue great plot, not just grind combat

4. **Why "per-move Qi cost" rather than "fixed action cost"?**
   Per-move cost provides tactical resource management — "use the ultimate this round? Is there enough Qi for the fights ahead? Settle breath or not?" These are meaningful tactical decisions. Fixed action cost degrades into a pure action-economy of "I can only do one thing per round," losing the wuxia-unique sense of "exhaustion."

### 5.2 Design Trade-offs

**Trade-off 1: numerical vs narrative martial arts**
The current martial system leans numerical (move lists, Qi cost, attribute thresholds…). A more narrative direction was considered (art descriptions primary, effects adjudicated by GM), but numerical was chosen because:
- The requirements analysis explicitly asked for "tactical choices" and "detailed Build space" — tactics without numbers are hollow
- Numerical reduces the GM's burden of free adjudication (less dispute over "how strong is this move")
- The Core Mechanics designer can later tune numerical balance

**Cost**: some of the martial arts' "poetry" is reduced. It is recommended to keep an artistic conception description for each art in the final rulebook (already shown in examples).

**Trade-off 2: random learning (adventure) vs deterministic learning (sect / purchase)**
Martial acquisition includes the random "adventure" path. Over-relying on adventure makes players feel unfair, but removing it entirely loses the "contingency of the Jianghu." Current approach:
- Initiate ~ Advanced arts are mainly deterministic (sect teaching, manual purchase), keeping the Build foundation controllable
- Superior-and-above arts keep the adventure path, but also offer deterministic alternatives like "sect-contribution exchange" and "high-price commission"
- Adventure does not decide "whether you can grow strong," but brings "the surprise of growing strong"

**Trade-off 3: martial co-cultivation conflict vs free combination**
Should conflicts like "Pure Yang inner art + Frost Palm" be kept? Reasons to keep:
- It creates meaningful trade-offs. Not all arts are compatible, forcing players to choose
- It adds Build personalization and memorable hooks
- It is part of wuxia culture ("yin and yang clash," "righteous and evil cannot coexist")

But note: conflict rules should not be too strict, or they stifle creative Builds. The current rule only forbids "obviously conflicting" combinations, judged by GM and common sense.

## 4.2 Initiative and First Strike

### 4.2.1 Initiative Check

At battle start, all combatants make an **Initiative Check**:

> **Initiative = 2d10 + Agility modifier + Comprehension modifier + Lightfoot skill level**

**Design rationale**: Agility represents reaction speed, Comprehension represents intuition about danger and battlefield insight, Lightfoot represents positional seizure in real combat. Combined, they reflect both physique (attributes) and martial training (skills), more reasonable than a single attribute. The 2d10 bell curve makes Initiative more stable, reducing "luck crushing skill."

**`[Interface]`** ✅ Attribute modifier calculation confirmed as "attribute value − 4"; Lightfoot skill defined by Ke Jiaoying in the character system.

### 4.2.2 Turn Order

- Act in descending Initiative order
- On tie, higher Agility goes first; if Agility also tied, negotiated by combatants or adjudicated by GM
- Once turn order is set, **it stays fixed for the entire battle** (fixed-initiative system, reduces tracking complexity)

### 4.2.3 Ambush and Sneak Attack

| Situation | Rule |
|-----------|------|
| **Surprise Round** | The ambushing side gets one **Surprise Round** before formal combat begins, able to take only one primary action (cannot do the full move + primary + minor combo). The ambushed side cannot act in the surprise round and their Defense (DC) is treated as **−4**. |
| **Initiative Advantage** | The ambushing side gains **+4 advantage** on the Initiative check (if entering formal turn order). |
| **Awareness Contest** | Whether the ambush succeeds is an **Stealth / Hide check** by the ambusher vs the ambushed side's **Comprehension check**. `[Interface]` ✅ Contest-check rules defined by Core Mechanics. |
| **Ambush** | A side that pre-set an ambush may take a **full round** (move + primary + minor) in the surprise round, not just one primary action. |

**Design rationale**: In wuxia, "striking first to gain advantage" is extremely important. The surprise round gives the first-mover a real edge but not an instant kill; the ambushed side loses a round but isn't helpless (acts normally in formal rounds). An ambush is stronger than an impromptu sneak attack, reflecting "the prepared mind defeating the unprepared."

## 4.3 Turn-Based Action System

### 4.3.1 Action Economy

Each character in their turn may perform:

| Action Type | Per Round | Description |
|-------------|-----------|-------------|
| **Move** | 1 | Change stance / distance |
| **Primary Action** | 1 | Core actions like attack, channeling Qi, using items |
| **Minor Action** | 1 | Auxiliary actions like observe, short adjust, switch weapon, shout |
| **Reaction** | see below | Triggered in others' turns; 1 usable per full round (all combatants' turns) |

### 4.3.2 Full Action List

#### Primary Actions

| Action | Cost | Brief |
|--------|------|-------|
| **Attack** | Primary | Perform a martial move to attack a target; see §4.4 |
| **Full Attack** | Primary + Minor | Forgo minor action; attack gains +2 hit; or attack twice, each at −4 hit |
| **Channel Qi** | Primary | Actively settle breath to recover Qi, see §4.5.3; or activate a sustained inner-art effect (protective aura, etc.) |
| **Use Item** | Primary | Take pills, use hidden weapons, throw items, etc. |
| **Observe / Read Move** | Primary | Make a Comprehension check on the enemy to read their move pattern, gaining tactical advantage (see §4.4.5 break-move mechanic) |
| **Aid** | Primary | Grant an adjacent ally +2 Defense or +hit on their next attack |
| **Retreat** | Move + Primary | Leave combat, make a Lightfoot contest check. Success disengages; failure stays in place and cannot move this round |
| **Special Martial** | Primary | Perform a special martial effect requiring a "primary action" |
| **Combo** | Primary + Minor | Perform two different martial moves in sequence (must share Swift/Subtle force type); auto-links after first move hits. Each move's Qi cost +1, second move hit −2. Successful combo may trigger extra effect (GM adjudicates by move combo) |

> **Weapon and move damage**: when performing a martial move, damage is set by the move itself (e.g. "1d8+STR"), already including the weapon's contribution. A normal attack (no martial performed) uses the weapon's base damage (see equipment table). The same weapon may deal different damage in different moves — because the move's essence lies in how force is delivered, not the weapon itself.

**Combo resolution rules**:
- The two moves must be force-related (Swift→Swift, Subtle→Swift, Gentle→Firm, etc., reasonable pairings, GM adjudicates)
- Only links after the first move hits (if the first whiffs, the action is wasted)
- Characters with Strength + Agility ≥ 10 reduce the second-move hit penalty to −1 (i.e. only −1)
- Characters with Comprehension + Willpower ≥ 10 halve the extra Qi cost of the combo (each move only +0.5, rounded)

#### Minor Actions

| Action | Cost | Brief |
|--------|------|-------|
| **Observe Battlefield** | Minor | Make a Comprehension check (DC 12); success reveals an enemy's approximate remaining Qi or HP state |
| **Short-distance Adjust** | Minor | Fine-tune stance (adjust relative position within the same distance band, e.g. from "front" to "flank") |
| **Switch Weapon** | Minor | Change held weapon (draw / sheathe / swap) |
| **Shout / Taunt** | Minor | Attempt to affect enemy morale or attention; target makes a Willpower contest; `[Interface]` ✅ Willpower used for mental resistance |
| **Retrieve Item** | Minor | Take an item (pill, hidden weapon, etc.) from pack; usable next round |
| **Bandage / First Aid** | Minor | Bandage self or adjacent ally: Medicine check DC 10, success recovers 1d6 + Medicine level HP (external injury only); consumes 1 Jin Chuang Medicine. One bandage per wound per battle |
| **Use Pill** | Minor | Consume a pill already retrieved / at hand (must be prepped via Retrieve Item / inventory slot) |

#### Reactions

| Reaction | Trigger | Effect |
|----------|---------|--------|
| **Parry / Block** | When attacked | Spend Qi for active defense, see §4.4.4 |
| **Evade** | When attacked | Spend Qi for Lightfoot evasion, replacing parry with an Agility contest |
| **Cover** | When adjacent ally attacked | Spend reaction to take the attack in the ally's stead |
| **Riposte** | After successful parry | Spend extra Qi to immediately counterattack on a successful parry (requires relevant martial) |

Each **full round** (all combatants have acted once) resets usable reactions to 1. Special martial arts or realms may raise the reaction cap.

**`[Interface]`** ✅ Unified to 1 reaction per round; realm / Agility may raise the cap.

## 4.4 Martial Move System (combat core)

### 4.4.1 Move Hit

When the attacker performs a martial move, make a **Hit Check**:

> **Hit Value = 2d10 + move hit bonus + Strength modifier (melee / blade) / Agility modifier (ranged / hidden) + proficiency bonus**

Against the target's **Defense (DC)**:

> **Defense (DC) = 11 + Agility modifier + armor bonus + Protective-Qi bonus (if active)**

| Hit Result | Effect |
|------------|--------|
| Hit Value < Defense (DC) | Move whiffs, or defender easily avoids / blocks |
| Hit Value ≥ Defense (DC) | Move hits, proceed to damage |
| Hit Value ≥ Defense (DC) + 8 | **Critical Strike**: extra +1 damage die or +50% damage (round down) |
| Hit Value ≥ Defense (DC) + 12 | **Perfect Strike**: maximize damage or trigger the move's special effect |
| Natural 18–20 (6%) | Always hits; if it would have hit, counts as Perfect Strike; if it would have missed, still a normal hit |
| Natural 2–4 (3%) | Always misses, and may expose an opening (GM adjudicates) |
| Natural double-10 (1%) | Always hits and counts as Perfect Strike, extra triggers the move's hidden effect (if any) |
| Natural double-1 (1%) | Always misses and exposes a major opening; next round Defense (DC) −4 |

**2d10 probability reference** (when attack/defense attributes comparable):

| Hit Level | Required Roll | Probability | Description |
|-----------|---------------|-------------|-------------|
| Normal hit | 2d10 ≥ 11 | **55%** | Baseline hit rate when attributes comparable |
| Critical Strike | 2d10 ≥ 19 | **≈ 5%** | DC +8, needs a high roll |
| Perfect Strike | 2d10 ≥ 23 | **≈ 1%** | DC +12, almost needs natural crit or clear attribute advantage |
| Crit Success band | 2d10 = 18–20 | **6%** | includes natural double-10 (1%) |
| Crit Failure band | 2d10 = 2–4 | **3%** | includes natural double-1 (1%) |

**Design rationale**: the 2d10 bell curve makes hit checks more stable — character attributes matter more than luck, fitting the wuxia logic that "the skilled naturally hit." Critical Strike (DC +8, ≈5%) and Perfect Strike (DC +12, ≈1%) total about 6%, keeping rarity without frequent triggers. The natural crit band (6%) ensures occasional dramatic amazing hits.

> **`[Interface]`** ✅ Critical/Perfect rules (DC +8 / DC +12) recalibrated against Core Mechanics' 2d10 distribution. Natural 18–20 crit-success and natural 2–4 crit-failure rules are consistent with this.

### 4.4.2 Move Power

After a hit, damage is calculated as:

> **Damage = move base damage + Strength modifier (melee / blade) + Qi Infusion bonus + Critical/Perfect bonus − target armor reduction**

#### Move Base Damage

Set by the martial move itself; examples (actual data defined by Ke Jiaoying's Martial Arts System):

| Move Type | Base Damage Range | Example |
|-----------|-------------------|---------|
| Light Swift move | 1d6 ~ 2d6 | Spirit Snake Leaves Den, Parting Flower Brushes Willow |
| Firm Heavy move | 2d6 ~ 3d6 | Mountain-Cleaving Palm, Vajra Subdues Demon |
| Gentle penetrating move | 1d8 ~ 2d8 | Soft Palm, Taiji Cloud Hands |
| Weapon move | weapon damage + move bonus | sword moves, saber arts, spearplay |
| Hidden / ranged | 1d4 ~ 1d8 | throwing dart, sleeve arrow, Finger-Snap Divine Power |

> **`[Character Interface]`** Martial move base damage is fully defined by Ke Jiaoying's `03_Martial Arts System.md`. This section provides the mechanical framework; specific numbers follow the Martial Arts System.

#### Qi Infusion Bonus

When attacking, you may choose to spend Qi (called **Qi Infusion**) to amplify the move's power:

| Qi Cost | Damage Bonus | Description |
|---------|--------------|-------------|
| 1 point | +1d4 | Light infusion |
| 3 points | +2d4 | Medium infusion |
| 5 points | +3d4 | All-out gamble |

> **`[Interface]`** ✅ Qi cost calibrated. Based on the Qi pool formula (Constitution × inner coefficient + realm base), a mid-game character's pool is ~20–35; 1/3/5 infusion costs are reasonable.

**Design rationale**: Qi infusion is classic wuxia combat — "this palm used seven parts of my power." As a consumable resource, the player decides how much to invest each attack. "All-out gamble" is high-risk (big Qi cost) but may turn the battle.

### 4.4.3 Move Countering

This is the core of tactical choice. Martial moves divide by **force type** into six base styles, forming a counter relationship:

```
         Swift move (speed)
       ↗              ↘
Long-range (far) ← →  Heavy move (force)
   ↕                    ↕
Gentle force (soft) ← → Firm force (hard)
       ↘              ↗
         Acupoint / Grapple (subtle)
```

#### Counter Relationship Table

| Attacker Style | Counters Target Style | Effect |
|----------------|-----------------------|--------|
| **Swift beats Heavy** | Swift → Heavy | When Swift used on Heavy, target Defense (DC) **−3** (Heavy can't react in time) |
| **Gentle beats Firm** | Gentle → Firm | When Gentle used on Firm, **ignore 50% of target armor reduction** (soft overcomes hard, force penetrates) |
| **Long-range beats Melee** | Ranged/hidden → all non-ranged | If the ranged attacker and target are not in the same distance band, attack gains **+2 hit** (opponent can't reach) |
| **Subtle beats Swift** | Acupoint/Grapple → Swift | When Acupoint/Grapple used on Swift, on hit additionally applies **slow / seal-move** effect |

**Design rationale**: the essence of wuxia combat is "read the move and break it, adapt to the foe." Counter relationships make move choice a meaningful tactical decision — not picking the highest-damage move every round, but considering the opponent's style to pick a countering move. Four counter relations form a layered but not complex strategic space.

> **`[Character Interface]`** Each martial move must be tagged with its force type (Swift / Heavy / Firm / Gentle / Long-range / Subtle), defined by Ke Jiaoying in `03_Martial Arts System.md`.

#### Change Move

In combat, a character may **Change Move** at the start of their turn — switch the martial routine in use. Changing move costs no action, but you may not switch back and forth within the same round.

**Design rationale**: wuxia fights often describe "the sword art shifts, then the palm art emerges." The Change-Move mechanic lets players dynamically adjust strategy by the battle state, truly achieving "variation to defeat the enemy."

### 4.4.4 Parry and Block

When attacked, the defender may spend a reaction and Qi for **active defense**:

#### Parry (break move with move)

> Spend 1 Qi, defender makes a **Parry Check**:
>
> **Parry Value = 2d10 + current move's parry bonus + Agility modifier + proficiency bonus**
>
> - If Parry Value ≥ attacker's Hit Value: **Parry succeeds**, no damage taken
> - If Parry Value ≥ attacker's Hit Value + 8: **Perfect Parry**, no damage and gain a tactical advantage (next round +2 hit on the parried foe, or recover 1 Qi)

#### Block (weapon block)

> Spend 1 Qi, use weapon or vambrace to block:
>
> - Gain **+4 temporary Defense (DC)** against that attack
> - If blocking with the matching weapon (sword vs sword, saber vs saber), extra +1
> - Block **always succeeds** (no roll), but cannot gain Perfect Parry bonus

**Active defense vs no defense**:

| Choice | Effect | Strategic meaning |
|--------|--------|-------------------|
| Spend no Qi / reaction | Rely only on static Defense (DC) | Save resources, but easily hit by masters |
| Block | Always +4 DC, costs 1 Qi + reaction | Safe defense, can't get worse |
| Parry | Roll contest, costs 1 Qi + reaction | High risk high reward — may Perfect Parry and counter |

**Design rationale**: active defense is the quintessence of wuxia combat. Before every attack the defender has a decision — hoard Qi and tough it out? Safe block? Or risk a parry for a reversal? The three choices map to different risk preferences and resource states. This ensures that even on the opponent's turn, the player is "playing the game" rather than "waiting for the result."

### 4.4.5 Break-Move Mechanic

**Read Move**: use the **Observe / Read Move** primary action, make a Comprehension check vs the target's "move concealment DC":

> **Move Concealment DC = 10 + target move proficiency modifier + Comprehension modifier**
>
> Success: within the next round, moves used on the target gain the **"Read"** status

**Break Move**: when using a move that counters the target's force type against a **"Read"** target:

> - **Automatically gain Perfect Strike effect** (no need for Hit Check DC +12)
> - Extra **+2d4 Qi-impact damage** (ignores armor)
> - After break-move, remove the **"Read"** status

The **"Read"** status lasts until a break-move occurs or the battle ends, or is auto-removed after the target **Changes Move** to a different martial routine.

**Design rationale**: Read → Break is a classic wuxia scene — "I've seen through your pattern!" then one move ends the foe. This mechanic gives a strong payoff to non-attacking rounds (spending a primary action to observe), and gives Change Move a defensive read-counter function. The whole counter chain (move countering → read → break) forms a complete wuxia tactical loop.

## 4.5 Qi Use in Combat

### 4.5.1 Qi Cost Overview

| Use | Cost | Timing | Effect |
|-----|------|--------|--------|
| Qi Infusion | 1/3/5 | On attack | Damage +1d4 / +2d4 / +3d4 |
| Parry | 1 | When attacked (reaction) | Active-defense roll |
| Block | 1 | When attacked (reaction) | Defense (DC) +4 |
| Evade | 2 | When attacked (reaction) | Replace Defense (DC) with Lightfoot check |
| Protective Aura | 3/round | Activate via Channel Qi primary action | Defense (DC) +2, lasts 1 + Constitution modifier rounds |
| Lightfoot Dash | 2 | On move | Increase move distance by one distance band |
| Lightfoot Levitate | 3 | On move | Gain "high ground" advantage for one round |
| Activate Supreme Art | per move | When using specific move | Spend extra Qi stated by the move, gain extra effect |

**Evade rule**: spend 2 Qi and a reaction, replace static Defense (DC) with the Lightfoot check result:
> **Evade Value = 2d10 + Agility modifier + Lightfoot skill level**

Evade cannot stack with Parry / Block (one attack allows only one active defense).

> **`[Interface]`** ✅ Qi cost values aligned and calibrated on the new Qi pool formula (Constitution × inner coefficient + realm base value).

### 4.5.2 Qi Depletion (Exhaustion)

When Qi drops to 0 or below:

| Exhaustion Effect | Description |
|-------------------|-------------|
| **Cannot spend Qi** | All Qi-costing actions unavailable (infusion, parry, block, evade, protective aura, lightfoot dash, etc.) |
| **Defense (DC) −4** | Qi dried up, body sluggish |
| **Damage −2** | All move damage halved (round down), no force to drive inner power |
| **Move halved** | Can only move one distance band (no dash) |
| **Extra damage per round** | While exhausted, at end of each round take 1 Internal Injury (Qi-disorder backlash) |

> **`[Interface]`** ✅ Qi may drop to 0 or below (negative Qi represents Qi-backlash damage; each −1 Qi causes +1 Internal Injury per round).

### 4.5.3 Recovering Qi in Combat

| Method | Cost | Recovery | Condition |
|--------|------|----------|-----------|
| **Settle Breath** | Whole round (cannot move / attack) | Constitution modifier + 1d4 | Not in close grappling, no enemy adjacent |
| **Quick Settle** | Primary action | 1d4 | Usable in close grappling, but cannot attack simultaneously |
| **Take Pill** | Primary action + consume item | See pill notes | Many pills have cooldown (e.g. 1 per battle) |
| **Realm natural recovery** | None | +1 per 3 rounds | Passive effect, ✅ realm recovery rule confirmed |

**Design rationale**: in-combat Qi recovery is not infinite — settling breath requires strategically leaving combat, pills are scarce. This ensures Qi spending is a meaningful long-term decision, not "chug a blue potion every round."

## 4.6 Damage and Status

### 4.6.1 Damage Types

| Type | Source | Feature |
|------|--------|---------|
| **External Injury** | Direct strikes of blade/fist/foot | Most common damage; armor can reduce. Accumulating a certain amount triggers Bleeding. |
| **Internal Injury** | Qi attacks, inner-art backlash, break-move Qi impact | Armor cannot reduce. Accumulation affects Qi recovery and channeling. |
| **Poison** | Poison arts, toxins, venomous insects | Sustained damage, auto-triggers each round. Some poisons corrode Qi. |
| **Acupoint / Sealed Meridian** | Acupoint strikes, grappling | No direct HP damage, but limits action and Qi flow. |

### 4.6.2 HP and Damage Calculation

A character has two vitality indicators:

| Indicator | Meaning | Calculation |
|-----------|---------|-------------|
| **Vitality (HP)** | Capacity to absorb bodily damage | **Constitution × 5 + realm bonus** (15 → 65, defined by Core Mechanics) |
| **Heavy-Injury Threshold** | Single damage over this causes severe injury | Constitution value (e.g. Constitution 14 → single hit of 14+ triggers severe injury) |

#### Final Damage Formula

> **Final Damage = (move base damage + attribute modifier + Qi infusion − armor external reduction) + Qi-impact / internal-injury portion (not reduced by armor)**

**Example**: Mountain-Cleaving Palm (base 2d6+3) + Strength modifier +2 + infusion 3 points (+2d4) − armor reduction 3 = final damage
- External portion: (2d6+3 + 2 + 2d4 − 3) → armor reduces the non-Qi portion
- If 1 of the Palm's 3 points is Qi damage, that 1 point is not reduced by armor

> **`[Character Interface]`** The ratio of external / internal / Qi damage in a move's damage is defined by move data.

### 4.6.3 Injury Debuffs

| State | HP Threshold | Effect |
|-------|--------------|--------|
| **Minor Injury** | HP < 75% | No obvious debuff. Surface wound. |
| **Injured** | HP < 50% | All checks **−1**. Performance drops. |
| **Severely Injured** | HP < 25% | All checks **−3**, can only move one distance band, cannot use "Full Attack." |
| **Dying** | HP < 5 or HP ≤ 0 | Each round must make a Constitution check (DC 15); failure means unconscious. Other characters' actions limited (only crawl or weak motions). |

> **`[Interface]`** ✅ Death rule defined by Core Mechanics. HP < 0 and below Constitution's negative value means death.

### 4.6.4 Status Effects Table

| Status | How Applied | Effect | Duration | Removal |
|--------|-------------|--------|----------|---------|
| **Stunned** | Heavy hit to head, specific move | Next round can only take one primary or minor action (not both) | 1 round | Auto-removed |
| **Bleeding** | External accumulation, specific move | Lose 1d4 HP at start of each round | Until bleeding stops | Minor action bandage (DC 12 Constitution check) or any recovery means |
| **Poisoned** | Poison arts, toxin | Lose 1d6 HP at start of each round and all checks −1 | Until cured | Detox pill / force out poison with Qi (DC by toxin level) |
| **Sealed Acupoint** | Acupoint strike | Struck part unusable: arm → cannot attack/block; leg → cannot move/evade | Per move description | Channel Qi to force open (cost 2 Qi + primary action, DC by acupoint strength) or natural time removal |
| **Inner-Breath Disorder** | Qi impact, break-move | Cannot spend Qi to infuse or protect; lose 1 Qi per round | Until regulated | Settle breath one round (DC 12) |
| **Guard-Break** | Repeated heavy hits | Armor / Protective Qi reduction vs later attacks **−3** | Until end of round | Use minor action "recover stance" to remove |
| **Slowed** | Acupoint / subtle force | Can only move one distance band, evade check −4 | 2 rounds | Auto-removed |
| **Blinded** | Special move / environment | All attack hits −4, cannot read moves | Until removed | Per source description |

**Design rationale**: status effects are important seasoning for tactical combat. Pure number-collision is boring precisely because it lacks the battle swings status changes bring. The above statuses cover classic wuxia beats (acupoint strike, poison, internal-injury backlash), yet each rule is concise (within 2 lines), not a lookup burden.

## 4.7 Distance, Stance, Terrain

### 4.7.1 Wuxia Distance Bands

Instead of precise meters / grids, use four **distance bands** to describe spatial relations:

| Distance Band | Description | Wuxia Scene | Attacks Possible |
|---------------|-------------|-------------|------------------|
| **Close** | Fists meet, grappling at close range | Two glued together, breath audible | Fist/palm, grapple, short weapon, acupoint |
| **One-Step** | Within attack range, one step away | Sword can reach, but not glued | Most melee weapons (sword, saber, spear, staff) |
| **Three-Step** | Several steps away, needs movement | Across several people, facing off | Long-weapon advantage distance (spear, staff, long whip) |
| **Far** | A dozen+ steps away, within sight | Standing on eaves or treetops, far apart | Hidden weapons, bow, Qi-release supreme arts |

#### Move Distance

| Move Type | Cost | Distance Change |
|-----------|------|-----------------|
| Normal Move | Move action | Change 1 distance band (e.g. One-Step → Close, or Three-Step → One-Step) |
| Dash | Move + Minor | Change 2 distance bands |
| Lightfoot Dash | Move + 2 Qi | Change 2 distance bands, and gain **+1 hit** on target before next round (leaping-strike might) |
| Disengage | Move + Primary | Change 1 distance band, and make Lightfoot contest to avoid pursuit |

### 4.7.2 Stance

Within the same distance band (especially One-Step and Close), there is stance distinction:

| Stance | Effect |
|--------|--------|
| **Front** | Default stance, normal attack/defense |
| **Flank** | If you successfully enter the flank via move / short-adjust, attack gains **+2 hit** (opponent awkward to block). Entering flank needs an Agility contest check. |
| **Rear** | Attack gains **+4 hit**, and target cannot use Block (only Evade or Parry). Entering rear needs a successful Stealth or Agility contest. |

**Design rationale**: stance gives high-Lightfoot / high-Agility characters "footwork" tactics, not just standing and trading blows. But stance isn't complex — only Front / Flank / Rear, and entering flank/rear needs a contest check, not free.

### 4.7.3 Terrain Interaction

| Terrain Element | Effect |
|-----------------|--------|
| **High-Ground Advantage** | High position vs low target attack hit **+2**. Lightfoot Levitate can gain temporary high-ground advantage. |
| **Narrow Terrain** | Long weapons hit **−3** in narrow terrain (can't deploy). Fist/palm and short weapons unaffected. |
| **Underwater Combat** | All moves count as halved; Heavy moves hit **−4** (water resistance); Gentle moves damage **+1d4** (conducts via water). Non-swimmers make a Constitution check (DC 12) each round or swallow water. |
| **Dark / Night Combat** | Comprehension check DC +5, ranged attack hit **−4**. Characters with night vision / special senses unaffected. |
| **Obstacles** | Provide **half cover** (Defense DC +2) or **full cover** (cannot be directly targeted by directed moves). Can serve as a landing point for Lightfoot displacement. |
| **Destructible Objects** | Wooden tables, bamboo racks, etc. can be destroyed. Destruction needs one attack check (DC 10); on success creates debris terrain (moving within the area needs DC 10 Agility check or fall). |

**Design rationale**: terrain interaction makes the same battle play completely differently across scenes. The GM can use terrain elements to build different tactical layouts. Rules stay concise — each terrain element's core effect stated in one sentence.

## 4.8 Group Combat Rules

### 4.8.1 Mook Rules (fighting many at once)

Wuxia often shows the protagonist outnumbered, sweeping through mooks. To lighten the GM's load, introduce the **Mook** concept.

> **Mook**: an enemy clearly weaker than the PC; individual HP is not tracked. Mooks operate as a **group**.

| Mook Group | Rule |
|------------|------|
| **Combined Action** | All same-type mooks act at the same initiative slot |
| **Combined Attack** | Every 3 mooks provide 1 combined attack (hit +2 per 3 beyond, max +6) |
| **Damage Overflow** | When a single attack's damage exceeds a mook's individual HP, overflow transfers to another mook in the group (one slash fells two!) |
| **Morale** | When the mook group loses half or the leader is defeated, the GM judges morale collapse (mooks flee / surrender) |
| **Simplified Checks** | Mooks don't track Qi (cannot infuse / parry); attack and defense use preset values |

**Example**: 6 mountain bandits surround one PC.
- PC attacks bandit A, damage 12, bandit HP 5 → kill A, overflow 7 transfers to bandit B → kill B, overflow 2 transfers to bandit C → C wounded
- One attack fells two bandits — full wuxia feel!

### 4.8.2 Multi-Attacker Bonus

When multiple characters surround one target:

| Number Attacking | Bonus | Description |
|------------------|-------|-------------|
| 2 attackers | Each attacker hit **+1** | Front-and-back pincer, can't cover all |
| 3+ attackers | Each attacker hit **+2** | Attack from many sides, impossible to defend all |
| The surrounded | Each time hit, next Defense (DC) **−1** (stacks, resets at round end) | Overwhelmed |

> **Cap**: at most 5 attackers can effectively surround one target (more can't fit).

### 4.8.3 Supreme Art Area Attack

Some Supreme / Sect-Guardian arts can damage multiple targets in one primary action:

| Area Type | Range | Damage Handling |
|-----------|-------|-----------------|
| Cone attack (e.g. Sweep Across the Army) | All targets in front within same distance band | Separate hit rolls, damage halved |
| Circular area (e.g. Sword-Qi Crisscross) | Own distance band and adjacent bands | **`[Interface]`** needs Qi-emission rules; a high-martial-stage ability |

> **`[Character Interface]`** Area attacks are tagged by Ke Jiaoying when defining specific arts in `03_Martial Arts System.md`.

**Design rationale**: mook rules free the GM from tracking each mook's state, greatly reducing operation burden. Damage overflow and combined attacks make the PC's one-against-many both smooth and satisfying. The surround bonus has a cap, preventing "numbers = invincible" stat inflation.

## 4.9 Design Trade-offs and Interface Summary

### 4.9.1 Core Design Trade-offs

**Trade-off 1: Active Defense (parry / block) vs pure static Defense (AC)**

| Approach | Pros | Cons |
|----------|------|------|
| Pure static Defense (AC) (D&D-style) | Fast resolution, low GM burden | Defender has no agency, "take-hit rounds" are pure spectating |
| Active Defense (used here) | Defender has decisions, very strong wuxia feel | One extra roll per attack, slightly slower pacing |

**Reason chosen**: one core experience of wuxia combat is "read the move and break it." If the defender can only await results, the fight loses the flavor of "unraveling the opponent's incoming move." The extra roll cost is offset by the rhythm of parry/block actions — it's not a dull die, but a tense moment of "can I catch it?"

**Trade-off 2: Precise distance grid vs abstract distance bands**

| Approach | Pros | Cons |
|----------|------|------|
| Wargame grid (meters / squares) | Precise, unambiguous | Needs a battle map, wuxia mood lost entirely |
| Abstract distance bands (used here) | Fits wuxia narrative, no map needed | Needs GM verbal description, may cause ambiguity |

**Reason chosen**: wuxia is not a wargame. "Kill one man in ten steps" is mood, not 50 feet of movement. Distance bands plus Lightfoot movement give GM and players enough tactical space (four distances + stance system) while keeping the wuxia narrative picture.

**Trade-off 3: Counter depth vs learning cost**

| Approach | Pros | Cons |
|----------|------|------|
| Simple counter (attacker-favored) | Easy to remember | Limited tactical depth |
| Complex counter (six-way cycle + multi-tag per move) | Great depth | Must look up tables each battle, slows pacing |
| Four-way counter + read/break (used here) | Layered but not overloaded | Still has learning cost |

**Reason chosen**: the four counter relations (Swift beats Heavy, Gentle beats Firm, Long-range beats Melee, Subtle beats Swift) basically cover the major wuxia move categories, with manageable memory burden (4 rules). Combined with the advanced read/break play, casual players can use only the four-way counter, while deep players can pursue read/break burst.

## Appendix A: Quick Reference — One-Round Flow

```
1. Round start
   ├── If sustained statuses, resolve status effects first (poison, bleeding, inner-breath disorder, etc.)
   ├── Decide whether to Change Move (switch martial routine)
   └── Decide this round's actions

2. Execute actions
   ├── Move (1): change distance band or stance
   ├── Primary action (1): attack / full attack / channel Qi / use item / observe-read / aid / retreat
   └── Minor action (1): observe battlefield / short-adjust / switch weapon / shout / retrieve item

3. Reaction window
   └── When attacked in others' turns (if reaction available): parry / block / evade / cover / riposte

4. Round end
   └── Resolve round-end effects (if any)
```

# The Jianghu World

> Design: Jing Shiwen (Worldview & Narrative Designer)
> Version: v0.1

---

## Design Rationale

The Jianghu World is the stage where player characters live, grow, and fight. A good wuxia world must satisfy three goals at once: provide enough "anchors" to make players feel they are in a real, believable world; leave enough "blanks" for the GM to freely improvise; and build dynamic tension between factions to drive the story. The design philosophy here is **"a loose but organic ecosystem"** — every faction and every region has its own survival logic and desires, and their interactions constitute the vitality of the Jianghu.

### Design Trade-off

**Trade-off: fictional dynasty vs real history**

This worldview uses a **fictional setting**, not bound to any real dynasty. Reasons:
- A real historical backdrop limits the GM's creative freedom (e.g. "the Tang had no gunpowder, so Tang Sect can't use Thunderclap Bombs")
- But a fully fictional world easily loses the weight of cultural depth
- Hence the **"Song-like but not Song"** strategy: the era's flavor is modeled on the period between Tang and Song (flourishing poetry, thriving commerce, prevalent martial culture), but not bound to specific emperors' reigns or historical events
- The GM may replace it with any real dynasty per preference; all mechanics are independent of concrete history

## I. Era Background

### 1.1 The Year and the Great Situation

**Year**: the Yonghe reign (fictional era name), about 120 years since the founding of the dynasty.

The realm has enjoyed long peace, with civil culture at its peak, but border troubles are rising. It is the best of times — the Jianghu blooms with a hundred flowers, and martial schools emerge endlessly; it is also the worst of times — comfort has corrupted many sects, and the new generation of Jianghu folk both crave achievement and are confused about what true "xia" (chivalry) is.

### 1.2 Court and Jianghu

The Court's attitude toward the Jianghu is **"grace and awe combined, divide and rule"**:

- **Six Doors** is the Court's formal representative in the Jianghu, responsible for arresting great outlaws and suppressing rebel sects. Six Doors masters enjoy Court salaries and practice the "orthodox martial arts" the Court has collected.
- The Court's attitude toward great sects is "win them over first, suppress as backup." Every year it holds the **Martial Exam**, absorbing outstanding Jianghu talent into the Court.
- But toward "destabilizing factors" like the Demon Cult and assassin organizations, the Court takes **decisive suppression**.
- The Jianghu folk's general attitude toward the Court: **keep at a respectful distance**. True xia believe "a martial artist who sells his life to the government loses the backbone of xia."

### 1.3 Current Events — The Four Clouds of the Age

The Jianghu is in troubled times; these four great events intertwine to form the core plot drivers:

| Cloud | Detail |
|-------|--------|
| **Leadership Succession** | Tianjian Alliance Leader Lu Chenyuan, past sixty, has announced he will abdicate in three years. The five sword-sect masters scheme openly and secretly; undercurrents roil within the Alliance. |
| **Demon Cult Unrest** | After a decade of silence, the Blood Moon Cult has recently appeared frequently on the Western Regions trade roads. Rumor says the new Cult Leader has mastered the century-lost "Blood Moon Demon Art." |
| **Legend of the Hidden Treasury** | The Jianghu widely spreads that the map to the former dynasty's martial treasury "Wanxiang Grotto" has been decoded by Qianji Pavilion. All factions are now seeking one who can interpret the map. |
| **Northern Frontier War** | Northern barbarians raid the border frequently; the Court means to conscript Jianghu masters to form the "Yi Wu Army." Whether to go or not splits the sects in fierce opposition. |

## IV. The "Favor Society" of the Jianghu

### 4.1 Visiting Card

In the Jianghu, visiting a famed master first requires sending a "visiting card" — a note bearing your name, lineage, and purpose. The card's quality (paper, calligraphy, wording) partly decides whether the other will see you.

- **Receiving the card**: means the other treats you as someone of standing
- **Returning the card**: the clearest contempt — "you are not worthy to see me"
- **Tearing the card**: if you receive the other's card and tear it in person — this is an act of declaration of war

### 4.2 Tokens and Recommendation Letters

- **Token**: each great faction has its own token; the holder represents that faction's will. Tianjian Alliance's "Alliance Command Token," Wanfo Temple's "Bodhi Beads," Sihai Alliance's "Golden Saber Tablet."
- **Recommendation Letter**: a young unknown in the Jianghu needs an elder's recommendation letter to enter a good sect or join high-level martial events.

### 4.3 Favor-Debt

One core of Chinese wuxia culture — "a favor-debt is the hardest to repay."

- Someone does you a great favor, and you "owe them a favor." When they come with a token, you must repay — or your reputation is ruined.
- **Favor-debts can transfer**: A owes B a favor; B can have A help C. This forms a complex interpersonal web.
- In play, favor-debts can drive side quests and are a GM tool for creating dilemmas.

## V. Creative Guidance for the GM

### 5.1 How to Use This World

This worldview is designed as a **"skeleton," not a "cage"**:

1. **Fill details**: the geography framework gives key landmarks, but you may insert your own towns, strongholds, hermit dwellings.
2. **Create NPCs**: the faction settings give representative figures, but you can absolutely create more — a faction's members cannot be only three or four people.
3. **Develop plot**: the current "Four Clouds of the Age" are catalysts; you may pick one as the campaign's main thread, or all as background dark threads.
4. **Change the world**: the players' actions should change the world. If they defeat the Blood Moon Cult Leader, the Cult should be reorganized; if they help a faction seize power, the Jianghu landscape should shift accordingly.

### 5.2 Faction Strength Reference (for combat-value balance)

| Tier | Martial Reference | Typical Representative |
|------|-------------------|------------------------|
| Peerless | One against a hundred, near the power ceiling | Tianjian Leader Lu Chenyuan, Blood Moon Leader Ye Xiuluo, Wanfo Abbot Huijue |

## I. General Principle: the Jianghu is not a one-way street

### 1.1 Design Philosophy

At character creation the player chose a sect — but this should not be a lifelong cage. A xia's life is full of choices: some find their sect's ways not righteous and resolve to leave; some wish to reach martial pinnacle by combining many schools; some tire of Jianghu strife and want to wash hands and retire to the hills.

This ruleset provides complete mechanical support for these choices. All rules follow three core principles:

1. **High risk, high reward**: defection and mixed cultivation are not "normal play" — they are more dangerous and more exciting than staying in one sect, but give no numerically crushing advantage.
2. **Drive story, not optimal Build**: the rules' purpose is to provide a pivot for character-driven narrative, not a path to the numerically optimal solution.
3. **Consequences have weight**: every choice has real game consequences — NPC attitudes, resource access, and martial advancement speed all change because of it.

## II. Sect Defection Rules

### 2.1 Definition of Sect Defection

**Sect Defection** means a character formally leaves their current sect, severing the master-disciple relationship with it. After defection the character is no longer seen as that sect's disciple, and also loses all resources and support the sect provided.

Defection is an irreversible major decision — even if later reconciled with the old sect, it is extremely hard to return to the pre-defection level of trust.

### 2.2 The Four Paths of Defection

#### Path One: Open and Aboveboard Exit

> "Master above, your disciple Liu is grateful for your grace. But our ways differ and we cannot scheme together; today your disciple resigns from Huashan, and begs Master's indulgence."

**Method**: face the sect master / master and resign in person, stating reasons.

**Process**:
1. The character makes a **Charisma check** (DC by the master's temperament, generally 15–20) to persuade the master to accept the resignation
2. If success, the master accepts; leaves as "graduated" not "traitor"
3. If fail, the master refuses — the character may submit (abandon defection) or force departure (consequence escalates to "sneak away")

**Cost and consequences**:
- Heroism unchanged (open and upright)
- Must return sect tokens (Alliance Command Token / Bodhi Beads, etc.) and sect-lent equipment
- May keep learned sect arts — because left through proper channels, not seen as "stolen learning"
- Sect reputation cleared (no longer that sect's disciple)
- **Former sect attitude**: cool but not hostile (from "same sect" to "old acquaintance")
- Later if the former sect is in trouble, may still return to help — the "graduated disciple" identity gives standing to come back

**Suitable sects**: famous orthodox sects (Sword Sect, Qi Sect, Vajra Sect, Skill Sect); these usually allow disciples to leave through proper channels.

---

#### Path Two: Sneak Away

> "When the third watch drum sounds, shoulder the pack, slip over the wall without a sound. Henceforth far apart, no more Huashan disciple Liu Rufeng."

**Method**: leave without farewell, slip out of the mountain gate by night, then hide in the Jianghu.

**Process**:
1. The character makes a **Stealth check** (DC by the sect's alert level, generally 12–18)
2. If success, leaves without anyone knowing
3. If fail, discovered — either stopped (defection fails) or escalates to worse "smash-and-leave" (conflict with the blocker, Infamy +5, sect reputation becomes **Sworn Enemy**)

**Cost and consequences**:
- Heroism −5 (abandoned master's grace without word)
- Infamy varies by the sect's Jianghu standing (leaving orthodox +3~5, leaving evil no change or −3)
- **Defected-art lock** (see §2.4 "Fate of Defected Arts")
- Sect reputation cleared; former sect attitude becomes "**cool → hostile**" (per GM, depends on your performance in the sect)
- Former sect may send people to investigate — but not assassinate, rather "bring the person back to ask clearly"
- If not found within three months, the sect usually gives up the search (unless you took sect treasures / manuals)

**Distinct from defecting to enemy sect**: sneaking away usually just leaves, not joining an opposing force. If the former sect later finds you joined an enemy sect, attitude escalates.

---

#### Path Three: Expelled from the Sect

> "From today, you are no longer my Huashan disciple! Hand over your sword and roll down the mountain!"

**Method**: expelled by the sect master / elder council for violating sect rules — this is "passive defection."

**Triggers** (any one suffices for expulsion):
- Heroism / Infamy severely diverges from the sect's stance (e.g. Vajra Sect disciple Infamy ≥ 20)
- Violates a sect core taboo (e.g. Medicine Sect disciple uses medicine to harm)
- Colludes with the sect's enemies
- Severe dereliction in a major event (e.g. abandons post while guarding the gate)
- Leaks the sect's secret untransmitted lore

**Process**:
1. GM declares expulsion (usually triggered at a plot node, not a random event)
2. Character's sect reputation cleared
3. Returns sect token
4. **Fate of defected arts** per §2.4

**Cost and consequences**:
- Heroism change per circumstance (wrongly expelled may not drop; deservedly −5~10)
- Infamy per crime (betrayal +5~10, colluding with outside enemies +5~15)
- Former sect relation: **Hostile** (public expulsion means you are seen as the sect's shame)
- Expelled disciples cannot again learn that sect's arts through proper channels (except manuals, but if found by the sect will be pursued)
- **Special**: after expulsion, if you can prove you were wronged (need to complete the relevant plot line), may be re-accepted — but must pass a harder "re-entry" trial

---

#### Path Four: Defect to Enemy Sect

> "Lu Chenyuan, your Tianjian Alliance calls itself righteous, but is only a pack of hypocrites! Today I abandon light for darkness, join the Blood Moon Cult — from now on, I and the Tianjian Alliance are irreconcilable!"

**Method**: not only leave the original sect, but join a sect (or power) opposed to it.

**Triggers**:
- The target sect must be in an **opposed** relation with the original (see The Jianghu World faction-relationship diagram)
  - Tianjian Alliance ↔ Blood Moon Cult (sworn enmity)
  - Wanfo Temple ↔ Blood Moon Cult (cautious opposition)
  - Yaowang Valley ↔ Wangchuan Tower (mutual wariness)
  - etc.
- The target sect willing to accept (may need to prove loyalty, see below)

**Process**:
1. **Leave original sect**: the character first leaves the original sect by any means (Path One/Two/Three all work, but the method affects later attitude)
2. **Pledge to new sect**:
   - Need the new sect's **sponsorship** — usually through a series of tasks proving loyalty
   - The new sect assigns trial tasks (DC 15–20 series of checks); pass to be accepted
   - Some powers demand you provide the original sect's intel as a "name-pledge token" — a deep dilemma
3. **Identity after acceptance**: the new sect usually does not immediately give full trust. Start from the lowest identity (even if original realm high); need time to rebuild trust

**Cost and consequences**:
- **Heroism**: orthodox → evil −10~20 (by defection method); evil → orthodox +10~20
- **Infamy**: orthodox → evil +10~20; evil → orthodox −10~20 (washing off some infamy)
- **Former sect relation**: **Sworn Enmity** (to the death — the original sect puts you on the "purge" list)
- **Jianghu reputation change**:
  - Orthodox → evil: "Jianghu scum" — the orthodox circle no longer accepts you
  - Evil → orthodox: "prodigal returns" — some orthodox are suspicious, need time to prove
- **New sect relation**: **Suspicion** (initially not trusted; need tasks and behavior to build sect reputation)
- **The double-distrust dilemma**: the enemy-defector is in the most vulnerable spot — old sect wants to kill you, new sect not fully trusts you. The GM should use this spot to create tense narrative.

**Enemy-defection exclusive hunt mechanism** (see §2.5).

### 2.3 Jianghu Relation Changes After Defection

After defection, the character's relation with the original sect and related powers updates per these rules:

| Pre-defection Relation | Change (open exit) | Change (sneak / expelled) | Change (enemy defection) |
|------------------------|--------------------|---------------------------|--------------------------|
| Same sect | → Old acquaintance (cool) | → Distant / Hostile | → Sworn Enemy |
| Ally / Friendly | Unchanged | May turn cool | Varies with original sect stance |
| Neutral | Unchanged | Unchanged | May turn cool |
| Opposed | Unchanged | Unchanged | Becomes "new same sect" (secretly guarded) |

### 2.4 Fate of Defected Arts

> Are the arts the master's, or your own? The Jianghu has no standard answer. But one iron law: **the moves you already learned can be used, but this path cannot go further.**

#### Core Rule: Defected-Art Lock

**Defected arts** = all arts learned through the original sect (including sect teaching, and sect-manual study).

| Defection Method | Defected-Art Handling |
|------------------|----------------------|
| Open resignation | Arts **retained and may keep advancing**. The master allows you to "take your art and graduate" — but Transcendent plot-unlock needs continued ties with the old sect, or self-epiphany (DC +3). |
| Sneak away | Arts **retained but locked** — unlocked moves still usable, but cannot keep advancing (Minor→Complete→Perfecting proficiency check DC +5; Transcendent cannot unlock). |
| Expelled | Arts **retained but locked** (same as sneak), and using that sect's signature art, Jianghu knowers may turn hostile — "stole the art and still flaunt it in the Jianghu?" |
| Enemy defection | Arts **retained but locked** (same as sneak). Moreover, the original sect holds a "purge" fixation on users of that art — chance of being discovered by the old sect when using it rises. |

**Specific meaning of "locked"**:
- The art's proficiency realm stays at the level at defection
- Unlocked moves used normally; Qi cost and damage unchanged
- Cannot advance this art through normal training (Comprehension check auto-fails)
- **Exception**: may continue advancing via self-study manual (if a complete manual exists and it is not sect-exclusive), but DC +5, and Qi-Deviation risk +1

**Independent-art exception**: arts tagged "Independent generic" (e.g. Five Tigers Door-Cutting Saber, Flower-Plucking Finger, Nine Yang Divine Art) are unaffected by defection — these arts already spread through the Jianghu, not dependent on sect transmission.

#### Art Memory and Forgetting

A character may choose to **actively forget** defected arts ("seal"), freeing a martial slot (see Martial Arts System §3.4 Forgetting and Replacing). After forgetting:
- Proficiency realm retained (reactivation later needs 1 session of "rehab")
- Active forgetting incurs no extra cost

### 2.5 Former-Sect Hunt Mechanism

> "Huashan purges its own, none of your business. Blades have no eyes — stand back!"

Whether the former sect hunts you after defection depends on **three factors**:

| Factor | Low Risk | Mid Risk | High Risk |
|--------|----------|----------|-----------|
| **Defection method** | Open resignation | Sneak away | Expelled / enemy defection |
| **What you took** | Only took your own arts | Took sect token / equipment | Took manual / sect-guardian treasure |
| **Your value / threat** | Nobody | Promising disciple | Core disciple / knows sect secrets |

**Hunt level** (D3 ladder):

| Hunt Level | Trigger | Frequency | Hunter Strength | Hunter Attitude |
|-----------|---------|-----------|-----------------|-----------------|
| **Lv.0 No Hunt** | Open exit, took no manual | — | — | — |
| **Lv.1 Investigation** | Sneak + nobody | Once per 1d4 sessions "someone is asking about you" | Second-Rate expert ×1, Third-Rate disciple ×2 | "Bring the person back to ask clearly" |
| **Lv.2 Apprehension** | Expelled / sneak + took token | Once per 1d3 sessions an encounter triggers | First-Rate master ×1, Second-Rate expert ×2 | "Bring back to the mountain for trial" |
| **Lv.3 Purge the Sect** | Enemy defection / took sect-guardian manual | Once per 1d2 sessions | Grandmaster-level ×1, First-Rate master ×2 | "Kill on sight" |

**Hunt mechanism details**:
- The GM inserts hunt events at fitting plot nodes by hunt level
- Hunts do not continue forever — Lv.1 after 1d4 sessions if not found drops to Lv.0; Lv.2 lasts until captured or reconciled; Lv.3 **does not stop** until one side is destroyed
- May actively reconcile: return what is owed, perform a great service to offset the crime

### 2.6 Resource Cutoff After Defection

After defection, the following sect resources are **all lost**:

| Resource Type | Specific Content Lost |
|---------------|----------------------|
| **Art teaching** | Can no longer learn new sect arts through sect teaching |
| **Manual borrowing** | Can no longer enter the Sutra Pavilion / Sword-Treasury Pavilion etc. |
| **Pills / materials** | Can no longer draw sect provisions (e.g. Shaolin pills, Tang hidden weapons, Yaowang herbs) |
| **Intel net** | Lose sect intel support (e.g. Gaibang net, Qianji net) |
| **Protection** | Lose the sect's armed protection — the master won't stand for you when chased by enemies |
| **Economic support** | Lose sect salary / pocket money |
| **Reputation bonus** | Lose the Jianghu-standing bonus from sect identity |

## III. Wash Hands in the Golden Basin

### 3.1 What is "Wash Hands in the Golden Basin"

Wash Hands in the Golden Basin is a ritual retirement in the Jianghu — in public (usually witnessed by Jianghu fellows) you declare exit from Jianghu strife, no longer involving yourself in martial grudges. It differs from defection — you keep sect identity (if anyone will keep you), but promise no longer to use force in Jianghu affairs.

> "From today, I Liu Rufeng wash hands in the golden basin, seal my sword and retire. The Jianghu's grudges, henceforth none of my concern."

### 3.2 Retirement Conditions

| Condition | Requirement | Note |
|-----------|-------------|------|
| **Realm floor** | At least Second Realm · Crossing the Threshold | A nobody at First Glimpse is not worth "washing hands" — the Jianghu has not yet remembered your name |
| **Jianghu Renown** | Heroism ≥ 20 or Infamy ≥ 20 | Must be "named" in the Jianghu — whether by xia-name or infamy |
| **No unresolved grudges** | No character holds a "Sworn Enemy" relation | Before retiring must settle all public grudges (may ask a mediator to guarantee "no pursuit after term") |
| **Witness** | At least one reputable Jianghu figure presides | e.g. Tianjian elder, Wanfo senior monk, Gaibang elder |
| **Cost** | Return tokens / borrowed items not your own | Your own arts and personal items may be kept |

### 3.3 Retirement Ceremony

**Process**:
1. Pick an auspicious day, send visiting cards inviting Jianghu fellows to witness
2. Before the witness, immerse both hands in a basin of clear water (golden or bronze)
3. Publicly recite the retirement vow
4. Hand your sword / weapon to the witness (symbolic sealing of martial arts)
5. Witness declares the rite complete

**After the ceremony**:
- All your **grudge records** are frozen (not deleted — old enemies may keep hating, but you promise not to start new grudges)
- Gain the special tag: **Retired** (the Jianghu renown system no longer records new grudge gains/losses)
- The sect usually does not hunt a retired disciple (even a defected one, given face)
- **Exception**: the ceremony's protection applies only if you "cause no more trouble." If you retire yet actively fight someone, the vow is void, and **extra Infamy +10** (deceiving the heroes of the realm)

### 3.4 Life After Retirement

A retired character is still a martial person — just no longer "mixing in the Jianghu." You may:
- Open a martial school and teach (but not under the sect name)
- Practice medicine and aid the world
- Retire to the hills to study martial arts
- Do business for a living
- Serve as a sect guest elder (not joining Jianghu strife)

After retirement you **cannot**:
- Join fights between sects
- Intervene in Jianghu grudges by force
- Attend public contests like the martial conclave
- Hold any real post in a Jianghu power

### 3.5 Cost of Return

Wash Hands is not irreversible. When a greater reason arises (family threatened, realm in chaos, old grudge comes calling), the retired xia may choose to **return**.

**Cost of return**:
- The ceremony's witness views you as "a person without word" (attitude drops one notch)
- All frozen grudges **reactivate**, and may intensify for being "played for a fool"
- Heroism −10 (orthodox), Infamy +5 (evil)
- Jianghu renown downgraded: "one without word"
- **This life may not wash hands again** — one may only wash once; this is an iron law

**Special — passive return**: if after retirement an old enemy comes to you, you may defend yourself without it counting as "active breach." But if you chase out for revenge, it counts as active return.

## IV. Multi-Sect Cultivation

### 4.1 The Concept of Mixed Cultivation

**Multi-Sect Cultivation** (also called "concurrent study," "multi-study") means a character cultivates other sects' arts while keeping original sect identity. Unlike defection, you remain the original sect's disciple, enjoy its resources, only broaden your arts.

> "I am a Huashan disciple, true — but I also learned Taiji Fist from a Wudang Daoist. This is no betrayal of Huashan — sword and fist arts complement each other."

### 4.2 Conditions for Mixed Cultivation

To start multi-sect cultivation, meet these conditions:

| Condition | Specific Requirement |
|-----------|----------------------|
| **Sect relation** | The target sect and your original are **not opposed**. Allied, friendly, neutral sects may mix. Opposed may not (unless special plot — e.g. secretly stealing learning). |
| **Comprehension threshold** | Each extra sect needs Comprehension ≥ 5 + (already-concurrent sects). First extra sect needs Comprehension ≥ 5, second needs ≥ 6. |
| **Enough martial slots** | The new sect's arts need slots. Check your current martial-slot cap (by Comprehension, see Martial Arts System §3.4) for free space. |
| **Sponsorship channel** | Need one of: ① a target-sect disciple's **recommendation letter** ② your studied art's **visiting card** accepted by the opposing master ③ a renowned guarantor ④ complete the target sect's **cultivation-entry trial** |

**Sect-relation quick reference** (based on The Jianghu World faction relations):

| Your sect's power | Sects you may mix | Sects you may not mix |
|-------------------|-------------------|----------------------|
| Tianjian Alliance (Sword Sect) | Taixu Palace / Wanfo Temple / Yaowang Valley / Skill Sect / Shadow Sect | Blood Moon Cult |
| Taixu Palace (Qi Sect) | Tianjian Alliance / Wanfo Temple / Yaowang Valley / Qianji Pavilion / Shadow Sect | Blood Moon Cult |
| Wanfo Temple (Vajra Sect) | Tianjian Alliance / Taixu Palace / Yaowang Valley / Skill Sect / Shadow Sect | Blood Moon Cult |
| Yaowang Valley (Medicine Sect) | All (absolute neutral) | None |
| Qianji Pavilion (Poison Sect) | Taixu Palace / Yaowang Valley / Sihai Alliance / Shadow Sect | Blood Moon Cult |
| Sihai Alliance (Skill Sect) | Tianjian Alliance / Wanfo Temple / Yaowang Valley / Qianji Pavilion / Shadow Sect | Blood Moon Cult (officially denies contact) |
| Shadow Sect | All neutral sects | No fixed enemy |
| Independent | All (no sect-stance limit) | None |

### 4.3 Limits of Mixed Cultivation

Cultivating multiple paths costs — "bite off more than you can chew" is basic martial truth.

#### 4.3.1 Proficiency Speed Decay

| Sects Cultivated | Proficiency Speed Mod | Description |
|------------------|----------------------|-------------|
| 1 (own) | Normal | One path focused |
| 2 (+1 extra) | All art proficiency sessions ×1.5 (round up) | Divided attention |
| 3 (+2 extra) | All art proficiency sessions ×2 | Scattered energy |
| 4 (+3 extra) | **Not allowed** — mixed cap is 3 sects (1 own + 2 extra) | Greed loses all |

**Example**: Huashan disciple Liu Rufeng concurrently cultivates Wudang Taiji Fist. Originally Taiji Fist First-Glimpse → Minor took 1–3 sessions; now needs 2–5 sessions (1.5×).

**Exception**: these cases do not slow proficiency:
- Same-source same-vein arts (e.g. Huashan Sword Art + Jade Maiden Sword Art, both Sword Sect sword arts)
- An art already cultivated to Transcendent taught to the concurrent sect does not affect proficiency (your mastered art needs no more energy)

#### 4.3.2 Art Conflict Rules

Not all arts coexist safely. These count as **art conflict**:

| Conflict Type | Judgment Condition | Effect |
|---------------|-------------------|--------|
| **Firm-Gentle clash** | Simultaneously cultivate inner mental methods of force type "Firm" and "Gentle" | After each cultivation make a Qi-Deviation check (DC 15 + concurrent-sect count) |
| **Righteous-Evil incompatible** | Simultaneously cultivate righteous arts needing Heroism and evil arts needing Infamy | Both arts' effects halved (round), and Qi-Deviation risk +2 |
| **Meridian reversal** | Run two sects' inner mental methods at once (switching inner art in combat counts as reversal) | The round you switch inner art, Qi cost doubles; first switch per battle triggers Qi-Deviation check (DC 15) |
| **Attribute clash** | An art's attribute need severely diverges from your current attribute bonuses | That art's cultivation DC +5, and cannot trigger same-source synergy |

**Inner arts you cannot mix**:
- Inner mental methods are the root of the art system. Cultivating two different inner mental methods at once (e.g. Pure Yang Limitless Art + Nine Yin Manual) is normally **impossible** — a character has only one set of meridians, can run only one Qi cycle.
- **Only exception**: some legendary arts (e.g. "Taixuan Jing," "Beiming Divine Art") may offer "compatible with many inner arts" special ability — but this is a Supreme-level special effect, adjudicated by GM per the specific art.

**Inner-art switching**: a character may choose to run a different inner art in different battles (provided you trained two), but:
- Switch out of combat: needs 10 minutes seated meditation
- Switch in combat: costs a primary action + 3 Qi, triggers Qi-Deviation check (DC 15)

#### 4.3.3 Mixed Cultivation Count Cap

| Comprehension | Total Sects Cultivatable (incl. own) | Advice |
|---------------|--------------------------------------|--------|
| 1–4 | 1 (own only) | Comprehension insufficient; focus one sect |
| 5–6 | 2 (own + 1 extra) | Surplus to broaden |
| 7–9 | 3 (own + 2 extra) | Learned and versatile |
| 10+ | 3 (own + 2 extra) | At cap — even top Comprehension cannot infinitely divide attention |

### 4.4 Advantages of Mixed Cultivation

The cost (slower proficiency + conflict risk) needs matching reward. Main advantages:

#### 4.4.1 Move Complementarity

Different sects' arts fill each other's tactical gaps:

| Own Sect | + Paired Sect | Complement Effect |
|----------|---------------|-------------------|
| Sword Sect (melee output) | + Poison Sect (ranged hidden) | Both near and far — sword up close, hidden at range |
| Vajra Sect (frontline defense) | + Qi Sect (deep inner) | Iron Shirt reduction + Pure Yang Qi recovery, unbeatable endurance |
| Shadow Sect (high evasion) | + Sword Sect (high output) | Un-evadable hard kill, un-killable evade |
| Medicine Sect (healing) | + Vajra Sect (frontline) | Can tank and heal, team core |

**Mechanic**: in combat you may **freely switch between different sects' martial moves** — as long as martial slots allow. This is the most direct tactical advantage of mixed cultivation.

#### 4.4.2 Composite Arts: Fuse the Strengths of Two

When these conditions are met, a character may attempt a **self-created composite art** — fusing two sects' strengths into a unique sect-guardian art:

| Condition | Requirement |
|-----------|-------------|
| Both arts cultivated to **Perfecting the Craft** or above | From two different sects, and not conflicting |
| Comprehension ≥ 7 | Need enough martial comprehension |
| One major **epiphany scene** | GM-arranged plot climax (life-death battle / seclusion breakthrough / master guidance) |
| At least 3 sessions of cultivation | Fusing two arts' concepts takes time |

**Composite art examples**:

| Art A | Art B | Composite Art | Effect |
|-------|-------|---------------|--------|
| Huashan Sword Art | Taiji Fist | **Hard-Soft Combined Sword** | Sword moves keep the swift-sword first-strike bonus, while gaining Taiji Fist's "block-and-recover-Qi" effect |
| Vajra Palm | Five Poisons Palm | **Vajra Poison Palm** | Keeps knockback, extra poison (but poison stacks capped at 1) |
| Treading Snow Without Trace | Cloud-Ladder Leap | **Free Wandering** | Initiative +6, evade twice per battle, may walk on water |

**GM guide for creating composite arts**:
1. Take one signature effect from each of the two arts
2. Qi cost = 1.5× the higher original (round)
3. Tier = the higher original's tier
4. GM and player may jointly design specifics — the composite art is a milestone of character growth

#### 4.4.3 Technique Reading Check Advantage

Characters cultivating many sects have broad experience, gaining a bonus on Technique Reading checks:

| Sects Cultivated | Technique Reading Bonus |
|------------------|-------------------------|
| 2 | +1 |
| 3 | +2 |

Additionally, for arts of already-mixed sects you automatically gain "familiarity" (Technique Reading DC −5).

## V. Independent-to-Formal and Formal-to-Independent

### 5.1 An Independent Joining a Sect

An Independent (a free wanderer with no sect affiliation) may join a sect during play. This differs from choosing a sect at character creation — you arrive bearing your Jianghu experience, with both advantages and drawbacks.

#### Joining Conditions

| Condition | Requirement |
|-----------|-------------|
| **Jianghu Renown** | Depends on the target sect: Sword Sect / Qi Sect require Heroism ≥ 10; Poison Sect requires any Renown ≥ 10 |
| **Entrance Trial** | Must pass the target sect's entrance trial (a series of Difficulty Class (DC) 15–20 checks, designed by the GM) |
| **Sponsor** | Requires recommendation from at least one formal disciple of the target sect |
| **Realm** | No hard threshold; but if your Realm is higher than most of the sect's disciples, you may be seen as a "threat" rather than "new blood" |
| **No Hostile Relation** | You cannot apply to a sect you were once enemies with (unless a special plot dictates otherwise) |

#### Differences from Joining at Character Creation

| Dimension | Normal Creation-Time Entry | In-Game Independent-to-Formal |
|-----------|----------------------------|-------------------------------|
| **Attribute Bonus** | Gain sect attribute +2 | Not granted (your attributes are already set by the Jianghu) |
| **Starting Arts** | Gain 2 entry-level sect martial arts | Gain 1 entry-level sect martial art (you already have your own foundation) |
| **Sect Skills** | Gain 2 free sect-granted skills (Level 1) | Gain 1 (you already have your own Jianghu experience) |
| **Sect Renown** | Starts as "Outer Disciple" | Starts as "Outer Disciple" (no favor for your Jianghu experience) |
| **Old-Guard Bias** | None | Some sect NPCs may suspect a "midway joiner" (starting Sect Renown −5) |

#### After Joining

- The Sect Renown system operates normally (see §VI).
- You may study that sect's martial arts (subject to martial-art slot and Mixed-Cultivation limits).
- You enjoy sect resources (unlocked by Renown tier).

---

### 5.2 A Sect Member Becoming an Independent

A character who has already joined a sect may choose to leave and become an Independent. This resembles Sect Defection, but is different in nature — you choose "to belong to no sect" rather than "to pledge to another sect."

#### How to Become Independent

Same four paths as Sect Defection (open departure / slipping away / being expelled), but:

- The attitude is generally milder than defecting to an enemy sect.
- Righteous-to-Independent does not trigger Lv.3 pursuit (worst case Lv.1–2).
- Evil-to-Independent may be seen as "betrayal," but is usually not pursued (unless you know too much).

#### Martial Arts After Becoming Independent

| Art Type | Treatment |
|----------|-----------|
| Sect martial arts | **Locked** (can no longer be advanced) — you are no longer that sect's disciple and cannot deepen your study of its arts |
| Independent generic arts | Unaffected |
| Self-created arts | Unaffected |
| Arts already at Transcendent | Retained in full (you have reached Great Accomplishment; the sect cannot take it back) |

#### After Becoming Independent

- The advancement restrictions on defected arts are lifted (if you obtain the art's complete manual through other means).
- Your Jianghu Renown alignment becomes "Unaffiliated."
- The Sect Renown system no longer applies (you are a member of no sect).
- You may reallocate martial-art slots previously occupied by sect arts.

#### Advantages of Becoming Independent

The Independent's core advantage (freedom in martial-art Build) still applies after going Independent:

- Not bound by sect alignment (you may freely study any sect's arts, given a channel).
- Not constrained by the Sect Renown system.
- You may freely choose Righteous or Evil alignment without a master's discipline.

## VI. Sect Renown and the Privilege Ladder

### 6.1 The Concept of Sect Renown

Sect Renown (also called "internal renown") measures a character's standing **within their own sect, independent of Jianghu Renown**. A character may be unknown in the Jianghu yet hold high office within their sect, or be famed across the martial world yet remain an ordinary disciple in their master's hall.

| Dimension | Jianghu Renown (Heroism/Infamy) | Sect Renown |
|-----------|--------------------------------|-------------|
| Measures what | The Jianghu's opinion of you | The sect's opinion of you |
| Scope | Attitude of all Jianghu NPCs | Only this sect's NPCs and resource access |
| How it grows | Jianghu conduct | Sect tasks / contribution |
| Highest Title | Grand Xia / Great Demon Head | Sect Leader / Elder |

### 6.2 The Five Tiers of Sect Renown

```
Outer Disciple → Inner Disciple → True-Inheritance Disciple → Elder → Successor Candidate
   0                20               50                      100      200
```

| Tier | Renown Range | Title | Core Privilege |
|------|--------------|-------|----------------|
| **Outer Disciple** | 0–19 | Registered Disciple / New Disciple | Basic room and board; may learn entry-level arts |
| **Inner Disciple** | 20–49 | Inner-Room Disciple / Direct-Inheritance Disciple | May learn advanced arts; sect stipend (small silver / pills) |
| **True-Inheritance Disciple** | 50–99 | Core Disciple / Dharma-Heir | May read superior arts; private courtyard; may handle minor sect affairs |
| **Elder** | 100–199 | Elder / Steward | May command sect disciples; join sect decisions; take disciples; may learn ultimate arts (Sect Leader approval) |
| **Successor Candidate** | 200+ | Sect-Leader Heir / Next Leader | Some acting-leader powers; join top decisions; may touch the sect's most core secret arts |

### 6.3 Detailed Privileges by Tier

#### Outer Disciple (Renown 0–19)

> Recently entered, still building foundations. The master occasionally gives pointers; senior brothers and sisters boss you around.

| Privilege | Detail |
|-----------|--------|
| Art Study | May practice **entry-level arts** (the first layer of the sect's signature arts) |
| Resource Access | Sect provides free **room and board**, basic weapons (standard longsword / wooden staff, etc.) |
| Connections | Know sect NPCs, but cannot actively command them |

**Ways to gain Renown**:

- Complete daily sect tasks (sweeping / patrol / herb-gathering, etc.): +1~2 Renown.
- Pass martial exams (e.g. Huashan's annual sword test): +3~5 Renown.
- Bring glory to the sect (doing something in the Jianghu that gives the sect face): +5~10 Renown.

---

#### Inner Disciple (Renown 20–49)

> Formally entered; the master truly treats you as a disciple. You have your own cultivation quarters and no longer do chores.

| Privilege | Detail |
|-----------|--------|
| Art Study | May practice **advanced arts** (with master's consent); entry-level advancement checks get Difficulty Class (DC) −2 (sect resource bonus) |
| Resource Access | Monthly sect **stipend** (10–30 taels silver or 1–2 pills, by sect wealth and your contribution) |
| Connections | May request 1–2 fellow disciples to assist (non-combat escort) |
| Library / Armory | May enter the **outer hall** of the sect's Scripture Pavilion / Sword Armory (entry to advanced arts) |
| Special | Eligible for the sect's **martial tournament** and outbound **training expeditions** |

**Ways to gain Renown**:

- Lead a newcomer through the entrance trial: +3~5.
- Complete important sect tasks (escort / guard / campaign, etc.): +5~15.
- Place in the sect tournament: +5~10.

---

#### True-Inheritance Disciple (Renown 50–99)

> Seen as the sect's future. The master will privately teach you exclusive tricks — not the kind written in manuals, but insights passed mouth-to-ear.

| Privilege | Detail |
|-----------|--------|
| Art Study | May practice **superior arts**; advancement checks below advanced tier get Difficulty Class (DC) −3 |
| Resource Access | Monthly stipend doubled; may apply for **special cultivation resources** (e.g. millennium ginseng, meteoric-iron-forged sword) |
| Connections | May command 3–5 Outer Disciples for tasks; may ask Inner disciples to assist in combat |
| Library / Armory | May enter the **inner hall** (superior-arts zone) |
| Special | May **represent the sect abroad**; exercise minor adjudication when the leader is absent; **may take Outer Disciples as students** |
| Exclusive Privilege | Once per month may ask the master (Sect Leader or Elder) for **personal instruction** — any one art's advancement check gets Difficulty Class (DC) −5 |

**Typical plot beat to reach True-Inheritance Disciple**: render great service to the sect (e.g. repel invaders, recover a lost art, win honor for the sect in a major tournament).

---

#### Elder (Renown 100–199)

> You are no longer just a "disciple." You are the sect's manager, decision-maker, and guardian.

| Privilege | Detail |
|-----------|--------|
| Art Study | May apply to study **ultimate arts** (Sect Leader or Elder-Council approval); advancement checks below superior tier get Difficulty Class (DC) −5 |
| Resource Access | Own **private courtyard** and personal pill-room / cultivation chamber; freely use materials in the sect storehouse |
| Connections | May command 10–20 disciples of all tiers; may dispatch Inner disciples on independent tasks |
| Library / Armory | May enter the **secret chamber** (ultimate-arts zone, but each entry must be logged) |
| Decision Power | May join the **Elder Council**, with a vote on major sect matters |
| Tutelage Right | May take **Inner Disciples** as students (true master-disciple bond, not proxy teaching) |
| Income | Annual sect dividend (100–500 taels silver or equivalent resources) |

**Typical plot beat to reach Elder**: play a decisive role when the sect's survival is at stake; or accumulate contribution past the threshold and pass the Elder-Council vote.

---

#### Successor Candidate (Renown 200+)

> You are not the Sect Leader — but everyone knows you are the next one. Your word in the sect ranks just below the leader's.

| Privilege | Detail |
|-----------|--------|
| Art Study | May touch the sect's **core secret teachings** (e.g. Tianjian Alliance's *Tianjian Formula*, Vajra Sect's *Tendon-Changing Classic*); all art advancement checks get Difficulty Class (DC) −5 |
| Resource Access | May freely allocate **all sect resources** (report to the leader, but rarely refused) |
| Connections | May mobilize **the whole sect** (wartime conscription); all tiers treat you as the de-facto number two |
| Library / Armory | **Free access** to all zones |
| Decision Power | Act in the leader's stead when absent; Elder-Council proposals must hear your opinion |
| Succession | When the current leader abdicates / dies, **automatically becomes leader** (unless the Elder Council votes to veto, but this is extremely rare) |

**Reaching Successor Candidate**: this is not merely a number — it needs a complete plot line. Typically involves:

- The leader personally declares you heir.
- The Elder Council passes unanimously.
- Completing the ultimate trial the leader assigns.

### 6.4 Renown Deduction

The following actions **lower Sect Renown**:

| Action | Renown Deduction |
|--------|------------------|
| Disobeying a master's order (minor) | −3~5 |
| Bringing shame to the sect in the Jianghu (e.g. losing a duel to a nobody) | −2~5 |
| Illegally leaking sect arts | −20~30 |
| Colluding with the sect's enemies | −50 (immediate expulsion) |
| Long-term neglect of sect duties (3 consecutive sessions skipping sect affairs) | −5 per 3 sessions |

### 6.5 Sect Renown and Its Relation to Defection / Mixed Cultivation

- **After defection**: Sect Renown is **cleared to zero** (no longer a member; the renown system stops).
- **Mixed Cultivation does not affect home-sect renown**: studying elsewhere on the side does not lower your original sect's renown — as long as you do not neglect sect duties because of it.
- **But note**: if in Mixed Cultivation you study the arts of a force hostile to your original sect, it counts as **colluding with the sect's enemies** (−50 renown, immediate expulsion).

## VIII. Design Notes

### 8.1 Not the Default Playstyle

The Sect-Defection and Mixed-Cultivation rules are designed to **expand the space of choices**, not to create an "optimal solution." The following design decisions embody this philosophy:

- **Defected arts locked**: the combat function you lose keeps growing. Those who stay in the original sect will out-master its ultimate arts — this is the reward of loyalty.
- **Advancement-speed penalty**: Mixed Cultivation lets you switch tactics flexibly, but your advancement speed in any single art is slower than a specialist's. You are more like a Swiss Army knife, not the sharpest great blade.
- **Conflict and risk**: internal arts cannot be mixed, hard and soft clash, demonic-deviation risk rises — these are real costs. Mixed Cultivation is not a free lunch.
- **Character-driven narrative**: the true cost of defection is not in numbers, but in relations with NPCs, in memories within the sect, in moral choice. The GM should prioritize digging into narrative-level consequences, not merely rolling dice to decide pursuit.

### 8.2 Why Defected Arts Can Be Kept

There are two philosophical views:

- **View A**: after defection the arts should be "abolished" — because the art is passed by the master; defection equals betraying master and ancestors.
- **View B**: the art is "practiced out"; the master only guided — you already paid the sweat and time of cultivation.

This ruleset adopts a **middle path**: the arts may be kept and used, but cannot be further advanced. This models the real logic of "you took the technique, but lost the guidance to keep progressing." It also makes a meaningful trade-off at the gameplay level — a defector's defected arts grow weaker over time (because others progress while yours stall).

### 8.3 On the "Irreversible" Design of Wash Hands

In reality, Wash Hands is extremely hard to take back — because going back means deceiving all the witnesses. The rule's "once in a lifetime" design creates a true watershed: once you wash hands, you give up the right to "have a voice in the Jianghu." The weight of this decision should make players ponder long at the table — which is exactly what we want.

### 8.4 Why Sect Renown Has No More Tiers

Five tiers (Outer → Inner → True-Inheritance → Elder → Successor Candidate) already fully cover the complete career from entry to leader. More tiers would raise system complexity for diminishing returns. The five-tier structure also quietly matches the "five-realm advancement" logic of martial cultivation — sect standing and martial Realm should roughly synchronize.

### 8.5 Creative Space Left for the GM

This chapter provides a framework, but the following are adjudicated by the GM per the specific plot:

- The exact identity and timing of pursuers.
- Plot locks on defection / Mixed Cultivation (when and how they trigger).
- Detail of NPC attitudes toward defectors (each NPC has a different personality and reaction).
- Concrete effects of composite arts (the rules give creation principles; specifics are co-created by GM and player).
- Intra-sect power struggles (each sect's "politics" is unfolded by the GM per the world-setting).

## I. Why Solo Rules Are Needed

The standard rules of the *Yonghe Martial Codex* assume a party of 3–5 PCs. But many GMs and players prefer a "duo party" mode of **one player + one host**. The standard rules face three core problems in a single-PC scene:

| Problem | Standard-Rules State | Solo-Rule Solution |
|---------|----------------------|-------------------|
| **Encounter balance** | Threat-value formula assumes 4 PCs | Rewrite the encounter-difficulty algorithm |
| **Action economy** | Boss acts 1 round vs 4 PCs' 4 rounds | NPC-companion system + Hero-Point mechanic |
| **Skill coverage** | 4 PCs share 19 skills | Skill-exemption rules + companion skill pool |

---

## II. Encounter Difficulty: The Solo Algorithm

### 2.1 Solo Threat Value

| Character | Threat Value |
|-----------|--------------|
| Single PC (by Realm) | Equal to standard PC value (1/2/3/5/8/13) |
| NPC Companion (each) | PC Threat Value × 0.5 (simplified companion, about half a PC's power) |

### 2.2 Difficulty Judgment (Single-PC Only)

| Total Enemy Threat / (PC + Companion Threat) | Difficulty | Note |
|----------------------------------------------|-----------|------|
| < 0.3 | Easy | No resource expenditure needed |
| 0.3–0.6 | Normal | Serious response suffices |
| 0.6–1.0 | Challenging | Needs strategy and resource spend |
| 1.0–1.5 | Dangerous | PC may reach Near Death |
| > 1.5 | Lethal | Not recommended — unless plot-supported |

**Versus standard encounters**: standard 4 PCs (Threat 4) vs Threat-4 enemy = ratio 1.0 (Normal); single PC (Threat 1) vs Threat-0.5 enemy = ratio 0.5 (Normal). The number of enemies a solo PC faces is about **1/4 to 1/2** of the standard.

### 2.3 Boss-Fight Solo Adjustment

| Standard Boss | Standard Health (HP) | Solo Health (HP) | Solo Damage | Other Adjustment |
|---------------|----------------------|------------------|-------------|------------------|
| Han Bao (First-Realm Boss) | 30 | 20 | 1d8+1 | Keeps enrage mechanic, but enrage cap lowered to +2 |
| Nangong Hui (Third-Realm Boss) | 45 | 30 | 2d6+2 | Recovers 1d3 Health (HP) every 3 rounds (instead of every round) |
| Li Canyang (Fourth-Realm Boss) | 60 | 40 | 2d8+3 | Backfire round extended from 3 to 4 |

| Jin Ridi (Sixth-Realm Boss) | 100 | 70 | 3d10+5 | Wolf-pack summon reduced from 3 to 1 |

|------|--------|--------|
| 1 point | Re-roll one check (take the higher) | Immediately after any check |
| 1 point | Execute one extra move (does not consume this round's move) | Within own turn |
| 2 points | Immediately remove one negative state (stun / bleed / sealed-acupoint / disordered breath) | At start of own turn |
| 2 points | "Desperate Strike" — this attack's damage takes max dice (e.g. 3d6 → 18) | After attack roll, before damage roll |
| 3 points | "Narrow Escape from Death" — when Health (HP) drops below 0, stabilize and recover Constitution-points of Health (HP) | On Near-Death check |

### 3.3 Earning Hero Points (Extra)

Beyond Long-Rest recovery, the GM may award 1 Hero Point in the following cases:

- The PC makes a choice of major significance to their personal main plot.
- The PC shows unique tactical wisdom in combat.
- The PC makes a sacrifice in social situations that fits the character's core values.

## IV. NPC Companion System

A solo PC is not truly "alone" — in the Jianghu you always meet trustworthy companions.

### 4.1 Companion Template

NPC companions use a simplified attribute block; no Qi pool is tracked (replaced by "move-use count").

| Attribute | Note |
|-----------|------|
| Health (HP) | Constitution × 4 + Realm bonus (slightly lower than a PC) |
| Initiative | Same standard formula |
| Defense | Same standard formula |
| Move use | May use X moves per battle (X = Realm tier); after used up, only normal attacks |

**Companions spend no Qi** — instead each battle has a use-count limit. The GM need not track companion Qi recovery.

### 4.2 Companion AI

In solo play the GM controls both the PC and the companion. The companion's combat behavior follows this priority (the GM's discretion may override):

1. Protect the PC — if the PC is adjacent to the companion and Health (HP) < 50%, the companion uses a minor action to aid (PC Defense +2).
2. Execute the PC's orders — if the PC shouts an order in combat (e.g. "Block that hidden-weapon user!"), the companion executes it.
3. Attack autonomously — attack the nearest enemy not yet attacked by a companion.
4. Self-preserve — if own Health (HP) < 25%, retreat to safe distance.

**Companions never turn**: they will not betray, flee, or deliberately harm the PC.

### 4.3 Companion Plot Roles

| Companion | Realm | Role | Join Timing | Learnable Skills |
|-----------|-------|------|-------------|------------------|
| Liu Shengyan (Yaowang Valley physician) | Second Realm | Healing support | Ch.2 Luoyang · optional | Medicine Lv.3, Read-People Lv.2 |
| Duan Tianya · Simplified (Tianjian Alliance) | Third Realm | Frontline damage | Ch.4 Tianjian Alliance · optional | Swordsmanship Lv.4, Art-Lore Lv.3 |
| Han Tieyi · Simplified (Imperial Guard) | Fourth Realm | Tank | Ch.5 Northern Frontier · optional | Intimidate Lv.4, Staff/Spear Lv.3 |

> **Introduction pace**: recommend the PC carry at most 1 companion per journey segment. After Chapter Two the PC may choose to let Liu Shengyan stay (she returns to Yaowang Valley) and swap in Duan Tianya — this simulates the Jianghu feeling of "different travel companions in different periods."

### 4.4 Companion Loyalty and Interaction

| Behavior | Loyalty Change |
|----------|----------------|
| PC prioritizes protecting the companion in combat (rather than rushing attacks) | +2 |
| PC chooses to uphold the companion's interests in the plot | +3 |
| PC ignores the companion's opinion or safety | −3 |
| Companion is Near Death in combat and the PC abandons them | −10 (companion leaves permanently) |

Loyalty cap is 10. Loyalty affects:

- 5+: the companion will automatically spend a Hero Point to aid the PC when the PC is Near Death (spending their own health / resources).
- 8+: the companion will share their personal items (e.g. Liu Shengyan gifts Wound Medicine ×3).
- 10: the companion gets one "die-to-save-the-PC" chance in the main plot — the GM may arrange a dramatic rescue scene.

## V. Skill Exemptions

A solo PC cannot master all 19 skills. The following skills are exempted or have their Difficulty Class (DC) lowered in solo play:

| Exempted Skill | Solo Adjustment |
|----------------|-----------------|
| Jianghu Lore | Difficulty Class (DC) −2 (you've traveled far and heard many tales) |
| Tracking / Wilderness Survival | May "hire a guide" (spend silver, Difficulty Class (DC) −3) |
| Lightfoot / Climbing | High-Agility characters may self-learn — Agility ≥ 6 gives Difficulty Class (DC) −2 |
| Art-Lore | Comprehension ≥ 5 gives Difficulty Class (DC) −2 (innate martial talent) |

### GM Principle

> Do not block the plot just because the PC didn't take a skill. The core of a solo party is the **character-driven story** — if the PC is a swordsman, give them chances to solve problems with the sword; if the PC is a physician, let their medicine be the key to the plot, not optional.

## VI. Experience (XP) and Growth Adjustment

### 6.1 Experience (XP) Reward

| Action | Standard Experience (XP) | Solo Experience (XP) |
|--------|--------------------------|----------------------|
| Complete a session | 8–12 | 10–15 (no teammates to split XP) |
| Defeat an important enemy | 3–6 | 5–8 (solo bears all risk) |
| Resolve a major plot node | 5–10 | Unchanged |
| Perform chivalrous deeds | 1–3 | Unchanged |

### 6.2 Realm-Breakthrough Pace

A solo PC gains XP faster; recommend the GM lower the Plot-Lock standard by one tier:

- Standard Third-Realm Plot Lock: "an event that shifts the faction landscape" → Solo version: "a choice that affects the fate of at least one NPC."
- Standard Fifth-Realm Plot Lock: "participate in a grand event that changes the world's landscape" → Solo version: "play an irreplaceable key role in a major event."
