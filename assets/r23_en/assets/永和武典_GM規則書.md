# Jianghu Simulation

> Design: Jing Shiwen (Worldbuilding & Narrative Designer)
> Version: v0.1

---

## Design Rationale

If martial-arts cultivation lets players feel the "joy of growing stronger," then Jianghu simulation is what lets players feel the "immersion of being in the Jianghu." A good Jianghu simulation system must: make the players' actions produce visible ripple effects, keep the world running even when the players are absent, and provide the GM with low-cost story-generation tools. The core design philosophy of this system is **"low burden, high yield"** — the fewer dice, the better, but what is rolled must carry weight.

### Design Trade-offs

**Trade-off: Precise Scale vs. Fuzzy Tiers**

The Renown and Grudges/Favors systems face a classic design problem: use precise numbers (e.g., +3 favorability) or fuzzy descriptions (e.g., "friendly")?

This system chooses **fuzzy tiers**. Reasons:
- Precise numbers tempt players to "grind" — "I'm just 2 points short of the hidden quest, let me gift again."
- Fuzzy tiers preserve the GM's discretion — "he thinks well of you" has more narrative room than "favorability 67."
- The essence of wuxia is "the human heart is hard to fathom" — precise numbers break that mystery.
- But keep dice rolls at key nodes to ensure the system's playability.

## I. Renown System

### 1.1 The Five Renown Tiers

| Tier | Title | Threshold | NPC Attitude | Unlocks |
|------|-------|-----------|--------------|---------|
| **Tier 0** | Unknown Nobody | Initial | Indifferent — "Who are you? Never heard of you." | Basic free action, but no one seeks you out |
| **Tier 1** | Slightly Known | 3 Renown accumulated locally | Noticed — "Oh, that XXX lately." | May attend local Wulin gatherings; some NPCs initiate conversation |
| **Tier 2** | Local Hero | 8 Renown | Respected — "I've long heard your name." | May enter high-tier venues (Sword Treasury, Sword Debate Platform, etc.); may be recommended as a sect guest elder |
| **Tier 3** | Renowned in the Jianghu | 16 Renown | Awed — "The great xia has arrived!" | May participate in sect decisions; may convene/host Wulin gatherings; NPCs may volunteer to join you |
| **Tier 4** | A Generation's Grandmaster | 28 Renown | Looked up to — "If only he'd give me a little guidance..." | May found a sect / take disciples; decisive voice in major Wulin events |

### 1.2 Gaining Renown

Renown growth must come from **meaningful actions**, not merely defeating more enemies. The table below gives the GM a reference baseline:

| Action | Renown | Note |
|--------|--------|------|
| Defeat an equally-ranked famous master | +1 | Note: a "famous" master; killing nobodies grants no Renown |
| Win a martial tournament | +1~3 | By tournament scale. Local small tournament +1, world Wulin tournament win +3 |
| Complete a major chivalrous deed | +1~2 | e.g., execute a local tyrant, save an exterminated righteous sect |
| Create a recognized martial technique | +1~3 | Requires endorsement by at least one Grandmaster-level figure. Ordinary self-creation grants no bonus |
| Give an influential opinion at a Wulin gathering/arbitration | +1 | One-time; repeats ineffective |
| Publicly praised by a Grandmaster-level figure | +1 | — "Alliance Leader Lu says this kid's swordsmanship is decent." |
| Chivalrous acts widely sung | +1 | Requires at least one "widely spread" event |
| Obtain an important manual or treasure (publicly) | +1 | — "Heard he got a pre-dynasty manual in Wanxiang Grotto!" |
| Complete sect promotion (to elite / personal-disciple rank) | +1 | Once per tier |
| Granted an important post by master / sect leader | +1~2 | e.g., become a sect elder, appointed branch hall master |

### 1.3 Losing Renown

Renown can decrease, and decreasing is easier than gaining:

| Action | Renown Loss | Note |
|--------|-------------|------|
| Public defeat | -1~2 | Losing to someone weaker -2, to stronger -1. Can recover if you bounce back |
| Betrayal exposed | -3~5 | Breaking a major promise and made public |
| Discovered collusion with Demon Cult / evil faction | -2~4 | Depending on nature and degree |
| Indiscriminate killing | -1~3 | By number and severity. Even if the other side is "bad," excessive killing lowers Renown |
| Long-term seclusion / fading from Jianghu | -1/year | "The Jianghu won't wait for you." But a Grandmaster-level figure's retirement may become legend — GM's discretion |
| Public humiliation | -1 | e.g., visiting card returned, defeated in public but not killed |

### 1.4 The "Regional" Nature of Renown

An important mechanic: Renown has **regionality**.

- Renown gained in Jiangnan may be unknown in the Western Regions.
- Spread of your Renown between regions has **delay** — the GM decides how long a major event takes to spread across the world.
- Jianghu rumors (see Section IV below) can accelerate the regional spread of Renown.
- **World-level Renown** (Tier 3+) is not region-bound — your name has reached the ends of the earth.

## II. Grudges / Favors System

### 2.1 The Grudges / Favors Scale

The Grudges/Favors system tracks the relationship between the player (or group) and each NPC / faction. It uses fuzzy tiers.

| Grudges/Favors Tier | Description | NPC/Faction Behavior |
|---------------------|-------------|----------------------|
| **Sworn Enemy** | Irreconcilable | Will fight on sight, actively hunts you, drags your friends and family in |
| **Hostile** | You or me, not both | High chance of conflict on meeting, spreads unfavorable rumors, refuses any cooperation |
| **Antagonistic** | Dislikes you | Bad attitude, may trip you up behind your back, but won't publicly break face |
| **Cold** | Won't engage | Strictly business, won't help or harm you |
| **Neutral** | Indifferent | Default state |
| **Amiable** | Fond of you | Willing to give small help, speaks well of you |
| **Friendly** | Treats you as friend | Willing to solve your problems, supports you in difficulty |
| **Reverent** | Genuine respect from heart | Willing to risk life for you, may volunteer to follow you |
| **Benefactor** | Owes you a life | Will help with anything, even sacrifice self for you |

### 2.2 Generation and Change of Grudges / Favors

#### Increasing Favor (moving toward Friendly)

| Action | Effect | Note |
|--------|--------|------|
| Help the other solve a difficulty | ↑1~2 tiers | Saving a life can skip tiers directly |
| Reciprocal courtesy | ↑0~1 tier | Gifts, recommendations, speaking well publicly |
| Long-term fighting side by side | ↑1 tier | Requires at least three cooperative experiences |
| Cater to preferences | ↑0~1 tier | Need to know the other's preferences |
| Complete an assigned task | ↑1~2 tiers | By task difficulty |

#### Increasing Hatred (moving toward Antagonistic)

| Action | Effect | Note |
|--------|--------|------|
| Kill / severely wound the other's friends or family | ↓3~4 tiers | Almost permanent hostility |
| Seize the other's treasure / manual | ↓2~3 tiers | — "Return the manual!" |
| Public humiliation / make them lose face | ↓1~2 tiers | In the wuxia world, "face" is extremely important |
| Break promise / violate agreement | ↓1~2 tiers | Promised but didn't deliver |
| Befriend the other's enemy | ↓0~1 tier | Who you're with shows what you are |
| Block the other's path (competing for same goal) | ↓0~1 tier | e.g., both want the same manual |

### 2.3 Resolving Grudges / Favors

Grudges can be resolved — but the cost rises sharply with the depth of hatred:

| Current Relation | Resolution Difficulty | Possible Paths |
|------------------|----------------------|----------------|
| Antagonistic (mild) | Medium | Public apology, gift of valuable item, do the other a big favor |
| Hostile | Hard | Needs third-party mediation (usually a highly respected figure) + substantial compensation |
| Sworn Enemy | Nearly impossible | May require "life for life" level sacrifice. Or both face a stronger common enemy — "the enemy of my enemy can be a temporary friend" |

**An important design detail**: Grudges are not one-directional. You may harbor hatred for the Blood Moon Cult leader, but the other may not care about you at all ("Who are you?"). This makes the Grudges system closer to reality — hatred needs to be mutual to be story-worthy.

### 2.4 Chain Reactions of Grudges / Favors

Grudges don't exist in isolation — your enemy's friend may come to dislike you, your benefactor's enemy may come trouble you. The GM can trigger the following chain reactions:

| Trigger Condition | Chain Reaction |
|-------------------|----------------|
| You are seen as "Hostile" or above by someone | That person's friends may lower favor toward you by 1 tier; once hatred goes public, others may step up to "stand up for a friend" |
| You are seen as a "Benefactor" of some Grandmaster | The Grandmaster's disciples gain +1 tier favor toward you; certain Jianghu figures may try to reach that Grandmaster through you |
| You clash with a major faction | That faction's allies may close their doors to you; your Renown within that faction's sphere temporarily drops |
| You've had contact with Demon Cult figures | Regardless of your intent, some righteous figures will begin to doubt your stance |

## III. Jianghu Rumors & Event Generator

### 3.1 Design Philosophy

The "Jianghu Rumors" mechanic solves a core problem: **how does the GM keep the world alive between sessions?**

The mechanic is simple: after each session ends, the GM rolls a d10 to determine "what happened in the world," then at the start of the next session tells the players in the form of "Jianghu Rumors." This needs no complex simulation — just a sufficiently rich random table.

### 3.2 Jianghu Event Types

| d10 | Event Type | Event Description |
|-----|------------|-------------------|
| 1 | **Faction Conflict** | Friction/conflict between two factions. Roll 2d6 to determine the two sides (ref. faction numbers) and conflict level (minor friction → all-out war) |
| 2 | **Rare Treasure Emerges** | Rumors that a manual / divine weapon / spirit medicine is about to appear somewhere. Draws masters from all sides to contest |
| 3 | **Wulin Gathering** | Someone/faction announces a Wulin gathering (formal or informal). May need preliminaries, finals, multiple rounds |
| 4 | **Mysterious Figure Appears** | A master vanished for years suddenly reappears, or an unknown youngster astounds all with skill |
| 5 | **Sect Rise or Fall** | A sect announces a major event — new leader inaugurated, sect merged/split, traitor discovered, or facing extermination crisis |
| 6 | **Court Intervention** | Six Doors / Court issues a new policy on the Jianghu — weapons ban, wanted order, conscription order, or amnesty for a sect |
| 7 | **Jianghu Rumor** | An unverifiable but widely discussed rumor — perhaps about a hidden treasure's location, a Grandmaster's dark secret, or an ancient prophecy |
| 8 | **Grudge Escalation** | An existing grudge intensifies — enemies meet, alliance breaks, or a former benefactor suddenly appears demanding repayment |
| 9 | **Natural or Man-made Disaster** | A natural disaster (plague, flood, earthquake) or man-made (village massacre, poisoning) somewhere, creating many who need help and suspenseful clues |
| 10 | **Double Event** | Roll twice, ignore 10. Two events happen simultaneously, possibly related or not |

### 3.3 Event–Player Relevance

Not every event directly affects players. The GM rolls a d6 to determine the event's relevance to players:

| d6 | Relevance | Effect |
|----|-----------|--------|
| 1 | **Directly Drawn In** | Event happens in the players' town/region, or directly involves someone they know |
| 2-3 | **Indirectly Related** | Event involves a faction/NPC in the players' activity sphere; players may choose to intervene |
| 4-5 | **Jianghu Rumor** | Event happens far away, players only hear of it. Background info, also a potential future foreshadowing |
| 6 | **No Relation** | Event completely unrelated to current player activity — but the GM can use it as "gossip" to add world depth |

### 3.4 Example Event Detail Generation

Taking "Faction Conflict" as example, the GM can further detail:

**Step 1: Determine the two sides (2d6)**

| 2d6 | Faction A | Faction B |
|-----|-----------|-----------|
| 2-3 | A Tianjian Alliance branch hall | A Blood Moon Cult branch altar |
| 4-5 | A Sihai Alliance trading house | A local government office |
| 6-7 | A Wulin rogue cultivator | A Wangchuan Tower assassin |
| 8-9 | A Qianji Pavilion intelligence station | The sect under investigation |
| 10-11 | Two local minor sects | Neither is a major faction |
| 12 | A combination you never expected | GM improvises |

**Step 2: Determine conflict level (d6)**

| d6 | Level | Manifestation |
|----|-------|---------------|
| 1-2 | Minor Friction | Verbal conflict, small-scale skirmish, someone injured but none dead |
| 3-4 | Moderate Conflict | Deaths and injuries, property loss, both sides gathering helpers |
| 5 | Escalation | One side suffers heavy losses, situation escalating, may evolve into all-out war |
| 6 | Third Party Intervenes | A third party's shadow appears in the conflict — someone may be sowing discord |

### 3.5 Event Chaining

Any event may produce follow-ups. The GM can reference the consequences of the previous session's events in the next session's "Jianghu Rumors":

- If the faction conflict from last time is unresolved, this time it may escalate or reconcile.
- An event you chose to intervene in may bring reward (or revenge) next session.
- An event you chose to ignore may reappear next session in more severe form.

## IV. Adventure (Qiyu) Mechanic

### 4.1 Design Philosophy

Adventure is a core element of wuxia culture — Zhang Wuji's Nine Yang Manual, Yang Guo's Black Iron Swordplay, Xuzhu receiving Wuyazi's transmitted power — these are all "Adventures." This system's Adventure mechanic follows three principles:

- **Adventure is not a free lunch**: every Adventure has a cost or risk.
- **Adventure is not manna from heaven**: Adventure needs trigger conditions, related to the player's actions and choices.
- **Adventure won't break balance**: frequency is controlled via "one Adventure period" and "cooldown" mechanics.

### 4.2 Adventure Trigger Conditions

Adventures should not be arranged at the GM's whim, but tied to the player's **actions** and **scenes**:

| Trigger Type | Specific Condition | Adventure Trigger Chance |
|--------------|-------------------|--------------------------|
| **Exploration Discovery** | Actively explore unfamiliar areas (deep mountains, ruins, antiquities) | After exploring 3+ areas, next time 30% chance to trigger |
| **Good-Deed Repay** | Help others without expecting return | After 3 good deeds, next time helping an elder / hermit, 40% chance they are actually a hidden master |
| **Survive Desperate Straits** | Survive while Dying / in extreme peril | Each time, 20% chance to find a predecessor's remaining martial legacy nearby |
| **Gathering Fortune** | Attend large Wulin events (tournament, sword debate, Wulin alliance-leader election) | Each event 15% chance to meet a strange elder / mysterious master willing to give pointers |
| **Sect Lineage** | Your sect has a hidden secret legacy | When active at sect-related locations, 25% chance to trigger |

**Key Mechanic**: Players **can actively pursue Adventures** (e.g., knowingly enter dangerous deep mountains to explore), but can't guarantee a trigger — this is a gamble between "risk" and "reward."

### 4.3 Adventure Types & Rewards

| d10 | Adventure Type | Description | Possible Reward |
|-----|---------------|-------------|-----------------|
| 1 | **Hermit Transmission** | Meet a hidden master willing to teach you a move or two | Learn or improve a martial technique |
| 2 | **Predecessor's Relic** | Discover a manual / cultivation notes left by a past master | Gain a manual (usually incomplete — need to collect all to fully master) |
| 3 | **Heaven-Sent Material** | Discover a rare spirit medicine or exotic-beast material | Gain materials to refine pills, or directly ingest for Qi growth |
| 4 | **Insight Epiphany** | Suddenly gain new understanding of martial arts in a specific scene | An existing martial technique "mutates" — derives new usage on its base |
| 5 | **Fated Connection** | Meet an NPC mysteriously linked to your background / lineage | Open an exclusive personal story line |
| 6 | **Spirit Weapon Acknowledges Master** | In an ancient ruin find a sentient weapon that seems to resonate with you | Gain a growable special weapon |
| 7 | **Exotic Legacy** | Contact a martial system from the Western Regions / overseas / other regions | Learn a "cross-border" martial technique outside your sect's scope |
| 8 | **Predecessor's Memory** | Contact the residual "martial intent" of a deceased master | Temporarily gain bonus in some martial art |
| 9 | **Heaven-Earth Anomaly** | Witness a rare natural phenomenon (meteor shower, aurora, etc.) and comprehend martial principle from it | Gain enlightenment — bonus when next creating a martial technique |
| 10 | **Double Adventure** | Roll twice (ignore 10 and duplicates) | But the two Adventures may conflict — "you can't have both fish and bear's paw" |

### 4.4 Adventure Cost / Limits

**Every Adventure has a cost** — this is the core balance mechanic of the system:

| Adventure Type | Possible Cost / Choice |
|---------------|------------------------|
| Hermit Transmission | The hermit may ask you to complete a task (not necessarily good); or the transmission method is extremely painful |
| Predecessor's Relic | The manual is incomplete, need to risk collecting other parts; or the manual is found by many, you must seize it |
| Heaven-Sent Material | The spirit medicine has a powerful guardian beast; or multiple factions contest it |
| Insight Epiphany | During epiphany you are defenseless; if interrupted may fall into Qi Deviation |
| Fated Connection | This story line may drag you into a conflict not of your choosing |
| Spirit Weapon Acknowledges Master | The weapon may have its own "will," not always cooperating; or attracts those coveting it |
| Exotic Legacy | Learning exotic martial arts may be seen by your sect as "betraying orthodoxy" |

### 4.5 Adventure Frequency Control

| Control Mechanism | Note |
|-------------------|------|
| **Cooldown** | After a player gains an Adventure in one session, at least one full session must pass before triggering again (even if conditions met) |
| **Party Cap** | In a single session, at most one Adventure triggers (shared by all players) — avoid "this whole session is just triggering Adventures" |
| **Difficulty Step-up** | The higher a character's Renown, the higher the threshold to meet Adventure triggers — a nobody may meet a master just by helping an elder, but a Grandmaster-level character must do something more unusual |
| **GM Veto** | All the above trigger chances are reference values. The GM always has the right to adjust or skip based on story needs |

## V. GM Running Advice

### 5.1 Session Interludes

After each session, the GM should do three things:

1. **Update Renown**: adjust each player's Renown tier based on this session's actions.
2. **Roll Jianghu Event**: roll a d10 to determine news, prepare to broadcast at next session's start.
3. **Record Grudges/Favors**: record NPC relationships established this session into the Grudges table.

### 5.2 Ripple of Player Actions

Encourage the GM to make players' choices produce **lasting impact**:

- If you saved a flower-selling girl in session one, she may reappear in an unexpected way in session five — "Great xia, my mother is with the Six Doors..."
- If you defeated an aristocrat's child in some tournament, his family may mark you "unwelcome" ever since.
- If you helped a sect through a crisis, that sect becomes your long-term ally — but when the ally has trouble, they'll come to you for help too.

### 5.3 Don't Let Systems Bind the Narrative

All tables and numbers in this system are **reference and guidance**, not rules that must be strictly followed. If a session's plot development needs a specific Grudges change or Jianghu event, just let it happen — don't limit the narrative because "per the table we should have rolled X."

### 1.2 Structure of Each Session (2–4 hours)

Whether it's a one-shot or one meeting of a long campaign, there is a common rhythm pattern:

| Phase | Time Share | Content | What the GM Does |
|-------|-----------|---------|------------------|
| **Opening** | 10% | Broadcast Jianghu rumors, recap last session, warm-up | Light the mood with one or two interesting news items; let players recall what they did last time |
| **Exploration / Social** | 30% | Free activity, interact with NPCs, investigate clues, prepare actions | Offer multiple directions to choose; give 2-3 interactable elements in each scene |
| **Encounter / Conflict** | 40% | Combat, chase, negotiation, puzzle — any resource-consuming challenge | Control difficulty (see GM Toolbox); ensure every player has a sense of participation |
| **Climax** | 15% | The core conflict of this session erupts or resolves | The part you prepared most carefully this session. Put in effort to make it "blaze." |
| **Wrap-up** | 5% | Clean the battlefield, hand out rewards, sow seeds for next time | Give players a clear sense of "ending"; but leave one or two small hooks to draw players back next week |

### 1.3 Pacing Control Techniques

- **"Three fast, one slow"**: after three fast-paced scenes (exploration, small combat, chasing clues), give a slow-paced scene (tea with an NPC, rest at the inn, alone with thoughts). Alternating fast and slow prevents player fatigue.
- **"Information dividend"**: each session give players at least one "new piece of information" — a secret about the world, a truth about an NPC, a new clue about the main plot. Information is the drive that brings players back.
- **"Resource roller-coaster"**: within each session, let the players' resources (Qi, medicine, favors) go through a "abundant → consumed → tense → recovered" cycle. If they stay abundant the whole time, there's no tension.

### 2.4 NPC Quick-Build Example

**Scene**: Players meet a person of unknown origin at a remote mountain inn.

The GM doesn't want to flip through books. Roll d20 surname, d20 given name, d10 appearance, d10 martial, d10 motive, d10 catchphrase:

- Surname: 7 = Zheng
- Given name: 15 = Shuanghua
- Appearance: 3 = delicate features, sword scar on right cheek
- Martial: 8 = strange movement, slippery
- Motive: 6 = wants to find a specific manual
- Catchphrase: 8 = "I owe you a favor."

**Result**: Zheng Shuanghua, delicate features but a sword scar on the right cheek. Movement strange as a loach, no one can catch him. He isn't at this remote inn by chance — he's waiting for someone who knows the whereabouts of a certain manual. If you help him ask around, he'll say: "I owe you a favor." — though you'll soon learn his owed favors are scattered all across the land.

## III. Wuxia Narrative Advice

### 3.1 Building Wuxia Atmosphere

Wuxia atmosphere isn't piled up by "description" — it's built on **rhythm** and **ritual**.

#### Atmosphere of a Duel

A good duel isn't "A hit B, lost 5 HP." It should be a drama with exposition, development, turn, and conclusion:

1. **Standoff** (30 sec): ten paces apart, eyes lock. Describe the setting — "Under the moon, in a bamboo grove, wind rustles the leaves; their robes flutter gently."
2. **Probing** (first round): the one who strikes first is probing — "This sword used only three-tenths power; he wants to see how the opponent receives it."
3. **Clash** (middle rounds): both begin to show their strengths. Moves are no longer "normal attacks" but named martial techniques — "She suddenly shifts, the Qingshuang Sword flicking up from below, drawing a cold arc in the air — this is the Qingshuang Sect's 'Snow Mud Wild Goose Tracks'!"
4. **Turn**: one side begins to gain the upper hand / show an opening — "He already has several sword wounds, but his eyes burn brighter. He's waiting for a moment."
5. **Decisive** : the crucial instant — "A flash of blade, no one saw what happened. Only when the wind passed did one of them slowly sit, looking down at the sword mark on his chest."
6. **Lingering** (after the outcome): winner and loser decided, but the story isn't over — "The winner didn't smile, the loser didn't cry. Only the sound of wind remained in the bamboo grove."

#### Atmosphere of a Sword Debate

A sword debate differs from a duel — its focus is "discussing the Way through martial arts":

- In a sword debate, the two exchange **martial principle**, not mere victory — "Your 'Whirling Wind Caresses the Willow' is beautiful, but when you gather power your left shoulder dips slightly, giving me an opening to counter."
- A good sword-debate scene should let the listeners (other players and NPCs) **all learn something**.
- After a sword debate, the loser often feels **respect** for the winner — this too is a kind of socializing.

#### Storming the Dragon's Lair Alone

"Storming the dragon's lair alone" is a classic wuxia scene. How to make it feel real:

- Before entering: full ritual and oppressive preparation — "From afar, that tower looks like a black finger pointing at the night sky. You know that once you go in, you may never come out. But you take a deep breath and take the first step."
- The storming: enemies layer by layer, but don't make it "monster rooms" — "Third floor, the guards already know you're coming. They wait in silence; in the dark you see only rows of cold glints from hidden weapons."
- If companions watch / support from outside, switch perspectives back and forth to build tension.

#### Moonlit Duel

For some reason, all the best wuxia duels happen under the moon:

- The environment is a character — "Moonlight spills on the river, shimmering. The two long shadows intertwine on the water."
- Sound matters — "Apart from the river's murmur, only the soft whistle of blade cutting wind."
- A moonlit duel needs no many onlookers — it's between two people.

### 3.2 Common Wuxia Plot Tropes & Variants

Classic tropes aren't bad — players like the familiar feel, and tropes give you a structure to rely on. The key is **adding variants within the trope**.

| Classic Trope | Description | Variant |
|---------------|-------------|--------|
| **Tournament for Marriage** | A Wulin elder sets a martial tournament for his daughter; winner may marry her | Variant: the daughter doesn't want to marry, she's looking for someone to help her escape; or it's a trap — all contestants will be slaughtered |
| **Sect Extermination Revenge** | A character's sect / family is wiped out; the sole survivor walks the path of revenge | Variant: the revenge target isn't the true culprit — the real culprit is among their own; or the extermination has hidden cause — the sect did do something unforgivable |
| **Manual Contest** | A legendary manual surfaces; factions contest for it | Variant: the manual is fake — it's a setup; or the manual is real but what it teaches is wrong — deliberate misdirection |
| **Undercover in the Jianghu** | A player (or important NPC) is sent to infiltrate the opponent | Variant: during infiltration discovers the opponent's ideals are actually more correct than ours; or after discovery the opponent gives the infiltrator a choice — "Join us" |
| **Sect Leader Struggle** | Old leader about to step down; multiple heirs scheme in the shadows | Variant: the old leader doesn't want to step down — he only pretends to see who's ambitious; or the leader's seat itself is a curse |
| **Jianghu Cataclysm** | A super-villain / Demon Cult tries to dominate the Wulin; the righteous join to resist | Variant: part of what the villain says is right — the Wulin does have problems; or the righteous alliance itself is full of exploitation and betrayal |

### 3.3 Let Every Player Have a Spotlight Moment

This is one of the GM's most important duties. The following techniques help ensure everyone has a shining moment:

1. **Rotate focus**: every 2-3 sessions, make a different character the "core of this session." Not that others are unimportant, but this session's plot / enemy / puzzle suits a certain character better.
2. **Designed "soft threshold"**: some encounters can only be solved by a character with a specific skill / martial art — "This door has a mechanism; only someone from Qianji Pavilion can open it." This isn't to limit others, but to give a specific character a solo stage.
3. **Let failure shine too**: you don't always have to let players win. Sometimes the most beautiful moment comes from failure — "You didn't catch that sword, but you smile at your companion and say — 'Run! I'll hold them!'"
4. **NPCs treat everyone differently**: certain NPCs may especially respect (or look down on) you because of your background / martial art / fame — making each player's social experience different.
5. **Emotional outlet**: give each character a chance to "speak from the heart" — it can be drinking under the moon, a fireside night talk, or silent facing after escaping death.

## IV. GM Toolbox

### 4.0 Encounter Difficulty Design

When designing combat encounters, use the following "Threat Value" to quickly estimate difficulty. Each PC contributes by Realm; enemies likewise. Compare the two sides to judge.

**PC Threat Value**:

| PC Realm | Threat Value |
|----------|--------------|
| First Realm | 1 |
| Second Realm | 2 |
| Third Realm | 3 |
| Fourth Realm | 5 |
| Fifth Realm | 8 |
| Sixth Realm | 13 |

**Enemy Threat Value**: calculated equal by Realm. Mook level (ordinary people with no martial art) counts as 0.5.

**Difficulty Judgment**:

| Total Enemy Threat / Total PC Threat | Difficulty |
|--------------------------------------|-----------|
| < 0.5 | Easy — almost no resource cost |
| 0.5–1 | Normal — needs serious response but high win rate |
| 1–1.5 | Challenging — someone may be wounded, strategy needed |
| 1.5–2 | Dangerous — someone may be Dying, full-team coordination needed |
| > 2 | Lethal — not advised to clash head-on; provide stealth / negotiation / cunning alternative routes |

**Example**: Four First-Realm PCs (threat 4) encounter:
- 2 First-Realm enemies + 4 mooks → threat 2×1+4×0.5=4 → ratio 1, **Normal** difficulty
- 1 Third-Realm elite + 3 First-Realm disciples → threat 3+3=6 → ratio 1.5, **Challenging**
- 1 Fourth-Realm master → threat 5 → ratio 1.25, near Challenging — GM should consider offering a weakness or environmental exploit

> **Taboo**: don't treat this formula as iron law. A clever player may defeat alone an enemy with twice the threat value; an overconfident player may be downed in one move by a same-Realm enemy. Threat value is a starting point; tactics, environment, and information decide the ending.

### 4.1 Quick Difficulty Adjustment

If during combat the difficulty proves unsuitable, adjust in real time with the following methods:

| Situation | Adjustment |
|-----------|------------|
| Combat too easy | ① Reinforcements arrive ("They have accomplices!") ② Enemy uses a hidden move — a ultimate not shown before ③ Environment worsens (poisoning, ground rupture, building collapse) |
| Combat too hard | ① Enemy's Qi is also spent — "His moves are fierce, but his breathing is clearly ragged" ② Environment favors you — "The collapse behind you stopped more enemies from coming" ③ A turn appears — another faction intervenes, the enemy's enemy enters |
| A player keeps doing nothing | ① Give a sub-goal only he can solve — "That hidden-weapon master is on the roof; need someone with good Lightfoot to deal with it" ② NPC names a challenge — "That Taoist over there, heard your Taiji Fist is quite something?" |
| A player keeps crushing | ① Enemy adjusts strategy — ranged focus fire, poison, entrap rather than frontal clash ② Distraction — "You're doing fine here, but you hear a companion's cry for help" |

### 4.2 Balance Rules for Self-Created Martial Arts

When a player wants to create a martial art (the ultimate goal of martial cultivation), follow these balance principles:

| Principle | Note |
|-----------|------|
| **Cost First** | A self-created martial art is 10-20% stronger than same-tier arts, but cultivation cost is also 10-20% higher (needs more Qi, longer cultivation, or sacrifice proficiency of other arts) |
| **Has Boundaries** | A self-created martial art can't be a "universal art" — it must have something it cannot do. A sword that cuts everything is too boring. |
| **Needs Validation** | A self-created martial art must succeed in real combat to "set." First use may have side effects or uncontrollable factors. |
| **Only One Ultimate** | Each character may own at most one self-created ultimate (highest-tier self-created martial art). This is a Jianghu iron law — you can't be an all-powerful god. |
| **Plot Foreshadowing** | A self-created martial art needs plot groundwork — not suddenly "I created a move." The GM should ask the player to describe: what was the inspiration for this move? Where / in what situation did you have the epiphany? How many times did you experiment before success? |

### 4.3 Ruling Suggestions for Common Questions

| Question | Ruling Suggestion |
|----------|-------------------|
| Player wants to use a martial art for an unconventional purpose (e.g., use Lightfoot to peek at someone's letter) | Allow — roll Lightfoot check. But remember: Lightfoot isn't invisibility; fast but can't fully erase traces of existence. |
| Player asks "can I learn two sects' martial arts at once" | Depends on the specific sect. Some sects have the precept "may not cultivate other sects' arts" (violators expelled + martial arts abolished). Some are looser. But inner-art manuals usually can't be mixed — easy to fall into Qi Deviation. |
| Two players both want the same treasure / manual | Don't allocate directly. Let it be resolved in play — they can duel, negotiate, concede for a favor. If it gets too stiff, introduce a third party — "This manual is my master's, stop fighting over it." |
| Player wants to kill an NPC, but another player objects | Stop the game, hold a brief out-of-character discussion. Clarify both sides' positions. If no agreement — "This may be a turning point in your team's relationship." Let them make their own choice; you only describe the consequences. |
| A player says "my character isn't interested in the current plot" | Take it seriously. ① Ask privately why — maybe his character isn't given enough room to shine ② Add a side plot for him without breaking the main line ③ If truly unfit — allow him to create a new character to join. |
| A player's character "doesn't play by the book" — e.g., suddenly attacks the alliance leader at a Wulin gathering | ① Confirm first — "Are you sure you want to do this?" ② If sure — let the consequences be real. All NPCs react reasonably. This isn't punishment, but making players feel their choices truly matter. ③ The resulting abrupt plot shift may become the most unforgettable moment of the whole session. |
| Mid-combat, a player wants to surrender | Depends on who the opponent is. Righteous NPCs usually accept surrender and tie you up. Evil-path NPCs may accept but with conditions ("A life for a hand, which hand do you choose?"). Beasts and armies in melee usually don't accept surrender. |

### 4.4 Final Advice for New GMs

1. **Prepare scenes, not plots**: you know "players will come to this mountain," but don't assume "they'll enter by the front gate."
2. **Failure is also story**: TRPG isn't a video game — no need to "retry if you lose." Sometimes the most brilliant scene in a session comes from total failure and a embarrassed retreat.
3. **"Yes, and"**: when a player proposes a reasonable creative idea, don't say "no, the rules don't say that," but "yes, and you can try — though there's risk." This is the core spirit of TRPG.
4. **Trust your intuition**: if you feel an NPC should be strong, he's strong. Not everything needs to strictly follow system rules — you are the GM, your ruling is above all rules.
5. **Enjoy**: your players come to play because they want to play with you. Relax, trust yourself; you tell stories better than you think.

## Reading Notes

This chapter provides **12 NPC character cards**, grouped by Jianghu faction, covering the Second to Fifth Realms. Each card is designed for quick GM call-up, containing combat data and narrative hooks.

**Data Format** (NPCs are more concise than PCs):

- **One-line persona**: quickly grasp the character's temper

- **Derived values**: Health Points (HP) / Qi / Initiative / Evasion / Defense
- **Martial arts**: 2–4 moves, noting Qi cost, hit bonus, damage, and key effects
- **Combat style**: how the GM manipulates this NPC in combat
- **Plot hooks**: how PCs may intersect with this NPC
- **Attitude**: default posture toward PCs

**Derived Value Formulas** (identical to PCs):

| Derived Value | Formula |
|---------------|--------|
| Health Points (HP) | Constitution × 5 + Realm bonus (+0/+5/+10/+15/+20/+25) |

| Initiative | Agility + Willpower |
| Evasion | Agility modifier + Lightfoot tier |

# Jianghu Chronicle

> Compiled by: Baixiaosheng
> Last update: Autumn, Yonghe Year 123
> Note: This chronicle is revised irregularly; each event is ordered by year and month. Of what is recorded, eight or nine in ten I saw with my own eyes or heard with my own ears; the rest, though from secondhand sources, were verified from many sides. If there are errors, this old man's eyes are dim — reader, judge for yourself.

## Baixiaosheng's Foreword

This old man Baixiaosheng, in the Jianghu I count as a name — though not a good one. An intelligence peddler, a news seller; clients call me "Mr. Bai," behind my back not a few call me "old fox." Can't be helped — I've walked this Jianghu over forty years, and stepped down from Qianji Pavilion's Tianji Hall twenty years ago. Seen heroes and good men, also seen human faces with beast hearts. I record these things not to leave some eternal masterpiece — just afraid of forgetting. Old age, poor memory, but the Jianghu's affairs deserve a keeper.

This chronicle only records, rarely comments — at least I try. But sometimes the brush strays as I write; blame me not, who told some people's deeds to be so absurd.

Enough, the tea's gone cold. Open it.

—— Baixiaosheng, Autumn Yonghe 123, at a teahouse in Luoyang, second-floor window seat

## I. Major Jianghu Events

### 1. Tianjian Alliance Leader Lu Chenyuan Announces Abdication — Spring, Yonghe 120

Lu Chenyuan, Tianjian Alliance Leader past sixty, suddenly announced publicly on the Sword Debate Platform: in three years, he will abdicate the leader's seat. The news out, the five great sword sects exploded.

Among the five, Lieyang Sword Master Duan Tianya is the most openly active. He's just over thirty, fierce and overbearing sword art, highest renown among the Tianjian young-turks. Rumor: he's privately recruited disciples from the Jinglei and Duanshui sects, preparing a "public endorsement" at abdication. Qingshuang Sword Master Liu Rushi — the five sects' only female leader — stays calm. Asked her stance, she only said "listen to the Leader." As for the Luomei Sect's old leader, eighty years old, probably doesn't care who leads, only whether his plum blossoms bloom next year.

Lu Chenyuan himself? Per reliable info, he's recently summoned Abbot Huijue frequently, seemingly seeking a "reason to let go." A Sword Sage who roamed the Jianghu thirty years, in the end the hardest thing isn't wielding the sword, but setting it down.

—— Baixiaosheng's note: Abdication done well is abdication; done poorly is infighting. Those placing bets, don't bet wrong.

---

### 2. Fall of Black Wind Stronghold — Late Autumn, Yonghe 121

Thirty li outside Luoyang, Black Wind Stronghold had entrenched for years; its chief "One-Eyed Dragon" Han Bao, relying on a Demon-Cult fragment art of unknown origin, plundered all around. Six Doors sent men several times but couldn't take it — not that they couldn't win, but the stronghold's terrain is too perilous; to assault you first lose half your men.

And the result? Taken by a bunch of green youngsters.

This old man inquired afterward and reconstructed the course: a group of young disciples from different sects — Tianjian Alliance, Taixu Palace, Wanfo Temple, Yaowang Valley, Tang Sect — by fate gathered, first joined to repel a Demon Cult outer raid, then drove straight into Black Wind Stronghold. Rumor: in that battle, chief Han Bao fought till blood ran from nose and mouth — signs of demonic-art backlash. After the stronghold fell, a page of Demon-Cult fragment was found, its end reading "remaining seven pages stored at Shen's Pawnshop in Luoyang city."

This group made their name in one battle. This old man asked after their names: Liu Rufeng (Sword Sect), Qingxu (Qi Sect), Tieshan (Vajra Sect), Su Xiaomei (Poison Sect), Yan Feiyun (Shadow Sect), Bao Xiaoyi (Mechanism Sect). Names noted first — to walk out of Black Wind Stronghold alive, they deserve a line from this old man. As for whether they'll make a name later, that's their own fortune.

—— Baixiaosheng's note: These young whelps had decent luck. Han Bao practiced demonic art halfway, effectively stacking negative status on himself. But the Jianghu is just so — luck is part of strength.

---

### 3. Luoyang Huagong Powder Turmoil — Spring, Yonghe 122

In the chill of early spring, a strange affair appeared in Luoyang: someone secretly buying up large amounts of Dragon-Saliva Grass, Cold-Stone Gall, Seven-Star Begonia. These three herbs alone are ordinary bruise medicines; together — they're the main ingredients of "Huagong Powder."

Sihai Alliance Chief Steward Shen Wanjin noticed first. The fat man's other skills are mediocre, but on matters of money he's sharper than a rat smelling rice. He sent men to investigate, found at least three buyers scrambling for the herbs: several merchants from the Western Regions acting furtively; a mysterious guest from Bashu spending lavishly; and a Luoyang local, surnamed Chu named Huaisha, unattached to any sect, yet buying the most aggressively.

What followed got lively. Six Doors Luoyang Branch chief Tie Jingxin led a team personally, intercepting the Western Regions merchants in Camel Lane west of the city — that fight to the end, my spy reported a gray shadow using "Yellow Springs Step" swept across the roof in the melee, taking a sealed letter. Yellow Springs Step is Wangchuan Tower's signature Lightfoot; this dragged Wangchuan Tower in too.

And that Chu Huaisha, investigation showed, is no villain — he bought the herbs to refine "Seal-Breaking Powder," preparing to enter Wanxiang Grotto to save his sister. The man holds several ancient maps, drawn precisely at Wanxiang Grotto's location. The Bashu buyer? A Qianji Pavilion token was found on him — but not of Gongshu Xuan's line. Someone inside Qianji Pavilion is operating privately.

—— Baixiaosheng's note: Huagong Powder isn't some remarkable poison, but someone stockpiling it in bulk signals a big move. Were I of the Tianjian Alliance, I'd be checking my own well right now.

---

### 4. Wanxiang Grotto Opens — Summer, Yonghe 122

This deserves a full write-up. The entrance to the predecessor dynasty's martial treasury "Wanxiang Grotto" was finally deciphered by Qianji Pavilion's Gongshu Xuan.

The entrance is behind Emei in Bashu. Per those who entered the outer ring: on the full-moon night, a stone cave mid-mountain emits blue light; at the cave mouth an ancient stele reads "All phenomena return to origin; the heart is the key." On the stele a palm print — not opened by Qi alone, but by the touch of one who "sincerely wishes to enter." This old man shook his head: these days, sincere people are rarer than masters.

Three months later, Gongshu Xuan issued a "Qianji Edict" at Qianji Pavilion headquarters, calling all able and strange folk under heaven to join the endeavor. My spy reported those present each with hidden agendas — Tianjian Alliance's Duan Tianya wanted the "Sword-Intent Stone," Yaowang Valley's Mo Ran wanted "Pure-Heart Lotus" to detox Chu Huaisha's sister, Chu Huaisha himself was there, and a Blood Moon Cult spy disguised as a rogue cultivator.

The three sides faced off outside the grotto, nearly fighting. Later three key fragments barely fit, the grotto's main door opened. Inside four trials: Pinnacle of Sword, Source of Qi, Life and Death, Mirror of Heart. To pass all gains ancient martial-vein inheritance. That martial vein — per Taixu Palace's Ziyang telling me afterward — is an embodiment of heaven-earth primordial qi, nourishing all life for a thousand li. Take too much, Emei Mountain collapses.

—— Baixiaosheng's note: Gongshu Xuan is an interesting man. He spent ten years cracking Wanxiang Grotto's map, yet cares little for the treasures inside. What he truly wants to study is the ancient seals themselves — the man's a madman, but a genius madman. My old Qianji Pavilion hand told me the price of deciphering the map was his pair of eyes. Of course that's rumor — I see his eyes fine, though when looking at people he always seems solving an equation.

---

### 5. Tianjian Alliance Gathering and Blood Moon Cult Raid — Autumn, Yonghe 122

Not long after Wanxiang Grotto closed, the Tianjian Alliance convened at the Sword Debate Platform its largest alliance meeting in years. Five sect sword masters gathered, over a thousand spectators — this old man went too, sitting in the spectator stand three terraces out, cracking sunflower seeds watching the show.

One topic only: Duan Tianya publicly challenged Lu Chenyuan's authority.

Duan Tianya stood center of the Sword Debate Platform, Lieyang Sword drawn, pointing at the leader's seat: "Defending the status quo is waiting to die. Northern barbarians eye us, Blood Moon Cult smolders; you still want to shut the door and ignore the world?" Lu Chenyuan sat in the leader's chair, silent. That day's sun was fierce, lighting Lu Chenyuan's white hair glaring.

Before the meeting ended, the Blood Moon Cult came. Grand Protector Li Canyang led the Blood-Robe Guard descending from the sky, straight at the Sword Debate Platform. Front court, central hall, roof — battle opened simultaneously. The Blood Moon Cult's goal was clear: exploit the Tianjian Alliance's infighting to slay Lu Chenyuan. Li Canyang is a battle maniac; his smile can scare children to tears. That battle, Tianjian Alliance disciples suffered, but in the end the Blood Moon Cult was repelled, Li Canyang heavily wounded — heard someone found on him a personal letter from Ye Xiuluo, its end reading the three characters "Plan B."

These three characters, to this day no one knows the exact meaning.

—— Baixiaosheng's note: Duan Tianya, Duan Tianya. Your biggest problem isn't sword art not good enough, it's you forgot — a leader who draws his sword on his own before the enemy is worse than no leader. That said, Ye Xiuluo's strike-while-fire-is-hot was beautifully done. This man deserves more writing.

### 6. Ye Xiuluo Appears, Plan B Exposed — Deep Autumn, Yonghe 122

Blood Moon Cult Master Ye Xiuluo — this man vanished ten years — finally met an intermediary at an abandoned earth-god shrine outside Luoyang. My first reaction on learning this: he actually came in the open.

Per that intermediary later revealing (name withheld — this old man was paid to keep it secret), Ye Xiuluo's "Plan B" had three layers. The first is misdirection: use Huagong Powder to cripple the Tianjian Alliance's disciples below Fourth Realm. The second is surface action: he personally infiltrates the Tianjian Alliance, duels Lu Chenyuan. The third is the true aim — negotiation. The Blood Moon Cult retreats to the Western Regions, opens trade routes; in exchange, the righteous tacitly accept the Blood Moon Cult's limited presence in the Western Regions.

The more shocking truth: Ye Xiuluo must break through the final layer of the "Blood Moon Demon Art." If he can't, he'll die of demonic-art backlash within a year. He needs a branch of the ancient martial vein to cultivate. In other words, everything he did — Huagong Powder, the raid, even "Plan B" — was to survive.

This old man can curse him a demon, but can't curse him mad. A madman wouldn't design a three-layer nested strategy.

—— Baixiaosheng's note: How complex a thing a person will do to survive, Ye Xiuluo is the best example. But this old man has a word he must spit: you hold Huagong Powder yet say you want to negotiate — that's the same as raising a blade and saying "let's talk," what do you say?

### 7. Blood Moon Cult and Tianjian Alliance Decisive Battle — Early Yonghe 123

The largest righteous-evil war in thirty years. The battlefield at Tianjian Alliance headquarters, from the Sword Debate Platform to the Sword Treasury, from the Sword Treasury to the sword-peak summit.

Lu Chenyuan personally took Ye Xiuluo's challenge. The two standing at the Jianghu's very top exchanged no fewer than three hundred moves on the rooftop. Witnesses said Lu Chenyuan's sword was blue-white — the color of the Heaven Sword Art; Ye Xiuluo's sword dark red — the mark of the Blood Moon Demon Art. Fighting on, tiles shattered, the two kept crossing moves stepping on rafters.

In the end Ye Xiuluo retreated. Not beaten away — he sheathed his sword himself, said "not the time today," then vanished in a flash of Blood Shadow Step. Lu Chenyuan stood in place, sword hanging to the ground, hand trembling. Not fear — exhaustion.

Three days after the great battle, Lu Chenyuan convened the five sword sects again. This time he said three things: one, abdication date unchanged; two, Duan Tianya performed excellently in the defense, merit offsetting fault, no longer pursued for the public challenge; three, the Tianjian Alliance immediately forms the "Yi Wu Army," to march north against the barbarians.

Duan Tianya stood below the platform, his expression this old man couldn't quite read — like a sigh of relief, yet like swallowing a breath. Qingshuang Sword Master Liu Rushi stood half a step behind Lu Chenyuan, from start to finish said not a word, but this old man noted a detail — her sword stayed in hand, not at her waist. Of all present, only she from the beginning was ready to strike at any moment.
—— Baixiaosheng's note: After this fight, the Tianjian Alliance's vitality was greatly wounded. But what was hurt was the "qi," not the "bone." Though Lu Chenyuan is about to step down, before leaving he did one right thing — forced the two factions to stand on the same side before the same enemy. Brilliant, truly brilliant.

---

### 8. Northern Frontier Decisive Battle — Spring-Summer Border, Yonghe 123

The barbarians broke through Yanmen Pass. A hundred thousand iron cavalry poured into the Northern Frontier like a bursting dike. The court collapsed on all lines. The Tianjian Alliance's Yi Wu Army — together with various Jianghu heroes who marched north on their own — became the final resistance.

This old man is old, didn't go to the front. But my intelligence net has three lines in the Northern Frontier, and every message back was much the same: tragic, but not hopeless.

A few things on the battlefield deserve note. First: after his fall at the Tianjian gathering, Duan Tianya became more desperate than anyone. Rumor: he alone held the Yanyun Pass gate, Lieyang Sword drawn till Qi exhausted, cut down no fewer than twenty barbarian cavalry; when finally dragged from the heap of horse corpses he was still breathing. Second: Qingshuang Sword Master Liu Rushi was responsible for guarding the wounded's retreat. Her sword art is famed for lightness, but that day she stood before the medical tent — whoever approached, the Qingshuang Sword became a frost blade — six or seven barbarian scouts trying to ambush the wounded died soundlessly.

The third thing, and most intriguing: Wangchuan Tower showed up. Not those faceless assassins — Wangchuan Tower's intelligence net, on the eve of the decisive battle, delivered the barbarians' troop deployment map to the Yi Wu Army camp. No way to know what Meng Po's game was. Maybe "we can't kill a hundred thousand people anyway"; maybe "nobody on the barbarian side paid either." In any case, overnight the Jianghu's view of Wangchuan Tower grew subtle — "assassins can serve country and people too," though the next day they shrank back into shadow.

Barbarian King Jin Ridi was finally surrounded by Wulin masters at the foot of Yanmen Pass, heavily wounded, forced to retreat. The Yi Wu Army won. The Jianghu survivors returned to the Central Plains like waking from a very long dream. Some say the residual sword-qi on the Great Wall, when the wind blows, can still be smelled.

—— Baixiaosheng's note: The battle was won, the men broken, the Jianghu scattered. In the three years after, the great factions all reorganized. New names began to surface, old names began to fade. This is the Jianghu — unless the old goes, the new won't come. But what this old man cares most about: when Jin Ridi retreated, did that signed "three-year non-aggression" actually count?

---

### 9. Jiangnan Wulin Gathering — Liu Rushi's Three-Sword Pact — Winter, Yonghe 121

Among the Tianjian Alliance's five sects, the Qingshuang Sect has always been the most low-key. On a winter day of Yonghe 121, Qingshuang Sword Master Liu Rushi held a "small gathering" by West Lake in Suzhou — called small, but those who came were all the Jiangnan Wulin's notable figures. The cause: someone questioned whether the Qingshuang Sect's swift sword was "only fit for daughters' self-defense."

Liu Rushi didn't argue. She stood at the bow, thrust three swords at the West Lake surface. First sword swift as a swallow skimming water, second soft as willow catkin in wind, third — sheathed the sword, and only then did the water begin to crack. Three sword marks crisscrossed, reflecting a "Heaven" character on the lake heart — the Qingshuang sword-qi entering water didn't disperse, lasting several breaths before slowly vanishing. Those who doubted her sobered up halfway.

Afterward someone asked Liu Rushi: "Why not just challenge him to a duel?" She said: "Fighting at a banquet is unsightly, hurts the Qingshuang Sect's name." Paused, then: "And he was drunk; I was afraid of hurting him."

—— Baixiaosheng's note: The least sword-guest-like sword guest among the five sects. But if you think she only serves tea and plays peacemaker — those three sword-qi are the best answer. This old man privately heard she once dueled Lu Chenyuan in her youth; who won none know. But after the duel, Lu Chenyuan gifted the Qingshuang Sect a long-lost "Qingshuang Sword Manual." The gift's meaning, profound.

---

### 10. Divine Sword "Cold Soul" Emerges — Winter, Yonghe 122

As the year's end approached, a great affair at Longquan Pavilion in Jiangnan. The old master smith kept vigil at the furnace forty-nine days, forged a sword — the night the sword was born, heaven sent heavy snow. Not rhetoric. Everyone present saw it — the furnace fire unextinguished, suddenly goose-feather snow fell from the sky, melting into white mist on the furnace.

That sword, blade ice-blue, cold to touch. The old master named it "Cold Soul." Per Longquan Pavilion's word, the sword itself is a piece of black iron — no, not ordinary black iron, but a meteoric iron from a hundred years ago, sitting in Longquan's storeroom a century, none daring to move it. The old master said his time was near; in life he'd made countless blades, and before dying wanted to do one thing to close his eyes — so he put the meteoric iron in the furnace.

After the sword was born, the great factions stirred. Tianjian Alliance said "sword to swordsman"; Sihai Alliance offered a hundred thousand taels; Qianji Pavilion's Gongshu Xuan sent word "don't sell, lend me to see the meteoric iron's molecular structure"; even Wangchuan Tower rarely issued a note — "using this sword for assassination is wasteful, but if others bid too high we can be a shill."

Who finally got it? This old man won't say much, only reveal one line — the sword's master is a middle-aged Taoist in plain white, unwilling to reveal his name. After getting the sword he said "a sword is not for hiding, but for returning to its sheath." None present understood. This old man pondered long — oh, he meant, one worthy of this sword will naturally find him.

—— Baixiaosheng's note: Every year divine weapons emerge, every year people lose their lives fighting for blades. This old man's advice to the young — a good weapon indeed raises combat by thirty percent, but if you're only worth fifty percent, the sum is eighty. And those truly at the top, forget weapons — barehanded they'll floor you. If you truly want a divine weapon, better first practice basic moves.

## II. Secrets of the Eight Great Sects

### Tianjian Alliance — The Five-Sect Struggle, Lu Chenyuan's Dilemma

The Tianjian Alliance looks like a mountain — towering, the righteous pillar. But beneath the mountain, many cracks.

The five great sword sects each harbor thoughts. Lieyang's Duan Tianya is the openly expansionist; his logic simple: a sword rusts in its sheath, if the Tianjian Alliance won't fight the Demon Cult, the Demon Cult will come fight you. Qingshuang's Liu Rushi says little, but privately often discusses alliance affairs alone with Lu Chenyuan — she's the five sects' only female leader, yet the calmest. Luomei Sect's old leader past eighty, said his martial art has degraded to Fourth Realm, but his seniority crushes — his attitude "you young ones fuss as you like, just don't touch my plum blossoms behind the mountain." Jinglei Sect's sword path is very "strange," style eerie and variable, its stance among the five most wavering. Duanshui Sect master "defense," most aligned with Lu Chenyuan — the problem is the defense faction is most numerous but weakest in combat, which hurts where fists speak.

Lu Chenyuan's dilemma: not that he doesn't want to manage, but can't. Conservatives uphold him, young-turks respect him, but neither side listens. He's like a bodhisattva in a temple — incense burns bright, but can't step down to walk.

—— Baixiaosheng's note: Lu Chenyuan announcing abdication is itself intriguing. With his martial art and prestige, he could sit ten more years and none would dare flip the table. His hurry to retreat can only mean he fears something. What? Fears living to see the Tianjian Alliance split. Die in the leader's seat, succession decided by others; retire alive, succession decided by him. The price is losing power. This choice commands more respect than his sword art.

---

### Taixu Palace — Yuxu Zhenren's Seclusion and Ziyang's Coldness

Taixu Palace is on Wudang Mountain in the Central Plains; sect master Yuxu Zhenren has scarcely left his rooms in five years. Three sayings in the Jianghu: one, he's cultivating the "Innate Art" at a critical point, needs long closed-door training; two, his duel with the former Blood Moon Cult master years ago left a hidden wound, unhealed; three — he's simply very old. This old man leans to the third, but the first two aren't impossible.

Yuxu Zhenren doesn't go out, so Taixu Palace's daily affairs fall to Sword Pavilion Chief Ziyang. Ziyang this man — how to say, one who's exchanged ten words with him can call himself "Ziyang's old friend." He's one who lives holding a sword. Sword undrawn, no killing intent; drawn, no choice — not his line, but his manner truly so.

A small tip: in his youth Ziyang once slew in Jiangnan a female assassin disguised as a zither master. Before dying she asked him "have you ever loved someone." Ziyang didn't answer. But said after, a ring of red silk quietly appeared on his sword hilt.

—— Baixiaosheng's note: This old man isn't a gossip rag, but this is too good not to record — Taixu Palace has a female Taoist, said exceedingly beautiful, named "Qingyin," in charge of the sect master's quarters. Some say she practiced sword with Ziyang in youth. Others say her sword art and Ziyang's are "mutually born and restrained" — Taixu Palace's Liangyi Sword Art is originally dual cultivation of man and woman. Which sword does Ziyang practice, the yin or the yang? Which does Qingyin? These things Taixu Palace never explained outward. This old man isn't a gossip rag, but this old man will keep investigating.

---

### Wanfo Temple — The Engaged vs. Reclusive Struggle

Wanfo Temple Abbot Huijue is the least-ultimate-master-like master this old man has met in his life. You stand before him, feel no pressure — just a frail old monk dozing in the sound of tides. But I can responsibly say: if to pick one "person I don't want to be enemy of," Huijue ranks first.

Wanfo Temple's core contradiction is "engaged" vs. "reclusive." Arhat Hall Chief Mingkong — called "Mad Monk" — is the engaged faction's representative. He holds Buddhism shouldn't only be chanted in the temple, martial art not only practiced in the yard; since the Jianghu needs the righteous, Wanfo Temple should step out. The reclusive faction, represented by Precept Court Chief Fazheng, holds "Buddhism touches no red dust," Wanfo Temple's great force should only be used for self-defense.

Huijue's own stance is subtle. He neither withdrew Mingkong's fist nor sealed Fazheng's mouth. He only said — "Argue ten more years, still empty. When it's time to engage, the door opens naturally. When it's time to retreat, the door closes naturally."

As for Huijue's "No-Self Realm" — said he already touched the Sixth Realm "Return to Simplicity" threshold. But he won't step forward. Why? This old man asked; he said: "One more step forward is completely setting down 'self.' I haven't set it down. Because I have unfinished business." What business? He didn't say. This old monk, always speaks only half.

—— Baixiaosheng's note: Of the "No-Self Realm" there's also a rumor — said one who cultivates it can, within one hour, foresee three future things, but each foreknowledge costs a year of life. Of course hearsay. But once Huijue before the crowd said to Lu Chenyuan "the matter of three years hence, no need to think now." At the time the Tianjian Alliance's abdication plan wasn't yet announced. Did he deduce it, or...?

### Yaowang Valley — The True Purpose of Youming Hall

Yaowang Valley is a strange place. Beside the Apricot Grove Hall that heals and saves, brazenly stands a "Youming Hall" — specializing in poison study. The Jianghu has long asked: a holy land of medicine, why study poison?

The answer is simple: because poison is also medicine.

Su Qingnang — Yaowang Valley Master, the era's number-one healer — never avoids this contradiction. She has a saying this old man admires: "The best physician must understand poison. Because without understanding poison, you don't know how the poisoned died." But Youming Hall's controversy goes further. Youming Hall Master Mo Ran, only twenty-eight yet master of all the world's poisons, a poison prodigy seen once a century. Yet his name in the valley isn't good — because three years ago in a hall poison contest he nearly poisoned his own younger brother to death. Though Su Qingnang personally saved him, this became a thorn in Mo Ran's back — not driven in by others, but planted by himself.

The later herb-loss incident worsened Youming Hall's plight. Someone stole the valley's Dragon-Saliva Grass, forged the out-stock record. All clues pointed to Youming Hall. But this old man's intel — the herb thief was a disciple named "Meng Chuan," coerced by Youming Hall's former vice-master Nangong Hui. Nangong Hui secretly defected to the Blood Moon Cult, perhaps simultaneously working for Qianji Pavilion — a double agent. Mo Ran chased him two thousand li, caught him only on Tang Sect's territory in Bashu.

—— Baixiaosheng's note: Su Qingnang's insisted Yaowang Valley principle — "ask not the stance, ask only the illness" — makes both righteous and evil owe her favors. But thus some in the righteous say she "ambiguous in stance." To this old man, these people's faces are ugliest — when you were poisoned near death, why didn't you mind Yaowang Valley's stance then? Su Qingnang's virtue is higher than ten slogan-shouting xia bound together. By the way, Su Qingnang past fifty looks barely thirty. The secret of health? Said she rises early daily to give herself a "meridian combing" with nine needles. But that's not from this old man — don't go asking her for some health secret.

### Qianji Pavilion — Gongshu Xuan's Price and Yin Jiuling's Betrayal

Qianji Pavilion sits atop a Bashu cliff, a group who take "knowledge" as faith. Pavilion Master Gongshu Xuan — early forties, fingers long as a zither master — is my former boss at Qianji Pavilion. I was once a Tianji Hall master there; he saw me graduate. His mathematical ability is uncanny. A legend: he can stand before you three seconds, then deduce the last banquet you ate five years ago and which shop.

But what price did he pay to crack Wanxiang Grotto's map? Many versions: some say he didn't close his eyes three days and nights, half his hair turned white; some say he entered the analysis room with ten disciples most versed in ancient script, came out alone; others say — and this old man most tends to believe — he used a forbidden technique in the Qianji Art called "Heaven-Machine Deduction," the price three years of life. Gongshu Xuan himself never discusses it. Ask him, he discusses the weather.

Another Qianji Pavilion case is Yin Jiuling's defection. Yin Jiuling — former Tianji Hall disciple, one of Gongshu Xuan's closed-door students — vanished after betraying and leaving. Rumor: outside Luoyang he used mechanism art for "humanity tests" — e.g., tampering with a carriage wheel to see who'd risk saving someone. This man also, in the Wanxiang Grotto affair, stole Gongshu Xuan's research and sold it to the Blood Moon Cult. Qianji Pavilion still wants him.

—— Baixiaosheng's note: I left Qianji Pavilion twenty years ago. Not for inner strife, not for pique — just because Gongshu Xuan is too smart. Working under too smart a man, you feel you'll never catch up. That said, he still sends me a letter yearly, one line only — "Senior brother, last month Luoyang's vegetable prices rose. Were you again eating big at the Yangzhou Tower?" This man when bored is borer than anyone.

### Sihai Alliance — How Much Money Does Shen Wanjin Really Have

Someone asks: how much money does Shen Wanjin have? The answer is direct — don't know. Not withholding, truly don't know.

His wealth divides into several blocks: operating capital of twelve trading houses; real estate and deeds across the nation (said thirty properties just in Luoyang); privately collected martial manuals (conservatively no fewer than twenty out-of-print manuals, not even countable in money); and most important — favor-debts. The list of famed masters who owe Shen Wanjin favors is probably longer than the Tianjian Alliance's disciple roster. This invisible wealth, when cashed, has no ceiling.

Shen Wanjin's martial art? Second Realm. The second realm. Meet a slightly famous Wulin person on the street, he might not win. But no one in the Jianghu dares touch him — not that they can't, but none can bear the consequences. Kill one Shen Wanjin, you offend at once the Tianjian Alliance (his partner), Yaowang Valley (his herb client), Qianji Pavilion (his intel ally), and every master who owes him a favor. This account, a smart man sees at a glance.

—— Baixiaosheng's note: This old man's life admires three kinds of people — good swordsmen, high healers, and those who can play silver into a peerless martial art. Shen Wanjin is the ceiling of the third kind. Said he has a ledger that records not accounts but favors — "such year such month such day, someone owed Shen a favor at such place, unpaid." This ledger is worth more than all his vaults combined.

### Blood Moon Cult — The Mystery of Ye Xiuluo's Origins

Blood Moon Cult Master Ye Xiuluo is the Jianghu's most mysterious pinnacle master. None has seen his true face — forever wearing the Asura mask. None knows if man or woman — though all assume "he." None knows where from — the Blood Moon Cult vanished ten years ago, three years ago he reappeared with followers as if fallen from heaven.

Of his origins, three sayings. One: he's the former cult master's bastard. The former master (name unmentioned, anyway killed by some Tianjian Alliance bigshot) left bloodline in the Western Regions, secretly raised then returned for revenge. Two: he's a survivor of some Tianjian Alliance demon-purge; righteous parents, whole family killed by the Demon Cult, but he was adopted by it — so his identity is a joke: a righteous descendant raised by his enemies. Three: he's not Western Regions at all, in youth learned martial art in Central Plains famous righteous sects, later found the "Blood Moon Demon Art" fragment, himself fell into Qi Deviation becoming a demon.

This old man investigated three years, no conclusion. But one thing is certain: among the Blood Moon Cult's four great protectors there are those who disobey the master. Li Canyang — confirmed dead — was loyal to "one who gives him opponents," not the master himself. Second Protector Baigu (name is a codename) cultivates mental demonic art, loyal to none. Third Protector Su Mei handles preaching and beguilement, should be one clinging to Ye Xiuluo. Fourth Protector weakest, lowest presence, but if someone stages a coup, the Fourth Protector is usually the key piece.

—— Baixiaosheng's note: Ye Xiuluo acts with style. Former cult masters couldn't wait to cut off all righteous heads for the city gate; Ye Xiuluo tries negotiation. Negotiation — a Demon Cult master wants to negotiate. This itself is the Jianghu's most abnormal thing. Either he's smarter than all, or more desperate than all. Maybe both.

### Wangchuan Tower — Meng Po's True Face and the "Three Don't Kill"

Meng Po — the name itself is a mystery. Wangchuan Tower Master, the Jianghu's most mysterious assassin king. None has seen the true face — may not be one person, may be a codename, wearing a white-porcelain mask, voice neither male nor female. None can even be sure if "Meng Po" is man or woman — though this old man uses "she," because in Jianghu rumor Meng Po's voice leans female.

Wangchuan Tower has three iron laws: the "Three Don't Kill" — don't kill infants, don't kill midwives, don't kill those of Yaowang Valley. These three iron laws were set by Meng Po, never broken. Why these three? This old man investigated twenty years, the puzzle: first, don't kill infants — means Meng Po saw an infant killed, and it changed her; second, don't kill midwives — a midwife delivers; Meng Po may have been born in a midwife's hands, or her mother died in childbirth and she lived because the midwife saved her; third, don't kill those of Yaowang Valley — simple logic, Wangchuan Tower is an assassin organization, assassins get hurt, when hurt need a doctor. And the Jianghu's best doctor is in Yaowang Valley.

Rumor says Meng Po is a woman named "Shen Suwen," once daughter of an army doctor. Her father, for saving all the wounded — including enemy troops — was nailed to the city wall by barbarians as warning. That girl who witnessed her father's death is the later Meng Po. But this is only a story this old man pieced from scattered evidence, not guaranteed accurate.

—— Baixiaosheng's note: Wangchuan Tower recently gave open intelligence aid in the Northern Frontier battle. Unprecedented. What does it mean? Either Meng Po's stance is shifting, or this involves her private grudge. This old man leans to the latter — if something can draw an assassin from shadow to light, it must be something heavier than money. Like hatred. Like that army doctor on Yanmen Pass twenty years ago.

## II.5 Five-Region Jianghu Record

The land under heaven divides into five regions. Each region has its own wind, its own rain, its own rules. This old man walked forty-plus years, trod all five regions; reverently records what he saw on the way. Those who would walk the Jianghu, read this.

—— Baixiaosheng

---

### Central Plains — The World's Belly, Source of Right and Wrong

Autumn Yonghe 123, this old man sat in this Luoyang teahouse's second floor, below is the crossroads. In one day you can see here Huashan Sect's green-robed swordsmen, Wanfo Temple's gray-robed monks, Six Doors' black-gold-lined official runners, and countless escort-agency men and street performers.

**Martial Character**: Central Plains martial art stresses one word "orthodox." Sword has sword's method, fist has fist's rules. The Tianjian Alliance's five sword sects claim the world's orthodox sword; whoever uses a sword in the Central Plains has some tie to the Tianjian Alliance. But "orthodox" overdone becomes "pedantic" — some Tianjian old-timers, when an opponent shows evil-school martial art, don't know how to fight back. In the last decade Jiangnan and Bashu's strange arts have poured into the Central Plains; old-timers shake heads, youngsters find it fresh.

**Key Landmarks**:
- **Huashan Sword Debate Platform**: the once-every-three-years world sword meet is held here. Said this year's meet opens spring Yonghe 124; the Tianjian Alliance's internal succession struggle will surely be decided on the platform.
- **Wudang Mountain Taixu Palace**: Taoist holy land. Yuxu Zhenren in recent years meets no visiting Wulin folk, only says "closed-door training." But you believe a sect master closes three years seeing no one? This old man doesn't. Either he's breaking through something, or something in Taixu Palace he dares not leave.
- **Songshan Wanfo Temple Lower Court**: unlike the main court at East Sea Putuo, the lower court's monks are more "engaged." Often martial monks go down to beg; occasionally meddle. Lower Court Chief Mingkong is an exception — this monk only fights, never chants; the Jianghu gave him the nickname "Martial Madman."

**Rumor**: Recently Luoyang's inn prices rose thirty percent. For no other reason — the Tianjian Alliance's abdication nears, all sects sending people to the Central Plains. Even the never-involved Qianji Pavilion secretly placed a post in the city. Plus Six Doors' Tie Jingxin seems abnormally attentive to Jianghu movement — what does this hint? Could the court be planning a "big sweep" of the Central Plains Wulin?

—— Baixiaosheng's note: The Central Plains is good in all ways, except too close to power. Where power is close, loyalty and betrayal are two faces of one coin.

---

### Jiangnan — Land of Mist and Flowers, Where Undercurrents Run Fastest

Everyone says Jiangnan is good. Liu Rushi lives in Jiangnan — not the Tianjian Alliance's Qingshuang Sword Master, but the courtesan by the Qiantang Sudi. Just a joke.

Jiangnan is this old man's favorite haunt. Not for the mist and willows — though that's a reason too — but because the "xia" here differs from elsewhere. The Central Plains' xia is the swordsman, the Northern Frontier's xia is the soldier, Jiangnan's xia is the scholar. A middle-aged man in a square cap, folding fan in hand, citing classics in speech, is likely a hermit Confucian-xia. Here grudges are often settled not on the arena, but between a cup of tea, a tune, a game of chess.

**Martial Character**: Jiangnan sword art is famed for "elegance," walking the flowing-water path. But don't be fooled by beauty — back then "Jiangnan's First Sword" Lu Changfeng with one move "Sudi Spring Dawn" blinded three Demon Cult masters' eyes within three moves. Fast, precise, graceful. Lightfoot and hidden weapons are especially developed in Jiangnan, because the water towns have many bridges and boats; borrowing a lotus leaf on the water is a leap — a skill the Central Plains horse-riders can't imagine.

**Key Landmarks**:
- **Yangzhou Sihai Alliance Headquarters**: where Sihai Alliance Chief Steward Shen Wanjin presides. From outside the headquarters is only a big trading house — hangs the "Sihai Trading" sign. But inside is an inner court none but alliance members may enter. Said there's a "Golden Saber Wall" hung with Golden Saber Tokens, each blade representing one of Shen Wanjin's Jianghu favor-debts. This wall is his backbone.
- **Suzhou Longquan Pavilion**: the world's most famous sword-forging workshop. Current master is "Ghost Hand" Xu Duan, said he can hear the carbon content in iron — by ear. The Qingshuang Sword, Lieyang Sword, Purple Lightning Sword all came from this workshop. But Xu Duan in ten years barely takes new clients; this old man guesses his hand is injured.
- **West Lake Wulin Gathering**: held on West Lake around each Mid-Autumn, very "Jiangnan" in form — no arena, but charters several painted boats, masters spar on the lake. Spectators rent small boats to watch. One year Ye Xiuluo reportedly mingled in the spectator boats, watched the whole thing undetected.

**Rumor**: Recently Jiangnan's biggest news is Huagong Powder. A strange poison that seals inner strength, leaked from some channel around Suzhou-Hangzhou. Shen Wanjin flew into a rage — because the poison hurt his business — already offered three thousand taels for the source. Liu Shengyan was urgently summoned from Yaowang Valley to Jiangnan; said she found a problem worse than Huagong Powder: someone is using live humans to experiment, refining the poison formula. This old man holds some clues, but can't write them in this chronicle. Wouldn't be good if stolen.

—— Baixiaosheng's note: Jiangnan's water is too deep. You think you see the lake surface, but beneath are undercurrents, whirlpools, corpses sunk decades not yet rotted.

---

### Bashu — Far From the Emperor, Where Mechanisms Are Calculated to the Limit

Bashu's roads, this old man walked six times. Six times nearly died on the road. Not robbed — the plank roads too perilous.

This land is naturally unfit for large sects' garrison. So here you see no Tianjian Alliance's forest of sword sects, but instead scattered strongholds, reclusive eccentrics, and Qianji Pavilion's ubiquitous net.

**Martial Character**: Bashu's martial art was "forced" into being. Because here everywhere are mountains, forests, mist, cliffs — you practice big-open fierce martial art and can't even deploy it; before you set your stance the enemy's already behind you. So Bashu produced the world's best hidden weapons, mechanism art, and strange arts. Tang Sect's hidden weapons, Qianji Pavilion's mechanisms, behind both the same logic: problems solvable by brain, don't use fist.

**Key Landmarks**:
- **Qianji Pavilion Cliff Pavilion**: Gongshu Xuan's base, built atop a Bashu cliff. Trespassers nine deaths one life — not a scare. The pavilion's outer ring has at least thirty-seven mechanisms. Last year a Wangchuan Tower master tried to infiltrate, retreated at the thirteenth mechanism — not dead, Gongshu Xuan actively shut the mechanisms. Because he felt "rare someone gets so far, a pity to kill."
- **Qingcheng Mountain Cave-Heaven**: said one entrance to Wanxiang Grotto — but there are many entrances; this Qingcheng one is said blocked by a giant stone urn. Gongshu Xuan's deciphered map has its location, but none has found the key.
- **Emei Mountain**: the Jianghu's only female martial-art holy land. The Emei Sect doesn't join the Tianjian Alliance — they have their own inheritance system. Said Emei sword art and the Tianjian Alliance's are completely different paths: one is "orthodox," one is "spirit."

**Rumor**: Qianji Pavilion has a traitor named Yin Jiuling. This man was once vice-master of the Earth-Work Hall — the mechanism-making hall. Defected ten years ago, stole part of Wanxiang Grotto's blueprints. He now hides under alias somewhere in the Central Plains; Gongshu Xuan offers his head — not alive, the head. That a usually smiling mechanism genius would go this hard means what Yin Jiuling stole is more important than it appears.

—— Baixiaosheng's note: Bashu — easy to enter, hard to leave. Not the road's fault — the people here are too hospitable. Too hospitable means: once you've drunk their wine, eaten their food, you become theirs. And becoming a Bashu person, you never want to go elsewhere.

---

### Western Regions — Long River, Setting Sun, Where the Demon Cult Hides

Beyond Yumen Pass is the great desert. In the great desert, a place called Blood Moon Fort.

No — the Western Regions aren't only the Blood Moon Cult. The Western Regions have blade masters. Not the Central Plains kind who wear swords as decoration, but true blade masters who with one saber drove off thirty horse thieves, scarred more than haired. They have their own rules: don't kill women and children — coinciding with Wangchuan Tower's iron law — don't cut those who haven't drawn; winner doesn't disgrace the corpse.

**Martial Character**: Western Regions martial art's core is "practical." Here beauty of form doesn't matter, only whether one saber settles it. Saber art more popular than sword (curved saber is the Western Regions' feature), external hard-training especially developed. A external art called "Vajra Body," said when mastered can hard-resist three saber cuts without breaking skin — but the price is ten years of life. Worth it? Those who ask don't live long in the Western Regions. Another feature: sound-wave martial art. The eagle-flute the desert shepherds blow, those who hear lose focus — not legend, a peerless art called "Beyond-the-Pass Demon Sound."

**Key Landmarks**:
- **Yumen Pass**: gateway into the Western Regions. On the gate wall old soldiers garrison year-round. Last year passing, this old man saw a stele carved "spring wind never crosses Yumen Pass." Beside, someone carved with a small knife a line: "The Western Regions don't get spring wind, but do get many who don't fear death."
- **Loulan Ruins**: a dead city buried in sand. Legend holds it hides a predecessor dynasty's martial treasury — not the same as Wanxiang Grotto, older. Every few years someone forms a team to dig, but either returns empty-handed or doesn't return at all. Of three this old man knew who went to explore Loulan, one came back — mad.
- **Kunlun Mountain**: birthplace of countless martial lineages. Rumor: among Taixu Palace's Taoist archives is a sole copy recording that somewhere in Kunlun Mountain hides an ancient martial vein — not martial art, but something that changes the body's meridians — gain it and gain thirty years' power from nothing. The credibility of such legend... this old man can only say believe if you will.

**Rumor**: After the Blood Moon Cult vanished ten years, recently frequently appears on Western Regions trade roads. Ye Xiuluo reportedly alone defeated three righteous masters in the Western Regions no-man's-land — one a Tianjian Alliance elder, another a Six Doors enshrined. Sihai Alliance's Western Regions trade road was robbed twice last month, the robbers left a Blood Moon mark. The problem: both robberies carried herbs. The Demon Cult now needs large amounts of herbs — what does this mean? Either someone is gravely wounded needing continuous treatment, or they're mass-refining some pill. Both explanations bad.

—— Baixiaosheng's note: In the great desert grows a flower called Blood-Moon Orchid, blooming only on the full-moon night. Gorgeous, extremely poisonous. When it blooms, ten li around grows no grass. The Blood Moon Cult took its name from it; you tell me what path that cult walks.

---

### Northern Frontier — Iron Banners and Horses, the Hardest Backbone

If in Jiangnan you learned the sword, in the Central Plains you learned to be a person, in the Western Regions you learned the saber — then I suggest you spend another year in the Northern Frontier. Because here you'll learn what "living" means.

The Northern Frontier has no Jiangnan's mist, Central Plains' calligraphy, Bashu's fine wine, Western Regions' curved saber. The Northern Frontier has barbarians. Every autumn they come, seize grain, iron, people. Northern Frontier men know one thing from childhood — you learn martial art not for fame on the arena, but to hold the wall behind you, because behind that wall live your parents.

**Martial Character**: Northern Frontier martial art is famed for "fierce." Horseback skill, long weapons, number one in the land. The Northern Frontier has a saying: "southern fist, northern leg, eastern spear, western saber" — the Northern Frontier's long spear, one thrust pierces the horse belly, two flips the rider, three seals the throat. No fourth thrust needed. Flying Eagle Fort is the Northern Frontier Wulin's alliance leader; their spear art is completely different from the Central Plains' path — said they can on horseback perform a "Seventy-Two Flying Eagle Spear," man borrows horse's power, spear follows man, like an eagle wheeling over the desert.

**Key Landmarks**:
- **Sixteen Prefectures of Yanyun**: the Northern Frontier's throat. Sixteen cities in a line, both military line and Jianghu boundary. Most famed is Yanmen Pass — thirty years ago barbarian King Aguda led eighty thousand iron cavalry to Yanmen; defender Han Yue held with three thousand men seventeen days, died with the city. Han Yue was no Wulin man, but every Northern Frontier warrior stops before Yanmen Pass, removes his cap, mourns three breaths. This is the Northern Frontier's rite.
- **Flying Eagle Fort**: the Northern Frontier Wulin's alliance leader. Flying Eagle Fort's rules differ — the fort master isn't hereditary, but won by strength. Every five years a "Eagle Trial," anyone may challenge the current fort master. Win and you're the new master. Current master Xiao Tieyi won ten years ago against the former master — his own father. Northern Frontier folk find this normal.
- **Changbai Mountain Heaven Lake**: legend says at the lake bottom a strange beast guards a thousand-year snow lotus; eating it gains fifty years' power. This old man visited Heaven Lake three times — not for the lotus, for the scenery. The water clear to the bottom, indeed no beast. But by the shore footprints. Very large footprints.

**Rumor**: The court's "Yi Wu Army" conscription order has reached the great sects. The Tianjian Alliance internally still argues whether to send — conservatives say Jianghu men fighting for the court loses backbone; hawk Duan Tianya says if the Northern Frontier falls the whole Central Plains Wulin becomes the front line. The Blood Moon Cult in the Northern Frontier actually has people active, not to cause trouble — this old man's intel says Ye Xiuluo sent men to scout the barbarians' grain road. A Demon Cult master helping the court scout enemy intel? The water here runs deep.

The Northern Frontier decisive battle's date is unset, but all know — soon. The barbarians' scout riders came a month earlier this year than usual. Xiao Tieyi has begun pulling men from each fort to reinforce Yanmen Pass. All signs point to one conclusion: next year, or at latest the year after, the Northern Frontier will see a great war. Then the whole Jianghu will be drawn in.

—— Baixiaosheng's note: The Northern Frontier is the simplest place I've been. Here the rule is only one — stand together and fight the enemy, and you're brothers. After the battle if you want to fight, out the city gate please. Such a place doesn't suit me, but without such a place, the Central Plains scholars who sit in teahouses writing poetry would long ago have been trampled to mud by hooves.

### The Jianghu's Gray Zones

Beyond the five regions, some places belong to no region, even no "Jianghu." But they exist.

**Three-No-Care Town**: at the junction of Central Plains, Jiangnan, Bashu. The town isn't big — one main street, two rows of houses, three gambling houses. But here Jianghu intel flows faster than Luoyang's teahouse — because these intel peddlers needn't write messages elegantly, only shout. If you want to ask after someone or something, come to Three-No-Care Town, sit in "Old Crooked-Mouth Tavern," order the cheapest local wine. Not to drink — pour half on the ground. The boss comes over. Then depends on how much silver you brought.

**Longmen Inn**: always a few in the Jianghu. You can never tell which is the real Longmen Inn — this itself is protection. But one test: if you enter a remote inn, find the boss-lady doesn't blink seeing you walk in with a saber, and the shop has only three customers — each wearing a bamboo hat, heads down — congratulations, you've likely walked into a Longmen Inn. Advice: order a bowl of noodles, eat, don't ask the price, leave a tael of silver, leave quickly.

**Wild Zen Grove**: an abandoned temple somewhere in the Central Plains. Now a paradise for rogue cultivators. Here you can see any school's martial art — because those with no sect come to spar. Occasionally a dying master passes, carving his life's learning on the wall. Last year someone found on a Wild Zen Grove wall a sword-art fragment — not on paper, but finger-carved into brick. To this day none has identified whose art. This old man went to see; can confirm not any Tianjian Alliance sect's sword art.

—— Baixiaosheng's note: The gray zones are the Jianghu's true base color. Righteous, evil, Central Plains, Western Regions — these labels are all pasted by people. In Three-No-Care Town, a Tianjian Alliance disciple may sit at the same table drinking with a Blood Moon Cult protector. No fight, because the town's rule is — no fighting in town. Out of town, fight however you like.

## III. Jianghu Figures

### 1. Lu Chenyuan — The Sword Sage in His Dusk Years

Title: Tianjian Alliance Leader, the era's Sword Sage

Realm: Fifth Realm · Peak of Perfection (said half a step from the Sixth Realm)

Lu Chenyuan is old. Not the kind you can ignore — the kind you feel the weight of years standing beside him. In youth he alone with one sword pacified the Western Regions' Thirteen Bandits; thirty years later sitting in the leader's seat, his greatest enemy isn't the Demon Cult, but time.

This old man had one meeting with him. That was a Qianji Pavilion intel exchange ten years ago — Lu Chenyuan not yet leader, but already the five sects' default helmsman. He sat among the seats, spoke least, ate slowest. When others argued he watched the clouds outside; when others fell silent he said a word. Each made one nod, yet none could refute.

The sword most remembered isn't an attack, but a giving-up. Yonghe 20 on the Sword Debate Platform he announced abdication — the young-turks below froze as if sealed at the throat. Not that he couldn't fight more, but wouldn't. A man of supreme sword art — the hardest isn't practicing the sword, but telling all "I no longer need this sword."

—— Baixiaosheng's note: Lu Chenyuan's sword-philosophy is "the sword is for protection." A nice principle, but its other face — if one day you can't protect? His abdication is essentially an admission of "can't protect." But one who admits his limits deserves more respect than one who thinks himself omnipotent.

> Classic line (Baixiaosheng's invention): "The scabbard is heavier than the sword. For the scabbard holds not iron — but a life's promise."

---

### 2. Duan Tianya — The Hawk-Faction Sword Master's Self-Redemption

Title: Tianjian Alliance Lieyang Sword Master, young-turks' hawk leader

Realm: Third Realm · Complete Mastery (rising)

Duan Tianya's name, in Yonghe 122, was a "trouble" — himself a trouble, the trouble he made, and the trouble-made him.

This man early thirties, fierce and overbearing sword art. His character is his sword — direct, dislikes beating around bushes, but thus often hurts. At the Tianjian gathering he challenged Lu Chenyuan before the full hall. Not from hating Lu Chenyuan, but from impatience — he wants to be leader, wants to lead the Tianjian Alliance to fight every battle worth fighting.

Then the Blood Moon Cult came.

After that raid, Duan Tianya changed much. Not character — his Lieyang Sword still knows no restraint — but his eyes changed. This old man later caught a glimpse of him in a crowd: he stood on the Tianjian courtyard's steps, sword planted on the ground, looking at the not-yet-cleaned blood in the yard. That expression — not admitting fault. Thinking.

—— Baixiaosheng's note: Duan Tianya's growth path is interesting. He didn't go from "bad" to "good" — he was never bad. He went from "impatient" to "able to wait." If he can steady further, in ten years he'll be the Jianghu's most dangerous swordsman to face. No — five years may suffice.

> Classic line (Baixiaosheng's invention): "Some guard with the sword, some open with the sword. I only want all who need a sword to have one in hand."

---

### 3. Su Qingnang — The Physician Who Asks Not the Stance

Title: Yaowang Valley Master, the world's number-one divine healer

Realm: Fourth Realm · Perfecting the Craft

Su Qingnang is an anomaly. In this Jianghu of either-righteous-or-evil, she insists "ask not the stance" — treats the righteous's wounds, also cures the Demon Cult's poison. Some curse her "no right and wrong," but to all censure her answer is four words: "He is a patient."

What this old man admires most isn't her medicine — it's that she, who could dazzle the Jianghu with face and health arts, instead plunged into the hardest, most exhausting clinical front line. Disciple Liu Shengyan says the Valley Master often stays up a night, guarding a critical patient till dawn. She says, see, good complexion comes from "nine needles self-applied" — but you apply meridians, not beauty.

Su Qingnang's ledger shows she saved over three hundred wounded, but never charged anyone — including the court — a coin.

—— Baixiaosheng's note: Su Qingnang is the kind "you won't specially recall her, but when you need her she can save your life." The whole Jianghu owes her a life. This isn't flattery — it's accurate statement.

### 4. Mo Ran — Youming Hall's Poison Prodigy

Realm: Third Realm · Complete Mastery

Mo Ran, twenty-eight, Youming Hall's century prodigy disciple. Masters over a hundred deadly poisons. Character... gloomy, few words, all in black, walks soundless. Unknowing disciples all thought he was the terrifying type who boils corpses in the pharmacy at midnight. Actually not. Mo Ran has a controversy: in his early twenties he once badly misfired in a Youming Hall poison contest, nearly poisoning his own younger brother to death. The window faces the yard's only orange tree. His brother liked oranges — the last words before the pharmacy accident were "brother, the oranges are red."

—— Baixiaosheng's note: A prodigy like Mo Ran, if his heart turns crooked, is the Jianghu's greatest disaster, like Yin Jiuling. ### 5. Ye Xiuluo — The Demon Lord With the Most Brains

Title: Blood Moon Cult Master

Realm: Fifth Realm · Peak of Perfection

Ye Xiuluo is the most uncomfortable opponent this old man has met in life — he isn't mad.

What were former Blood Moon Cult masters like? Wild laughter, slaughter, cut off all ears as decoration. Ye Xiuluo differs. Before hitting you, he first sends a letter. The letter says "we can talk." And while you guess where the trap hides, he's already prepared the Huagong Powder.

His martial data says it all: Moon Fall — area attack plus mental control; Asura Flash — miss and get hit back; Heart-Biting Finger — directly attacks the mind. These moves in another's hands are a pile of "you're dead" signals. But in Ye Xiuluo's hands, they're more like a "you'd best behave" deterrence.
His one soft spot — Blood Moon Demon Art backlash. If he can't break through the final layer within a year, he'll die. In other words, Ye Xiuluo doesn't want to rule the world. He just doesn't want to die. And to not die, he'll do anything.

—— Baixiaosheng's note: Someone asked who's stronger, Ye Xiuluo or Baigu. The answer is obvious — if Ye Xiuluo couldn't even beat Li Canyang he'd never be cult master. The real question: Fifth-Realm-peak Ye Xiuluo vs. Abbot Huijue, who's stronger? This old man asked Huijue once; the old monk just said "Amitabha" then went back to chanting. That's the best answer — Buddhism doesn't make such comparisons, but he has zero interest in proving he'd win.

### 6. Ziyang — The Cold Taoist Who Only Speaks With the Sword

Title: Taixu Palace Sword Pavilion Chief, cold sword guest

Realm: Fourth Realm · Perfecting the Craft

Ziyang teaches sword at Taixu Palace. But disciples fear him more than respect him — not that he's fierce, but too quiet. You exhaust yourself performing a sword set before him; he only says "again." After ten agains, still "again." Only when you abandon the thought of pleasing him does he nod — "this time better than just now." This is Ziyang-brand chicken soup — not a trace of encouragement to be tasted. But after drinking it you'll never again ask "am I good enough."

Ziyang never comes to you. But when he comes to you, he won't ask if you agree. This old man met him once — once, at a tea shed below Wuyi Mountain. He sat drinking tea; I passed opposite him; without raising his head he said "Qianji Pavilion senior — what are you investigating?"

I very much wanted to ask him — that red silk on the sword hilt, who is it tied for. But I didn't. Because his silence was clearer than any answer: that is an old matter, an old matter he doesn't wish to mention again.

—— Baixiaosheng's note: Ziyang's realm is Fourth-Realm peak, but the Jianghu agrees — if he wished, he could break through to Fifth Realm anytime. He stops at the Fourth Realm for one reason only: not lack of talent, but Taoism holds "act when the time comes." He waits for some moment that should arrive. What moment, he won't say, and this old man doesn't know.

> Classic line (Baixiaosheng's invention): "The sword in its sheath is quietest, and most dangerous. Like a person."

### 7. Shen Wanjin — The Jianghu's Most Dangerous "Cannot Tie a Chicken"

Title: Sihai Alliance Chief Steward

Shen Wanjin is a fat man. Not metaphor, objective description — he's frankly fat, fat enough to make you want to invite him to dinner. But this man with one sentence can send six righteous sects to march on one thing. Not by martial art, but by two things called "silver," and "favor."

How many out-of-print martial manuals in his private collection? Conservatively no fewer than twenty. From the Nine Yang Fragment to the Dragon-Subduing Palm secret lost half a century, all locked in some secret room at his Yangzhou headquarters. He never practices. He says "useful is enough; needn't myself know how."

Shen Wanjin's life philosophy: always smile doing business, never strike first. You give him a horse, he can chat till you buy a team of mules; you curse him, he returns a pot of good wine, making you feel your rudeness — then next day the bill comes, doubled.

—— Baixiaosheng's note: Shen Wanjin owed me one intel fee, dragged three years unpaid. Not because he owed me — the fan is truly handy.

### 8. Huijue — The Perplexed One in the No-Self Realm

Title: Wanfo Temple Abbot, enlightened monk

Realm: Fifth Realm · Peak of Perfection (touching the Sixth Realm threshold)

Abbot Huijue about seventy, thin frame, white brows and beard. The way he appears in Jianghu news is never "defeated so-and-so in combat" — but "so-and-so went to Huijue to ask such-and-such." But all know he mustn't be provoked. Even Ye Xiuluo detours around Wanfo Temple — not for fear of Buddha, but because Buddha's fist is truly huge.

Huijue's martial data says it all: with Vajra Indestructible active, external reduction plus defense stacks to eleven, meaning most attacks tickle him. Great Compassion Thousand-Leaf Palm can hit three targets at once — extra damage to evil-aligned (Infamy over 20). Plus the Fifth-Realm Inner Strength Infusion — can take eighteen on key checks. This is what martial art looks like after reaching the philosophical level: you don't even need to prove how strong you are.

But why won't he open the temple gate? Not unable. Unwilling to step into red dust. Wanfo Temple's engaged and reclusive factions argue endlessly; Huijue only said "when it should open, it will open." Whether this is wisdom or avoidance, only he knows.

—— Baixiaosheng's note: Once I sat with Huijue on Putuo Island's shore; I asked: all the Jianghu's wars, you who can fight ten, why not act? He said: "A fight can stop a day's war; compassion can stop ten years' hatred. Which do you choose?" I said I'm not that great, I choose fighting. He laughed — "when you're old you'll know, fighting is the easiest thing."

> Classic line (Baixiaosheng's invention): "You ask who in the Jianghu is strongest — I don't know. But I know who is least strong — the one who thinks himself strongest."

### 9. Gongshu Xuan — The End of Mechanism Art Is the Human Heart

Title: Qianji Pavilion Master

Realm: Fourth Realm · Perfecting the Craft (combat not measured thus — his value is in the brain)

Gongshu Xuan's brain is the Jianghu's most precise instrument. He can discuss today's weather with you while calculating your birth chart in his head — not exaggeration. Someone tested him: given a set of chaotic data, he reconstructed a complete trend graph within one incense stick.

His combat isn't by brawn. He has no strong physique — his Strength is only three. He uses mechanism art and mathematics — before combat lays mechanisms on the ground, in combat activates the bronze-men formation as shield, marks a target with "Qianji Art · Deduction," next turn Storm Pear-Blossom Needles harvests.

But his achievement always trails shadow — cracking Wanxiang Grotto's map, per my understanding, cost a near-suicidal "Heaven-Machine Deduction." He himself never mentions what that deduction consumed. And I, as the disciple he raised hands-on at Qianji Pavilion — know the cost: his wife fell critically ill the night of the deduction. But he was in the analysis room, couldn't come out.

The wife was saved. But he wasn't. Some guilt, mathematics can't compute.

—— Baixiaosheng's note: Gongshu Xuan is my master, also friend, also — in some sense rival. When I left Qianji Pavilion he asked what I wanted. I said I wanted nothing. Next morning, on my desk appeared his most precious deduction notebook of ten years. On the flyleaf: "Senior brother, wherever you go, this may be useful." I flipped through — what he spent ten years writing, just given away. This is probably why I don't hate him. One who can trust you so, you can't hate.

> Classic line (Baixiaosheng's invention): "Mathematics can deduce all things, only cannot compute one choice — why you took this road, not another. That answer can only be found on the road."

### 10. Meng Po — The Assassin King's Merciful Heart

Title: Wangchuan Tower Master

Realm: Fourth Realm · Perfecting the Craft

Meng Po is Wangchuan Tower's founder — also its only "Tower Master." Rumor she isn't a fixed person, but a title inherited by successive masters. But my investigation shows — at least from twenty years ago to now, Meng Po is the same person. She never shows her true face, wears a white-porcelain mask, her voice distorted by inner strength, even gender unjudgeable.

Her martial art is simple: one-hit kill. Combat style: pick a target, mark with "Shadowless Art," then from shadow launch "Soul-Reaping Thirteen Forms" — double damage in stealth ambush. After combat, Yellow Springs Step leaves, no opportunity attack. Never lingers, never misses.

Every ten years Meng Po takes one "free" contract herself — on condition the assassination target must be "one who gave up choosing." What is "giving up choice"? She never explained. Said to have taken three such: a retired Six Doors chief constable, a traitor who betrayed his sect, and a clan leader who owed a hundred lives yet refused atonement. These three's common point — they could have avoided this step, but gave up the chance to take another road.

—— Baixiaosheng's note: A Jianghu saying — "Meng Po isn't a person, is a post. Whoever kills the previous one is the new Meng Po." Of course nonsense — Wangchuan Tower in twenty years has no record of master change. Another rumor: "Meng Po is actually Huijue's junior sister." If true, then Wanfo Temple's Precept Court's first sin is letting its own junior sister become assassin chief. Amitabha, don't tell Fazheng.

> Classic line (Baixiaosheng's invention): "I'm not killing people. I'm choosing — for those who cannot choose."

### 11. Liu Shengyan — The Angel Walking the Jianghu

Title: Yaowang Valley Jishi Hall Chief, young female doctor wandering the Jianghu

Realm: Second Realm · Crossing the Threshold

Liu Shengyan is nineteen. Already two years in the Jianghu. Carries a medicine box heavier than her body, holding everything that saves in the wild — wound medicine, antidote powder, nine-needle set, and a small bag of sugar (said for the children she treats).

After graduating she made a name around Jiangnan — a doctor who charges no money is rare in any world. Said she has her own rule: charge only three coins a day — for her own three meals. Not a coin more. Reason: "my master saves and saves, a disciple is embarrassed to spend." And these three coins she often can't bear to spend on herself — meeting a hungry refugee, she'll stuff an egg into their hand.

Her presence makes Yaowang Valley's renown extremely high among commoners. But Su Qingnang worries about her — though Yaowang Valley "asks not the stance," Liu Shengyan is too kind, will meet bad people sooner or later. This old man agrees. But on reflection, she's walked two years with the box, met many bad people — those ended up her patients. The non-charging kind. You wound her, she still treats you — then you feel bad. Not the wound's hurt, conscience's.

—— Baixiaosheng's note: This old man has seen too many mighty martial artists. But those who truly change the world aren't necessarily the highest. Liu Shengyan treats three wounds daily, persists twenty years. Think — over ten thousand cured, among them perhaps a future Sword Sage, perhaps a future Medicine King, perhaps a future you or me. The Jianghu owes this girl a "thank you," though she surely won't take it.

> Classic line (Baixiaosheng's invention): "The Medicine King Needle Art may not save everyone — but if you don't save because you might not, that is when a person truly dies."

### 12. Li Canyang — The Battle Maniac Who Worships Combat

Title: Blood Moon Cult Grand Protector, war god

Realm: Fourth Realm · Perfecting the Craft (early Yonghe 123, self-detonated after defeat in the Tianjian Alliance battle)

Li Canyang's entrance is usually — a laugh. Scarred all over, smiling on the battlefield. His greatest hobby is picking fights with masters; win is trophy, loss is lesson. In life no challenge he wouldn't take. He hates no one — he just wants to see how many moves you take.

His combat style grows wilder the longer he fights. Opens with Soul-Chasing Saber to probe, feels opponent strong enough activates Blood Shadow Step, presses with Soul-Chasing Saber, then Setting-Sun Slash for blood-for-blood burst. Cornered activates Heaven Demon Release — stats soar but self-damage per turn (HP). He won't insult opponents in combat — even the most hateful righteous xia, if they show a good move, he lavishly praises aloud.

He died without glory — surrounded by many masters in the Tianjian melee, Heaven Demon Release self-detonation, dealing grim damage to enemies in the final instant. But what he did before dying fit his character: he said "enough — thanks all for letting me fight to the end." Then closed his eyes. This may be true "martial art supreme" — weight no differing creed can erase.

—— Baixiaosheng's note: This old man dislikes the Blood Moon Cult, but doesn't dislike Li Canyang. He was used by Ye Xiuluo — his loyalty to Ye Xiuluo wasn't belief-identity, only that Ye Xiuluo gave him the best opponents. Li Canyang fought all his life, in the end never seriously thought "why fight." His death is the Blood Moon Cult's loss, but I think for Li Canyang himself, dying in combat is far cleaner than living dragged into intrigue.

> Classic line (Baixiaosheng's invention): "Don't preach benevolence and morality. I only want to know — how many moves can you take. If you take them, I won't kill you today — till I've trained more and come again."

Chu Huaisha shouldn't appear in this book. He's Third Realm, third-rate master, cultivates a Wind-Chasing Palm and five or six mechanism traps. But this man made me break the rule — because what he does is too "human."

### 14. Liu Rushi — No Blood Under the Qingshuang Sword

Title: Tianjian Alliance Qingshuang Sword Master, the five sects' only female leader

Realm: Fourth Realm · Perfecting the Craft

Liu Rushi is the only sect leader among the five great sword sects who has never killed in public. In this age of "sword drawn means blood seen," that's an anomaly. Her sword art is famed for speed — fast you can't even react. But her sword tip only points, never stabs. After the three-sword lake "Heaven" demonstration, voices doubting her never reappeared.

Between Duan Tianya and Lu Chenyuan, she's always the buffer. Not from fear of picking sides, but because she knows — if the Qingshuang Sect leans one way, the five-sect balance breaks. Her position is subtle: as the only female leader, some in the Jianghu disobey; as centrist, both extremes dissatisfied; as swordsman, her sword is strong enough yet never used for power struggle.

This old man asked after her master — Qingshuang Sect's previous leader "White Frost Sword," said to be a swordwoman who'd not even talk to men. What she passed Liu Rushi wasn't sword art, but a brain and the prettiest three-word creed: "use the sword to act, use the heart to be a person."

—— Baixiaosheng's note: If the Tianjian Alliance is a chessboard, Liu Rushi is true to her name — willow in wind, unbent suffices; is water, following the slope down, naturally finds the sea. Opposite Duan Tianya — Duan charges straight, Liu is silken thread. This old man privately thinks: the leader's seat truly falling to her isn't impossible. But she herself doesn't want it. The Jianghu's most frightening isn't too weak, is not wanting to win.

> Classic line (Baixiaosheng's invention): "I won't let anyone die under my sword. Not because I'm soft. But once I start, I fear I can't stop."

## Postscript

Writing to here, the tea has gone cold three times.

This chronicle front and back codes nearly ten thousand characters, recording the Jianghu affairs of Yonghe 120 to 123. Many years later looking back, perhaps these matters will seem passing clouds. But at this moment, they are real — every drop of blood, every choice, every life.

Some unsolved mysteries rest here. What does Ye Xiuluo really look like? Is Yuxu Zhenren dead or alive? What did Gongshu Xuan discover from the Wanxiang Grotto crystal? Who is that mysterious person who used Yellow Springs Step in Camel Lane? These suspensees, who knows when they'll be unveiled.

The next decade — whose world will it be? If Duan Tianya can steady, the Tianjian Alliance leader's seat is nine-tenths certain; if Liu Rufeng's new talents can cross the Third Realm within ten years, the Jianghu will be another heaven and earth; and if Ye Xiuluo survives and breaks the demon art's final layer... then the decade-later Jianghu pattern absolutely cannot be lightly judged.

This old man is only a spectator's eye. See much, yet change little. But I have an obsession — what happens in this Jianghu deserves to be recorded. Not to judge merit and fault, but to let later generations know: once a group of people, in this world, seriously lived.

Last line: Spring Yonghe 124, said someone near East Sea Putuo Island waters saw a blurred-face, almost-virtual figure standing still on the sea surface. That wasn't Lightfoot — it was standing on the waves. Who was it? Is there truly a Seventh Realm? Abbot Huijue may know, but he's gone chanting again.

The Jianghu is never finished.

—— Baixiaosheng, pen laid down, Autumn Yonghe 123

## How to Use This Table

The GM rolls a d10 (or d6, per the specific table) to determine the encounter type, then improvises. Each encounter includes: a type tag, brief description, data reference (pointing to 14_Enemy Quick Reference or 11_NPC Character Cards), and recommended Difficulty Class (DC) (if a check applies).

**Encounter Types**:
- ⚔️ Combat — a real-time combat encounter needing initiative
- 💬 Social — dialogue, negotiation, intel exchange
- ✨ Adventure — unexpected joy (or unexpected danger)
- 🌿 Environment — terrain, weather, natural phenomena
- 📜 Information — a chance to gain key info

**Reference Format**:
- `14_§N` = 14_Enemy Quick Reference entry N
- `11_§N` = 11_NPC Character Card entry N (NPC numbers see NPC Quick Index)

## Appendix · Imperial Martial Exam Rules

The triennial Imperial Martial Exam is the main channel by which the court absorbs Jianghu talent into the system. The exam is not simple combat — it comprises three stages: the literary test, the martial test, and the palace test, examining not only martial skill but "whether one can be of use to the court."

### Participation Conditions and Process

| Stage | Form | Check | Pass Condition | Elimination Consequence |
|------|------|------|---------|---------|
| **Registration** | Go to the capital/prefecture yamen, pay a 5-tael registration fee, receive a Martial Exam Waist Tablet | — | — | — |
| **Preliminary · Policy Essay** | Written exam — "What is a Xia?" "On the relation between martial art and law" (about 30 minutes) | Comprehension Difficulty Class (DC) 12 (or Charisma Difficulty Class (DC) 14 alternative — using ornate rhetoric to cover shallowness of content) | Pass to enter martial test | Eliminated — may retry in three years |
| **Preliminary · Physical** | Strength test (lift 200-jin stone lock) + Agility test (plum-post stakes) | Strength Difficulty Class (DC) 10 + Agility Difficulty Class (DC) 10 | Pass both | Single failure may be retaken once (Difficulty Class (DC) +2) |
| **Martial Test · Arena** | Three rounds of bouts against random opponents (non-lethal — hidden weapons and poison forbidden) | Three rounds of combat | Win at least two rounds | Eliminated — but losing one round to a later top scorer may still be recruited by Six Doors |
| **Palace Test** | Among the top three, personally interviewed by the Emperor/imperial commissioner | Charisma Difficulty Class (DC) 15 (respond with propriety) or Willpower Difficulty Class (DC) 14 (keep composure) | Decides final ranking — top scorer/second/third | Interview failure does not eliminate, but can only get third place |

### Rewards

| Rank | Reward |
|------|------|
| **Top Scorer (Zhuangyuan)** | ① Appointed Six Doors Deputy Bureau Chief (monthly salary 50 taels) ② Receives "Imperial Gold Token" — outranks officials by one grade ③ Choose one superior martial art (from the court arsenal) ④ Heroism +20 |
| **Second (Bangyan)** | ① Appointed Six Doors Steward (monthly salary 30 taels) ② Receives "Imperial Silver Token" ③ Choose one advanced martial art ④ Heroism +15 |
| **Third (Tanhua)** | ① Appointed Six Doors Probation Constable (monthly salary 15 taels) ② Receives "Imperial Bronze Token" ③ One entry-level martial art ④ Heroism +10 |
| **Failed but excellent performance** | Six Doors may proactively recruit — into the Six Doors probation system, monthly salary 8 taels |

### Opponent Generation

The GM draws opponents for the three martial-test rounds from the following random table (may choose whether to repeat):

| d6 | Opponent | Realm | Style |
|----|------|------|------|
| 1 | Border garrison general's son | Second Realm | Firm heavy spear art — high Strength, weak defense |
| 2 | Declined-gentry scion | Second Realm | Swift sword — high Agility, medium damage |
| 3 | Jianghu independent cultivator | Third Realm | Counter-attack flow — skilled at borrowing force to deflect force |
| 4 | Serving constable's brother | Third Realm | Official saber — balanced attack/defense, uses official authority to intimidate |
| 5 | Western-Regions envoy warrior | Third Realm | Curved saber + lightfoot — high mobility, low Qi |
| 6 | Past-year failed veteran general | Third Realm | Rich experience — high Comprehension, skilled at reading moves |

### GM Pacing Suggestions

- **Do not turn the martial exam into a mere combat level**: the preliminary policy essay can draw out the character's view of heroism and values; the palace-test interview can show the character's mettle.
- **Make the exam a story, not a checklist**: at the examination ground you may meet important NPCs (future colleagues/opponents/lead figures); someone peddles "inside slots" at the ground — investigate or not is a moral choice.
- **The exam is not the only way out**: failing is not failure — Six Doors may proactively recruit because they noticed some aspect of the character at the ground.
- **Officialdom is no ideal realm**: after entering the system, the character will find faction struggles, corruption, and gray zones within the court — this is a later plot hook.
