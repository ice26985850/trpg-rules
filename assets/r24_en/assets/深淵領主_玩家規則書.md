# Chapter One: Core Rules

## 1.1 Dice System

This rulebook uses the **d6 Dice Pool** system.

| Die Face | Result |
|------|------|
| **6** | Success + **Explosion**: counts as 1 success, grants +1 extra die (can chain) |
| **5** | Success: counts as 1 success |
| 1–4 | No effect |

Expected successes per die = 0.40 (5–6 success rule, 1/3 chance + explosion recursion).

### Dice Pool Composition

```
Dice Pool = Attribute Dice + Skill Dice + Environmental Modifier
```

| Source | Description | Cap |
|------|------|------|
| Attribute Dice | Attribute value = base dice (1–5) | 5d6 |
| Skill Dice | Skill level provides extra dice (0–5) | +5d6 |
| Environmental Bonus | Gear/terrain/aid temporary bonus dice | +3d6 |
| Environmental Penalty | Adverse condition removes dice | -3d6 |
| Advantage | Fixed dice added before roll | +2d6 |
| Disadvantage | Remove highest N dice from pool before roll | -2d6 |

**Dice Pool Cap**: No single check exceeds **10d6**.

### Degree of Success Levels

| Net Successes | Result Tier | Description |
|----------|---------|------|
| 0 | **Failure** | Action does not achieve goal, possible minor negative consequence |
| 1 | **Partial Success** | Achieves goal but with a cost, delay, or reduced effect |
| 2 | **Full Success** | Achieves goal cleanly |
| 3 | **Critical Success** | Exceeds expectations, with extra benefit |
| 4+ | **Legendary Success** | Effect doubled or unlocks hidden benefit |

### Difficulty Levels

| Difficulty | Successes Required | Typical Scenario |
|------|-----------|---------|
| Easy | 1 | Order loyal minions, basic construction |
| Standard | 2 | Build room, interrogate T2 captive, recruit monster |
| Hard | 3 | Interrogate high-Will captive, magic trap, deep excavation |
| Extreme | 4 | Convert legendary captive, ultimate ritual |
| Epic | 5+ | World-altering magic |

### Key Probability Quick Reference

| Dice Pool | P(≥1 success) | P(≥2 successes) | P(≥3 successes) | Expected Successes |
|------|-----------|-----------|-----------|-----------|
| 1d6 | 33.3% | — | — | 0.40 |
| 2d6 | 55.6% | 11.1% | — | 0.80 |
| 3d6 | 70.4% | 33.3% | 11.1% | 1.20 |
| 4d6 | 80.2% | 48.1% | 21.0% | 1.60 |
| 5d6 | 86.8% | 60.5% | 32.2% | 2.00 |
| 6d6 | 91.2% | 70.8% | 43.7% | 2.40 |
| 7d6 | 94.1% | 78.9% | 54.4% | 2.80 |
| 8d6 | 96.1% | 85.1% | 64.1% | 3.20 |

> Sweet spot: 3–5d6. At 3d6, standard difficulty success is ~33%; at 5d6, ~60%.

## 1.3 Check Types

### Standard Check

1. GM announces difficulty level
2. Player builds dice pool
3. Roll and count successes
4. Net successes ≥ difficulty = success

### Opposed Check

Both sides build their dice pools and roll simultaneously. **Net Successes = active side successes − opposed side successes**.

### Critical Success and Critical Failure

- **Critical Success**: net successes ≥ 3 or 3+ sixes → extra benefit
- **Critical Failure**: successes = 0 and 3+ ones appear in the pool → unexpected negative event

## 1.5 Game Example

> The following is a typical turn-flow example to help newcomers understand the game's rhythm.

**Management Phase**:
*GM*: "Another Abyss week. In your throne hall, a gargoyle crouches in the corner polishing its wings, cultists pore over ancient tomes in the research chamber. The rhythmic clinking from the mine drifts up—skeleton soldiers need no rest."

*Player (Ancient Wyrm, Lv2)*: "Good. First I assign monster posts—gargoyle to the outer layer guard, cultists back to research, skeletons to the mine. Then I'll spend Building Materials to excavate a second small space."

*GM*: "Roll IND + Engineering check."

*Player rolls*: "Industry (IND)=4, Engineering=1… I roll 5d6. Got 6, 5, 3, 2, 1—2 successes! Bonus die… 3. Total successes=2."

*GM*: "Full Success! You excavated a medium cavern—and magical crystals glimmer on the rock wall. You found a mana node; that space's mana-room efficiency gains a permanent +1 die."

*Player*: "Great! I spend ◇ to build a trap workshop here. Then end of turn—roll the invasion check?"

**Invasion Phase**:
*GM*: "Roll Infamy (INF)(2)+1d6… got 5. 2+5=7, under 8—safe this week. But your scout outpost notices firelight in the distance. Next turn, someone may come."

**Second Turn**:
*GM*: "Invasion check: INF(2)+1d6=7… wait, this time a 6. 2+6=8—invasion! Two adventurers approaching."

*Player*: "I deploy the gargoyle to the outer layer, recall cultists to the combat post to assist. The trap workshop's rockfall trap is in place."

*GM*: "Adventurers enter the outer layer. Warrior Alan steps on the rockfall trap—2d6=7 damage! But he has 3 HP left, pushes on. Rogue Mira spots the gargoyle. Combat begins!"

> This is the rhythm loop of the Abyss Lord: expand → build → assign → (possibly) invade → post-battle settlement → loop again.

## 2.3 Territory Expansion System ⭐

> *From a tiny throne hall, dig into the dark depths.*

### Spaces and Excavation

A dungeon is made of "spaces". Initial dungeon: **Throne Hall (free) + 2 small spaces (undeveloped)**.

| Space Tier | Name | Room Slots | Excavation Difficulty | Building Material Cost | Turns | Unlock Condition |
|----------|------|--------|---------|---------|------|---------|
| — | Throne Hall | 1 | — | — | — | Free initially |
| Lv1 | Small Cavern | 1 | 1 success | ■×2 | 1 | Available from start |
| Lv2 | Medium Cavern | 2 | 2 successes | ■×5 | 2 | Depth (DEP)≥2 |
| Lv3 | Large Dungeon Hall | 3 | 3 successes | ■×8 | 3 | Depth (DEP)≥5 |
| Lv4 | Abyssal Grand Hall | 4 | 4 successes | ■×12, ◇×3 | 4 | Depth (DEP)≥7 |

### Two-Step Construction Flow

```
Step 1: Excavate and unlock a space (spend materials + time, roll IND check)
Step 2: Within the unlocked space, choose a room type to build
```

You must **excavate before building**. You cannot build directly in an unexcavated space. A space's room capacity is set by its tier.

### Excavation Check

**Dice Pool**: IND + Engineering skill
**Difficulty**: see Space Tier table
**Net Success Results**:

| Net Success | Result |
|--------|------|
| 0 | Excavation failed, materials doubled, this spot cannot be excavated again |
| 1 | Unlock space, attached discovery canceled |
| 2+ | Full success, unlock space + roll d6 for discovery |

### Excavation Discovery Table (d6)

| d6 | Discovery | Effect |
|----|------|------|
| 1 | Nothing special | Standard space only |
| 2–3 | Ore Vein | That space's mine output +50% (permanent) |
| 4 | Mana Node | That space's magic-room efficiency +1 die (permanent) |
| 5 | Underground Lake | Monster upkeep -50%, may build aquaculture |
| 6 | Ancient Ruins | Random treasure / magic item / hidden plot thread |

### Expansion Risk Table (d6, triggered on excavation failure)

| d6 | Risk | Effect |
|----|------|------|
| 1 | Cave-in | Passage collapses, adjacent rooms damaged |

### Equipment Rules

- Each monster may equip at most **1 weapon + 1 armor**
- The Lord may equip at most **1 Lord Armament**
- Equipment may be swapped freely (free action, during Management Phase)
- Monster dies → equipment drops back to dungeon (recoverable), unless "captured" by an adventurer in battle (Boss adventurers have 1/6 chance to capture equipment)
- Equipment may be sold as trade goods to other dungeons (at trade value × relationship coefficient)

See §3.8.5 "Enchanting Spells"—enchanted equipment stacks extra magic effects atop base forged equipment.

## 2.4.2 Enchanting System ⭐⭐⭐

Enchanting infuses the Abyss energy within Mana Crystals into equipment or creatures, granting them supernatural properties.

### Enchanting Procedure

1. **Prepare**: own Forge Lv2 + Research Chamber (satisfies "Arcane Workshop" synergy)
2. **Choose Target**: a forged piece of equipment or a monster
3. **Choose Enchant Type**: pick from the enchant table below
4. **Enchant Check**: roll `Arcana (ARC) + Enchanting skill`, difficulty per enchant table
5. **Spend Resources**: pay ◇ and ●
6. **Check Result**:
   - Failure (0 successes): resources spent, enchant not applied. May retry
   - Partial Success (1 success): enchant applied but at half strength (fades after 3 turns, needs re-enchant)
   - Full Success (2 successes): enchant stable
   - Critical Success (3+ successes): enchant strength doubled or + random extra effect

### Enchant Types

#### Elemental Enchant (weapon/armor)

| Enchant | ◇ Cost | Difficulty | Effect |
|------|:---:|:---:|------|
| **Flame Enchant** | 3 | 2 | Equipped monster attack +1 fire damage; target burns 1/turn ×2 turns |
| **Frost Enchant** | 3 | 2 | Equipped monster attack adds slow: target movement -1 room (1 turn) |
| **Lightning Enchant** | 4 | 3 | Equipped monster attack may jump to adjacent enemy (second target CP-2 dice) |
| **Dark Erosion Enchant** | 5+●1 | 3 | Equipped monster attack recovers HP = damage×0.5 (rounded up) |
| **Holy Light Resistance** | 3 | 3 | Equipped monster takes 50% less Holy Light/divine damage (specialized vs Order of Light) |

#### Enhancement Enchant (weapon/armor)

| Enchant | ◇ Cost | Difficulty | Effect |
|------|:---:|:---:|------|
| **Keen** | 2 | 1 | CP extra +1 (stacks atop equipment base CP) |
| **Sturdy** | 2 | 1 | Defense die extra +1 (stacks atop equipment base defense) |
| **Vampiric** | 5+●2 | 4 | Recover 1 HP per 3 damage dealt |
| **Indestructible** | 4 | 3 | Equipment cannot be captured/destroyed. Always recovered on monster death |
| **Lightweight** | 2 | 1 | Removes weight penalty (equipment with -1 movement also unaffected) |

#### Creature Enchant (applied directly to monster, no equipment slot)

| Enchant | ◇ Cost | Difficulty | Effect |
|------|:---:|:---:|------|
| **Magic Skin** | 3 | 2 | Monster gains magic defense dice pool = Arcana (ARC)/2 (rounded up) |
| **Elemental Bloodline** | 4+●1 | 3 | Monster gains chosen element attack (fire/ice/lightning/acid); +50% vs opposite element |
| **Regeneration** | 5+●2 | 4 | Monster auto-recovers 1 HP per turn |
| **Shadow Affinity** | 3 | 2 | Monster gains Stealth (CUN+1 die); +1 attack die in darkness |
| **Loyalty Mark** | 2+●1 | 1 | Monster LOY permanent +1 (no stacking, 1 time per monster) |

### Enchant Stacking Rules

- Each equipment slot holds at most **2 enchants** (Lord Armament holds 3)
- Same-name enchants don't stack—when identical, take the highest-tier effect
- Different-name enchants stack
- Enchant effects persist when equipment is removed/transferred

### Enchant Trade

Enchanted equipment's trade value rises greatly: base equipment value × (1 + enchant ◇ cost × 50%).
Example: Iron Claw (◆8) + Flame Enchant (◇3) → trade value = 8 + 3×0.5 = ◆9.5 → ◆10.

Enchanted equipment is a popular trade good among NPC dungeons—especially Holy Light Resistance gear (all dungeons in Order of Light threat zones demand +50% premium).

## 2.6 Monster Assignment System ⭐

> Each monster has two dimensions of use—management vs combat. Assignment is the core strategy.

### Monster Dual-Dimension Attributes

| Attribute | Meaning | Use |
|------|------|------|
| **Management (MV)** (Management Value) | Contribution when deployed to production/management | Management dice pool |
| **Combat (CV)** (Combat Value) | Contribution when deployed to defense/combat | Combat dice pool |

### Assignment Rules

- At the start of each turn, every monster must be assigned to **Management Group** or **Combat Group**
- A monster cannot contribute to both management and combat in the same turn
- Assignment is a free action (costs no action points)
- Emergency Conscription: when an invasion occurs, management monsters may be urgently pulled into combat at the cost of 1 action → first-turn dice pool -1 → monsters with Loyalty (LOY)≤2 need a loyalty-shock check

### Management Dispatch Dice Pool

> Total management dice pool = Industry (IND) + relevant skill + floor(Σ Management (MV) ÷ 2)

### Production Output Formula

> After a successful production check, resources are produced per the following rules:

| Dice Pool Source | Allocation |
|---------|------|
| IND dice + skill dice successes | Allocated to **producing room** (each success = that room's Lv output ×1). Prioritize Mine (■), Research Chamber (◇), Treasury (◆). Unabsorbed successes → ◆×1 |
| Monster MV bonus-dice successes | Allocated to **the room the monster belongs to** (e.g., skeleton in mine → Building Materials; cultist in research chamber → Mana Crystals). No designated room → corresponding monster's upkeep resource ×1 |

> **Example**: Ancient Wyrm (IND=4, Engineering=1) rolls 5d6 → 3 successes. 2 Gargoyles (ΣMV=4, bonus 2d6) → 1 success. Mine Lv1 present, Research Chamber Lv1 present. Allocation: IND's 3 successes → Mine produces 3■ (Mine Lv1 = ■×1/success). Monster MV's 1 success → Gargoyle in mine → extra 1■. Total turn output: 4■.

### Combat Defense Dice Pool

> Total combat dice pool = Warfare (WAR) + monster command/tactical deployment + floor(Σ Combat (CV) ÷ 2)

### Efficiency Guide

| Monster Group | Σ Management (MV)/Combat (CV) | Bonus Dice | Efficiency (dice/monster) |
|--------|--------|------|-------------|
| 4 Skeleton Soldiers (Combat (CV) 1×4) | 4 | 2 | 0.50 |
| 2 Hellhounds (Combat (CV) 3×2) | 6 | 3 | 1.50 |
| 1 Elemental Construct (Combat (CV) 4) | 4 | 2 | 2.00 |

> The floor(Σ/2) formula makes high-quality monsters more efficient than low-quality swarms, encouraging pursuit of elites over quantity.

### Ability Upgrade Mechanism

#### Upgrade Paths (four routes)

|------|------|------|------|
| **🟢 Training Ground Grind** | Monster completes training in Training Ground (base=4 turns/enhanced=8 turns/expert=16 turns) | Training Ground Lv2+ (enhanced), Lv3 (expert); each training costs ◆×(3/6/12) | Ability up 1 tier |

#### Upgrade Example

> Your **Gargoyle** initially rolled d66=21, gaining "Fast Builder (Common)": build check +1 die. You put it in Training Ground Lv2, spend ◆×6, after 8 turns of training—ability upgrades to "Fast Builder (Enhanced)": build check +2 dice. Later, in the holy war against the Order of Light, this gargoyle participated in 12 battles and personally shattered the Order's Boss shield—after battle, live combat evolution auto-triggers: "Fast Builder (Expert)": build check +3 dice, build time halved.

|---------|:---:|------|

## 2.8 Captive Management System ⭐

### Captive Flow (six stages)

```
Defeat adventurer (HP to 0 = unconscious) → Imprison → Interrogate/torture/charm → WIL lowered → Collapse or Convert
```

### Captive Attributes

| Attribute | Meaning | Range |
|------|------|------|
| Willpower (WIL) | Will: mental resistance to interrogation/brainwashing | 1–5 |
| PL | Profession Level: captive's class skill level | 1–5 |
| Stamina (STA) | Physical condition, determines labor dice pool | 1–5 |

### Will Resistance

> Dungeon Lord rolls **Dominion (DOM) + Interrogation skill** vs captive rolls **Willpower (WIL)**

| Net Successes | WIL Change | Side Effect |
|----------|---------|---------|
| ≤0 | No change | Captive resists, GM secretly rolls vague intel |
| 1 | Willpower (WIL) -1 | Captive begins to waver |
| 2 | Willpower (WIL) -1 | Next interrogation captive WIL dice pool -1 die |
| 3+ | Willpower (WIL) -2 | Will greatly weakened, gain confirmed intel |

### Five Will-Lowering Means

| Means | Attribute Used | Effect | Risk |
|------|---------|------|------|
| Interrogation | Dominion (DOM) | Willpower (WIL)-1 (stable) | None |
| Torture | Warfare (WAR) | Willpower (WIL)-2 (efficient) | STA-2, captive may be crippled |
| Charm | Dominion (DOM) | Slow but accelerates conversion | Spend Dark Coins |
| Isolation | — | Passive: WIL-1 per turn (first time only) | Needs solitary cell |
| Brainwashing | Arcana (ARC) | Willpower (WIL)-3 (strongest) | May collapse (WIL instantly to 0) |

### WIL-Zero Endings (roll 2d6)

| 2d6 | Ending | Effect |
|-----|------|------|
| 2–3 | **Mental Collapse** | Captive becomes a worthless wreck, worth only 1 Soul Essence |
| 4–6 | **Numb Obedience** | Labor efficiency 50%, won't flee, but no special value |
| 7–9 | **Conversion Success** | Becomes loyal minion, retains 50% class skills, may serve as room supervisor |
| 10–11 | **Awakening Resonance** | Full conversion! Retains all class skills, LOY=4, gains 1 random management ability |
| 12 | **Dark Awakening** | Converted and potential erupts—all attributes +1, LOY=5, may develop into plot NPC |

**Means Modifier**: Charm +1 ending die, Torture -1 ending die, Brainwashing -2 ending die.

### Five Labor Types

| Labor | Dice Pool Formula | Output |
|------|---------|------|
| Excavate/Expand | STA + PL (warrior +1 die) | Building Materials × successes |
| Trap Making | STA + PL (rogue +1 die) | Trap components × successes |
| Magic Research | STA + PL (mage +1 die) | Mana Crystals × successes |
| Resource Production | STA + PL | Corresponding resource × successes |
| Combat Cannon Fodder | PL dice pool (Combat = PL) | STA-1 per battle |

### Escape Check

At end of each turn, captives with WIL≥2 attempt escape:
- Captive rolls WIL dice pool vs dungeon rolls DEF dice pool + Prison level
- Captive success > dungeon → escape launches, enter pursuit phase
- Pursuit: captive rolls PL dice pool vs dungeon rolls Warfare (WAR)+deterrence tactics (Hellhound +2 dice)
- Captive escapes → remove captive, may expose dungeon intel

### Moral Paths

| Path | Means Preference | Advantage | Disadvantage |
|------|---------|------|---------|
| **Tyrant** | Torture + Brainwashing | Fast conversion | Low conversion quality (Numb Obedience), high captive death rate |
| **Benevolent Lord** | Charm + Interrogation | High conversion quality (Awakening Resonance) | Slow conversion |
| **Pragmatist** | Mixed | Highest flexibility | No specialty bonus |

### Per-Turn Settlement Flow

### Core Resource Details

**Dark Coins (◆)**: the dark currency circulating in the Abyss. Not ordinary gold—each Dark Coin is forged from Abyssal mana, glowing dim red in darkness. Surface gold cannot be used underground; adventurers' gold must be "converted" in the treasury to Dark Coins (1 surface gold = 1d2 Dark Coins, rolled). Dark Coins are the foundation of the dungeon economy, usable for trade, recruitment, bribery, magic-material purchase. The Ancient Wyrm archetype has natural affinity (treasury interest).

**Mana Crystals (◇)**: solidified Abyssal mana. Naturally forms near mana nodes, violet-black translucent crystals. Touching them gives a faint pulse—that is Nexis's heartbeat. Mana Crystals power magic rituals, combat arts, magic traps. Mana-intensive dungeons (MAG≥8) may slowly generate their own. Mad Sorcerer and Necromancer Lord consume large amounts.

**Building Materials (■)**: standard unit for stone, wood (underground fungus-wood), metal ore dug underground. 1 unit builds 1 wall segment, 1 door, or 3 sq m of floor. Three quality tiers: rough stone (base), obsidian (defense +1), abyssal alloy (defense +2 and magic-conductive). Mine produces base materials; high-quality materials need trade or special excavation.

**Soul Essence (●)**: Abyssal energy extracted from a defeated adventurer's life force. A floating ghost-blue orb containing the defeated one's last wisp of consciousness—a trained Abyss Lord can read vague memories from it. Soul Essence is the core fuel of undead spells, the price of sacrifice rituals, and the currency to recruit undead/demon monsters. The Order of Light views Soul Essence as the deepest blasphemy—being found holding large amounts invites religious trial.

#### Forging Material Details

**Iron Ingot**: dark-gray metal ingot refined from Abyss iron ore. Denser than surface iron—forms naturally under underground high pressure, found everywhere in mines. Entry material for basic equipment, also circulates as "small change" in trade (public rate 2 Dark Coins = 1 iron ingot).

**Obsidian Steel**: reinforced metal formed when iron ingots are long steeped near mana nodes. Surface has violet-black glassy luster, rings crystalline when struck. Needs Forge Lv2 furnace temperature to shape. Core material of fine equipment—tight supply-demand; Goblin Tinkerer dungeons demand it most.

**Abyssal Alloy**: rarest ore of the deepest layer, forms only where Nexis's breath seeps deepest. Dim green, faintly self-glowing. Needs no fuel to forge—abyssal alloy auto-heats to forging temperature on contact with Mana Crystals. Sole material for epic and legendary equipment. Extremely low yield—a full Lv15 abyssal set needs long accumulation or heavy trade.

> **Storage**: the three forging materials don't count against core resource storage cap, but each has max storage = Wealth (WLH)×5 units.

## 2.11 Character Creation Flow (Quick Guide)

Creating a Dungeon Lord character takes just seven steps:

### Step 1: Choose Archetype
Pick one of the six archetypes. Each determines your attribute bonuses, core abilities, and playstyle.
> Newcomer recommendation: **Abyssal Overlord** (frontal crushing, intuitive) or **Ancient Wyrm** (economy build, high fault tolerance).
> Advanced challenge: **Mad Sorcerer** (high-risk high-reward) or **Shadow Council** (intel build, needs foresight).

### Step 2: Allocate Lord Attributes
You have **12 attribute points** to allocate. All five attributes start at 1; raising by 1 costs 1 point.
Archetype bonus stacks after allocation (e.g., Mad Sorcerer Arcana (ARC)+2 → allocate ARC=3+2=5).
- Suggest prioritizing your archetype's main attribute to 4 (reach 5 after bonus)
- At least allocate 2 points to DOM (Dominion)—interrogating captives and diplomacy require it

### Step 3: Allocate Skill Points
You have **6 initial skill points**. Lv0→Lv1 costs 1 point, Lv1→Lv2 costs 1 point.
- Prioritize upgrading archetype-related skills (Necromancer Lord→Ritual Master, Demon→Tactical Command…)
- At least invest in 1 engineering/construction skill—excavation expansion requires it

### Step 4: Record Archetype Abilities
Write down your archetype's passive and active abilities. This is your core combat/management advantage.

### Step 5: Configure Initial Dungeon
All archetypes share the same initial config:
- Throne Hall Lv1 (free)
- 2 small spaces (undeveloped, need excavation)
- Initial monsters per archetype table (3–5)
- Initial resources per archetype table

### Step 6: Record Monsters and Roll Management Ability
Roll d66 for each initial monster to gain a random management ability (see §2.7). Mark monster positions on the dungeon layout sketch.

### Step 7: Name and Fill Card
Name the dungeon and Lord, fill the character card. Done!

### Quick Start Suggestions
| What to do on Turn 1 | Suggestion |
|------|------|
| Assign monster posts | At least 1 combat post (defense), rest management posts (produce resources) |
| First excavation | Spend 1 action + Building Materials×2, roll IND+Engineering check, dig first small space |
| First build | Recommend: Trap Workshop (auto-defense) or Mine (steady output) |
| Wait for invasion | After Management Phase, roll invasion check (INF+1d6≥8) |

> **Newcomer GM Tip**: Lv1 first invasion uses 2 T1 adventurers (warrior + rogue), letting players experience the full combat loop without being too hard.



## 2.12 Co-Management Mode (1 GM + 2~4 players)

When multiple players play together, all players **jointly manage the same dungeon**. Each plays a "Abyssal Lieutenant"—the Dungeon Lord's right-hand.

### Role Division

| Player Role | Responsible Area | Main Attribute | Exclusive Action/turn |
|------|------|------|------|
| **Chief Lord** | Final decisions, diplomacy, expedition | Dominion (DOM)/Warfare (WAR) | 1 diplomacy/expedition action |
| **Defense Commander** | Combat deployment, monster dispatch, trap planning | Warfare (WAR)/Cunning (CUN) | 1 free monster redeploy |
| **Management Steward** | Construction, resource output, captive management | Industry (IND) | Build/upgrade check +1 die |
| **Magic Advisor** | Magic research, rituals, spell prep | Arcana (ARC) | Casting ◇ cost -1 (min 1) |

**Roles optional**: 1~4 players; unchosen roles handled by GM or doubled by players.

### Decision Mechanism

- **Lord Council**: before each turn, players discuss and decide the turn's strategy (5-min limit)
- **Veto**: Chief Lord has 1 veto per turn, may overturn any single person's decision
- **Expert Respect**: checks in a specific domain are rolled by that domain's owner, others may assist (+1 die)
- **Tie**: roll 1d6, odd = maintain status quo, even = Chief Lord decides

### Conflict and Fun

| Mechanism | Effect |
|------|------|
| **Lieutenant Ambition** | Every 3 turns, each lieutenant secretly rolls 1d6. A 6 = that lieutenant proposes a "personal goal" (e.g., "I'll lead the interrogation this turn", "I'll deploy the traps myself"). Complete → +1 EXP reward |
| **Loyalty Test** | If Chief Lord ignores the same lieutenant's advice two turns running, that lieutenant's assist-check dice pool -1 (morale hit) |
| **Joint Ritual** | Multi-player ritual check, each extra player +1 die (cap +3 dice) |

### Multi-Player Combat Operation

- Each lieutenant may directly command 1–2 monsters (control their actions)
- Chief Lord controls overall tactics
- Different defense layers may be commanded by different lieutenants

> **Design Intent**: The core of co-management is "cooperative decision + role division", avoiding each player going their own way. The Lieutenant Ambition mechanism injects moderate competitive fun into long-term cooperation.

## 3.1 Lord Combat System

A Dungeon Lord is no weakling hiding in the throne hall issuing orders. The Lord may fight in person, leave the dungeon on expedition, and clash with other dungeons.

### 3.1.1 Lord Combat Attributes

**Lord HP**: `(Dominion (DOM) + Warfare (WAR) × 2 + Dungeon Level`

| Stage | Typical Attributes | HP Example | Description |
|------|---------|--------|------|
| Lv1 New Lord | Dominion (DOM)2 + Warfare (WAR)2 | 9 | Withstand 2–3 medium attacks |
| Lv5 Grown Lord | Dominion (DOM)3 + Warfare (WAR)3 | 17 | May frontally engage T3 adventurers |
| Lv9 Abyss Lord | Dominion (DOM)4 + Warfare (WAR)4 | 25 | Hold the inner layer alone |
| Lv15 Legendary Overlord | Dominion (DOM)5 + Warfare (WAR)5 | 35 | May trade blows with legendary adventurer Boss |

**Lord Defense Dice Pool** (three stances, + Dungeon Level÷3 growth bonus):

| Defense Stance | Dice Pool | Use Case |
|---------|---------|---------|
| Will Shield (default) | DOM dice pool + Dungeon Level÷3 | Spell-type lord, forms invisible barrier with Abyssal majesty |
| Dodge/Parry | WAR dice pool + Dungeon Level÷3 | Warfare-type lord, combat-experience dodge |
| Magic Barrier | ARC dice pool + Dungeon Level÷3 | Magic-type lord, arcane energy shield |

**Lord Attack Dice Pool**:

| Attack Type | Dice Pool | Archetype |
|---------|------|---------|
| Physical Attack | WAR dice pool | Abyssal Overlord, Ancient Wyrm (melee) |
| Magic Attack | ARC dice pool | Necromancer Lord, Mad Sorcerer, Ancient Wyrm (dragon breath) |
| Engineering Attack | IND dice pool | Goblin Tinkerer |
| Cunning Attack | CUN dice pool | Shadow Council |

**HP Recovery**: rest in throne hall recovers `Dominion (DOM) + Dungeon Level` HP/turn.

### 3.1.2 Lord Deployment Rules

Lord may spend 1 action in Phase 0 (Preparation Phase) to deploy to any defense layer:

| Deployed Layer | Lord Bonus | Throne Hall Penalty |
|--------|---------|-----------|
| Core Layer | All attribute checks +2 dice (ultimate home field) | None |
| Inner Layer | Arcana (ARC)/DOM check +1 die (home advantage) | — |
| Middle Layer | Monster Lair/Training Ground synergy bonus | Throne Hall DEF to 0 |
| Outer Layer | Scout Outpost early-warning synced real-time | Throne Hall DEF to 0, all dungeon checks -1 die |

**"Lord Present" Bonus**: same-layer monsters LOY+1, same-layer monster morale won't rout in battle, enemy adventurer morale -1, may use reactive guard on adjacent monsters.

### 3.1.3 Lord Combat Actions

Lord uses same action economy as monsters in battle (move + standard + bonus + reaction):

| Action Type | Lord Exclusive Options |
|---------|------------|
| **Standard Action** | Normal attack, cast combat art, use archetype active ability, command monster (forfeit attack to give same-layer monster +1 die and immediate action) |
| **Bonus Action** | Switch attack dice pool, brief command (monster move +1 room), quick combat art, mana charge (1● → 1◇ / 1 per battle) |
| **Reaction** | Guard adjacent monster, interrupt adventurer casting (ARC opposed), opportunity attack, dark counter (after being hit spend 1◇ magic counter) |
| **Move** | Any room on same layer |

### 3.1.4 Lord Defeated — Banishment

When Lord HP hits 0, the Lord is **not killed** (Abyss Lords are undying), but banished back to the throne hall:

| Stage | Effect |
|------|------|
| Banishment Blast | All adjacent units take DOM dice pool damage (half to allies) |
| Soul Repatriation | Teleport to throne hall, 1d3 turns to reform flesh (cannot act/use abilities) |
| Whole-Dungeon Penalty | All checks Disadvantage -3 (remove highest 3 dice) ×2 turns, all monster LOY -1, all archetype abilities disabled |
| Resource Loss | Lose 20% current Dark Coins, that invasion wave gains extra +3 EXP |

Consecutive banishment (defeated again in same invasion): Disadvantage upgraded to -5 ×3 turns, LOY -1 again, recovery speed halved.

## 3.3 Duel Mechanics

When an adventurer Boss (Phase 2+) is on the same layer as the Lord, the Boss challenges the Lord to a duel.

### Duel Three Stages

```
A. Standoff → DOM opposed (Lord vs Boss will dice pool), winner +1 attack die next turn
B. Clash → both roll attack vs defense, damage = net successes (pure skill, no weapon bonus)
C. Settlement → HP≤0 ends, or choose continue/retreat
```

### Win/Loss Effects

| Lord Defeats Boss | Boss Defeats Lord |
|-------------|------------|
| All enemy morale -2, immediate rout check | Lord banished to throne hall (see §3.1.4) |
| Boss capture chance +30%, WIL-1 | Whole-dungeon penalty + monster LOY-2 |
| Infamy (INF)+1 | Dungeon intel leaked, next invasion AI upgraded |
| Lord recovers 1d6 HP | Boss morale +2 |

### 3.4.1 Abyssal Embassy (new room)

| Attribute | Content |
|------|------|
| Build Condition | Depth (DEP)≥3, Dominion (DOM)≥2, ■×6+◇×4+◆×10, 2 turns |
| Lv1 | Unlock 1 diplomacy slot. May dispatch envoy for diplomacy check |

### 3.4.2 Diplomacy Relation Dice Pool

> Relation value range **-10 ~ +10**, initial **0 (neutral stranger)**

**Diplomacy Check**: `Dominion (DOM) + Diplomacy skill + Embassy bonus` vs `min(target dungeon INF, 10)`

| Net Successes | Relation Δ | Effect |
|:---:|:---:|------|
| ≤-3 | -2 | Diplomatic disaster—other side views as insult, no diplomacy this turn |
| -2~-1 | -1 | Diplomatic misstep—relation cools |
| 0 | 0 | No progress—may retry next turn |
| 1 | +1 | Initial success—unlock basic trade |
| 2 | +1 | Full success—may make 1 simple request |
| 3 | +2 | Critical success—may sign 1 treaty |
| 4+ | +2 | Legendary diplomacy—may sign 2 treaties or military alliance, gain gift |

### 3.4.3 Relation Levels

| Relation Value | Level | Trade Price | May Request |
|:---:|------|:---:|------|
| -10~-6 | Sworn Enemy | 400% | None. Other may send assassin |
| -5~-1 | Cold | 200% | None. Other refuses non-trade interaction |
| 0~3 | Neutral | 100% | Basic intel |
| 4~7 | Friendly | 80% | Intel sharing, captive exchange |
| 8~10 | Ally | 60% | May request reinforcements (1–2 monsters assist defense) |

### 3.4.4 Treaties

On Critical Success (MoS≥3) may propose signing a treaty:

| Treaty | Effect | Condition | Duration |
|------|------|------|---------|
| Non-Aggression | Neither may actively lower relation. If one attacked, other may not intervene | MoS≥3, relation≥2 | Permanent (may tear up) |
| Trade Agreement | Price locked 80%, both trade checks +1 die | MoS≥3, relation≥3 | Permanent |
| Intel Sharing | Auto-exchange 1 intel/turn | MoS≥3, relation≥5 | Permanent |

**Tear Up Treaty**: relation -3, Infamy (INF)+2 (betrayal infamy). Torn-up side gains revenge casus (next diplomacy +2 dice).

## 3.7 Lord Expedition System

A Lord may spend 1 full turn leaving the dungeon for the outer world.

### 3.7.1 Expedition Destinations

| Destination | Check | Difficulty | Base Reward | Typical Scenario |
|--------|------|:---:|------|------|
| Surface Recon | Cunning (CUN)+Espionage | 2 | Reveal next invasion intel (Tier/headcount/class/weakness) | Scout adventurer camp |
| Dungeon Diplomacy | Dominion (DOM)+Diplomacy | see §3.4 | Face-to-face visit +2 dice (better than envoy) | Sign treaty/negotiate |
| Abyssal Trade | Industry (IND)+Negotiation | 2 | Find rare goods, price locked ×0.75 | Abyssal black-market spree |
| Raid Camp | Warfare (WAR)+Tactics | 3 | Weaken next invasion (-1 head or enemy CP-1) | Preemptive strike |
| Ancient Ruins | Arcana (ARC)+Arcane | 3 | Hidden knowledge/artifact shard/unlock Lv12+ advance | Lost-civilization exploration |

### 3.7.2 Expedition Risk (d66)

Roll d66 at expedition end:

| d66 | Risk | Effect | Response |
|:---:|------|------|------|
| 11–15 | Patrol Encounter | 1d3+1 adventurers (Tier = Infamy (INF)÷3), Lord fights alone | WAR combat / CUN stealth avoid |
| 16–21 | Spotted on Surface | Infamy (INF)+1d2. Undead/Demon → next invasion +50% | Cunning (CUN)+Stealth |
| 22–26 | Environment Damage | HP-1d3 (undead sunlight / demon holy-mark etc.) | Auto-take |
| 31–36 | Lost/Trapped | Expedition extended 1 turn, spend ◆×3 | CUN reorient |
| 41–45 | Hostile Ambush | Hostile dungeon sends monster (CP = other's WAR+2) | Warfare (WAR)/Cunning (CUN)/Dominion (DOM) |
| 46–52 | Unexpected Find | d6: 1=vein 2=treasure 3=relic 4=monster 5=shortcut 6=ally | — |
| 53–56 | Dungeon Attacked! | Dungeon invaded during expedition (perception-return check) | see §3.7.4 |
| 61–63 | Mana Turbulence | ARC difficulty 2 → success=◇×2d6 / fail=HP-2 | Arcana (ARC) |
| 64–66 | GM Special | Plot event | GM ruling |

### 3.7.3 Dungeon Status During Expedition

| Impact | Value |
|------|:---:|
| Dungeon Defense | Defense (DEF) -2 |
| Monster Loyalty | All LOY -1 |
| Archetype Active & Advanced Abilities | All unavailable |
| Dungeon Actions | -1 (Lord action consumed by expedition) |
| Invasion Response | Defense dice pool -2, no Lord-in-person option |

### 3.7.4 Perception-Return Check

If dungeon attacked during expedition (d66=53–56):

> Perception Threshold = `floor(destination distance÷2) + 1d6` ≥ 6 → Lord notices in time, may urgently return

| Destination | Distance Value | Timely-Return Chance |
|--------|:---:|:---:|
| Adjacent dungeon/nearby | 2 | 16.7% |
| Same-region target | 4 | 33.3% |
| Far target | 6 | 50.0% |
| Very far | 8 | 66.7% |

**Teleport Beacon**: spend ◇×3 to deploy → perception threshold +2

### 3.7.5 Expedition Frequency

One expedition per 3 dungeon levels. Ancient Ruins limited to 1 per 6 dungeon levels. After expedition need 1 turn rest.

### 3.7.6 Expedition Encounter Combat Rules

Expedition combat uses simplified three-stage turn order (initiative → action → settlement), no dungeon defense-layer bonus, no monster support (unless archetype allows, e.g., Necromancer Lord's skeleton retinue).

**Six-Archetype Expedition Traits**:

| Archetype | Expedition Trait |
|------|---------|
| Necromancer Lord | May bring up to 3 skeleton retainers, each surviving +1 die undead resonance |
| Abyssal Overlord | Surface WAR+1 die (human fear), auto enemy morale -1 |
| Mad Sorcerer | After each encounter may harvest surface rare magic materials |
| Ancient Wyrm | Flight halves expedition time + encounter chance -1/6, may high-altitude recon avoid encounters |
| Shadow Council | Encounter-avoid success doubled (Cunning (CUN)+2 dice), INF doesn't grow during expedition |
| Goblin Tinkerer | May bring 1 portable trap (used in combat), IND check +1 die |

### 3.7.7 Expedition Narrative: Motivation & Senses

**Why Expedition (d6)**: 1=recon, 2=diplomacy, 3=trade, 4=revenge pursuit, 5=explore ruins, 6=recruit legendary monster

**Six-Archetype Expedition Sensory Descriptions**: Necromancer Lord first exposed to sunlight (sunlight stinging between bones), Abyssal Overlord spreading panic on surface (humans scream fleeing), Mad Sorcerer's rapture at discovering surface odd materials, Ancient Wyrm's disdain overlooking human realms from clouds, Shadow Council member's composure infiltrating human towns, Goblin Tinkerer's rapture at human technology.
