# Abyss Lord TRPG — Worldbuilding Setting and GM Running Guide (Revised v2.0)

> System: Dungeon Lord manages a dungeon against adventurers TRPG
> Dice System: d6 Dice Pool (5–6 is a success, 6 explodes for +1 die)
> Designer: Jing Shiwen (World Architect)
> Revision: Post cross-review v2.0 — added expansion narrative, monster management ecology, captive labor narrative, management-phase GM guidance

## Chapter One: Core Worldbuilding Setting

### 1.1 The Basic Truth of the World

The humans of this world—or, to be precise, the surface sapient races—believe a simple story:

> Dungeons are nests of evil, monsters are a threat to civilization, and adventurers are the shining blade of light.

**This is a lie.**

The truth is: a dungeon is the kingdom of an "Abyss Lord" (you, the player). Adventurers are armed invaders who kill your minions, plunder your wealth, and then call themselves "heroes." What they call "exploration" is robbery, "crusade" is murder, "purification" is genocide.

The core narrative reversal of this rule system is precisely this: **let the player step out of the "invader's" perspective and become the "invaded."**

You are not playing an adventurer who broke into someone else's home—you are the dark lord defending your own home.

### 1.2 The Era Setting

**The 7th Century of the "Rift Era"**

About 700 years ago, the "Great Rift" event tore the world apart. Before that, the Surface and the Abyss were two separate planes, each running independently. The Great Rift opened countless passages connecting the two worlds; Abyssal mana poured into the Surface, and the Surface's light-power seeped into the Abyss.

War followed. The Surface nations called it the "Dark Invasion"; the Abyssal races called it the "Light Aggression."

Seven hundred years later, the war has cooled into a daily routine. Surface civilization has expanded to the Rift's edge, building border cities; Abyss Lords build dungeons below the Rift—both home and the first line of defense against Surface invasion.

This is a cold-war, interlocking world: peace never truly arrived, and total war can no longer be sustained. Both sides wait for the other to show a weakness first.

### 1.3 Magic / Technology Level

**Surface setting (from the Dungeon Lord's perspective)**:

- **Dark Magic (Abyssal side)**: Powered by "Mana Crystals" as energy, an internally-driven type of magic. Abyssal mana originates from the primal chaotic energy deep in the earth's veins; refined into Mana Crystals, it can be used for casting, construction, and sustaining minions. Dark magic leans toward control, transformation, corruption, and summoning.
- **Light Magic (Surface side)**: Powered by "faith" and "ritual" as energy, an externally-beseeching type of magic. Light-power originates from the grace of the Surface gods, channeled through prayer and holy relics. Light magic leans toward healing, purification, protection, and banishment.
- **Technology Level**: Roughly a mix of early Renaissance and medieval. The Surface has firearm prototypes (expensive, unreliable muskets); the Abyss has mana-driven mechanical devices. Overall, magic replaces a fair portion of technological function.

### 1.4 Core Conflict

The core conflict of this world is not "justice vs evil," but **two understandings of "order vs freedom"**:

- **Surface perspective**: Civilization needs unified law, morality, and religion. Dungeons are symbols of disorder and chaos that must be cleared.
- **Abyssal perspective**: Surface order is oppressive homogenization. Dungeons are the last refuge where all manner ofalien beings survive freely. Each lord rules by their own law—it may be tyranny, sanctuary, or experiment.

**The Dungeon Lord's fundamental dilemma**:

1. You need adventurers from the Surface—their corpses feed monsters, their gear is resources, and once captured they are labor.
2. But you cannot let adventurers run rampant—they will destroy your home.
3. Nor can you eliminate adventurers entirely—without invaders, the Surface's attention turns to bigger threats (armies, legendary heroes, divine intervention).

**The perfect balance**: Make adventurers feel "just one step from winning," and keep coming back to die.

## Chapter Two: Creation Myth and Historical Timeline

### 2.1 Creation Myth: The Primordial Duality

(The Abyssal version—this is the story your NPCs believe. Surface people tell a different version.)

In the beginning, the universe was a chaos. From the chaos were born two primal existences:

- **Aoth**, the beginning of light, pursuing order, boundaries, and certainty.
- **Nexis**, the source of the Abyss, embracing change, fusion, and possibility.

The two primal beings shaped the world together. But they diverged on how to shape it. Aoth divided the world into heaven and earth, light and dark, life and death, drawing boundaries for all things. Nexis mixed her creations together, creating beings that did not follow fixed forms—the first monsters, shape-shifters, and dragons.

The conflict escalated into war. Finally, the two beings reached a truce: the world was divided into two layers—

- **Surface** (Aoth's Domain): a world of light, order, and drawn boundaries.
- **Deep** (Nexis's Domain): a world of darkness, change, and infinite possibility.

The two layers were sealed apart by the "World Shell"—until the Great Rift event.

### 2.3 Core Historical Lessons

The GM may use the following historical fragments as in-game legends/dialogue material:

- **Modiras's Conversion Method**: proved that "adventurers need not only be killed"—this changed the dungeon ecology.
- **Edwin's Fall**: proved that "even the brightest paladin can be converted"—devastating to the adventurers' worldview.
- **Lord Wars**: proved that the Abyss is not unified internally; intrigue among lords is the norm—this gives players room for diplomatic play.

### 3.2 Surface World (Realms of Light)

#### Kingdom of Dawn

- **Capital**: Radiance City
- **Government**: Monarchy
- **Feature**: The largest military power, with a regular army and the "Royal Investigation Corps."
- **Attitude toward dungeons**: Seen as a national-defense threat. But pragmatic—if a dungeon does not threaten border trade routes, the Kingdom of Dawn won't waste troops clearing it.
- **Adventurer source**: Retired soldiers, nobles seeking glory.

#### Silver Federation

- **Capital**: Glimmerstone City (largest city in the Rift Borderlands)
- **Government**: Commercial oligarchic republic
- **Feature**: Headquarters of the Adventurers' Guild. Built on trade, treats the adventurer industry as a lucrative business.
- **Attitude toward dungeons**: Pragmatic. Dungeons are a source of resources (magic items, rare materials); the Silver Federation doesn't mind dealing with some dungeon lords as long as there's profit.
- **Adventurer source**: Professional adventurers, mercenaries, treasure hunters.

#### The Holy See

- **Capital**: City of Holy Light
- **Government**: Theocracy
- **Feature**: Core of the Order of Light. Worships Aoth and the light gods. The most radical "clearance faction."
- **Attitude toward dungeons**: Absolutely intolerant. Goal is to completely exterminate all dungeons.
- **Adventurer source**: Paladins, Inquisitors, fanatics.

### 3.3 The Deep World (Abyssal Realm)

#### Sea of Shadows

- A vast cave network full of shadow creatures and mist. Birthplace of the Shadow Council.
- Mana density: medium-high. Suited to dungeons skilled in intel and intrigue.

#### Magma Plains

- Volcanic terrain with magma rivers running through. Territory of the Ancient Wyrm, also home to fire-elemental creatures and hellhounds.
- Mana density: extremely high (but hard to control). Suited to power-type dungeons.

#### Gloom Forest

- A giant underground fungal forest where mana-eating glowing plants form a dim ecosystem. Territory of the Necromancer Lord and Mad Sorcerer.
- Mana density: medium (but extremely stable). Suited to magic-research dungeons.

#### Scrap Canyon

- A canyon strewn with ancient mechanical wreckage and ore. Gathering place of Goblin Tinkerers.
- Mana density: low (but rich in mineral resources). Suited to mechanical/trap dungeons.

#### Abyssal Core

- The source of primal chaotic energy. The ultimate source of all Abyssal mana. Too dangerous—no dungeon dares build here.
- Is the exploration target of advanced modules.

### 3.4 Rift Borderlands

A transitional zone between the Surface and the Abyss. The terrain is complex—abandoned mines, natural caves, and ancient ruins interweave—and is the main area where adventurers and dungeons clash.

Each dungeon has one or more "rift entrances" connecting to the Rift Borderlands, through which adventurers invade.

## Chapter Four: Factions and Alliances

### 4.1 Adventurer Factions (GM-Controlled)

#### The Order of Light

> "Purify the darkness, until death."

- **Structure**: Archbishop → Cardinal Bishop → Inquisitor-General → Paladin / Inquisitor
- **Core motivation**: Religious mission—Aoth's light reaches every corner
- **Tactical style**: Light magic (counters undead and demons), heavy-armor push, dispel and purify
- **View of dungeons**: Dungeons are the embodiment of "original sin"; their existence is itself evil, and the lord is beyond redemption
- **Tier range**: T2–T5 (no T1—the Order of Light sends no novices)
- **Signature classes**: Paladin, Inquisitor, Light Cleric
- **Narrative hook**: The Order of Light is more corrupt than you imagine—their dungeon clearing sometimes hides their own secrets

#### Adventurers' Guild

> "Adventuring is a business."

- **Structure**: Guild Master → Branch Masters → Quest Officers → Adventurer squads
- **Core motivation**: Wealth and reputation. A bronze-tier adventurer can earn an ordinary person's yearly income by clearing a small dungeon
- **Tactical style**: Balanced class mix, flexible tactics, goal is completing the quest rather than killing enemies
- **Quest system**: The Guild issues quests by risk rating (Bronze → Silver → Gold → Platinum → Legend). The higher a dungeon's "Infamy value," the higher the quest tier and the richer the reward
- **Tier range**: T1–T5 (most comprehensive adventurer source)
- **Signature classes**: Warrior, Thief, Ranger, Battle Mage
- **Narrative hook**: Inside the Adventurers' Guild is a "Sympathize-with-the-Abyss faction"—they feel a dungeon is also a form of civilization, advocating coexistence rather than clearance. This is the Guild's internal political struggle line

#### Royal Investigation Corps (RIC)

> "For the King's eye."

- **Structure**: Royal Commander → Regional Commander → Investigation Captain → Investigator
- **Core motivation**: National security and intel. The Corps's task is to assess a dungeon's threat level to the nation, clearing it if necessary
- **Tactical style**: Well-equipped (state-funded), intel-first (won't attack rashly), tends toward recruitment or negotiation rather than hard fighting
- **View of dungeons**: Tiered management. Some dungeons are "tolerable low threats," some are "must-clear high threats." The Corps even privately exchanges intel with some dungeons
- **Tier range**: T3–T5 (the Corps sends only elites)
- **Signature classes**: Battle Mage, Spy, Tactical Commander
- **Narrative hook**: The Corps may be the only Surface force willing to "negotiate" with a Dungeon Lord—but this may be a trap

#### Freelance Hunters

> "We are not heroes; we are just harsher than the monsters."

- **Structure**: No formal organization. Loose master-apprentice lineages, family businesses, or lone wolves
- **Core motivation**: Diverse—revenge (family died to monsters), survival (hunting monsters is the fastest way to get rich), exile (those who can't get by on the Surface), even some who simply enjoy killing
- **Tactical style**: Varied means, unscrupulous, often use tactics dangerous to both sides (gas, explosions, curse items)
- **View of dungeons**: Seen as prey rather than evil. The most "pragmatic" group
- **Tier range**: T1–T4 (extreme individual variation)
- **Signature classes**: Monster Hunter (exclusive class), Bounty Hunter, Wild Ranger, Renegade Mage
- **Narrative hook**: Among hunters are no few Abyssal half-breeds or former-adventurer defectors—their understanding of dungeons may run deeper than the dungeon lord's own

#### (Optional) Renegade Alliance

> "The Surface abandoned us; the Abyss took us in."

- A hidden faction—composed of defectors to the Abyss, former adventurers / Surface exiles
- They may be allies, spies, or advisors to the Dungeon Lord
- Narratively important: proves that "the Surface is not monolithic"

### 4.2 Dungeon Alliances

#### Pact of the Abyss

- A loose diplomatic alliance based on the "Abyssal Accord"
- Holds an "Abyssal Council" once a year in the Sea of Shadows to discuss common threats
- Forbids lords from devouring each other
- In practice: great lords secretly manipulate small ones; the covenant is often "technically" violated

#### Inter-Dungeon Relationship Types

| Relationship | Description | Game Effect |
|------|------|----------|
| Ally | Mutual support, shared intel | May request reinforcements when facing a strong enemy; trade preferences |
| Neutral | Live and let live | Default state |
| Rival | Compete for resources/fame/territory | May be secretly sabotaged by the other; adventurers may be led to your dungeon |
| Hostile | Open conflict | The other may hire adventurers to harass your dungeon |
| Vassal | Pledge fealty and tribute to a stronger lord | Gain protection, but lose independence |

## Chapter Five: Dungeon Ecology and Society

### 5.1 Mana Ecology

Abyssal mana (also called the "Breath of Nexis") is the foundation of all dungeons:

- **Mana penetration**: Abyssal mana spreads outward from the Abyssal Core, its density decreasing with distance. Dungeons are usually built where mana density is moderate—too low and they can't function, too high and they can't be controlled.
- **Mana crystallization**: High-density mana naturally condenses on rock walls into "Mana Crystals," the most important energy source of a dungeon.
- **Biological influence**: Creatures exposed to Abyssal mana over long periods gradually become "Abyssalized"—form mutation, mana affinity, even evolved intelligence. A dungeon's monster minions are products of Abyssalization.
- **Mana anomaly**: Occasionally mana erupts (like a Surface volcanic eruption), possibly causing monsters to frenzy, new rifts to open, or random magic effects.

### 5.3 Dungeon Types (matching lord archetypes)

#### Necropolis

- **Lord**: Necromancer Lord (lich, death knight, vampire lord)
- **Visual style**: Bones, obsidian, ghostly blue fire, eternal chill
- **Mana source**: Soul Essence—death energy
- **Signature minions**: Skeleton Soldier (cheap, renewable), Ghost (scouting), Wight (elite)
- **Advantage**: No need to pay the undead wages; morale concept doesn't apply
- **Disadvantage**: Light magic counters severely; living Surface visitors feel extreme discomfort (diplomacy difficult)
- **Motto**: "Death is not an end, but the workforce."

#### Abyssal Citadel

- **Lord**: Abyssal Overlord (various demon lords)
- **Visual style**: Black iron, magma, sulfur, flame runes
- **Mana source**: Sulfur springs—high density but extremely unstable mana
- **Signature minions**: Imp (versatile drudge), Hellhound (guard), Succubus/Nightmare (psychological warfare)
- **Advantage**: Demon minions are strong fighters; fear aura lowers adventurer morale
- **Disadvantage**: Demon minions may revolt—loyalty was never a demon's strength
- **Motto**: "Fear is the most effective management tool."

#### Wizard's Spire

- **Lord**: Mad Sorcerer
- **Visual style**: Twisted towers, floating books, ever-shifting corridors, experiments
- **Mana source**: Refined Mana Crystals—the most efficient mana use
- **Signature minions**: Constructs, mutant creatures, cursed intelligent items
- **Advantage**: Strongest magic research; can create unique defense mechanisms
- **Disadvantage**: Weak physical defense; the wizard's own "madness" may cause accidents
- **Motto**: "Experiment always comes first. If the dungeon collapses, that's also experimental data."

#### Dragon's Lair

- **Lord**: Ancient Wyrm
- **Visual style**: Giant caverns, piles of gold and gems, scorching breath
- **Mana source**: The dragon itself is a mana source—dragon breath naturally produces Mana Crystals
- **Signature minions**: Dragonborn (loyal dragon-blooded creatures), elementals, other races subdued by intimidation
- **Advantage**: Unmatched when the lord fights in person; fastest wealth accumulation
- **Disadvantage**: Dragon's Lair is too big a target—attracts highest-Tier adventurers; dragons are arrogant, poor at fine management
- **Motto**: "What's mine is mine. Including you."

#### Shadow Sanctum

- **Lord**: Shadow Council (a collective consciousness of multiple shadow beings or former adventurers)
- **Visual style**: Permanent mist, faint silhouettes, whispering corridors
- **Mana source**: Shadow Essence—extracted from fear and secrets
- **Signature minions**: Shadow Assassin, Spy Avatar, Shape-shifter
- **Advantage**: Strongest intel ability; can infiltrate the Adventurers' Guild for intel
- **Disadvantage**: Weakest frontal combat; internal council divisions may cause decision paralysis
- **Motto**: "We know their secrets. Secrets are power."

#### Gearworks

- **Lord**: Goblin Tinkerer
- **Visual style**: Steam pipes, gear devices, explosive barrels, various unstable machines
- **Mana source**: Mana Crystals drive machinery—fusion of tech and magic
- **Signature minions**: Clockwork automatons, self-destruct goblins, various engineering machines
- **Advantage**: Lowest trap-crafting cost, highest efficiency; can build defense mechanisms adventurers can't understand
- **Disadvantage**: Goblins are individually fragile; 30% chance their own trap blows up their own people first
- **Motto**: "If it didn't explode, we didn't pack enough powder."

## Chapter Six: Culture and Faith

### 6.1 Faith of the Abyss

The Abyss has no unified religion. Different Abyss Lords may have different faiths:

- **Nexis Worship**: Worships the "change" aspect of the creator being. Believes change, mutation, and chaos are the natural state of the universe; the Surface's "order" is the abnormality.
- **Ancestor Worship**: The path usually taken by Necromancer Lords—believes post-death existence is more real than life.
- **Power Worship**: The faith of Abyssal Overlords. Power is truth. The strong define good and evil.
- **Atheism / Pragmatism**: The stance of Shadow Council and Goblin Tinkerer. Whether gods exist doesn't matter; what matters is what chips you hold.
- **Self-Deification**: Ancient Wyrms usually consider themselves gods.

### 6.2 Abyssal Social Views

- **"Survival of the fittest" is accepted**: Abyss dwellers think Surface people are hypocritical—the Surface also has the strong exploiting the weak, just packaged as "law" and "morality."
- **"Conversion" is not slavery**: In many Abyssal cultures, being defeated and converted into a minion is seen as part of the natural order—like the Surface taking prisoners of war.
- **"Shape-shifting" is not a curse**: Surface transformation magic is seen as abnormal, while Abyssal transformation/mutation is seen as a mark of evolution.
- **"Contract" is sacred**: Abyssal contracts are usually magically bound; violators suffer curses. This is more effective than the Surface's "law."

### 6.3 Festivals and Rituals

| Festival | Time | Meaning |
|------|------|------|
| Rift Day | Annual commemoration of the Rift event | Mourn those who fell in the Abyssal War. A tacit non-aggression day among dungeons |
| Mana Tide Festival | Day of highest mana density (once per season) | Magic research and build efficiency +20%. Traditionally lords issue "extra rations" to minions |
| Conversion Celebration | When a captive is successfully converted | Celebrate "the birth of a new member." The converted is formally given a name |
| Council Day | Once a year | Annual meeting of the Pact of the Abyss. A major diplomatic event in-game |
| Hunt Season | When adventurer activity is highest | Actually not a "festival" but "survival mode." Abyss Lords strengthen defense, reduce outings |

## Chapter Seven: Narrative Framework for Territory Expansion (Core Addition) ⭐

> "Start from a small cavern, dig into the unknown darkness. Every pick strike is a gamble—you never know what hides behind the next rock."

### 7.1 Core of Expansion Narrative

Territory expansion is the core difference that sets this rule apart from traditional dungeon-building games. Players do not "place" rooms from a god's-eye view—they start from an initial small cavern, command monsters and captives to dig outward, expanding their dark kingdom inch by inch. Every dig carries narrative weight.

**The GM's core task**: Make every expansion feel like an "exploration." What is behind the rock? Only by digging open do you know.

### 7.2 The First Outward Dig

This is one of the most ritualistic moments in the game. The player has gained a foothold in the initial Throne Room and now expands outward.

**Narrative Guidance Template**:

> You stand before the north wall of the Throne Room. The wall is rough and uneven—the end of the natural cavern. Your minions wait behind you—perhaps a squad of silent skeleton soldiers, perhaps a group of goblins with picks and explosives. You reach out and touch the cold rock wall. Behind this rock is the unknown. A mineral vein? An empty space? A sleeping ancient creature? Or a Surface adventurer's tunnel?
>
> "Start digging," you say.
>
> When the first pick falls, the whole cavern trembles slightly. Rubble tumbles. Something in the darkness glints faintly—perhaps Mana Crystals, perhaps something else.

**GM Guidance Points**:
- **Emphasize the senses**: sound (pick-echo, tumbling rubble), touch (wall temperature changes—the closer to magma, the hotter), sight (occasional glints in the dark)
- **Build suspense**: After the first pick, don't reveal the result immediately. Describe the sound, the tremor, the minions' reactions—then let the player roll
- **Give choices**: A fork found while digging (left wall softer but damp vs right wall hard but dry), let the player decide direction
- **Record traces**: Prompt the player to record digging direction—this may later become another route for invaders

**Dig Check Narrative (Industry + Engineering skill → opposed by expansion difficulty)**:

| Net Successes | Result | Narrative Hint |
|----------|------|----------|
| 0 | Dig fails | "The pick tip hits granite—much harder than expected. Sparks fly, the pick breaks. Seems we need a different direction." |
| 1 | Small space dug | "The rock finally crumbles. A cramped little hollow appears—barely enough for one small room. The air is thick with damp earth smell." |
| 2 | Medium space dug | "The wall collapses with a roar, revealing a natural cavern. Stalactites glitter in the firelight; space enough for two rooms. And—something in the corner reflects light…" |
| 3+ | Large cavern + discovery | "The whole wall collapses! Before you is a spectacular cavern dome, too high to see the top. Mana trembles in the air—here is a natural mana node. A gift of fate, or a trap of fate?" |

### 7.3 Discovering Natural Veins / Mana Nodes

The most exciting part of digging is the unexpected discovery. Below is a narrative hook table for the GM:

**Mineral Vein Discovery Narrative Table (d6)**:

| d6 | Discovery | Narrative Description |
|----|------|----------|
| 1 | **Dark Gold Vein** | "A thin black streak appears on the rock wall. The pick taps it lightly; the black streak emits a faint golden glow—it's dark gold. This vein looks sizeable." (Resource: Dark Coin output +2/turn) |
| 2 | **Mana Crystal Cluster** | "A cluster of transparent purple crystal columns grows on the rock wall, each as thick as an arm. Touching them, a faint electric current runs to your fingertips—pure Mana Crystals, excellent quality." (Resource: Mana Crystal output +2/turn) |
| 3 | **Underground Hot Spring** | "Hot steam jets from a crack. Once dug open, warm spring water flows out—a natural hot spring rich in minerals. The monsters like soaking here, and so do the captives." (Effect: all monster Loyalty auto-recovers +1/turn) |
| 4 | **Ancient Civilization Ruins** | "Behind the rock is not a natural cavern—it's a stone wall carved with ancient runes. These characters belong to no known civilization. What is behind the wall?" (Adventure hook: opens an exploration side-quest) |
| 5 | **Giant Mushroom Forest** | "The moment you dig through, a spore cloud erupts. After coughing, before you is an underground mushroom forest—mushrooms tall as trees, caps emitting dim blue bioluminescence." (Resource: food source, lowers monster upkeep) |
| 6 | **Abandoned Mining Tunnel** | "The pick tip suddenly meets nothing—you've dug through. Before you is a neat tunnel obviously carved by human (or dwarven) hands, rails extending into the dark." (Adventure hook: connects to unknown area, possibly dwarven ruins or adventurer passage) |

**Mana Node Discovery Narrative** (triggered when dig check gets 3+ successes):

> The air suddenly grows heavy. Everyone's breath becomes visible—not from cold, but from mana. At the cavern's center, space seems to warp slightly. Visible purple glimmers seep from cracks in the floor, spreading across the surface like veins. You have found a "Mana Node"—here the Breath of Nexis breaks through the World Shell, forming a micro-rift.
>
> This means endless mana. It also means… this place will attract every being that can sense mana. Including the Surface mages.

**The two sides of a Mana Node**:
- Positive: Mana Crystal yield doubled, magic room efficiency +50%
- Negative: Attracts more adventurers (invasion chance permanently +1), may disturb ancient beings deep below

### 7.4 Dig Accident Hook Table

Digging is not safe. Every pick strike is an adventure.

**Dig Accident Table (d8, used when dig check fails or GM needs narrative push)**:

| d8 | Accident | Narrative Hook |
|----|------|----------|
| 1 | **Cave-in** | "A loud crack—the tunnel roof above collapses. Two skeleton soldiers are buried in rubble. But the cave-in exposes a hollow above—what's there?" (Effect: lose one monster/captive, but reveals a new dig direction) |
| 2 | **Underground River** | "Water gushes from a crack, instantly knee-deep. You didn't dig to an underground river—the underground river was waiting for you." (Effect: gain water source, but current dig direction must detour) |
| 3 | **Poison Gas** | "A digger suddenly collapses. The air is thick with a sickly-sweet smell—a natural poison pocket." (Effect: must build ventilation before continuing this direction) |
| 4 | **Disturb Burrowing Swarm** | "After the rock cracks, a giant beetle swarm pours out—not attacking, fleeing. What's behind them?" (Effect: adds a random monster lair/threat source near the dungeon) |
| 5 | **Accidental Surface Breach** | "Sunlight. You've lived underground too long and nearly forgot what sunlight looks like. A small hole—tiny—but sunlight is sunlight. And sunlight means…" (Effect: new rift entrance. May attract adventurers, or become the player's ambush point) |
| 6 | **Earth Groan** | "The whole dungeon trembles. Not a cave-in—a deeper tremor. Something huge is moving. It may be far, or near." (Effect: introduces a long-term threat—ancient beast / sleeping god / giant burrowing worm) |
| 7 | **Lost Miner** | "What you dug through was not a rock wall—but another tunnel. In it stands a lantern-bearing, coal-smudged dwarf miner, mouth agape enough to fit a fist." (Effect: encounter neutral NPC—possibly ally, trade partner, or trouble source) |
| 8 | **Mana Eruption** | "It's not you digging—it's mana erupting outward. Purple light jets from the cracks, illuminating the whole cavern. All Mana Crystals glow at once; all monsters look up at once." (Effect: all magic-related checks +2 dice for 1 turn, but may trigger a mana anomaly event) |

### 7.5 The Cost of "Expansion Greed"

Bigger is not always better. Every inch of expansion brings new risk.

**"Deep-Dig Curse" Narrative Framework**:

A saying runs through the Abyss: **"The deeper you dig, the more you are seen."**

Over-fast/over-far expansion triggers the following narrative threats:

| Expansion Stage | DEP Range | Threats Attracted | GM Narrative Hint |
|----------|---------|-------------|------------|
| Micro Dungeon | 1–3 | Scattered adventurers, beasts | "You are too small to threaten anyone." |
| Small Dungeon | 4–6 | Adventurers' Guild formal attention, neighboring lords notice | "Your name begins to appear on the map. A map is a dangerous thing." |
| Medium Dungeon | 7–9 | Order of Light clearance list, Abyssal Council courtship | "Light falls on you. Not just the Surface's—also from inside the Abyss." |
| Large Dungeon | 10–12 | Legendary heroes deployed, Abyss Lords covet | "They no longer send adventurers to 'explore.' Now come 'armies.'" |
| Giant Crypt | 13–15 | Attention of the gods, will of the Abyssal Core | "You dug too deep. Something down there should not be touched." |

**GM Intervention Means—when players expand too fast**:

1. **Resource constraint**: "Your Building Material reserves have hit bottom. The skeleton soldiers dig with their fingernails—efficiency is surprisingly low."
2. **Thinned defense**: "Your dungeon now has fifteen rooms but only five monsters. Patrol routes stretch too long; some corridors haven't been walked in three days. Does that feel safe?"
3. **Neighbor reaction**: "A goblin messenger appears in your newly dug tunnel. The letter has one word—'Stop.' Signed by a lord's name you've never heard."
4. **Dungeon instability**: "The newly opened area begins to show fine cracks. The Research Chamber mage warns you: the dungeon's structural support is at its limit. If you don't reinforce existing areas, a cave-in is only a matter of time."
5. **Unknown terror**: "In the last dig, you heard an answer from the other side of the digging face—three heavy knocks. Exactly in rhythm with your pick strikes. Your minions refuse to keep digging that direction."

### 7.6 Expansion Styles by Lord Archetype

#### Necromancer Lord: Silent Bone-Pick Burrowing

> Skeleton soldiers need no breath, no rest, no light. They dig with finger-bones, flaking off rock layer by layer. No pick sounds, no panting—only the faint rasp of bone joints rubbing.
>
> One skeleton falls apart, then replaces another's broken finger with its own bone shard. The undead's digging is a silent, endless, unstoppable seepage.
>
> Occasionally a skeleton digs up another skeleton from the soil. "New colleague," says the Necromancer Lord. The ranks grow by one.

**GM Narrative Hint**:
- Undead digging is silent and constant, but slow (efficiency −1 die, but no rest turns needed)
- Occasionally digs up ancient bones—Necromancer Lord may gain a free new skeleton soldier
- Tunnels dug by the undead leave a cold feeling—living people instinctively feel uneasy passing through

#### Abyssal Overlord: Frenzy Under Fear Command

> "Dig." The demon lord says only one word.
>
> The imps swarm up, with claws, horns, teeth—anything that can scrape rock. They are driven not by wages but by fear. The last imp to meet its dig quota gets eaten. So they dig fast.
>
> Occasionally an imp digs halfway and gets a face full of magma. The others don't stop—they don't even glance.

**GM Narrative Hint**:
- Demon digging is extremely fast (efficiency +1 die), but at the cost of doubled accident rate
- Demon minions may fight each other while digging—GM may randomly trigger a "tunnel brawl" event
- Tunnel walls dug by demons are covered in claw marks, like a territorial mark

#### Mad Sorcerer: Magic Dissolution and Experiment

> "Explosion? Too crude." The Mad Sorcerer raises a hand; purple mana seeps from the fingertips, and the rock wall begins to melt like wax. Mana dissolution is an elegant way to dig—quiet, precise, no rubble.
>
> But occasionally the dissolution potion "over-reacts." A spell meant to dissolve three feet of rock dissolves twenty—including the slime sleeping next door.
>
> "Data," the wizard writes in notes, "slime split into seven smaller individuals after mana dissolution. Experiment successful."

**GM Narrative Hint**:
- Magic digging is precise but unpredictable—each dig check carries a random magic effect (d6 decides good or bad)
- May accidentally create mutant creatures, mana residue zones, or anti-magic fields
- Wizard-dug tunnel walls are mirror-smooth; occasionally you see your own reflection—and details in the reflection that shouldn't be there

#### Ancient Wyrm: Dragon Breath Clears the Way

> The dragon needs no pick. The dragon is the pick.
>
> One dragon breath, and rock first melts then solidifies under extreme heat, becoming a smooth glaze. The tunnel is not "dug"—it is "burned" out. Amazingly efficient, amazingly loud—the whole dungeon hears the dragon's digging roar.
>
> The cost is obvious: dragon-breath digging consumes not just stamina but patience. The dragon hates digging—that's "minion work."

**GM Narrative Hint**:
- Dragon-breath digging is fastest (efficiency +2 dice), but consumes Dark Coins (the dragon needs equivalent treasure to be "appeased")
- Dragon digging personally may cause dungeon mana fluctuation—all magic checks temporarily −1
- Dragon-breath-dug tunnels are coated in a glaze layer, naturally fireproof and beautiful—the mark of a Dragon's Lair

#### Shadow Council: Infiltration Not Digging

> The Shadow Council doesn't "dig"—they "infiltrate." Shadow beings need no physical space; they seep into rock crevices, seeking natural cave networks in the earth's strata.
>
> "Digging is Surface thinking," a shadow councilor whispers, "the Abyss already has countless hollows, cracks, tunnels. We only need to find them."
>
> So the Shadow's expansion is not a straight line—it's a web. Sometimes you start digging at point A and break ground at point Z.

**GM Narrative Hint**:
- Shadow infiltration takes longer but may discover hidden natural cave networks
- Shadow Council may find hidden info before digging (underground forces, resource locations, threat warnings)
- Shadow-infilitrated tunnels are invisible—enemies don't know your true scale

#### Goblin Tinkerer: Precise Demolition and Math

> "The charge ratio is 73% abyss saltpeter plus 27% mana dust—not 75-25! Those two percentage points decide whether we're digging or committing suicide!"
>
> The Goblin Tinkerer's digging is a precise engineering demonstration. Measuring rods, blast calculations, support designs—every step has a blueprint, every shot a formula.
>
> Of course, correct theory doesn't mean correct practice. There's a 30% chance "precise demolition" becomes "precise cave-in."

**GM Narrative Hint**:
- Goblin engineering digging is most efficient and lowest cost (Building Material cost −30%)
- Each dig carries a 1/6 chance of an "engineering accident"—GM rolls d6 for consequences
- Goblin-dug tunnels have neat support structures, looking like industrial-age mines—full of safety, until a support beam starts to creak

## Chapter Eight: Narrative Ecology of Monster Management (Core Addition) ⭐

> "Monsters are not just combat data. They live, work, play cards, complain about food, and secretly fall in love in the dungeon. When you're not in the Throne Room, the dungeon still runs—because the monsters are living their lives."

### 8.1 Monsters Are Not Just Combat Units

An important narrativephilosophy of this rule is: **every monster is a character.** They have names (if you give them), habits, quirks, their own little agendas. They are not just "Combat Power +2" placed on a defense floor—they are the dungeon's residents.

The GM should, each management turn, use a paragraph or two of narrative to make the monsters "come alive."

### 8.2 Daily Management Narrative Snippets

#### The Black Humor of Skeletons Mining

> "Skeleton #3 fell apart again. The third time this week. Always the same spot—the left leg hip joint. You're almost certain it's deliberate. Every time it falls apart, it can lie on the ground until someone puts it back together. Probably the skeleton version of 'lunch break.'"
>
> "When putting it back, you notice someone drew two eyes and a smile on its skull. With charcoal. The prime suspect is Skeleton #1—lately it stands guard straighter than the others."

**More skeleton narrative snippets (GM usable)**:
- Skeletons need no rest, but at midnight they automatically line up in a row, silently facing the same direction (maybe a habit from life)
- Two skeletons start swapping limbs, no one knows why—maybe some experiment, maybe just boredom
- A newly recruited skeleton's bones are carved with tiny writing—its diary from life

#### Slimes Cleaning Waste

> Slimes are the most underrated creatures in the dungeon. Yes, their combat is mediocre, their loyalty questionable. But they have one irreplaceable talent: eating.
>
> Slimes can eat any organic waste—leftovers, corpse remains, the slime produced by failed goblin experiments. They gurgle while eating, like a satisfied cat—if you can accept a cat that's semi-transparent jelly.
>
> Sometimes a slime eats something it shouldn't. Last time it swallowed half a goblin engineer's blueprint. The engineer chased it through the tunnel for three hours. The slime was happy. The goblin was not. The blueprint was finally "recovered"—in a way the goblin refuses to describe.

#### Imps' Overtime Protest

> The imps recently invented a new form of protest: they don't refuse labor—they labor "over-enthusiastically."
>
> Imp #3 volunteered to "reinforce" the prison gate—by welding it shut. Imp #4 "organized" the Mana Crystal storeroom—re-sorted by color, mixing crystals of different purity together.
>
> You understand their demand: improve the food. Current rations are "what the slimes left over."

#### Goblin Engineering Crew's Daily Life

> The goblins drew a detailed construction diagram on the digging face with chalk—with annotations, arrows, and a little figure (wearing glasses, probably the captain) smashing another little figure (no glasses, probably the inspector) with a sledgehammer.
>
> They also drew a counter beside it: "Days since last accident: 0." The count resets to zero around 2 PM every day.

### 8.3 Ecological Interaction Among Monsters

Different races of monsters sharing a room is the most interesting (and most dangerous) dynamic in a dungeon.

**Racial Tension and Coexistence Narrative Table (d6)**:

| d6 | Tension Type | Narrative Example |
|----|---------|----------|
| 1 | **Predator and Prey** | "The hellhound looks at the slime oddly. Not hostility—more like sizing up a dish." (Risk: hellhound may 'accidentally' eat the slime someday unless player intervenes) |
| 2 | **Intelligence Gap** | "The Elemental Construct can't understand why skeleton soldiers draw smiles on the wall. It has stood before the wall analyzing for six hours." (Narrative fun: absurd interaction between low- and high-intelligence monsters) |
| 3 | **Faction Opposition** | "A newly recruited cultist refuses to share a room with shadow beings—his faith tells him shadow is 'unclean.' The shadow being's response: places his holy symbol on the ceiling while he sleeps." (Risk: efficiency drops, possibly even infighting) |
| 4 | **Unexpected Friendship** | "A symbiotic relationship forms between the gargoyle and the spider swarm—the swarm weaves webs on the gargoyle, the gargoyle provides a high-altitude movement platform. Now they refuse to be deployed separately." (Effect: these two monsters must be deployed in the same room, each +1 Combat Power) |
| 5 | **Territorial Competition** | "The Monster Lair corner is divided into several 'territories'—left is the skeletons' (very tidy, just a pile of bones), right is the imps' (messy as a junkyard), middle is the slime's (slippery, don't step). No one drew boundaries, but boundaries exist." (Risk: if the lair gets too crowded, conflict is inevitable) |
| 6 | **Generational Inheritance** | "The oldest skeleton—the one with a crack in its eye socket—starts 'training' new skeletons. No one taught it to. It issues commands in a creaky bone-language, and the recruits actually listen." (Narrative hook: may birth a monster leader) |

**GM Guidance Technique—Monster Interaction Daily**:
- At the start of each management turn, briefly describe a "dungeon life snippet"
- Let the player notice monsters are not just numbers—they have personalities
- If the player names a monster, the GM must address it by name
- Monster interaction can produce game effects—good interaction raises Loyalty/efficiency, bad interaction needs player intervention

### 8.4 "Legendary Foreman" Narrative Line

In every great dungeon, there is a great foreman monster—not outstanding for combat power, but legendary for management ability.

**Legendary Foreman Birth Condition**: When a monster is deployed in the same management post for 3+ consecutive turns and performs excellently (management checks succeed consecutively), the GM may trigger the "Legendary Foreman" narrative line.

**Legendary Foreman Template (for GM improvisation)**:

> **Crag "The Pick"**
>
> A goblin, but he probably understands mining engineering better than most humans. Crag doesn't fight—he was born in the mine (literally, his mother gave birth to him in the mine), grew up in the mine, and will die in the mine (his own words—"I hope it's natural death, not a cave-in").
>
> Crag knows every vein's name (he named them), knows every support beam's load (he installed most personally), knows every miner's physical condition (including captives—"that former paladin has a bad back, assign him to light work").
>
> His authority comes not from strength—he cries when a hellhound glares at him—but from the simple fact that he doubled the mine's output.

**Legendary Foreman Narrative Ability Table (d6, randomly gain 1 when recruiting or gaining a legendary foreman)**:

| d6 | Legendary Ability | Narrative Description |
|----|---------|----------|
| 1 | **Vein Intuition** | "He only needs to touch the rock wall to know which way to dig. You're not sure if it's science or magic, maybe both." (Effect: dig check +1 die) |
| 2 | **Monster Manager** | "He can't talk fancy management, but he knows every monster's name, favorite food, and angry face. That's probably leadership." (Effect: same-room monster Loyalty +1) |
| 3 | **Thrift Genius** | "He can find usable things in scrap—a nail, a support beam, half a bag of cement. It adds up." (Effect: build/upkeep Building Material cost −20%) |
| 4 | **Captive Translator** | "He's the only monster in the dungeon who can 'chat' with captives—in mixed Common, gestures, and squeaks. Captives aren't afraid of him. That's crucial." (Effect: captive labor efficiency +25%) |
| 5 | **Danger Warning** | "He worked deepest in the dungeon for ten years and is still alive. Not luck—he always senses 'something's off' five minutes before a cave-in." (Effect: dig accident chance halved) |
| 6 | **Morale Engine** | "He tells jokes. Not necessarily good jokes—but in a dungeon any humor is precious." (Effect: management-phase whole-dungeon monster Loyalty +1 temporary/turn) |

**Legendary Foreman's Highlight Moments (narrative climaxes usable by GM)**:
- **Rescuing from Mine Disaster**: "While everyone ran out, Crag ran toward the cave-in—because he knew Skeleton #3 was still inside. 'No one left behind!' he shouted, then tripped on a rubble stone. Not a handsome image, but commendable spirit."
- **Negotiation Representative**: "When an angry Abyss Lord sent an envoy to question why he dug into his territory, it was Crag—a goblin with trembling knees—who went to negotiate. He brought blueprints, evidence, and a small bag of Dark Coins. Result: the other lord laughed, withdrew the accusation, and even granted three extra days of grace."
- **Heroic Sacrifice (optional)**: "Crag could have run. But he chose to stay, propping the digging face with his last support beam so other miners could escape. When the cave-in stopped, Crag was gone—but his pick still stuck in the rock wall, carved with a line: 'Keep digging.' The dungeon's mine is named after him from then on."

## Chapter Nine: Captive Labor Narrative (Core Addition) ⭐

> "A captive is a labor unit. But before that, a captive is a person. They once had dreams. Now their dreams are replaced by iron bars and chains. Then what?"

### 9.1 A Former Adventurer's First Labor After Capture

A captive's first assignment to labor is a key narrative turning point—the first day of their identity shift from "hero" to "laborer."

**First Labor Narrative Pattern**:

> **[Captive Name]** stands at the mine entrance. The handcuffs are off—unnecessary, because they already know the consequences of escaping. A pick is thrust into their hand. The pick is old, its wooden handle cracked deep.
>
> They look back at the escorting skeleton soldier. The skeleton has no expression—it never does. But that's irrelevant. The captive needs no one to remind them of their situation. The weight of the pick says it all.
>
> "Dig."
>
> On the first strike, the captive's hand trembles. Not from fatigue—from anger, from humiliation, from an unnamed emotion. They once held a longsword, swore before the rift entrance to destroy the darkness. Now they mine in the darkness, for the darkness.
>
> Beside them, another captive—a thief who's been here three months—says without looking up: "Don't overthink. Don't stop moving. Stop moving and the mind starts thinking. The mind thinks—"
>
> He pauses, unfinished.
>
> The pick tip hits rock. Sparks flash briefly in the darkness, then go out.

**GM Guidance—First Labor Narrative Points**:
1. **Class contrast**: Let class traits contrast with labor content
   - Former Warrior → assigned to mine: "His arms were for swinging swords, not picks. But muscles don't know the difference."
   - Former Mage → assigned to Research Chamber: "His hands once pinched out flame spells—now they copy magic recipes. Same fingers, different master."
   - Former Thief → assigned to Trap Workshop: "Ironically, she used to disarm traps. Now she makes them. 'Role reversal,' she thinks bitterly."
   - Former Cleric → assigned to Altar: "He once prayed here to the light god. Now he prays to the dark god. The holy symbol is replaced by an Abyssal sigil. He replaced it himself."
2. **Detailed psychology**: The GM should describe the captive's five internal stages—
   - **Denial**: "This isn't real. I'll wake up soon."
   - **Anger**: "These monsters—they're wasting my talent! I'm an adventurer, not a miner!"
   - **Bargaining**: "Maybe… maybe I can talk to the Dungeon Lord. Maybe I can do some other work."
   - **Depression**: "It's all futile. I'll die here, no one will remember my name."
   - **Acceptance** (possibly months later): "This pick fits my hand well now. That's terrifying, because it means I've changed."
3. **Other captives' reactions**: First labor shouldn't be lonely—other captives's reactions are important narrative tools
   - Cold type: "Don't block the way, newbie. Newbies come every day. You're nothing special."
   - Sympathetic type: "The first day is the hardest. Here—I have an extra cloth, wrap it around your hand to prevent blisters."
   - Hostile type: "It's because rookies like you get caught that the Guild sends more people to die! You know what they'll do to us? They'll torture us—then softies like you break and spill everything!"

### 9.2 Daily Tension Between Captives and Monsters

Captives and monsters share the same dungeon space—this is full of narrative possibility.

**Daily Tension Event Table (d8)**:

| d8 | Tension Event | Narrative Guidance |
|----|---------|----------|
| 1 | **Food Distribution** | "Skeletons need no food. But one is assigned to deliver food to captives—a bucket of grey porridge. Captives aren't sure if this is kindness (skeletons don't steal food) or insult (letting a lifeless thing feed them)." |
| 2 | **Shared Facilities** | "Only one water bucket beside the mine. A captive and a goblin reach for the ladle at once—eyes meet. Air freezes—then the goblin says: 'You first. I don't want a fight, my bone-fracture quota's used up today.'" |
| 3 | **Language Barrier** | "An imp is assigned to supervise captive labor. Problem: the imp only knows swear words in Abyssal. Captives speak Common. It took three days to learn 'Gazakrug' doesn't mean 'work hard'—but 'keep slacking and I'll burn your eyebrows.'" |
| 4 | **Unexpected Kindness** | "A captive faints in the mine; a hellhound nudges him awake with its nose—not attacking, checking. The hellhound's owner is confused: 'It only ever nudged its own food before.' The captive wakes: 'It touched my face with its wet nose. I dreamt I was home. Then the wet nose told me I wasn't. I still don't know if that was kindness or malice.'" |
| 5 | **Competition** | "Two captives and two goblins race to dig faster. Stake is a piece of dry bread. Captives win. Goblins protest: 'You're humans! Your hands are bigger! Unfair!' The captive laughs—his first laugh since capture." |
| 6 | **Common Threat** | "In a cave-in, a captive and a gargoyle are buried in the same corner. In six hours of darkness together, they have the longest conversation of their lives—the captive learns the gargoyle fears not water but rats; the gargoyle learns the captive has a daughter." |
| 7 | **Culture Clash** | "Captives make a simple chessboard from bedsheets in the prison. The hellhound guard stares at the board for an hour, then knocks over a piece with its claw—it's playing. The rules it invented itself. Captives accept the new game—nothing else to do anyway." |
| 8 | **Class Consciousness** | "A shadow being shunned by other monsters starts hovering outside the captive prison. Not to guard—it's lonely. A strange tacit understanding forms between captives and monsters: neither belongs where they're supposed to be." |

**GM Guidance—Narrative Principles for Captive-Monster Relations**:
- Don't write all monsters as "cruel guards"—diverse interaction is more interesting
- Monsters are also "workers"—they may show unexpected empathy for the captives' situation
- "Friendship" between captives and monsters is the most subversive narrative weapon against the Surface worldview
- Occasionally let a monster "speak" for a captive—this makes the player rethink their relationship with minions

### 9.3 "Former Paladin Becomes Prison Warden" Conversion Narrative Arc

This is the most classic and poignant narrative arc in a dungeon—how a light paladin step by step becomes a dark manager.

**Conversion Narrative Five-Step**:

#### Step One: Arrival—"You monsters, the light will judge you."

> The paladin **[Name]** is pushed into the prison; armor stripped, but back straight as a sword. Their first words are not a plea but a curse. In the most standard cathedral Common, word by word, they declare—the light's judgment will come, the dark nest will turn to ash.
>
> The words echo through the dungeon corridors several times.
>
> The skeletons don't react. The slime gurgles—probably wondering if this human is edible.

#### Step Two: Refusing Labor—"I'd rather die than swing a pick for darkness."

> The first three days, the paladin refuses labor. Hunger strike. Silence. Sits in the prison corner facing the wall—some say praying.
>
> Day four, another captive is escorted out—never returns. (Actually just transferred to another mine, but the paladin doesn't know.)
>
> Day five, the paladin picks up the pick. "The light's will needs me alive," they say. No one contradicts them.

#### Step Three: First Professional Contribution—"Your prison management has problems."

> Two months later. The paladin has grown used to the mine's rhythm. They notice a problem: the prison guard shift schedule is unreasonable. Under the current system there's a five-minute window when guards change—smart captives could exploit it to escape.
>
> On the way back to prison one day, the paladin says to the escorting imp: "You should tell your master—if any captive truly intends to escape, they'll act at midnight guard change."
>
> The imp tilts its head. The paladin sighs: "Forget it, I'll tell him myself."
>
> **This is the paladin's firstproactive conversation with the Dungeon Lord—not interrogation, but advice.** A subtle turning point.

#### Step Four: Recognized Transformation—"I need someone who understands the captive mind."

> Half a year later. The paladin is no longer an ordinary captive. They hold a vague status in the dungeon—not prisoner, not minion. An "advisor"—a word they won't admit themselves.
>
> The Dungeon Lord says publicly: "I need someone who understands the captive psyche—who's plotting escape, who's wavering, who can be converted. Who better than a former paladin?"
>
> The paladin accepts the role. They tell themselves it's to protect captives from abuse. Maybe it started that way.

#### Step Five: Prison Warden—"I'm not a traitor. I just… figured it out."

> A year later. The new prison warden stands at the prison entrance, two hellhounds behind—they obey his commands not from fear but respect.
>
> New captives look at him with hatred—a former paladin wearing Abyssal insignia. "Traitor! You betrayed the light!"
>
> The warden isn't angry. Calmly: "The light never came here. I waited a year; the light didn't save me. But the darkness—the darkness gave me a position."
>
> As he turns to leave, the key ring at his waist clinks softly. Not the prison keys—the dungeon's keys. He is now a manager, not a prisoner.

**Conversion Narrative GM Guidance Points**:
- **Conversion doesn't happen overnight**—give each stage enough time (in months, across multiple management turns)
- **The player must be the catalyst**—the player's kindness, trust, or at least "fairness" is the core driving force of conversion
- **The mark of conversion is not "forgetting the light" but "finding new meaning in the darkness"**
- **Survivor's guilt**: a converted former paladin shouldn't be wholly happy—they should carry a certain "guilt toward their past self," which is itself a source of dramatic tension
- **Give the converted a highlight moment**: let the converted prove loyalty through action at a key moment (invasion/disaster)—perhaps saving the Dungeon Lord, or stopping a riot

**Other Captive Conversion Paths (short narrative templates, for GM improvisation)**:

| Class | Converted Role | Conversion Narrative Core |
|------|-----------|------------|
| Former Warrior → Training Ground Instructor | "He no longer trains adventurers how to beat monsters—he trains monsters how to beat adventurers. His course: 'Adventurers' Ten Common Mistakes.'" |
| Former Thief → Trap Workshop Master | "She was the Adventurers' Guild's best trap-disarmer. Now she designs traps—and she knows how a disarmer thinks. Her traps never miss." |
| Former Mage → Research Chamber Head | "He was mocked at the Surface academy for a 'too dark' research direction. Now he has infinite budget, infinite samples, and zero ethical constraints." |

## Chapter Ten: Management-Phase GM Running Guide (New) ⭐

### 10.1 The Complete Rhythm of a Management Turn

The original GM guide focused on invasion building. Below is the expanded management-phase guidance—covering the full cycle of expansion, building, assignment, and settlement.

**Management Turn = Expansion → Build → Assign → Settle (→ Invasion)**

```
                    ┌──────────────────────┐
                    │  1. Dig Phase (DIG)     │
                    │  Dig check → unlock space   │
                    │  Discover resources/accidents    │
                    └──────────┬───────────┘
                               │
                               ▼
                    ┌──────────────────────┐
                    │  2. Build Phase (BUILD)  │
                    │  Choose what room to build      │
                    │  Spend materials + time      │
                    └──────────┬───────────┘
                               │
                               ▼
                    ┌──────────────────────┐
                    │  3. Assign Phase (ASSIGN) │
                    │  Monsters: mgmt vs combat    │
                    │  Captives: assign labor post    │
                    └──────────┬───────────┘
                               │
                               ▼
                    ┌──────────────────────┐
                    │  4. Settle Phase (TALLY)  │
                    │  Resource income logged           │
                    │  Upkeep deducted           │
                    │  Captive Willpower/Stamina updated      │
                    │  Monster Loyalty check         │
                    └──────────┬───────────┘
                               │
                               ▼
                    ┌──────────────────────┐
                    │  5. Invade Phase (INVADE) │
                    │  Infamy check → invasion scale   │
                    │  Defense deploy → combat      │
                    │  (may interleave any phase)  │
                    └──────────────────────┘
```

### 10.2 GM Guidance for Each Phase

#### Dig Phase (DIG)

**Duration**: about 10–15 min/turn

**GM Opening Template**:

> "The dungeon map spreads before you—only **[X]** rooms of dark space. Your **[minions/monsters]** wait by the digging face. This turn, which direction will you expand? North? East? Or down—into deeper darkness?"

**Pace Control**:
- Don't let digging become pure mechanical dice-rolling. Before each dig, ask the player: "What do you hope to dig up?"—then don't necessarily give the player what they want
- Alternate "find something good" (vein, mana node) and "find trouble" (underground river, creature nest) to maintain tension
- If the player expands without building for several turns—remind them of the under-defense risk

**GM Guidance Checklist**:
- [ ] Describe the current digging face's appearance (rock color, temperature, sound)
- [ ] Let the player choose expansion direction
- [ ] Make the Industry (IND) dig check
- [ ] Narrate the check result
- [ ] If a discovery, trigger the discovery table
- [ ] If an accident, trigger the accident table
- [ ] Record Depth (DEP) growth

#### Build Phase (BUILD)

**Duration**: about 5–10 min/turn

**GM Opening Template**:

> "The new space is cleared—rough rock walls, damp air, a few Mana Crystals glinting in the corner. You can build one of the **[ten room types]** here. Or you can hold off deciding, leaving space for future discovery."

**10 Standard Rooms (by build priority)**:

| Room Type | Core Function | Suggested Build Timing |
|----------|---------|------------|
| **Throne Room** | Command center, +actions | Initially free, must always exist |
| **Mines** | Resource output (materials/dark coins) | 1st–2nd build, economic base |
| **Monster Lair** | House/recruit monsters | 1st–2nd build, defense base |
| **Trap Workshop** | Craft traps | Build at Depth (DEP) 2–3 |
| **Prison Cells** | Hold captives | Must have before first captive |
| **Research Chamber** | Magic research / Mana Crystals | After stable material income |
| **Treasure Vault** | Store wealth / interest | When Dark Coins have surplus |
| **Altar (Ritual Chamber)** | Rituals / Soul Essence | When sacrifice/curse needed |
| **Training Ground** | Raise monster Combat Power | Consider with 3+ monsters |
| **Scrying Post** | InvasionWarning / intel | Strongly recommended at Infamy (INF) 3+ |

#### Assign Phase (ASSIGN)

**Duration**: about 10 min/turn

**GM Opening Template**:

> "Now you must decide each monster's post. **[List monster names]** may be deployed to management—mining, building, research—or to defense—standing guard, patrolling, watching. But one monster can't be in two places. Your choice determines this turn's resource output and defense strength."

**Narrating Assignment Decisions**:
- When the player deploys a monster to management: describe it walking to the mine/workshop daily scene
- When the player deploys a monster to defense: describe its alert posture at the tunnel entrance
- If a monster is deployed to the same post for several turns: trigger a "habit" narrative (efficiency +1, but becomes predictable)

**"Emergency Summons" Narrative**:

> The invasion alarm sounds. The skeleton soldiers mining with picks look up at once—though they have no eyes, the motion still accurately expresses "now?" They drop picks, grab shields, bone joints clicking densely as they run. The first defense floor needs them. The mine's output log will show a blank today—but that's a concern for after survival.

#### Settle Phase (TALLY)

**Duration**: about 5 min/turn

**GM Settlement Checklist**:
- [ ] All room output credited to resource stock
- [ ] Captive labor output calculated
- [ ] Monster upkeep deducted
- [ ] Room upkeep deducted
- [ ] Captive Stamina −1 (per laboring captive)
- [ ] Monster Loyalty check (monsters in continuous combat / unmaintained)
- [ ] Captive escape check (Willpower vs Defense)
- [ ] Infamy value updated (if new event)
- [ ] World event advanced (if any)

### 10.3 Describing the Dungeon's Visual Growth

The dungeon is not just growing numbers—it is alive; every inch of expansion changes its face.

**Dungeon Growth Visual Narrative Guide**:

| Depth (DEP) | Dungeon State | GM-Usable Visual Description |
|-----|---------|-----------------|
| 1–2 | **Small Cave** | "Your dungeon extends a few rough tunnels out from the Throne Room. Walls still bear fresh-chisel marks. The air has a damp earth smell and Mana Crystal glimmer. Standing in the Throne Room you hear every tunnel's sound—pick strikes, low growls, captives' footsteps. The dungeon is still small; every corner is within your perception." |
| 3–5 | **Medium Cave Cluster** | "The dungeon has some depth now. Tunnels fork and fork again, forming an initial underground network. Some corridors are paved with stone, some still natural earth. Torches are no longer one every five steps—some places grow dim. You must deliberately walk to inspect all areas." |
| 6–8 | **Large Crypt** | "Standing in the Throne Room, you can no longer hear the mine's pick sound—too far. The dungeon has a 'floor' concept: outer sentry posts, middle lairs, inner chambers. Different zones have different smells—sentry post sulfur, prison iron-rust, vault metal sheen. Your dungeon is large enough for strangers to get lost." |
| 9–12 | **Grand Crypt Cluster** | "Your dungeon is no longer one building—it's a landscape. Multi-level structure, ring corridors, secret passages, hidden rooms. Minions havededicated tunnel networks; captives travel fixed routes. New adventurers need a map not to get lost—and your map is alive, because corridors keep growing." |
| 13–15 | **Heart of the Abyss Empire** | "Words fail. Your dungeon is an underground city—no, an underground kingdom. Mine linked to mine, lair nested in lair. A command from the Throne Room passes through three layers of officers, five tunnels, two transfer stations to reach the farthest corner. Your dungeon has its own ecology, its own economy, its own legends. You're not running a dungeon—you're ruling a civilization." |

**GM Description Technique—Five Senses Method**:
- **Sight**: light source (torches / Mana Crystals / bioluminescence), wall material (natural rock / stone slab / glaze / bone), space height
- **Hearing**: pick-echo, monster growl, water drips, wind (if rift connects to Surface)
- **Smell**: sulfur, earth, metal, blood, ozone of magic residue
- **Touch**: wall temperature, air humidity, ground smoothness
- **"Sixth sense"**: direct feel of mana density—does skin prickle, does hair stand

### 10.4 GM Intervention When Players Expand Too Fast

**Intervention Principle**: Don't say "no" directly—use narrative to create consequences, letting the player realize "expansion is too fast" themselves.

**Intervention Toolbox**:

| Intervention Level | Means | Narrative Example | Game Effect |
|----------|------|----------|----------|
| **Light Reminder** | Minion feedback | "Foreman Crag wipes sweat: 'Boss, we now have eight rooms to maintain but only five workers. Each corridor takes three days to get one cleaning.'" | Info hint, no forced penalty |
| **Moderate Warning** | Structural problem | "The Research Chamber mage sends a note: support beam load analysis shows 17% collapse risk if two more rooms are added without reinforcing existing structure." | Next dig difficulty +1 |
| **Forced Slowdown** | Resource shortage | "Building Material reserves hit zero. The next pick down, you can only dig with fingernails." | Cannot make dig check until new materials gained |
| **Event Intervention** | Monster revolt | "The imps went on strike collectively. Reason: 'overwork, lack of rest, and excessive slime residue in rations.' The lead imp holds a sign: 'We demand basic monster rights.'" | This turn's management output halved |
| **External Pressure** | Neighbor covets | "A neighboring Abyss Lord sends an envoy: 'You expand too fast. My master worries you'll dig into his territory. He suggests—slow down. In return he promises not to send adventurers to your dungeon.'" | Diplomatic crisis, must handle or face penalty |
| **Severe Threat** | Deep dig disturbs ancient being | "Earth groan. Not illusion this time. The whole dungeon trembles; wall Mana Crystals all flash a color you've never seen—dark red. Your Arcana check tells you: something is below. It has been sleeping. You woke it." | Introduce legendary-level threat, may need multiple turns to resolve |

**GM "Yes, and" Rule**:

> Player: "I'll keep digging! This time I send all skeletons, three shifts, non-stop digging!"
>
> GM: "Sure—you're expanding like mad. Two weeks later your Depth hits 7. The dungeon's scale is impressive. **And**—last night a squad of adventurers entered through a tunnel you don't even remember digging. You showed off your muscles but forgot to armor each one."

### 11.2 Time Scale

**Suggested management turn = 1 month (in-game time)**

- Each management turn, the player makes expansion, building, research, diplomacy decisions

## Chapter Twelve: GM Running Guide (original Chapter Eight, revised)

### 12.1 GM Role Positioning

In this rule, the GM plays the following roles:

| Role | Duty |
|------|------|
| **Adventurer Commander** | Controls invading adventurers—their tactics, goals, and narrative arc |
| **World Engine** | Controls weather, seasons, mana anomalies, world events and other external factors |
| **NPC Voice** | Plays captives, allies, enemies, neutral-force NPCs. **Especially including monsters' "personality" and captives' "humanity"** |
| **Expansion Narrator** | Creates discovery and unknown feeling for every dig—what's behind the rock? |
| **Rule Judge** | Makes final rulings when rules are ambiguous |
| **Narrative Pacemaster** | Controls management/combat pace switching, ensures the game doesn't sink into dullness—the expand→build→assign→settle→invade loop needs breathing room |

**The GM is not the player's enemy.** The GM's goal is not to "beat the player" but to "create challenges and stories for the player." Your adventurers should be smart but not cheat, dangerous but beatable.

### 12.2 GM Core Principles

#### Principle One: "Yes, and…"

The player's creativity should be accepted and advanced, not rejected.

> Player: "I'll have my captive former paladin manage my Trap Workshop."
> GM: "Sure. He not only manages well—he starts improving traps with light magic, making them especially effective against the Order of Light. But he struggles inside: this is betraying former comrades."

> Player: "I'll dig straight down—keep digging, see what's deepest."
> GM: "Sure. Your skeletons start burrowing down. Three days later the rock wall grows warm. A week later the pick tip starts melting. **And**—when you dig to a certain depth, all Mana Crystals pulse purple at once, then you hear a voice. Not from the rock wall—from inside your head."

#### Principle Two: The Dungeon is "Alive"

Make the dungeon feel like a real, living place.

- NPC minions have their own personalities and desires—they're not just numbers
- Captives are not just numbers—they have a past, emotions, change
- The dungeon's state affects atmosphere: triumph is cheery, failure is oppressive
- Different-faction adventurers bring different "flavor" when invading
- **The dungeon is growing**—every expansion changes its "personality," like a city growing

#### Principle Three: Every Invasion Has a Story

Don't let invasions become pure number clashes. Every invasion should have a narrative hook.

#### Principle Four: Captives Are People

They were once adventurers—with their own dreams, fears, and stories. Make them characters, not just "+1 labor."

#### Principle Five: Every Expansion Has a Discovery

Don't let digging become a mechanical loop of "roll→success→gain space." **There's always something behind the rock.** It may be good, may be bad, but should never be "nothing."

### 12.3 How to Build an Invasion

#### Step 1: Check if there's an invasion this month

Roll 1d6, referencing the following difficulty (GM may adjust per plot needs):

| Dungeon Infamy Value (Infamy (INF)) | Invasion Chance (d6 ≥ threshold) |
|------------------|---------------------|
| 1–2 | 4+ |
| 3–5 | 3+ |
| 6–9 | 2+ |
| 10+ | Automatic invasion (possibly more than one wave) |

> **Alignment note**: Infamy range is 1–15. The above table is aligned with the character system's Infamy (INF) range (adjusted original 0–2 to 1–2, since Infamy (INF) minimum is 1).

**Special modifiers**:
- Dungeon in hidden state: +1 threshold (harder to find)
- Major Surface event (war, disaster): −1 threshold (adventurers have no time to come)
- Order of Light launches Holy War: −2 threshold (many adventurers flood the Rift Borderlands)
- Dungeon has a Mana Node: −1 threshold (mana fluctuation attracts adventurers)
- Dungeon uses Shadow Council's false intel: +1 threshold (misdirection succeeds)

#### Step 2: Determine invasion source (adventurer faction)

Roll 1d6:

| d6 | Faction | Motivation |
|----|------|------|
| 1–2 | Adventurers' Guild | Quest posting: wealth / fame |
| 3 | Order of Light | Religious mission: purification |
| 4 | Royal Investigation Corps | Intel gathering / threat assessment |
| 5 | Freelance Hunters | Diverse private motives |
| 6 | GM's choice or mixed | Multi-faction joint invasion (special event) |

#### Step 3: Determine invasion scale (adventurer count)

Roll 1d6, adjust by Infamy value:

| Infamy Value (Infamy (INF)) | 1–2 | 3–4 | 5–6 |
|-------------|-----|-----|-----|
| 1–2 | 1–2 people | 2–3 people | 3–4 people |
| 3–5 | 2–3 people | 3–4 people | 4–5 people |
| 6–9 | 3–4 people | 4–5 people | 5–6 people |
| 10+ | 4–5 people | 5–6 people | 6–8 people |

> **Alignment note**: Adjusted original Infamy (INF) 0–2 to 1–2.

#### Step 4: Determine adventurer Tier

| Dungeon Infamy Value (Infamy (INF)) | Base Tier | Variance |
|-----------------|----------|------|
| 1 | Tier 1 | ±0 |
| 2–4 | Tier 1–2 | ±1 |
| 5–7 | Tier 2–3 | ±1 |
| 8–10 | Tier 3–4 | ±1 |
| 11–15 | Tier 4–5 | ±1 |

> **Alignment note**: Adjusted original Infamy (INF) 0–1 to 1, original 11+ to 11–15.

- Roll 1d6: 1–2 = lower 1 level, 3–4 = unchanged, 5–6 = raise 1 level
- Overall invasion threat = Tier × adventurer count (for difficulty assessment)

#### Step 5: Determine class composition

Roll 1d6 to decide each adventurer's class:

| d6 | Class |
|----|------|
| 1–2 | Warrior |
| 3 | Mage |
| 4 | Thief |
| 5 | Cleric |
| 6 | Ranger (or reroll) |

The squad's class composition should have basic complementarity. GM may fine-tune.

#### Step 6: Assign a narrative hook

Roll 1d6 to decide this invasion's "why":

| d6 | Narrative Hook |
|----|---------|
| 1 | **Greed**: Adventurers heard of your treasure, want to get rich |
| 2 | **Revenge**: An adventurer's friend/relative was harmed by your dungeon (or another) |
| 3 | **Fame**: A young adventurer wants to make a name through you |
| 4 | **Mistake**: They were actually looking for another dungeon, took a wrong turn |
| 5 | **Mission**: Formally assigned by the Order of Light or Guild |
| 6 | **Secret**: Adventurers came with another purpose—possibly spy mission, seeking a specific item, or fleeing Surface pursuit |

### 12.4 Pace Control

**Complete management-turn pace (about 45–60 min/turn)**:

| Phase | Duration | GM Focus |
|------|------|---------|
| Expansion | 10–15 min | Create discovery feeling and suspense |
| Build | 5–10 min | Offer strategic choices, describe dungeon change |
| Assign | 10 min | Narrate monster and captive deployment |
| Settle | 5 min | Quick numbers, insert narrative snippets |
| Invasion (if any) | 45–60 min | See below |

**Invasion Phase (about 45–60 min per session)**:
- Warning → deploy → floor-by-floor defense → final showdown → settle
- Leave space between floor defenses for players to discuss strategy
- Pace should be tight but not rushed—make every roll carry tension

**Transition Hints**:
- End of expansion phase: "The pick falls for the last time. After the rubble clears, a brand-new space opens before you. Empty, damp, full of possibility. The question now is—what will you put here?"
- End of management phase: "Just as you inspect the new trap, the gargoyle at the sentry post sounds the alarm—someone's coming."
- End of invasion phase: "The last adventurer falls. The dungeon falls silent again, but you know it's only temporary."

### 12.5 Difficulty Adjustment

#### Real-Time Adjustment Means

| Situation | Adjustment Method |
|------|------|
| Players too easy | Adventurers gain reinforcements (roll 1d6, 4+ hidden adventurer appears); adventurers use special items (holy water, dispel arrows) |
| Players too hard | Adventurers infight (roll 1d6, 4+ adventurers waste a turn arguing); dungeon mana burst (player gains temporary buff) |
| Bad dice luck | "Yes, and"—failure brings interesting consequences, not just loss |
| Good dice luck | Adventurers pull out a "trump card"—stronger magic or hidden ability |

#### Long-Term Difficulty Curve

| Campaign Stage | Infamy Value | Typical Threat | GM Suggestion |
|----------|--------|----------|---------|
| Novice | 1–3 | T1 adventurers, 1–3 people | Let players learn the system. First captive should be an "easy win." **Focus guidance on expand-build-assign loop** |
| Growth | 4–7 | T2–3 adventurers, 3–5 people | Introduce adventurer faction differences. Captives start having complex backstories. **Expansion discovers more resources/threats** |
| Threat | 8–12 | T3–4 adventurers, 4–6 people | Legendary heroes may appear. Dungeon diplomacy becomes important. **Cost of expansion greed begins to show** |
| Overlord | 13–15 | T4–5 adventurers, 5–8 people | Multi-faction joint attacks. Abyssal politics becomes main play. **Risk of deep dig disturbing ancient beings** |

> **Alignment note**: Campaign-stage Infamy (INF) range aligned with character system (Novice 1–3, Overlord 13–15).

### 12.6 Captive Narrative System (retained and enhanced)

Captives are this system's most unique narrative driver. Below are ways to make captives "come alive."

#### Captive Background Quick Generator

Roll 3d6, consult tables separately:

**Die A: Captive's Origin**

| d6 | Origin |
|----|------|
| 1 | Farmer's child—became adventurer to escape poverty |
| 2 | Noble bastard—unacknowledged by family, adventure proves self |
| 3 | Orphan—grew up in the Adventurers' Guild, adventure is the only home |
| 4 | Retired soldier—found no belonging after the war ended |
| 5 | Scholar—came to study the Abyss, didn't expect to actually be caught |
| 6 | Criminal—wanted on the Surface, adventure is means of escape |

**Die B: Captive's Motivation**

| d6 | Motivation |
|----|------|
| 1 | Earn medicine money for family (sick relative at home) |
| 2 | Pursue honor (wants to be written into history) |
| 3 | Repay debt (owes a underground loan shark a large sum) |
| 4 | Escape (something on the Surface they don't want to face) |
| 5 | Faith (truly believes they're doing right) |
| 6 | Boredom (Surface life too dull, Abyss is thrilling) |

**Die C: Captive's Secret**

| d6 | Secret |
|----|------|
| 1 | Actually child of a Surface bigwig |
| 2 | Carries something valuable/dangerous on person |
| 3 | Knows a skill very useful to the dungeon (but won't reveal it) |
| 4 | Once captured by another dungeon (not strange to the Abyss) |
| 5 | Has a terminal illness—less than a year left |
| 6 | Betrayed/abandoned by teammates on this mission (resentment toward Surface) |

**Example Combinations**:
- A4/B2/C6: A retired soldier, came for honor, but abandoned as bait by teammates at a key moment. His belief in "glory" is shaken—high conversion possibility.
- A5/B4/C3: A scholar, came to the Abyss to escape Surface academic politics, actually masters ancient runes—if the Dungeon Lord discovers and uses this.
- A1/B1/C5: A farm girl, became adventurer for her mother's medicine money, but she herself has a terminal illness. She just wants to earn a bit more before being discovered.

#### Captive Narrative Four Stages

| Stage | Time | Narrative Focus |
|------|------|----------|
| **Resistance** | 1–3 months after capture | Captive tries to keep dignity, refuses labor, may attempt escape. This stage's interaction decides the later path |
| **Wavering** | 3–6 months after capture | After failed escape (or after one kind treatment), captive begins to doubt Surface life and values |
| **Adapting** | 6–12 months after capture | Captive finds some belonging or purpose in the dungeon. May start offering suggestions proactively |
| **Conversion** | Key event after adapting | A decisive event (e.g.: given trust, protected the dungeon, or discovered the Surface "justice" was false) makes the captive completely "turn" |

#### Conversion Narrative Triggers

Conversion shouldn't just be "Willpower hits zero." The GM may design key narrative moments:

- **Betrayal Revealed**: Captive discovers the Adventurers' Guild once sold out his squad for profit
- **Rescue**: The Dungeon Lord chooses to protect the captive when in danger (even if "irrational")
- **Recognition**: The lord publicly commends the captive's contribution, giving respect unprecedented in the dungeon
- **Disillusionment**: Captive witnesses the Order of Light doing "un-light" things firsthand
- **Belonging**: Captive finds the dungeon's "dark creatures" treat him better than the Surface's "decent people"

#### Famous Former-Adventurer Lieutenants (narrative reference)

- **Gal "Iron Glove"**: Former T4 Paladin, deliberately "captured" mid-mission after discovering the Order of Light's high ranks embezzled donor funds. Now a Necromancer Lord's lieutenant, trains undead legion tactics—"the dead are more honest than the living."
- **Lina "Darkfire"**: Former T3 Mage, top student at the Silver Federation Magic Academy. Expelled after a lab accident; her adventurer squad used her as cannon fodder. After capture by an Abyssal Overlord, the demon lord gave her a lab of her own. Now she researches "fusion of light and dark magic"—a subject that would get her burned on the Surface.

### 12.7 Campaign Progression Suggestions (revised)

#### Phase One: Establishment (Infamy 1–3, about 4–8 sessions)

- **Theme**: Survival
- **Core activity**: Build initial dungeon, first dig expansion, repel scattered adventurers, first captive
- **Key events**: First dig (wholly unknown experience); first successful defense; first captive interrogation
- **World state**: Adventurers don't know you yet. You're just "another new dungeon"
- **GM suggestion**: Keep threats mild, let players enjoy building the dungeon. **First two sessions focus guidance on expand-build-assign loop**

#### Phase Two: Growth (Infamy 4–7, about 6–10 sessions)

- **Theme**: Expansion
- **Core activity**: Expand dungeon, recruit stronger minions, accumulate captive labor. **Discover resource veins / mana nodes**
- **Key events**: Adventurers' Guild posts quests targeting you; first high-tier adventurer invasion; may trigger "Legendary Foreman" narrative line
- **World state**: You start gaining "fame" among Surface adventurers. Other dungeon lords notice you
- **GM suggestion**: Introduce adventurer faction differences and captives' complex backgrounds. **Start showing the "cost" of expansion**

#### Phase Three: Threat (Infamy 8–12, about 6–10 sessions)

- **Theme**: Diplomacy and War
- **Core activity**: Dungeon diplomacy (ally/rival), face legendary heroes. **After expanding to a scale, face structural problems and external threats**
- **Key events**: Legendary hero deployed; Abyssal Council intervention; first alliance with another dungeon; may trigger "former paladin conversion" narrative line
- **World state**: You're seen as an "important player." Surface reaction upgrades from ignore to attention
- **GM suggestion**: Combat difficulty rises, but give diplomacy and intel means to prepare ahead. **Increase the "deep-dig curse" narrative tension**

#### Phase Four: Overlord (Infamy 13–15, about 4–8 sessions)

- **Theme**: Rule
- **Core activity**: Manage multiple dungeons/vassals, participate in high-level Abyssal politics, may change the world order
- **Key events**: Power struggle at the Abyssal Council; Surface multi-nation allied invasion; major choices deciding the Abyss's future
- **World state**: You're among the most powerful lords in the Abyss. Your every move changes the world
- **GM suggestion**: Let players' choices have far-reaching impact. This is the campaign's endgame. Don't rush to end—let players enjoy the taste of power. **Make the ancient-being threat the final challenge**

## Chapter Thirteen: Starter Module—"First Incursion" (revised v2.0)

### Module Info

- **Difficulty**: Novice
- **Recommended players**: 1–4
- **Estimated length**: 2–3 sessions (about 4–6 hours)
- **Prerequisite**: Players have read the core rules and character creation chapters

### Module Overview

The player is a new Abyss Lord who just inherited/founded a small dungeon. A few years ago, the player's "predecessor" (master/parent/former lord) was killed in an adventurer invasion, and the dungeon has been abandoned since. Now it's the player's turn to rebuild it.

The dungeon's initial space is tiny—only the Throne Room and two undeveloped small spaces. The first outward dig is the module's core narrative experience.

Just as the dungeon's basic infrastructure resumes operation, three young Surface adventurers barge in—they think it's an abandoned dungeon, a good chance to "level up."

They're wrong.

### Scene 1: Beginning in the Ruins → Build Initial Dungeon

**Goal**: Player builds initial dungeon configuration

**Setup**:
- Dungeon lies at the edge of Gloom Forest, in a forgotten cavern
- Remains of the predecessor lord still there—collapsed Throne Room, rusty traps, scattered bones
- Mana density: low but stable, enough to sustain basic operation
- **Initial space**: Throne Room (free) + two small undeveloped spaces (must be dug to use)

**Initial Resources**:
- 3 units Mana Crystals
- 5 units Building Materials
- 2 loyaljunior minions (GM provides per player's chosen archetype: Necromancer Lord gets 2 skeleton soldiers, Abyssal Overlord gets 2 imps, and so on)
- No captives
- Initial Depth (DEP) = 1

**GM Hint**:
- Describe the dungeon's desolation: "Dust covers the predecessor lord's throne. In the corner is a skeleton still clutching an unsent letter—to the Abyssal Council, requesting aid. Dated three years ago."
- Leave a narrative thread: the truth of the predecessor lord's death (may be revealed later in the module)
- **Don't let the player build too much at this stage—two undeveloped spaces must be dug first**

### Scene 2 (New ⭐): First Dig Expansion

**Goal**: Player experiences the complete expansion loop—dig → discover → build initial facilities

**This is one of the module's most important tutorial scenes.** The player starts from a small cavern with only the Throne Room, commanding minions outward to dig unknown space for the first time.

**GM Opening Narrative**:

> The north wall of the Throne Room is a rough natural rock wall—the end of the cavern. You touch the rock surface with your fingertips: cold, hard, slightly damp. Behind you the skeleton soldiers (or your initial minions) wait silently.
>
> The predecessor lord never expanded this dungeon. Maybe no time, maybe killed too early. Whatever the reason—you're the first lord who truly wants this dungeon to "grow."
>
> What's behind the rock? Only by digging open do you know.
>
> "Begin."

**Dig Check** (tutorial nature, GM suggested to lower initial difficulty):
- Expansion difficulty: 2 (initial minimum)
- Player rolls Industry (IND) + any relevant skill dice pool
- As a tutorial scene, GM should guide before rolling: "Your attribute value determines dice pool size. Industry (IND) is the core attribute for digging—if you have relevant archetype or support abilities, add them to the pool too."

**Result Narrative**:

| Net Successes | Result | Narrative |
|----------|------|------|
| 0 | Failure | "The pick tip hits granite. Very hard. Very hard. Skeleton #3's hand bone cracks—it swaps to the left hand unfazed. But you need a different direction." (Allow lossless retry, since tutorial) |
| 1 | Small space | "After the rock crumbles, a cramped little hollow appears. Barely enough for one small room. You smell faint sulfur in the air—maybe groundwater, maybe something else." (Gain 1 buildable space) |
| 2+ | Medium space | "The wall collapses with a roar. Before you is a natural cavern—a bit bigger than the Throne Room. Stalactites glimmer in firelight. More importantly, dark gold streaks on the rock wall—a dark gold vein! Low quality, but for a new dungeon it's the first pot of gold." (Gain 2 buildable spaces + discover micro dark gold vein: mine output +1 Dark Coin/turn) |

**Extra Discovery Narrative Hooks (d6, GM optional)**:

| d6 | Extra Discovery |
|----|---------|
| 1–2 | Predecessor lord's notes carved on rock: "Dig north—I sense mana flow that way." (Guides next expansion direction) |
| 3–4 | Discover a fist-sized high-purity Mana Crystal—emergency reserve left by predecessor lord |
| 5 | Dig up a rusty iron box with a faded map—marks known nearby dungeon locations (diplomatic intel) |
| 6 | Faint knocking from deep in the rock—three knocks, pause, three more. Not echo. An answer. (Introduce mystery narrative hook, may reveal later) |

**GM Guidance Points**:
- Make expansion feel fun—first dig should feel like "opening a gift"
- Even on check failure, give some interesting narrative compensation (e.g. "though not dug open, you notice strange marks on the wall")
- After completion prompt: "Now you have space. You can choose what room to build—mine, monster lair, trap workshop, prison…"

**First Build Decision Guidance**:

> "Now you have two buildable spaces before you. You can choose what to build. Remember—you need resources (mine produces materials and dark coins), you need defense (monster lair holds more monsters), and you'll soon need prison (because once adventurers invade, you'll want to capture them not kill them). But you only have two spaces. Choose."

Suggested build order: Mine (economic base) + Monster Lair (defense base) or Mine + Prison (prepare for captives).

### Scene 3: First Wave of Invasion

**Invasion source**: Adventurers' Guild (three T1 adventurers—they just want to "level up")

**Adventurer Squad**:

| Name | Class | Tier | Trait |
|------|------|------|------|
| Allen Anvil | Warrior | T1 | Hot-blooded but reckless. Nominal squad captain |
| Mira Shadowveil | Thief | T1 | Cautious, always advises retreat. Actually doesn't trust Allen's leadership |
| Old Tom | Mage (apprentice) | T1 | Learned magic late in life, wants to prove he's "not old yet" |

**Narrative Hook**: They think it's an abandoned dungeon. Allen bragged in a Surface tavern about "clearing a dungeon single-handedly"; Mira can't talk him out of it; Old Tom doesn't want to be seen as a coward.

**GM Hint**:
- Adventurer tactics should have flaws—Allen charges front, Mira tries to scout but is interrupted by Allen, Old Tom's magic may glitch
- They're not evil, just young and stupid
- If player has overwhelming advantage, let Mira shout "Retreat!" but Old Tom can't run fast—at least one captive

**Suggested Combat Dialogue**:
- Allen (after first trap triggers): "Uh… this might not be abandoned?"
- Mira: "I said we should scout first!"
- Old Tom (startled by skeleton): "I retired too late…"
- Allen (seeing Throne Room lights): "Wait—this place is operated by someone?"

### Scene 4: First Captive and Interrogation

**Goal**: Player experiences the captive system

At least one adventurer should be captured (Old Tom recommended—most narrative potential).

**Interrogation Scene**:
- Old Tom is frightened at first, but quickly resigns: "This old bone, labor is labor wherever I am."
- He reveals: Allen picked this in the Guild's bronze-tier quests—"new dungeon, should be easy"
- Secret (Die C = 3): Old Tom was an architect's apprentice in youth, switched to magic later. He knows building structure—if treated well after capture, he can advise on dungeon building (build efficiency +1)

**First Captive Labor Assignment** (new narrative):

> Old Tom is taken to the mine. He looks at the pick, then the rock wall, sighs: "You know, I did this thirty years ago. Took me thirty years to escape the mine, to learn magic. And here I am again."
>
> He picks up the pick, moves with surprising skill. "At least the stone here doesn't look so hard. And—" he lowers his voice, "—at least no Surface mine foreman with a whip."
>
> The first pick falls, precisely splitting a rock seam. Old Tom, architect-apprentice by training, is unexpectedly useful.

**GM Hint**:
- Let the player choose: throw Old Tom in prison? Assign what labor? What attitude in interrogation?
- If player chooses torture/intimidation, Old Tom's will drops fast but he won't truly submit
- If player chooses appeasement (food, conversation), Old Tom's conversion time shortens
- **In the first interrogation, show "captives are people with stories" not just labor**

### Scene 5: Post-War Settlement and Second Expansion (New ⭐)

**Goal**: Let player experience the complete "post-war recovery → expand again → build → assign" loop

**Post-War Settlement**:
- Adventurer gear drops: broken sword, apprentice staff, thief tools (quality: common)
- If captured Old Tom: gain 1 captive laborer (class skill: building + magic research)
- If Allen or Mira escaped: they bring back news of "a new dungeon"—Infamy +1
- Infamy update: +1 (base) + escapee bonus
- Experience gained: repel invasion +3 EXP, captive +1 EXP, build room +1 Experience (EXP)

**Second Dig Narrative Guidance**:

> Traces of the first invasion remain—blood in corridors, cut spider webs, a wall scratched by Allen's sword. But the dungeon lives. And living things should grow.
>
> Old Tom has worked the mine two weeks. His pick rhythm changed from initial anger to some pattern—he even starts humming an old miner's song. Skeletons don't sing, but they seem to listen intently.
>
> Now, the south wall waits. Or dig straight down? Each direction has its own possibility.

**Second Expansion**:
- Expansion difficulty raised to 3 (second expansion)
- Let player choose direction: north (may find more veins), south (may approach Rift Borderlands), down (closer to Abyssal mana source)
- If 3+ successes, trigger a truly meaningful discovery—e.g. small mana node or ancient ruins fragment

**Ending Narrative**:

> The dungeon falls silent again. But this silence is not desolation—it's a fulfilled, growing silence. Faint pick sounds from the mine—Old Tom still works. Occasional low growl from the lair. Torches in corridors sway gently in the breeze.
>
> You stand in the Throne Room. This is no longer ruins. New chisel marks on the wall. New footprints on the floor—monsters', captives', yours.
>
> Most important—the dungeon is bigger. Just one room, but it grew. This is the first time, but not the last.
>
> In Old Tom's eyes, you see a seed of transformation—disillusionment with the Surface, curiosity about the Abyss. Perhaps the first lieutenant is growing in this prison.
>
> And behind the south wall, you can still hear the faint three knocks. It's still there. Waiting for you to dig through.

### Module Expansion (if players want to continue)

- Allen and Mira bring "Old Tom captured" news back to the Guild. The Guild decides to send a stronger squad to "rescue" (next invasion wave)
- Player may interrogate Old Tom about Guild intel, prepare ahead
- This naturally transitions to the next narrative node: Adventurers' Guild's attention
- The knocking behind the south wall—GM may reveal its truth 2–3 sessions later (trapped ancient creature? Another dungeon's distress signal? Or something deeper mimicking human knocking?)

## Chapter Fourteen: Advanced Module Framework—"Adventurers' Guild Revenge"

### Module Info

- **Difficulty**: Intermediate (suited for dungeons with Infamy 5–8)
- **Recommended players**: 1–4
- **Estimated length**: 2–3 sessions

### Act One: Warning Signals

- Player gains intel (scouting minions / Shadow Council ally): Adventurers' Guild posted a "Gold-tier" quest in Glimmerstone City—targeting your dungeon
- Quest bounty: 500 gold coins + 10% of dungeon treasure
- Many sign up. GM rolls to decide final invasion squad composition

### Act Two: Preparation Time

Player has 1–2 management turns to prepare:
- Strengthen defense
- Keep expanding (but weigh resource allocation between expansion and defense—classic "expansion dilemma")
- Build extra traps
- Redeploy minions
- Possible countermeasures:
  - Bribe the "Sympathize-with-Abyss" faction in the Guild to lower quest tier → needs Wealth check
  - Send spy to infiltrate Guild for invasion squad info → needs Cunning (CUN) check
  - Ally with another dungeon for reinforcements → needs Diplomacy check

### Act Three: Floor-by-Floor Defense

**Invasion Squad**: T3 adventurers, 4–5 people

Typical "Guild Gold Squad" composition:
- 1 T3 Warrior (captain, experienced)
- 1 T3 Mage (ranged firepower)
- 1 T2 Thief (scout / trap-disarm)
- 1 T3 Cleric (heal / dispel)
- Possibly 1 T2 Ranger (ranged support)

**Tactics**: These aren't rookies. The Guild squad will:
- First send thief to scout each floor
- Mage clears traps at range
- Warrior and Cleric form frontline / heal line
- Have a retreat plan—unlike T1 adventurers who fight to the death

**GM Special Note**: If the player expanded too fast causing a weak-defense zone to be breached—let adventurers break through to the middle layer rather than straight to the Throne Room, teaching a lesson without ending the game.

### Act Four: Captive or Repel

- If player wins: high-Tier captive brings qualitative leap (T3 Warrior may be retired officer, T3 Mage knows rare magic)
- If player loses (dungeon core breached): not necessarily Game Over. Adventurers may choose to loot rather than destroy—"leave you, next time more to grab"
- If stalemate: adventurers may retreat after losing 1–2 members, but swear "next time bring stronger people"

**Post-War Expansion Reflection**: If the player's over-fast expansion caused weak defense, let a converted lieutenant or legendary foreman advise: "Master, we dug too fast. Walls not reinforced, new corridors no traps. Maybe stop and consolidate existing territory."

## Chapter Fifteen: Optional Variant Rules

### 15.1 Co-op Mode

**Multiple players co-manage one dungeon**:
- Each player plays a "department head" role in the dungeon:
  - Expansion Head: responsible for dig direction and expansion strategy
  - Defense Head: responsible for traps and monster deployment
  - Interior Head: responsible for captive management and resources
  - Diplomacy Head: responsible for interaction with other dungeons and Surface forces
  - Research Head: responsible for magic research and building
- Co-op decisions by vote; tie broken by the "Dungeon Lord" (players rotate or fixed)
- Suited for a table with 3–4 players

### 15.2 Competition Mode

**Each manages own dungeon, compete on Infamy**:
- Each player owns an independent dungeon
- Operates on the same timeline—GM handles each player's management turn in turn
- Player with highest Infamy gains "Most Watched Abyss Lord" title
- Players may trade, betray, ally
- Eventually a player may become "Abyss Overlord," others become vassals
- Suited for long campaigns with 2–3 players

### 15.3 Dungeon vs Dungeon Mode

**War between two players' dungeons**:
- Trigger: Infamy competition intensifies, resource conflict, or personal grudge
- Rules: Defender follows normal dungeon defense rules, attacker follows adventurer invasion rules (but uses monsters/minions not adventurers)
- Attacker's minion data calculated by adventurer template
- Outcome may lead to annexation, vassalage, or both sides weakened for the Surface to exploit
- Suggested in mid-late campaign

### 15.4 Adventurer Play Mode

**Flip perspective**:
- Players play adventurers, attack GM-controlled dungeons
- Uses same rule framework, but perspective flipped
- Adventurer skills map to different dice pools
- This is a "palate cleanser"—lets players feel the "attacker" side
- Can use this mode to test if your own dungeon defense is reasonable—"if we can't break in ourselves, defense is solid"
