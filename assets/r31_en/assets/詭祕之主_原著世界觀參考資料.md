# I. World History — The Five Epochs

| Epoch | Name | Characteristics |
|------|------|------|
| First Epoch | Epoch of Chaos | The Original Creator (the Primordial) existed alone, then split Himself, becoming all things and the Pathway system |
| Second Epoch | Dark Epoch | Giants, Elves, and Hetero-species dominated; humanity was enslaved; Ancient Gods (impure Sequence 0) ruled |
| Third Epoch | Cataclysm Epoch | The Ancient Sun God (Creator of Silver City) rose, established the orthodox pantheon; ultimately fell, splitting into multiple deities |
| Fourth Epoch | Era of the Gods | The Seven Gods pattern formed; Emperor Roselle (a transmigrator) drove the Industrial Revolution; the Great Cataclysm caused a historical break |
| Fifth Epoch | Iron Epoch | The main stage of the story; the Steam Industrial Age; the Seven Gods' churches rule; Outer Deity threats intensify; the doomsday countdown |

## Other Regions

- **Land of the Forsaken**: The Eastern Continent, a wilderness abandoned by the gods, where Silver City and Moon City lie.
- **Western Continent**: A mysterious region sealed by the "Lord of the Mysteries."

- **Astral World**: A deep dimension, the battlefield of gods and the slumbering place of Old Ones.
- **Sefirah Castle (Above the Gray Fog)**: The protagonist's mental-domain space, where Tarot Club meetings are held, secluded from outside prying.

## 3.4 Core Mechanics

### Acting Method
- The key to Beyonder power is not simply "leveling up," but **"digesting the potion."**
- The potion's name is itself the key — the Beyonder must "act as" the role the potion name represents.
- A Seer must truly divine, a Marauder must truly steal — perfunctory acting grants no progress.
- The key point of acting: **"remember you are only acting,"** for losing yourself in the role instead gets you assimilated by the potion.
- Under strict low-Sequence acting, it takes as little as a month or as much as half a year to fully digest.

### Law of Beyonder Characteristic Conservation
- The total amount of Beyonder characteristics in the world is conserved.
- After a Beyonder dies, the characteristic crystallizes, becoming the material for the next person's advancement.
- This creates the cruel ecology of "hunting Beyonders for advancement materials."
- High-Sequence characteristics are extremely limited (only 9–11 copies of Sequence 2, only 3 of Sequence 1).

### Uniqueness
- Each Pathway has exactly **one** Uniqueness.
- Containing the Uniqueness is a necessary condition for advancing from Sequence 1 to Sequence 0 (True God).
- Therefore only one True God can exist per Pathway.
- An Angel King who holds the Uniqueness is already a "Quasi-God."

### Loss of Control
- Every time a potion is consumed or powers overused, the risk of madness accumulates.
- The Acting Method is the most effective means against loss of control.
- The endpoint of loss of control: transform into a mindless monster, taken into custody or eliminated by the GM (or official organization).
- A high-Sequence existence is itself already a "Mythical Creature"; directly viewing its Mythical Form causes low-Sequence individuals to lose control immediately.

Of the 22 Pathways on Earth, 6 (Apothecary, Planter, Lawyer, Arbiter, Prisoner, Criminal) originally belonged to the Outer Deities. The Outer Deities want to reclaim these — this is the root of the "doomsday."

# VI. Plot Summary — Klein's Path to Godhood

## Protagonist Background

**Zhou Mingrui** (an Earth human) → transmigrated to the other world's Loen Kingdom → possessed the body of **Klein Moretti** (a history-department graduate) who had just committed suicide. The original host collapsed and killed himself after contacting mystical knowledge while deciphering the *Antigonus Family Notes*.

## Eight-Volume Plot Summary

### Volume 1: The Clown (Tingen Arc)
- Joined the Nighthawks, became Sequence 9 "Seer" → Sequence 8 "Clown."
- Accidentally entered Sefirah Castle (Above the Gray Fog), pretended to be the mysterious entity "the Fool," founded the Tarot Club.
- Founding members: Justice (Audrey), The Hanged Man (Alger), The Sun (Derrick).
- Ending: Ince Zangwill (Sequence 4 demigod) triggered an evil god's descent; captain Dunn sacrificed himself, Klein was killed — but revived through Sefirah Castle, faked his death and left Tingen.

### Volume 2: The Faceless (Backlund Arc)
- Used the alias Sherlock Moriarty, a private detective.
- Advanced Sequence 7 "Magician" → Sequence 6 "Faceless."
- Exposed the Backlund Great Smog event — plotted by Ince Zangwill and the Aurora Order.
- The Tarot Club grew, adding The Moon (Emlyn), The Magician (Fors), and others.
- Ending: Hunted by the church, left under the alias Gehrman Sparrow.

### Volume 3: The Traveler (At Sea Arc)
- Became the mad adventurer Gehrman Sparrow, sailing the pirate seas.
- Advanced Sequence 5 "Marionettist" → Sequence 4 "Bizarro Sorcerer" (demigod).
- Explored the Land of the Forsaken, rescued Silver City's residents.
- Maneuvered among pirates and churches, collecting advancement materials.

### Volume 4: The Undying (Return to Backlund)
- Used the alias Dwayne Dantès, a wealthy man.
- Advanced Sequence 3 "Scholar of Yore."
- Deeply investigated the truth of the Great Smog, opposed the Rose School of Thought.
- Touched ancient-civilization secrets, entered the gaps of history.

### Volume 5: The Red Priest (War Arc)
- Intervened in the war between Loen and the Feysac Empire.
- Advanced Sequence 2 "Miracle Invoker" (Angel).
- Exposed the layouts of Adam (Angel of Imagination) and Amon (Worm of Time).
- Opposed Outer Deity erosion, prevented evil-god descents.

### Volume 6: The Lightseeker (Land of the Forsaken Arc)
- Captured by Amon and taken to the Land of the Forsaken.
- Led the survival gambit in Silver City and Moon City.
- Advanced Sequence 1 "Attendant of Mysteries" (Archangel).
- Obtained the "Fool" card (Blasphemy Slate), learned the godhood ritual.

### Volume 7: The Hanged Man (Eve of the Final Battle)
- Returned to the civilized world, made final preparations for godhood.
- Liberated the residents of the Land of the Forsaken.
- Integrated memories — reclaimed knowledge of the "Old Ones" and "Outer Deities."
- Created the identity "Merlin Hermes" (Miracle Invoker) to accumulate legends.
- Tarot Club members advanced one after another, the alliance's configuration took shape.

### Volume 8: The Fool (Endgame)
- The final confrontation with Amon — a ultimate gambit of will, wisdom, and fate within Sefirah Castle.
- The Evernight Goddess intervened at the critical moment, heavily wounding Amon.
- Klein contained the "Fool" Uniqueness + three Sequence 1 characteristics → advanced to Sequence 0 "Fool."
- Mastered the authority of Sefirah Castle.
- **Ending:** Klein falls into slumber, stabilizing Sefirah Castle's state with himself, preparing to face the Old-One-level threat. Tarot Club members each take up their missions. The story ends in an open yet hopeful atmosphere, foreshadowing the continuation of guarding and resistance.

# VII. Comparison Notes with the TRPG Rules

## Settings Already Accurately Adapted
- 22 Pathways + Sequence 9→0 structure ✓
- Acting Method as the experience system ✓
- Loss of Control (LoC) / Loss-of-Control Threshold (LoCT) mechanic ✓
- Spirit Pool (SP) as power fuel ✓
- Sealed Artifact (Sealed Artifact) tiers and side effects ✓
- d100 percentile die roll-under check ✓
- Five attributes (Spirituality, Mind, Body, Agility, Charisma) ✓

## Differences from the Original in the Current Rules (Notes for Reference)
| Original Setting | Handling in Current Rules | Suggestion |
|---------|----------------|------|
| Above the Sequence (Old One) requires containing the Source + adjacent Pathways | Advanced scenarios use a simplified "cosmic gambit" concept | Can keep — the TRPG endgame need not fully restore the Source mechanic |
| Three Pillars (Lord of the Mysteries / God / Fallen Mother Goddess) | Scenario 8 refers to the "Original Creator" as a single existence | **Needs correction** — in the original, the Original Creator already split, not a single consciousness |
| Multiple Outer Deities, each with different motives | Advanced scenarios mainly use "Primordial Hunger" | Can expand — add Mother Tree of Desire, Fallen Mother Goddess, etc. as alternative antagonists |
| Key maxim of the Acting Method: "remember you are only acting" | Rules have the Acting Method but do not emphasize this point | Can add — a warning against over-immersing in the acting |
| Adjacent Pathways interchangeable (Sequence 5+) | Rules do not mention this mechanic | Can add as optional rule — provides high-Sequence characters a class-change route |
| Law of Beyonder Characteristic Conservation (crystallizes after death) | Rules do not explicitly mention it | Strongly suggested to add — this is core to the original, and provides the GM natural plot hooks |

## Expandable Scenario Directions
- **Outer Deity threat**: not limited to Primordial Hunger — Mother Tree of Desire excels at spiritual pollution, Fallen Mother Goddess affects life and reproduction.
- **Tarot Club model**: The player's "faction" can be designed as a secret society like the Tarot Club, operating by codename.
- **Transmigrator puzzle**: The revealing narrative of Roselle's diaries — players find messages left by ancient transmigrators in ruins.
- **Adjacent Pathway conversion**: "Class-change" plots for high-Sequence players — to face a specific threat, must walk an adjacent Pathway.
- **The dark side of the Acting Method**: Over-acting leads to identity loss — one's self-awareness begins to blur, becoming the psychological-horror element of the original.

---

> **Usage suggestion:** This document is for the GM's reference on the original setting when designing scenarios, ensuring the plot and worldview have no major contradictions. For simplifications made for playability in the TRPG rules (such as simplified Source mechanics, merged Outer Deity concepts), the difference can be noted in GM remarks, but there is no need to force-restore every detail of the original — the core of the TRPG is "playability," not "setting fidelity."
