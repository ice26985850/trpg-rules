# Introduction

## What Kind of Game Is This?

*Urban Survivors* is a tabletop role-playing game (TRPG) about **ordinary people surviving a siege**.

You are not a soldier, not a hero, not any kind of "war role." You are simply a person—trying to keep yourself and those around you alive to tomorrow in a besieged city. You might be a teacher, a baker, a university student, a retired elder, or a mother with an infant. You have no combat training, no special abilities—only the will to survive.

**The odds of killing a zombie or enemy soldier are low, but the real challenge lies in harder decisions: do you give that last can of medicine to the feverish child tonight, or save it for yourself, who might be wounded tomorrow?**

## What Do You Need to Play?

- This rulebook
- Three six-sided dice (3d6)—available almost anywhere
- A character sheet (you may print the blank character sheet in this rulebook's appendix)
- A Game Master (GM), responsible for describing the world, playing non-player characters (NPCs), and adjudicating the rules
- Pencil and eraser

## Core Design Philosophy

> "War doesn't happen at the front line—war happens in your kitchen, when you count out the cans deciding who gets to eat tonight."

This game focuses on:
1. **Moral choices under resource scarcity**—every choice has a cost
2. **Stealth and infiltration**—being discovered may mean death
3. **Maintaining interpersonal relationships**—you cannot survive alone
4. **Mental-state management**—the siege destroys not only the city but also the human heart

**This is not a combat game.** Frontal confrontation is extremely lethal. Your goal is not to "win the fight," but to "survive today."

# Chapter One: Core Rules

## 1-1 Dice System—the 3d6 Bell Curve

*Urban Survivors* uses a **3d6 + attribute value** check system.

Roll three six-sided dice, take their sum, add your corresponding attribute value, then compare against the **Difficulty Class (DC)**.

**Why 3d6?**
The results of three dice cluster toward the middle—most results fall between 9 and 12, while extreme values (3 or 18) are rare. This perfectly simulates "the steady competence of ordinary people": a baker rarely does anything earth-shattering, and also rarely makes incomprehensible mistakes. You rely on consistency, not luck.

## 1-2 The Five Attributes

You have five attributes representing your basic capabilities across different domains. Attribute values range from **0 to 5**. When creating a character, you have **20 points** to distribute freely.

| Attribute | Full Name | Meaning | Typical Check Situations |
|------|------|------|-------------|
| **Physique (PHY)** | Physique (Body) | Physical health and stamina | Carrying heavy loads, prolonged labor, resisting disease and cold |
| **Agility (AGI)** | Agility (Dexterity) | Alertness, speed, and nimbleness | Dodging patrols, crossing open ground quickly, scavenging items, hearing footsteps |
| **Willpower (WIL)** | Willpower (Will) | Mental resilience and psychological endurance | Not breaking down after witnessing atrocities, staying rational amid hunger and fear, resisting temptation |
| **Social (SOC)** | Social (Social) | Ability to communicate and build trust | Negotiating with survivors, soothing panicked companions, persuading neighbors to share resources |
| **Craft (CRA)** | Craft (Craft) | Practical skills and adaptability | Repairing a broken window, making tools from scrap, judging whether food is edible, treating wounds |

**Attribute values are used directly as check bonuses.** If your Physique is 4, a Physique check is rolled as `3d6 + 4`. No table look-up needed.

## 1-3 Difficulty Class (DC)

| Difficulty Class (DC) | Difficulty Name | Typical Situation |
|---------------|---------|---------|
| **DC 6** | Effortless | Finding an obviously visible can in an intact kitchen |
| **DC 8** | Simple | Boarding up a small window with planks, memorizing a frequently used route |
| **DC 10** | Ordinary | Finding an unbared cabinet in a ruined home, persuading a friendly neighbor to share information |
| **DC 12** | Challenging | Not getting cheated at the Black Market, staying absolutely silent as enemy patrols pass |
| **DC 14** | Difficult | Bandaging a deep wound with crude tools, crossing a square covered by a sniper's field of view |
| **DC 16** | Severe | Staying calm under artillery shelling to lead companions to evacuation, repairing a bombed radio |
| **DC 18** | Extreme | Moving a collapsed concrete beam by hand, identifying poisonous plants in total darkness |
| **DC 20** | Near Impossible | Carrying a wounded companion five kilometers after three days without food |

## 1-4 Check Rules

### Standard Check (Attribute Check)

Roll `3d6 + corresponding attribute value` and compare with DC:

| Result | Condition | Meaning |
|------|---------|------|
| **Critical Failure** | Natural roll ≤ 4 (i.e. 3 or 4) | Not only failure, but the situation clearly worsens—tools break, a loud noise is made, an important opportunity is lost |
| **Failure** | Total ≤ DC − 3 | Action not achieved, normal consequences suffered |
| **Costly Success** | Total = DC − 2 or DC − 1 | You did it—but at an unexpected cost |
| **Success** | Total ≥ DC | Action completed as expected |
| **Critical Success** | Natural roll ≥ 17 (i.e. 17 or 18) | Outstanding success—extra supplies found, action faster and quieter than expected |

> **On Costly Success:** This is one of the game's core mechanics. In a siege environment of resource scarcity, "succeeded but at a cost" often drives the story more than "direct failure"—the door opens but you cut your finger, you found the can but made a noise, you persuaded them but they demand something in return.

### Contest Check

Both sides roll `3d6 + corresponding attribute value` and compare totals. The higher wins. On a tie, the active party achieves the goal as a Costly Success.

### Example

> Anna (attributes: Agility 4) attempts to silently cross a corridor strewn with broken glass at Night. The GM sets DC 12 (Challenging).
>
> Anna rolls 3d6 = 9, total = 9 + 4 = 13. **Success**—she passes silently.
>
> If Anna had been starving for days (Satiety below 3, all Physique-related actions at Disadvantage), even though this is an Agility check, her weakened body still affects her. The GM rules the check is at Disadvantage. She rolls 4d6 keep lowest three, result 2+1+4 = 7, total = 7 + 4 = 11. 11 falls between DC − 2 and DC − 1 → **Costly Success**. She crosses the corridor, but a shard of glass pierces the sole of her shoe. Now she leaves faint traces of blood with every step.

## 1-5 Advantage & Disadvantage System

| State | Roll Method | Effect |
|------|---------|------|
| **Advantage** | Roll 4d6, keep the highest three | Favorable environment—good tools, sufficient preparation, professional expertise |
| **Normal** | Roll 3d6 | Standard check |
| **Disadvantage** | Roll 4d6, keep the lowest three | Unfavorable environment—hunger, cold, injury, fear, darkness |

**Sources of Advantage:**
- Possessing the corresponding professional expertise (see Chapter Two)
- Spending extra time to prepare (spend 2 Stamina for a single Advantage)
- A companion provides effective assistance (the assistant also spends 1 Stamina)
- Using high-quality tools or ample materials
- Spending 1 Fate Point (see 1-7)

**Sources of Disadvantage:**
- Satiety < 3 → Disadvantage on all Physique checks
- Warmth < 3 → Disadvantage on all Physique checks
- Mental State below half → Disadvantage on all Willpower checks
- Wounded state → Disadvantage on related checks
- Acting in darkness (no light source) → Disadvantage on all Agility checks

**Advantage and Disadvantage do not stack.** If you have both Advantage and Disadvantage, they cancel out and revert to a normal 3d6 roll.

## 1-6 Derived Attributes

| Derived Attribute | Formula | Use |
|---------|---------|------|
| **Stamina** | Physique × 2 + 5 | Daily action point cap. At zero, cannot act and must rest |
| **Satiety** | Range 0–10 (starts at 7) | Below 3 → Disadvantage on Physique checks. At zero → daily Stamina cap −3 |
| **Warmth** | Range 0–10 (starts per season) | Below 3 → Physique decreases by 1 daily. At zero → Physique check DC +5 (hypothermia) |
| **Mental State** | Willpower × 2 | Endures psychological pressure—at zero → character enters Breakdown State |
| **Stealth** | Agility + environmental cover bonus | The DC for enemy forces to spot you. Higher is harder to detect |

## 1-7 Rest & Recovery

### Rest Types

| Rest Type | Description | Stamina Recovery | Other Effects |
|---------|------|---------|---------|
| **Short Rest** | 1–2 hours of light activity that does not consume Stamina (eating, talking, reading) | Recovers "Physique + 2" Stamina | Removes 1 level of Exhaustion |
| **Long Rest (Sleep)** | 6–8 hours of sleep, requires a safe environment (structure ≥ 1, sealing ≥ 2) | Recovers full Stamina | Mental State recovery +Willpower value (requires Willpower DC 10); Trauma Accumulation −1 (sealing ≥ 3) |
| **Full Rest Day** | Staying in the Shelter all day without going out | Recovers full Stamina | Mental State +2; removes all Exhaustion; wound recovery advances 1 day |

> **Insufficient safe environment:** If Shelter sealing < 2, Long Rest only recovers half Stamina, and mental recovery effects are halved. If Structural Integrity = 0, Long Rest cannot be performed.

### Exhaustion State

Exhaustion is temporary weakness after a chase or extreme physical exertion.

| Exhaustion Level | Effect | Removal |
|---------|------|------|
| **Level 1** | All Physique checks −1 | Short Rest |
| **Level 2** | All Physique and Agility checks −2 | Long Rest (Sleep) |
| **Level 3** | All checks −3, movement speed halved | Full Rest Day |

Exhaustion levels stack (cap 3 levels). If you already have Level 3 and gain more Exhaustion → Stamina −2 and collapse for 10 minutes.

### Morale

Morale is the psychological atmosphere of the character and Shelter members, granted by the GM based on the situation.

| Morale State | Example Trigger | Effect |
|---------|------------|------|
| **Inspired** | Artist's performance succeeds, important goal achieved, good news heard | All Willpower checks +1, lasting until next rest |
| **Normal** | Daily state | No effect |
| **Low** | Consecutive failures, NPC death, prolonged hunger | All Social checks −1 |

Morale effects do not stack—take the highest state.

## 1-8 Fate Points—Your Narrative Insurance

Each player character starts with **3 Fate Points** (may hold at most 5).

| Usage | Cost |
|---------|------|
| After any check roll, reroll (must accept the new result) | 1 point |
| Before any check roll, grant the check Advantage | 1 point |
| Turn a Costly Success into a normal Success (avoid the cost) | 1 point |

**Recovery:** Recover 1 point upon completing each scenario Chapter; recover 1 point when making a major moral sacrifice (GM's ruling); recover 1 point after experiencing a major Trauma event.

# Chapter Two: Character Creation

You are not creating a hero—you are creating **an ordinary person who was still alive the day before the war**.

## 2-1 Nine-Step Character Creation

### Step One: Who Are You?

Answer the following questions:
1. **Name:** What is your character's name?
2. **Age:** How old were you when the Siege began?
3. **Pre-war Profession:** What did you do to make a living before the siege? (Choose one from the 12 Pre-war Professions below)

### Step Two: Where Are You Trapped?

Where were you when the Siege began? Were you alone or with family? What was your first reaction?

### Step Three: Attribute Allocation

You have **20 attribute points** to distribute among the five attributes: Physique, Agility, Willpower, Social, Craft. Each attribute ranges from **0–5**.

**Bonus skills:** For each attribute with a value ≥ 3, you may choose one skill under that attribute to reach "Novice (+1)".

### Step Four: Free Skill Selection

Choose **2 skills** to reach "Novice (+1)", representing your hobbies or life experience.

### Step Five: Your Shelter

Where is your Shelter? A basement, an apartment rooftop, an abandoned subway station? You have **3 points** to distribute among the following three attributes:

| Shelter Attribute | Range | Effect |
|-----------|------|------|
| **Structural Integrity** | 0–5 | Resists attack and natural damage. 0 = cannot perform Long Rest. Below 3 → increased collapse risk during Artillery Shelling |
| **Sealing** | 0–5 | Blocks cold, rain, and sound leakage. Below 2 → Long Rest effects halved. At zero → may be invaded at any time |
| **Comfort** | 0–5 | Affects rest quality and Trauma recovery |

**Specific mechanical effects of Comfort:**

| Comfort | Effect |
|--------|------|
| 0–1 | Long Rest's Trauma reduction effect halved (i.e. −1 becomes no effect; requires two consecutive rest nights to get −1) |
| 2–3 | Normal effect |
| 4–5 | Long Rest Mental State recovery extra +2; Trauma reduction effect doubled (one Long Rest reduces −2 Trauma Accumulation) |

> Shelter attributes can be improved in-game through "Shelter Improvement Action" (see Chapter Six).

### Step Six: Who Did You Lose?

From the start of the Siege until now, you have already lost someone—who? How did you lose them? What happened the last time you saw them?

**Mechanical effect:** Starting Trauma Accumulation +1. Record a "Bond Memory"—once per game, you may reroll a Willpower check at a critical moment.

### Step Seven: What Do You Still Want to Protect?

Choose a **Protected One**:
- **An item:** An item of great emotional significance (ring, diary, photo, etc.) → Once per day, gazing at this item allows a Willpower check (low DC); success recovers Mental State
- **A person:** Someone you are still protecting (child, elderly neighbor, etc.) → Permanent +1 to Willpower checks, but if this person is wounded, you gain double Trauma

### Step Eight: Personal Belongings

Choose starting items from the item list, not exceeding your Carrying Capacity slots (Physique value × 2, minimum 2 slots). Suggestion: 1–2 Food items, 1–2 Water items, 1 Tool item.

### Step Nine: Final Touches

- **Personality Traits:** Describe your character in three words
- **Catchphrase or Habit:** A small detail that gives the character more life
- **Secret:** You have something you don't want anyone to know

## 2-2 The 12 Pre-war Professions

Your Pre-war Profession is not a "class"—it cannot define your role in the siege, but it defines **who you once were**.

### Profession Quick Reference

|------|---------|---------------------------|-------------|
| 1 | Teacher | Knowledge, Soothing, Expression | Social, Willpower |
| 2 | Medic | First Aid, Pharmacology, Diagnosis | Craft, Willpower |
| 3 | Baker/Chef | Cooking, Craft (Food), Scent Identification | Craft, Physique |
| 4 | Plumber/Mechanic | Repair, Electrical, Plumbing | Craft, Agility |
| 5 | Vendor/Peddler | Bargaining, Read Intent, Contact Memory | Social, Agility |
| 6 | Journalist/Writer | Observation, Recording, Interview | Agility, Social |
| 7 | Student | Fast Learning, Physical Fitness, one of choice | Free choice |
| 8 | Veteran ⚠️ | Weapon Use, Tactics, Discipline | Physique, Willpower |
| 9 | Social Worker/Chaplain | Counseling, Negotiation, Organization | Social, Willpower |
| 10 | Driver/Deliveryman | Driving, Navigation, Neighborhood Knowledge | Agility, Physique |

| 12 | Accountant/Civil Servant | Organization, Memory, Document Handling | Willpower, Social |

> ⚠️ **Veteran** is a rare profession requiring GM approval. The Veteran starts with 1 extra Trauma Brand, and must make a Willpower check when encountering triggering situations such as explosions or gunfire.

### Profession Traits Detail

#### Teacher—A Model for Others
When you explain or teach a piece of knowledge/skill to others, they gain a temporary +1 to their next related check. Gain Advantage when persuading children or young people.

#### Medic—Hippocratic Oath
When treating the wounded, First Aid checks gain a +1 bonus. Can perform emergency treatment at lower efficiency when resources are insufficient.

#### Baker/Chef—Waste Nothing
When cooking with ingredients, produces an extra 20% output from the same quantity of raw materials. Can identify whether food has spoiled or been contaminated.

#### Plumber/Mechanic—A Thousand Uses for Two Hands
When repairing or modifying mechanical/electronic/plumbing items, Repair checks gain a +1 bonus. Can use non-standard materials for emergency repairs.

#### Vendor/Peddler—Trader's Intuition
When bartering, can roughly sense how much the other party craves the item (GM gives a vague hint). Bargaining checks gain a +1 bonus.

#### Journalist/Writer—Eye for Truth
When observing the environment or people, can notice details easily overlooked by ordinary people (GM provides one extra piece of detail). Recorded documents have actual mechanical value.

#### Student—Sponge Mind
When learning a new skill, required time or number of attempts reduced by 30%. Starts with 1 extra Vulnerability point (representing lack of life experience).

#### Veteran—War Brand
Gain +1 bonus when using firearms or conducting tactical planning. Starts with 1 Trauma Brand. ⚠️ Requires GM approval.

#### Social Worker/Chaplain—Pillar of Spirit
Spend time (at least 10 minutes) talking with a character in fear, sorrow, or despair, making a Social check. On success, reduce their 1 point of mental pressure (same character can only benefit once within 24 hours).

#### Driver/Deliveryman—Urban Veins
When moving in urban environments, can find the safest or most concealed route. Movement speed within the city increases, and the chance of encountering patrols decreases.

> **Driving situations:** Vehicles may still be found in the city (abandoned cars, Federal Army leftover jeeps). Driving skill is used for: starting abandoned vehicles (Craft + Driving), driving in dangerous road conditions, evading pursuit, judging route safety. Vehicles need fuel (gasoline)—every 1 hour of driving consumes 1 unit of gasoline.

#### Artist/Musician—The Power of Beauty
Provide mental solace to others through artistic forms, making a Craft or Social check. On success, provide +1 Morale bonus to all friendly characters present, lasting until next rest.

#### Accountant/Civil Servant—Systematic Thinking
Gain +1 bonus when organizing supply lists, planning resource allocation, and interpreting official documents. Can precisely track inventory quantities in your head.

# Chapter Three: Survival & Conflict

> **Important reminder: This is not a combat rulebook—this is a survival rulebook.** Every rule reminds you: you are flesh and blood, not a war machine. Frontal confrontation should be your last resort.

## 3-1 Stealth & Infiltration

### Stealth Value

```
Stealth Value = Agility + environmental cover bonus
```

| Environmental Condition | Cover Bonus |
|---------|---------|
| Open ground (squares, flattened streets) | −3 |
| Sparse cover (ordinary streets, corridors) | +0 |
| Moderate cover (inside ruins, behind vehicles) | +3 |
| Good cover (basements, among rubble, deep in dark alleys) | +5 |
| Fully concealed (sealed attic, tunnels, behind deliberate camouflage) | +8 |

**Dynamic factors affecting Stealth Value:**

| Factor | Adjustment |
|------|------|
| Staying still (did not move this turn) | +2 |
| Moving slowly (half-speed movement) | +0 |
| Normal movement (full-speed movement) | −2 |
| Running | −5 |
| Wearing dark/camouflage clothing | +1 |
| Wounded (Minor Wound) | −1 |
| Wounded (Serious Wound or worse) | −3 |

### Enemy Detection Mechanism

Enemy forces make an Awareness check to try to spot you:

```
Enemy Awareness Check = 3d6 + enemy Awareness Value vs your Stealth Value
```

### Noise Level

Each of your actions produces a different level of noise:

| Noise Level | Propagation Distance | Typical Actions |
|---------|---------|---------|
| Silent (0) | 0 meters | Holding breath motionless, eye contact, gesture signals |
| Very Low (1) | 5 meters | Slow crawling, whispering |
| Low (2) | 15 meters | Careful normal walking, soft conversation, opening an unlocked door |
| Medium (3) | 30 meters | Brisk walking, normal conversation, picking locks, rummaging drawers |
| High (4) | 60 meters | Running, breaking glass, barging through doors, dropping items |
| Very High (5) | 150 meters | Gunshots, explosions, screaming, building collapse |
| Deafening (6) | 500+ meters | Artillery shelling, airstrike explosions, sustained gunfire |

### Light Level

| Light Level | Effect on Awareness | Effect on Stealth Value |
|---------|-------------|--------------|
| Bright (Daytime, brightly lit) | Normal | Normal |
| Dim (Dawn/dusk, candlelight) | Awareness Disadvantage | Stealth Value +2 |
| Dark (moonless Night, unlit basement) | Awareness Disadvantage, vision halved | Stealth Value +5 |
| Pitch Black (underground tunnel, sealed room) | Cannot rely on sight (switches to hearing) | If silent: automatic concealment succeeds |

> ⚠️ **Any light source is a lighthouse.** A flashlight in hand lets you see, but also lets all enemy forces see you from 200 meters away.

### Group Stealth

When multiple people sneak together:

```
Group Stealth Value = lowest Stealth Value in the party − (number of people − 1)
```

More people makes concealment harder—solo stealth is the safest choice.

## 3-2 Fleeing & Chasing

When an enemy's Awareness check clearly spots you, a chase begins immediately.

### Chase Distance Bands

| Distance Band | Distance Range | Effect |
|--------|---------|------|
| **Close** | 0–10 meters | Enemy can make melee attacks. You cannot hide |
| **Mid** | 11–30 meters | Enemy can fire. Your hiding check at Disadvantage |
| **Far** | 31–60 meters | Enemy can fire. You may attempt to hide |
| **Breaking Away** | 61–100 meters | Enemy fire at Disadvantage. You may hide normally |
| **Escaped** | 100+ meters | Out of sight range, may re-establish concealment |

### Your Action Options (choose one per turn)

| Action | Check | Success Effect | Failure Effect |
|------|------|---------|---------|
| **Full Sprint** | Physique DC 10 | Gain 2 distance bands | Gain 1 distance band, gain 1 level of Exhaustion |
| **Nimble Weaving** | Agility DC 12 | Gain 1 distance band, pursuer at Disadvantage next turn | Distance unchanged |
| **Use Obstacles** | Agility DC 10 | Gain 2 distance bands | Distance shortened by 1 band |
| **Hide** | Agility DC 14 | If at Far or beyond: enter hiding | Hiding fails, distance shortened by 1 band |

### Re-establishing Concealment

After reaching the "Escaped" distance band:
1. Find a hiding spot (Agility DC 12)
2. Stay silent for 3 turns
3. Wait for enemy to give up search (1d6+1 turns)

## 3-3 Desperate Self-Defense

> ⚠️ **Warning:** If you need to use the rules in this section, it means you have already made a serious mistake. The way to survive is to never need these rules.

### No Hit Points—the Wound System

This game does not use Hit Points (HP). All damage is tracked through the **Wound levels**:

| Civilian character suffers… | Wound Level |
|-------------|---------|
| 1 successful Hit | **Minor Wound**—still mobile, but begins to be affected |
| 2 successful Hits | **Serious Wound**—actions greatly restricted, life-threatening |
| 3 successful Hits | **Fatal Wound**—collapsed, heavy bleeding, dies within minutes |
| Firearm Hit | At least Serious Wound; Sniper Rifle → at least Fatal Wound |

### Attack Resolution

```
Attack Check = 3d6 + Physique (or Agility, depending on weapon)
Target DC = target's Defense Value
Defense Value = 5 + Agility + cover bonus + armor bonus
```

| Defense Factor | Bonus |
|---------|------|
| No cover standing | +0 |
| Half cover (behind an overturned table, behind a vehicle) | +3 |
| Full cover (behind a thick wall, only eyes showing) | +5 |
| Heavy clothing (leather jacket) | +1 |
| Improvised armor (boards tied to body) | +1 |
| **Full Defense action** (forgo attack, focus on dodging/blocking) | **+5** (replaces normal action) |

> **Defense Value example:** A civilian (Agility 3) with no cover → Defense Value 8. A Patrol Soldier's 3d6+3 hit rate is about 84%. The same civilian hiding behind full cover and in Full Defense → Defense Value 18. The Patrol Soldier's hit rate drops to about 5%. **Cover and defensive tactics significantly improve survival—but the best defense is still not being discovered.**

| Attack Result vs Defense Value | Wound Level |
|-------------------|---------|
| Just meets threshold | **Minor Wound** |
| Exceeds DC by 3–6 | **Serious Wound** |
| Exceeds DC by 7+ | **Fatal Wound** |
| Natural roll ≥ 17 and exceeds DC | **Fatal Wound** + special effect |

### Improvised Weapons

| Weapon | Attack Attribute | Base Damage | Special |
|------|---------|---------|------|
| Unarmed | Physique | Minor Wound | vs armed enemy: attack at Disadvantage |
| Broken Glass/Bottle | Agility | Minor Wound | After use, 50% chance to cut self |
| Kitchen Knife | Agility | Serious Wound | Noise Level 2 |
| Pipe/Wooden Club | Physique | Serious Wound | Can be used to block: Defense Value +1 |
| Hammer/Wrench | Physique | Serious Wound | Cumbersome: can only attack each turn |
| Rock/Brick (thrown) | Agility | Minor Wound | Throwing range 10 meters |
| Frying Pan | Physique | Minor Wound | Can serve as improvised shield: Defense Value +1 |
| Rope/Wire (strangulation) | Agility DC 15 | Special | Requires back attack and being undetected |

### Special Rules for Firearms

If enemy forces use firearms (extremely difficult for characters to obtain):

| Firearm Type | Effect After Hit |
|---------|-----------|
| Pistol | Minor Wound (graze) to Serious Wound (torso hit) |
| Rifle/Assault Rifle | At least Serious Wound; exceeds DC by 5+ → Fatal Wound |
| Sniper Rifle | At least Fatal Wound; exceeds DC → immediate death |

### The Chain Reaction of Noise

Noise is settled at the end of each turn—the longer desperate combat drags on, the more enemy forces it attracts. The escape window keeps narrowing.

## 3-4 Wounds & Recovery

### Wound Level Details

| Wound Level | Immediate Effect | Unhandled Deterioration |
|---------|---------|------------|
| **Minor Wound** | All action checks −1 | 3 days unhandled → deteriorates to Serious Wound |
| **Serious Wound** | All action checks −3, movement speed halved, lose 1 Stamina per hour (bleeding) | 2 days unhandled → deteriorates to Fatal Wound |
| **Fatal Wound** | Collapsed, lose d3 Stamina per 10 minutes; any action requires Willpower DC 16 | 1 day unhandled → death |

### Wound Stacking

- 3 Minor Wounds = 1 Serious Wound
- 2 Serious Wounds = 1 Fatal Wound
- If already having a Fatal Wound when receiving one → death

### Wound Infection

If not treated within the "treatment window," an infection check is required:

| Wound | Treatment Window | Infection Check DC |
|------|---------|-----------|
| Minor Wound | 24 hours | Physique DC 8 |
| Serious Wound | 12 hours | Physique DC 12 |
| Fatal Wound | 1 hour | Physique DC 16 |

### Medical Treatment

| Treatment Action | Check | Requirement | Effect |
|---------|------|------|------|
| Basic Bandage | Craft DC 8 | Cloth/bandage | Minor Wound: stops bleeding |
| Suture Wound | Craft DC 14 | Needle & thread + alcohol disinfection | Serious Wound: stops bleeding |
| Emergency Surgery | Craft DC 16 | Blade + alcohol + light source | Fatal Wound: prevents immediate death |

### Recovery Time

| Wound | Base | With Medical Care | Best Case |
|------|------|--------|---------|
| Minor Wound | 7 days | 5 days | 2 days |
| Serious Wound | 30 days | 21 days | 7 days |
| Fatal Wound | 90 days | 60 days | 21 days |

## 3-5 Status Effects

### Hunger

| Satiety | Stage | Effect |
|--------|------|------|
| 7–10 | Satiated | Normal. Stamina recovery +1/day |
| 4–6 | Normal | No effect |
| 2–3 | Hungry | All Physique and Agility checks at Disadvantage. Stamina cap −2 |
| 1 | Starving | All checks at Disadvantage. Stamina cap −5 |
| 0 | Famine | Physique −1 daily. Cannot recover Stamina |

### Dehydration

| Days Without Water | Stage | Effect |
|---------|------|------|
| 0 days | Normal | No effect |
| 1 day | Thirsty | Physique checks −1. Social −2 |
| 2 days | Dehydrated | All checks −2. Stamina cap −3 |
| 3 days | Severe Dehydration | All checks at Disadvantage. Physique −1 daily |
| 4+ days | Organ Failure | Physique DC 16 daily. Failure → death |

### Hypothermia

| Warmth | Stage | Effect |
|--------|------|------|
| 7–10 | Warm | Normal |
| 4–6 | Normal | No effect |
| 2–3 | Cold | Agility −2. Stamina consumption ×1.5 |
| 1 | Hypothermic | All checks −3. Fine manipulation impossible |
| 0 | Severe Hypothermia | Physique −2 daily. Willpower DC 16 or fall into sleep |

### Sleep Deprivation

| Consecutive Sleepless | Stage | Effect |
|---------|------|------|
| 24 hours | Tired | All checks −1 |
| 48 hours | Drowsy | All checks −2 |
| 72 hours | Exhausted | All checks at Disadvantage. Hallucinations appear |
| 96+ hours | Breakdown | Automatically falls into sleep |

## 3-6 Environmental Hazards

### Artillery/Airstrike

When Artillery Shelling occurs:
1. **Screech Phase** (1 turn): Hear the shell whistling through the air, may take 1 reaction action
2. **Impact Phase:** All characters make a Physique check
   - Light shelling DC 10 / Moderate DC 14 / Heavy DC 18 / Carpet bombing DC 20
3. **Aftermath Phase:** Buildings may collapse, fires may break out

### Building Collapse

- First reaction: Agility DC 14, success means leap out of the danger zone
- After being buried: Physique DC 16, success means find breathing space
- Companion rescue: Craft DC 14 to locate + 5 Stamina to dig

### Fire

Fire spreads once every 5 minutes. Crossing flames requires Physique DC 12, failure means Minor Wound (burn) + clothing catches fire.

### Unexploded Ordnance

> ⚠️ **Never touch unexploded ordnance. Go around it. If you can't go around—take another route.**

In areas that have been shelled, the GM may rule an encounter with unexploded ordnance. Discovery requires Agility DC 14 (passive) or DC 10 (active search). Safe bypass requires Agility DC 12. Attempting to defuse requires Craft DC 20 (strongly discouraged—failure means explosion, instant death within range).

# Chapter Five: Trauma & Growth

## 5-1 Trauma System

In a siege, you don't level up by "defeating monsters." Your growth comes from **experiencing hardship, loss, and choices**—sometimes becoming more resilient, sometimes more broken.

### Trauma Events

When a character witnesses or experiences a Trauma event, make `3d6 + Willpower` against the **Trauma Difficulty Class (TDC)**:

| Trauma Event | TDC | Failure Effect | Trauma Accumulation |
|---------|-----|---------|---------|
| First time seeing a corpse | 8 | Mental State −1 | +1 |
| Forced to make a life-or-death choice | 10 | Mental State −2 | +3 |
| Continuous hunger over three days | 10 | Mental State −1, hallucination risk | +2 |
| First time seeing a child's corpse | 14 | Mental State −3, requires second check | +5 |
| Watching a companion die firsthand | 16 | Mental State −4, gains permanent Trauma | +7 |
| Forced to harm others to survive | 14 | Mental State −3, insomnia | +4 |

### Trauma Accumulation Effects

| Accumulated Trauma Value | Effect |
|-----------|------|
| 10+ | Minor Trauma—Disadvantage on Willpower checks in specific situations |
| 20+ | Moderate Trauma—gains compulsive behavior. Violating it requires Willpower DC 12 |
| 30+ | Severe Trauma—Mental State cap permanently −3. Character permanently changed |

### Reducing Trauma

| Method | Effect |
|------|------|
| Safe deep sleep (8 consecutive hours in a safe Shelter) | Trauma Accumulation −1 |
| Honest conversation with a trusted person (Social check success + sincere roleplay) | Trauma Accumulation −1 (same person once per week) |
| Completing something meaningful (helping others, completing an important goal) | Trauma Accumulation −1 |
| Obtaining and enjoying a Luxury & Comfort item | Temporarily suppresses Trauma effects for 24 hours |

## 5-2 Character Growth—Experience Points

### Gaining Experience Points

At the end of each game session, the GM grants Experience Points based on the following conditions (suggested 2–4 points per session):

- Completing main objective: 1 point
- Successfully surviving a fatal crisis: 1 point
- Making a major moral choice: 1 point
- Losing an important person or thing: 1 point (plus Trauma Accumulation)
- Helping others: 1 point
- Discovering important information or location: 1 point

### Spending Experience Points

| Growth Item | Cost |
|---------|------|
| Raise a skill from Untrained to Novice (+1) | 1 point |
| Raise a skill from Novice to Proficient (+2) | 2 points |
| Raise a skill from Proficient to Seasoned (+3) | 3 points |
| Raise a skill from Seasoned to Expert (+3 + Advantage) | 5 points |
| Learn a new Talent (aptitude) | 3–5 points |
| Increase 1 attribute value (cap 5) | 8 points (extremely rare—each attribute may be raised at most once per scenario, and must be heavily used in-game) |

**Growth limit:** Skill improvement must be supported by corresponding in-game behavior. After each game session, each skill may be raised at most 1 level.

## 5-3 Adaptive Talents (Trauma-Triggered)

When Trauma Accumulation reaches specific thresholds, you may gain Adaptive Talents—things learned in the dark:

| Trauma Threshold | Available Talent Type | Description |
|---------|------------|------|
| 5 points | Initial Adaptation | Minor survival bonus (e.g. Hypervigilance: Vigilance +2, but safe sleep does not reduce Trauma) |
| 12 points | Intermediate Adaptation | Significant but limited bonus (e.g. Survival Instinct: when life is threatened, Physique/Agility +2) |
| 20 points | Deep Adaptation | Powerful but comes with a cost (e.g. Break and Rebuild: Trauma −4, character permanently changed) |

**Example—Hypervigilance:** Vigilance checks gain a permanent +2 bonus. But you cannot gain Trauma reduction from safe sleep.

**Example—Numbing:** Trauma Accumulation permanently −2. But Social checks −1, and Soothing others' effect is halved.

# Chapter Six: World Setting Primer

## 6-1 Ostwin—the Besieged "Window to the East"

The city you are in is called **Ostwin**, a river-valley city of about 1.2 million people, located in the northern Balkans of Eastern Europe. The city sits on both banks of the River Velta, surrounded by mountains on three sides.

Before the Siege, this was a vibrant mid-sized European city—the cafés of the University District, the cobblestone street markets of Old Town, the factory chimneys of the Industrial District, the villa gardens of the North Suburb. The Siege changed everything.

## 6-2 Reason for the Siege (GM Will Choose One)

Your GM will choose one from the following settings:

- **Setting A: Civil War**—ethnic tensions erupt, the city becomes contested ground for both sides
- **Setting B: Foreign Invasion**—a powerful neighbor launches an invasion under the pretext of "protecting expatriates"
- **Setting C: Compound Crisis**—multiple factions fight in chaos, the city falls into anarchy

Regardless of the setting, your situation is the same: trapped in a siege, resources steadily depleting, every day requiring difficult choices.

## 6-3 City Districts

Ostwin is divided into six major districts:

| District | Features | Danger Level |
|------|------|--------|
| **Old Town** | Centuries-old cobblestone streets, churches, narrow alleys, underground passages | ★★★★ |
| **City Center** | Government buildings, banks, malls, squares—key artillery targets | ★★★★★ |
| **Industrial District** | Large factories, warehouse clusters, power plant (out of operation) | ★★★ |
| **University District** | Campus, library, medical school clinic | ★★ |
| **North Suburb** | Upscale residential area, embassy district, international hospital | ★★★ |
| **South Suburb** | Warehousing & logistics zone, railway marshaling yard, Refugee Camp, Black Market | ★★★★ |

## 6-4 Time System—Every Day Is a Battlefield

Each "day" is divided into three time periods:

| Time Period | Light | Risk | Suitable Actions |
|------|------|------|---------|
| **Dawn** | Faint | Medium | Quickly cross streets, check traps, collect dew |
| **Daytime** | Bright | Highest—sniper's field of view clear | Stay inside Shelter, repair items, care for wounded |
| **Night** | Dark | Lower—but nighttime patrols have searchlights | Stealth scavenging, climbing over roadblocks, contacting other survivors |

### Daily Consumption

Automatically triggered at the end of each day:

| Consumption | Change |
|--------|------|
| Food | Each Shelter member −1 food unit |
| Water | Each Shelter member −1 water unit |
| Medicine | Each wounded or sick person −1 unit |
| Fuel | Each Shelter −1 unit (winter −2) |
| Mental | Each member Mental State −1 |

## 6-5 Scavenging Outside

### Scavenging Location Types

| Location Type | Potential Gains | Potential Risks |
|---------|---------|---------|
| Abandoned supermarket | Canned food, batteries, cigarettes | Other survivors, collapsed shelves, Patrol Soldiers |
| Ruined home | Medicine, clothing, fuel, personal items | Unexploded ordnance, trapped survivors, corpses |
| Hospital | Large quantities of medicine, medical equipment | Heavy guard, severely ill patients |
| Factory | Tools, materials, fuel | Structurally unstable |
| Border Black Market | Anything—if you can pay | Swindlers, informants, military/police raids |
| Other Shelters | Intelligence, alliances, trade opportunities | The other side may be unfriendly |

### Scavenging Mechanism

Upon arriving at a location, make a `3d6 + Agility` check, with DC depending on the location's level of plunder:

| Check Result | Material Gains | Extra Event |
|---------|---------|---------|
| Critical Success | 3 items + 1 rare item | None |
| Success | 2 items | None |
| Costly Success | 1 item | Minor complication |
| Failure | 0 items | Dangerous event |
| Critical Failure | 0 items | Severe danger + loss of supplies or Stamina |

## 6-6 Shelter Improvement

While staying inside the Shelter, you may perform the following actions to improve it:

| Action | Stamina Cost | Check/Requirement | Effect |
|------|---------|----------|------|
| **Reinforce Structure** | 3 | Materials ×1 (nails, planks, wire, etc.) | Structural Integrity +1 (cap 5) |

| **Make Tools** | 2 | Craft DC 10 + Materials ×1 | Craft a simple item (see Crafting System) |

## 6-7 Crafting System

In the Shelter, you can use materials and tools to craft useful items.

### Craftable Items

| Item | Required Materials | Craft DC | Stamina Cost | Output |
|------|---------|---------|---------|------|
| **Simple Bandage** | Cloth ×1 | DC 6 | 1 | Can treat 1 Minor Wound |
| **Improvised Torch** | Cloth + Fuel ×1 | DC 6 | 1 | 2 hours of light (Noise Level 0) |
| **Simple Trap** | Wire + Nails ×1 | DC 12 | 2 | Once set, can cause Minor Wound or raise alarm |

- Failure: Materials consumed, time wasted

- Having the corresponding profession expertise (Plumber/Mechanic) → check gains Advantage
- Crafting time: Each item about 30 minutes to 2 hours (GM rules by complexity)

# Appendix C: Multiplayer Mode Guide (Optional)

*Urban Survivors* is designed for solo play, but supports a multiplayer mode (2–4 players + 1 GM).

## Multiplayer Character Creation Coordination

- Each player creates a character independently (following the same nine-step process)
- Suggested players choose **different Pre-war Professions**—complementary skill sets make the team more resilient
- Relationships between characters must be negotiated during creation: did you know each other before the Siege? Or did you only meet after?
- The team may share one Shelter, or each have their own

## Shared Shelter Rules

- Structural Integrity and Sealing take the **highest value among all members**
- Comfort takes the **average** (rounded down)
- Daily resource consumption = each member consumes separately
- Storage capacity = base 10 + (team size × 5) units

## Team Actions

- **Division of Labor:** The team may perform multiple actions simultaneously (one scavenges outside, one stays to care for the wounded)
- **Group Check:** All members roll; if more than half succeed, the overall action succeeds (see 1-4)
- **Group Stealth:** Use the Group Stealth Value formula (see 3-1)—multi-person stealth is harder than solo
- **Conflict Resolution:** Disagreements between characters are negotiated by the players themselves. If no consensus is reached, the GM rules or rolls a contested Social check

## Special Considerations for Multiplayer Mode

- **Resource Allocation Conflict:** The biggest challenge of multiplayer—how to distribute limited supplies? This is the natural extension of the game's core moral dilemma in multiplayer mode
- **Trust & Secrets:** Each player has their own Secret (Step Nine)—other players may never know
- **Protected One Conflict:** Different players' Protected Ones may contradict each other (one wants to protect a child, another wants to protect an armory)
- **GM Balance:** In multiplayer mode, slightly increase encounter frequency and resource consumption (+25%) to maintain survival pressure

## Multiplayer Scenario Adjustments

- S1 "The First Day": Suggested 2–3 players, total starting resources unchanged (team must distribute)
- S2 "The Long Winter": Suggested 2–4 players, doubled winter consumption puts more pressure on the team
- S3 "The Final Gate": Multiple players' final choices may differ—this itself is the most brilliant plot
