# Galaxy Civilization — Solo Rules Supplement

> **For**: 1 Player + 1 GM
> **Version**: v1.0 (Based on core rules v1.3)
> **References**: All section references in this supplement point to the *Galaxy Civilization Player Rulebook* and the *Galaxy Civilization GM Rulebook*
>
> In the core rules, the game is designed for 1–4 players each controlling a stellar civilization. When only 1 player is present, the core systems require no changes, but the following dimensions need targeted adjustments to ensure a complete solo experience.

---

## Chapter 1: Core Adjustments for Solo Play

### 1.1 Action Points (AP) Increase

**Reference**: *Player Rulebook* Section 1.3, "Action Economy"

In solo play, the scope of affairs the player must handle remains unchanged (research, construction, diplomacy, and military are all borne by a single person), but there is no other player to share the multi-front pressure. Therefore, the Action Points (AP) formula is slightly raised:

```
Solo Action Points (AP) = 4 + ⌊Economy Power (ECO) / 3⌋
```

| Economy Power (ECO) | Solo Action Points (AP) / Turn | vs. Multiplayer |
|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| 1-2 | 4 | +1 |
| 3-5 | 5 | +1 |
| 6-8 | 6 | +1 |
| 9-10 | 7 | +1 |

> The extra 1 Action Points (AP) compensates for the lack of player collaboration space; the GM may choose whether to enable this adjustment based on the actual game pace.

### 1.2 Crisis Scale Adjustment

**Reference**: *GM Rulebook* Chapter 1 (formerly Chapter 4), Section 4.6, "Crisis System"

In multiplayer, Crisis catastrophes are confronted jointly by multiple civilizations. In solo play, the Crisis is reduced per the following rules:

| Number of Player Civilizations | Crisis Fleet Scale | Boss Hull Value (HP) Modifier | Stage Progression Speed |
|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| 4 Players (Baseline) | 100% (Baseline) | 100% | Baseline |
| 3 Players | 80% | 85% | Baseline |
| 2 Players | 65% | 70% | ×0.8 |
| **1 Player** | **50%** | **55%** | **×0.6** |

> NPC companion civilizations (see Chapter 2) can share the pressure based on their Military Power (MIL) attribute: each loyal NPC companion civilization makes the Crisis scale equivalent to +1 player (i.e., 1 player + 2 NPC companions → counted as 3 players).

### 1.3 Galactic Community Voting Adjustment

**Reference**: *GM Rulebook* Chapter 1 (formerly Chapter 4), Section 4.3, "Galactic Community"

In solo play, the voting dynamics of the Galactic Community shift to GM-led:

- Proposals raised by the player: follow the standard Diplomacy Power (DIP) check procedure
- Proposals raised by NPCs: the GM randomly triggers an NPC proposal every 5 Turns, providing the player with diplomatic choice opportunities
- Voting results: narrative takes priority; there is no need to strictly tally every NPC's votes—the GM only needs to decide "whether this proposal passes, and what it means for the story"

### 1.4 GM Narrative Pacing Adjustment

**Reference**: *GM Rulebook* Chapter 1 (formerly Chapter 4), Section 4.7, "GM Running Guide"

The core advantage of solo play is that the GM can devote full attention to a single player. Key adjustments:

- **Deepening the "Magnifying Glass" principle**: Since there is only one player civilization, the GM can depict the people, events, and emotions within that civilization more deeply
- **Three-line narrative reconstruction**: Galactic line + Civilization line + **Hero line** (the personal story of a representative character becomes the main axis, rather than a garnish)
- **Turn rhythm**: Insert a pure narrative event every 3–4 Turns (no Action Points (AP) cost required) to maintain roleplaying density

## Chapter 2: NPC Companion Civilization System

> This is the most important supplementary system for solo play. Companion civilizations are NPC civilizations controlled by the GM that maintain a long-term cooperative relationship with the player civilization. They are not ordinary diplomatic counterparts—they have personalities, growth arcs, their own goals, and may even betray or be sacrificed.

### 2.1 Companion Creation

Use the NPC Civilization Generator from Chapter 4 of the *GM Rulebook*, but additionally set the following dimensions:

| Dimension | Description |
|------|------|
| **Personality Archetype** | See Section 2.2 |
| **Initial Loyalty** | 1–10 (see Section 2.3) |
| **Personal Goal** | The civilization's core pursuit (may align with or conflict with the player's) |
| **Key Figures** | 1–2 named, story-bearing leaders (the GM designs a simple background for them) |
| **Special Bond** | The historical connection with the player civilization (how they met, what they experienced together) |

#### Companion Creation Example

> **Civilization Name**: Verdant Guardian
> **Personality Archetype**: Loyal Guardian
> **Initial Loyalty**: 8
> **Personal Goal**: "To prove in the galaxy that even a small civilization can exist with dignity"
> **Key Figure**: Seran the Arch-Druid—a calm yet stubborn plant-type species leader who once witnessed their homeworld destroyed by raiders, and now regards the player's civilization as "a future worth protecting"
> **Special Bond**: The player extended a helping hand to Verdant Guardian in its time of greatest peril (this happened many years ago; the GM may co-author this history with the player)

### 2.2 Companion Personality Archetypes

| Archetype | Behavioral Tendency | Loyalty Change Sensitivities |
|------|------|------|
| **Loyal Guardian** | Proactively offers military aid and resource sharing | Player betrays trust → sharp drop; fighting together → sharp rise |
| **Cautious Observer** | Reluctant to commit, tends to act alone | Player demonstrates strength → slow rise; player over-demands → rapid drop |
| **Ambitious Ally** | Shrewdly calculates interests, bargains | Player provides benefits → rise; unequal benefit → drop |
| **Mentor Civilization** | Provides technology and knowledge, protects weaker players | Player respects its wisdom → rise; player misuses technology → extremely rapid drop |
| **Avenger** | Harbors deep hatred for a third party, seeks allies | Player helps revenge → extremely rapid rise; player reconciles with its enemy → betrayal risk |
| **Nomadic Civilization** | No fixed home, may leave at any time | Player provides a home → rise; restrains its freedom → drop |

### 2.3 Loyalty System

Loyalty (1–10) is a numerical expression of an NPC's willingness to cooperate with the player.

#### Loyalty Change Events

| Event | Loyalty Change |
|------|:---:|
| Player aids companion in war | +1~2 |
| Player allies with companion's enemy | -2~3 |
| Player shares a technology | +1 |
| Player refuses companion's plea for help | -2 |
| Companion's key figure is saved by the player | +3 |
| Player supports companion's proposal in the Galactic Community | +1 |
| Player sacrifices companion's interests in a conflict | -3 |
| Jointly complete a major goal (e.g., defeat the Crisis Boss) | +4 (cap 10) |

#### Loyalty Effects

| Loyalty | Status | Effect |
|:---:|------|------|
| 9-10 | Oathbound | Companion gives unconditional support; shares all technology; fleets may be merged under unified command |
| 7-8 | Trusted | Active military collaboration; resource mutual aid; high willingness for tech exchange |
| 5-6 | Friendly | Normal diplomatic interaction; partial military assistance |
| 3-4 | Cold | Only executes existing agreements; no further expansion of cooperation |
| 1-2 | Distant | May withdraw from alliance; refuses all new requests |
| 0 | Severed | Hostile or defects to the player's rival (triggers a GM narrative event) |

### 2.4 Companion Growth System

Companion civilizations grow in sync with the player, but do not use the full Civilization XP (CXP) system. Instead, the following simplified rules apply:

- **Level Sync**: The companion civilization always stays at the player's Level −1 to −2 (the GM fine-tunes based on the companion's scale and investment)
- **Tech Sync**: When the player unlocks a new Tier, the companion automatically follows up after 2–4 Turns (the GM may set a specific delay to reflect the companion's technological strength)
- **Fleet Sync**: The companion's total fleet Military Power (MP) is 40%–80% of the player's total fleet Military Power (MP), with the specific ratio depending on the companion's Military Power (MIL) attribute and loyalty

### 2.5 Companion Betrayal Mechanic

Companion betrayal is one of the most dramatic turns in solo play. Trigger conditions:

- Loyalty drops to 0
- Player ignores the companion's core goal for a prolonged period (5+ Turns)
- A third party (hostile civilization / Fallen Empire / Crisis) offers terms far exceeding what the player provides

**Betrayal Narrative Template**:
1. The GM plants hints 2–3 Turns in advance (companion's behavior becomes suspicious, communications decrease, key figures' attitudes shift)
2. When the betrayal occurs, the GM designs a major narrative twist (fleet mutiny, leakage of a joint defense plan, theft of key technology)
3. The story does not end after the betrayal—it turns into a new conflict line, and the player's response determines the subsequent direction

## Chapter 3: Rules Specific to Solo Campaigns

### 3.1 Time Scale Flexibility

**Reference**: *Player Rulebook* Section 0.3, "Core Loop"

In solo play, the GM may relax the time scale as the narrative requires:

- **Standard Turn**: 1 Turn ≈ 1 year (consistent with multiplayer)
- **Fast-Forward Turn**: When the player enters an intensive period of large-scale construction or tech research, the GM may merge 2–3 Turns into one "Stage," performing only key checks
- **Slow Turn**: When the narrative is at a key diplomatic negotiation or character interaction, the GM may expand the events within a single Turn, depicting them through multiple scenes

### 3.2 In-Depth Rules for Hero Units

**Reference**: *Player Rulebook* Section 2.11, "Leader System"

In solo play, the personal stories of leaders (rulers, admirals, scientists, etc.) become more important:

- **Personal Quest**: Each leader may have a personal quest line interwoven with the main campaign (e.g., an admiral searching for a lost family, a scientist pursuing proof of a certain theory)
- **Leader Interaction**: The GM periodically has key figures of NPC companions converse and interact with the player's leaders
- **Sacrifice and Legacy**: A leader's death is no longer a mechanical "roll dice → replace," but an important narrative node

### 3.3 Multi-Threaded Quest System

In solo play, the player may need to handle multiple story lines simultaneously. The GM should:

- At the start of each Turn, list all currently active **story lines** and their current status
- Let the player choose 1–2 lines to advance this Turn (consuming Action Points (AP))
- Story lines not advanced do not fully stagnate—the GM describes background changes and may introduce pressure
