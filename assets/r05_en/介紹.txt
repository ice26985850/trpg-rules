# The Hero's Proof

The Hero's Proof is a classic Japanese-style "hero vs. Demon Lord" orthodox fantasy TRPG. Here, anyone can call themselves a hero; but only those who defeat the Demon Lord—or reconcile with them—are recognized by the world and awarded the "Hero's Proof." One-line pitch: from a nameless stranger who declares themselves a hero, fight your way through (or talk your way through) the Demon Lord Army, and personally decide the world's ending—a hot-blooded comedy.

On references: This work's worldbuilding is original; only the comedic setup of "the Four Demon Generals, yet a fifth member exists" pays homage to the Dragon Ball "Fifth of the Four Heavenly Kings" meme—this is inspired borrowing, not an adaptation.

Setting: The Altia Continent, in the year 1024 of the Demon Lord Calendar. Humanity has prospered for a thousand years, while the Demon Lord Army holds the border in a cold-war standoff. Demon Lord Vercellia seeks to end the cycle of divine-and-demonic hatred through sheer force; guarding the four directions under her banner are the Four Demon Generals, plus a renegade human, Jeno, who insists "I am the Fifth." The Seven Temples scheme against one another, and even the Demon Lord unexpectedly loves sweets—orthodox hot-blooded adventure blended with lighthearted comedy.

You play a traveler who claims the title of hero, building your character from the Seven Races, Six Origins, and Seven Classes (Warrior / Monk / Mage / Cleric / Thief / Hunter / Dancer), and set out on a path of conquest or reconciliation. Gameplay blends dungeon combat, exploration, gathering and crafting, and social persuasion—you can even win over, turn, or negotiate the surrender of the Demon Generals and the Demon Lord.

System highlights: First, the Influence Points (IP) system—every choice outside of combat accumulates IP, determining whether you can unlock the "Reconciliation Ending"; your qualification as a hero is decided by choices, not level. Second, Three-track Progression and Single-class Four-stage Promotion: the Character, Class, and Skill experience tracks are independent, and at each Class Level milestone you may deepen along one of three paths, growing ever more personal in style. Third, material-driven homemade equipment: Mining, Alchemy, and Smithing—the materials you gather determine the finished item's stats.

Suited for players who enjoy orthodox tales, relish combat, yet wish to experience multiple endings and character interaction—and also for the management-minded who love running their own crafting. Supports 1–6 players; solo play is fully enjoyable too (no sidekick, difficulty auto-adjusts).

Style tags: Combat Weight | Significant; Strategic Depth | Moderate Strategy; Story-driven | Story-supported; Resource Management | Moderate Management; Character Customization | Highly Customizable; Character Creation Complexity | Moderate; Rule Density | Mid-weight Rules; GM Requirement | GM Required.

Estimated Play Time: A single scenario takes about 3–6 hours; a full campaign (from Prologue to Demon Lord Castle, Character Level 1–50) takes about 40–80 hours.

Basic Info: 1–6 players (each session requires 1 GM to run; solo is 1 player + 1 GM, no sidekick), core die type: d20 single die.
