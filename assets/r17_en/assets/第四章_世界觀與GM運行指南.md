# Chapter 4: Worldbuilding and GM Running Guide

> **Designer's Memo**
> The purpose of this chapter is to give the GM a "living sandbox" — every zone has a history, every NPC has a motive, and every piece of junk in the ruins once belonged to someone. The GM should be able to flip open any section and find the material needed to run it on the fly. At the same time, we deliberately leave blanks: there is no complete world map, no omniscient narration, because the essence of the Quarantine Zone (QZ) is uncertainty.

---

## 4.1 Core Worldbuilding

### 4.1.1 The Origin of the Quarantine Zone — "Curtain Collapse"

Three years ago, the Northern Federation's "Ashila Research Facility" suffered a catastrophic loss of control during a high-energy physics experiment codenamed "Curtain" (codename). The experiment was intended to develop a new type of energy transmission protocol — using quantum entanglement to transfer energy instantaneously at a macroscopic scale. But the experiment touched a dimensional rift that should never have been opened.

**Official Account of the Incident**:
- **Day 0**: The "Curtain" experiment entered its third phase; energy output exceeded projections by 4700%
- **Day 0 + 6 hours**: All electronic devices within a 3 km radius of the Ashila Facility failed simultaneously
- **Day 0 + 18 hours**: The first "Anomalous Pulse" spread outward from Ashila as its center, expanding to a radius of 15 km
- **Day 1**: The Federation declared a "Contamination Control Zone" and urgently evacuated 500,000 residents
- **Day 3**: The military sealed off the perimeter and built the "Perimeter Wall" — from then on, no one could legally enter or leave

**Actual Situation (the GM may adjust how much is revealed)**:
- What the "Curtain" experiment opened was not an energy channel, but an unstable dimensional interface
- The Anomalous Pulse is essentially a "reality-rewriting wave" — it rewrites the physical constants, biological structures, and even the flow of time within the zone
- The facility's core area (the Deep Laboratory) has to this day never been entered and returned from
- The Federation was not powerless — they chose "blockade and observation," and secretly dispatched research teams inside to collect data

### 4.1.2 Timeline

| Time | Event |
|---|---|
| **Collapse Day (Day 0)** | Ashila Facility loses control; the Dimensional Rift opens |
| **1 Week After Collapse** | Military blockade; construction of the "Perimeter Wall" begins |
| **1 Month After Collapse** | The first "Scavengers" appear — desperate refugees, former employees, deserters |
| **3 Months After Collapse** | The first "Pulse Surge" — the Anomalous Pulse periodically intensifies, and life within the zone begins to mutate |
| **6 Months After Collapse** | The Quarantine Zone stabilizes into four main regions (Core Zone, Industrial Zone, Old Town, Edge Zone) |
| **1 Year After Collapse** | The trader network forms; the Hideout economy is established |
| **1.5 Years After Collapse** | The Federation establishes the "Quarantine Zone Administration Commission" (QZAC) and begins organized management of external contact |
| **2 Years After Collapse** | Faction divisions roughly stabilize; the "Extraction Protocol" becomes the common rule within the zone |
| **Now (3 Years After Collapse)** | The Quarantine Zone sits in a fragile dynamic equilibrium — the player characters enter at this moment |

### 4.1.3 The Ecological Status Quo of the Quarantine Zone

**Anomalous Phenomena**:
- **Pulse Surge**: Occurs once every 4-6 days; during it, electronic devices may fail (see Chapter Two [Dice System] of the core rules for specific dice-rolling rules). High-value areas "refresh" after a Pulse Surge — new rifts may open, new supplies may appear
- **Gravity Anomaly Zones**: Localized gravity fluctuations, ranging from slight weight reduction to instant overload; a shortcut or a trap for experienced Scavengers
- **Time Bubbles**: Within a small area, the flow of time is abnormal — five minutes outside may be five hours inside. Legend says some missing Scavengers are still trapped in a Time Bubble
- **Residuals**: The non-human entities formed when the facility's original staff and residents who could not evacuate in time were distorted by the Anomalous Pulse. They are not zombies — they retain some memory and behavioral patterns, but their motives become unpredictable

**Mutated Creatures**:
- Most come from animals originally in the Quarantine Zone (dogs, rats, birds) transformed by pulse energy
- The closer to the Core Zone, the higher the degree of mutation
- Some mutated creatures possess "anomalous abilities" — short-range teleportation, localized gravity manipulation, energy absorption

**Climate and Environment**:
- A gray-yellow "haze layer" hangs over the Quarantine Zone year-round; daylight is dim, and nighttime visibility is extremely low
- Rainfall carries faint anomalous energy; long-term exposure is risky but short-term exposure is tolerable
- Some buildings are in a strange state of "half-collapsed but structurally stable" — direct evidence of shifted physical constants

### 4.1.4 "The Outside World"

The world outside the Quarantine Zone has not collapsed. The Northern Federation maintains normal operation; it merely gained a permanent military control zone. To ordinary people, the "Quarantine Zone" is both a horror story in the news and a stage for legendary get-rich-quick dreams.

- **Official Stance**: The Quarantine Zone is a "national security incident"; any unauthorized entry is treated as a crime
- **Gray Area**: The QZAC (Quarantine Zone Administration Commission) tacitly permits some "licensed Scavengers" to operate — they buy valuable research data and anomalous samples from Scavengers
- **Economic Ecology**: The "anomalous supplies" (items with unconventional properties) produced by the Quarantine Zone fetch extremely high prices on the outside black market, giving rise to a persistent Scavenger economy
- **Media Control**: Outside reporting on the Quarantine Zone is strictly censored; most people only know of a "dangerous contamination zone"

> **GM Narration Tip**: The outside world is vague and distant. The GM does not need to describe external politics in detail — the player characters, like them, have only fragmented knowledge of the outside. Focus on the personal motive of "why go back in."

## 4.2 Faction System

### 4.2.1 The Five Factions

#### Faction One: Quarantine Zone Administration Commission (QZAC) — "The Wall"

| Item | Content |
|---|---|
| **Role** | Official institution responsible for the military control of the Quarantine Zone and management of external contact |
| **Territory** | The Perimeter Wall and surrounding military bases, official extraction stations, checkpoints |
| **Goal** | Maintain the blockade, control the outflow of information, secretly research anomalous phenomena |
| **Resources** | Regular military equipment, helicopters, drone surveillance, satellite communications |
| **Attitude Toward Scavengers** | Turns a blind eye — as long as they don't touch the Core Zone or take out "sensitive items" |

**Impact on Players**:
- QZAC (Quarantine Zone Administration Commission) checkpoints are possible Extraction Points in the Edge Zone — but using them means accepting inspection and paying taxes
- If players hold "sensitive items" (GM's discretion), the QZAC (Quarantine Zone Administration Commission) becomes a potential threat at entry
- Occasionally the QZAC (Quarantine Zone Administration Commission) issues "official Commissions" — generous rewards but harsh conditions

#### Faction Two: Scavengers' Compact — "The Underground Order"

| Item | Content |
|---|---|
| **Role** | An informal alliance of Scavengers within the Quarantine Zone, founded by a few veteran Scavengers |
| **Territory** | Most of the safehouse network in Old Town, the underground black market "The Grotto" |
| **Goal** | Protect the basic rights of Scavengers, maintain a minimum set of rules within the zone, oppose the warlords |
| **Resources** | Information network, safehouses, weapon caches, the connections of veteran Scavengers |
| **Attitude Toward Scavengers** | Welcomes all Scavengers who follow the basic rule of "never shoot fellow Scavengers first" |

**Key Figures**:
- **"Old Ke"**: Former head of security at the Ashila Facility, one of the first to enter the Quarantine Zone after the Collapse. Never speaks of what he saw in the Core Zone.
- **"The Doctor"**: A codename; true name unknown. Runs an underground clinic in Old Town, reasonably priced and open to all — including members of hostile factions.

**Interaction With Players**:
- Joining the Compact requires a sponsor or completing a trust trial
- Provides safehouse keys, intel, and secondhand equipment
- Violating the Compact's rules (betraying fellow Scavengers) marks you as unwelcome

#### Faction Three: Iron Brotherhood — "The Warlord"

| Item | Content |
|---|---|
| **Role** | A militarized organization led by the mutinous former officer "Steel Colonel" |
| **Territory** | Most of the Industrial Zone, former military supply stations |
| **Goal** | Monopolize military-grade equipment and anomalous supplies within the Quarantine Zone, establish an in-zone regime |
| **Resources** | Heavy weapons, armored vehicles, former regular army soldiers, a strict chain of command |
| **Attitude Toward Scavengers** | Extremely territorial — unauthorized entry into the Industrial Zone is tantamount to a declaration of war |

**Patrol Pattern**:
- Armed patrols on fixed routes at fixed times
- Opens fire on sight, rarely warns
- The core of the Industrial Zone is fully controlled by the Brotherhood

**Interaction With Players**:
- In principle, no negotiation — but the GM may design a "toll" exception
- If players hold a specific item the Brotherhood is searching for, it may trigger a bounty
- In very rare cases, the Brotherhood may hire outside Scavengers for dangerous missions

#### Faction Four: Order of the Watchers — "The Fanatics"

| Item | Content |
|---|---|
| **Role** | A secret religious organization that views anomalous phenomena as "sacred manifestations" |
| **Territory** | The edge of the Core Zone, abandoned religious buildings, underground sanctuaries |
| **Goal** | Study and worship the Dimensional Rift, believing it to be the opportunity for human evolution |
| **Resources** | Special knowledge of anomalous phenomena, mental resistance training, fanatical followers |
| **Attitude Toward Scavengers** | Not actively hostile, but will stop anyone from entering the "Holy Land" (specific locations in the Core Zone) |

**Belief System**:
- "Curtain" is a sacred boundary; the rift is a bridge between the divine and humanity
- The Anomalous Pulse is the whisper of the gods — cult members "listen to the truth" through long-term exposure
- Residuals are the chosen "forerunners"; mutated creatures are sacred guardians

**Interaction With Players**:
- Can provide intel and navigation related to anomalous phenomena
- May hire Scavengers to obtain "sacred relics" (anomalous items)
- If players attack creatures or places the cult considers sacred, they become permanent enemies

#### Faction Five: Smuggler's Ring — "The Shadow Merchants"

| Item | Content |
|---|---|
| **Role** | An illegal trade network connecting the Quarantine Zone to the outside world |
| **Territory** | Secret passages in the Edge Zone, underground trading posts, outside black-market strongholds |
| **Goal** | Maximize profit — move anomalous supplies out, bring weapons and luxuries in |
| **Resources** | Secret entry/exit passages, forged documents, outside contacts and buyers |
| **Attitude Toward Scavengers** | Purely transactional — welcome if you have goods, ignored if you don't |

**Method of Operation**:
- Smugglers control several entry/exit passages unknown to the QZAC (Quarantine Zone Administration Commission) (the "rat holes")
- They are the main conduit for equipment and information flowing into the Quarantine Zone
- Prices are high but reputation is good — as long as you can pay, they can get anything

**Interaction With Players**:
- Players can purchase rare equipment through smugglers
- Occasionally smugglers issue "courier" Commissions — transporting specific items in or out of the Quarantine Zone
- Using the smugglers' exit tunnels is more expensive but safer than official Extraction Points (no inspection)

### 4.2.3 Reputation System

| Reputation Tier | Value Range | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| **Nemesis** | Below -50 | This faction actively hunts you down |
| **Hostile** | -20 ~ -49 | Opens fire on sight, refuses all interaction |
| **Cold** | -19 ~ -1 | Distrustful; trade prices marked up 20% |
| **Neutral** | 0 | Default state; basic trades possible |
| **Friendly** | 1 ~ 20 | 10% discount on trade prices; may receive intel |
| **Honored** | 21 ~ 50 | 20% discount on trade prices; unlocks special Commissions and rare items |
| **Worship** | Above 51 | This faction treats you as one of their own, providing unique support |

**Examples of Reputation Gain/Loss**:

| Action | Impact |
|---|---|
| Complete a faction Commission | +5 ~ +15 (depending on difficulty and faction) |
| Kill a member of the faction | -10 ~ -20 |
| Rescue a member of the faction | +5 ~ +10 |
| Hand over items the faction needs (free of charge) | +3 ~ +8 |
| Betray an agreement | -15 ~ -25 |
| Help its enemies within the faction's territory | Double impact |
| Provide valuable intel to the faction | +2 ~ +10 |

> **GM Tip**: Reputation is hidden. Players should not know the exact value — they feel the change in their standing through NPC attitudes and available options.

## 4.4 Sortie Cycle Narrative Framework

### 4.4.1 Hideout: Quick Resolution or Detailed Roleplay?

The Hideout is the players' safe space — and also where the plot advances. The GM should adjust the pace according to the group's style:

**Quick Mode** (suited to a 3-4 hour one-shot):
- Hideout scenes controlled to 10-15 minutes
- Focus: Choose Commission → Prepare equipment → Confirm Entry Point → Enter
- Use summary-style menu interactions: "You can buy the following items from the trader…" "You've received three Commissions…"

**Deep Mode** (suited to long campaigns):
- The Hideout is the core stage for character development and faction interaction
- Every trader has personality and side stories
- Hideout upgrades come with narrative events (finding the previous owner's keepsake when installing new equipment, finding a hidden message inside a wall)
- Other Scavengers may visit — cooperation offers, warnings, or threats

**Narrative Pace Example (Quick Mode)**:

> **GM**: "Back at the Hideout. You sling your backpack into the corner; the wound still aches dully. Your cash is up by 2,400, and you've brought back a purple military radio module. Old Ke left a message on the table — he says he has work to discuss. Your Infirmary tells you it'll take three days to recover from your injuries… but three days with no income. Want to hear Old Ke's Commission, or check the Trading Post first?"

### 4.4.2 Narrative Guidance for the Entry Phase

Entry is the transition from safety to danger — the GM should make the players feel this shift.

**Entry Guidance Template**:

> **GM**: "You've chosen to enter through the south wall breach of the Rusted Factory. The vehicle stops five hundred meters from the wall — it can't get any closer. Final equipment check. The weight on your backs, the weapons in your hands — that's all you can take in. Beyond the wall, the yellow haze layer hangs silently in the air. No sound at all. The breach is twenty meters ahead. Who goes first?"

**Key Narrative Elements**:
- Emphasize the ritual of the "final check" — let players realize the weight of their equipment choices
- Depict the physical presence of the boundary (wall, haze layer, ruins)
- Use silence and environmental sound — don't drop an encounter immediately upon entry
- Give players a brief "illusion of safety" — the first few minutes after entry should be relatively calm

### 4.4.3 Pace Control for In-Zone Exploration

This is the core narrative challenge of Search, Attack, Retreat TRPG. The "Tension → Breathing Room → Tension" three-beat rhythm is recommended:

**Rhythm Template (using 60 minutes of in-zone time as example)**:

```
[0-10 min] Entry Phase → Tension: Low
  - Mostly environmental description
  - Give players space to familiarize with terrain and make plans
  - At most one low-threat encounter (small mutated creature or distant sound)

[10-25 min] First Scavenging Phase → Tension: Medium
  - First loot point
  - Possible encounter with patrol-type AI
  - Distant gunfire — hints at the presence of other Scavengers

[25-35 min] Turning Point → Tension: High
  - Discover high-value loot (but exposed location or guarded)
  - Encounter an NPC Scavenger squad
  - Environmental change (Pulse Surge begins, weather worsens)

[35-45 min] Decision Phase → Tension: Highest
  - Player backpacks nearly full — keep scavenging or extract?
  - New threat appears on the extraction route
  - Multiple NPC squads may gather near the Extraction Point

[45-60 min] Extraction Phase → Tension: Extreme
  - Extraction countdown
  - Extraction Point conflict
  - Final escape or failure
```

**GM Tools for Tension**:

| Tool | Effect |
|---|---|
| **Time Pressure** | "The Pulse Surge will arrive in 30 minutes — by then all electronic devices may fail (see Chapter Two [Dice System] of the core rules)" |
| **Auditory Cues** | Distant gunfire, footsteps, inhuman roars — let players sense the presence of a threat without locating it |
| **Loot Temptation** | "Through a crack in the warehouse, you see a blue-marked crate — but it's at the other end of the hall, fully exposed" |
| **Terrain Pressure** | The only retreat is blocked, the door locks behind you, the floor begins to collapse |
| **NPC Pressure** | Hearing another squad's radio chatter, seeing a flashlight beam sway in the distance down the corridor |

> **Most Important GM Principle**: Always give players a choice. Don't force encounters. The best tension comes from "I choose to keep looking, but I know it's dangerous" — not from "the GM threw another wave of monsters."

## 4.5 Commission System

### 4.5.1 Commission Types

| Type | Description | Example | Reward Type |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Scavenge Commission** | Find and bring back a specific item | "The factory needs 3 military-grade electronic parts" | Cash + Reputation |
| **Elimination Commission** | Eliminate a specific target | "Clear the mutated creatures in the Ash Mall underground parking garage" | Cash + Reputation + specific loot |
| **Reconnaissance Commission** | Go to a specific location and report back | "Confirm the Brotherhood's strength at the Rusted Factory Administration Building" | Cash + intel bonus |
| **Rescue Commission** | Find and escort someone to extraction | "A Newbie Team went missing in Silent Community — find them and get them out" | High cash + Reputation + special relationship |
| **Courier Commission** | Transport an item from A to B | "Deliver this sealed crate to the smugglers' safehouse" | Cash (no questions asked) |
| **Marking Commission** | Place a beacon/device at a specific location | "Place three monitoring beacons at the edge of the Core Zone" | Cash + rare equipment |

### 4.5.2 Commission Difficulty and Rewards

| Difficulty | Recommended Zone | Number of Hostile Encounters | Time (in-game) | Base Reward | Risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Simple** | Edge Zone | 0-2 | 1-2 hours | 500-1,000 | Low — may lose a small amount of equipment |
| **Regular** | Edge/Old Town | 1-3 | 2-4 hours | 1,000-3,000 | Moderate — may encounter NPC squads |
| **Hard** | Old Town/Industrial | 3-5 | 3-6 hours | 3,000-8,000 | High — likely to encounter hostile factions |
| **Extreme** | Industrial/Core | 5+ | 4-8 hours | 8,000-20,000+ | Extreme — high chance of encountering Boss-level threats |

### 4.5.3 Timed Commission vs Regular Commission

**Regular Commission**:
- Can be accepted anytime, no time limit
- Multiple Regular Commissions can be active simultaneously
- Stable but lower rewards

**Timed Commission**:
- Has a clear completion deadline (before the next Pulse Surge, within 24 hours)
- Rewards significantly higher than Regular (1.5x to 3x)
- Failure may incur a Reputation penalty
- Usually comes from special factions or time-sensitive needs

**Boss Commission** (special timed):
- A special Commission that appears only once every few sorties
- Target is a zone Boss (a powerful Mutant or hostile NPC leader)
- Multiple factions may issue Commissions for the same target simultaneously
- Highest reward but highest risk

## 4.6 Complete NPC Scavenger Behavior Templates

### 4.6.1 Lone Wolf Veteran

**Basic Info**:
| Item | Content |
|---|---|
| **Squad Size** | 1 person |
| **Equipment Tier** | Mostly Military (Purple), may hold Legendary items |
| **Operating Area** | All zones, prefers high-value low-traffic locations |
| **Appearance Rate** | 10% (any zone) |

**AI Behavior Script**:

| Phase | Behavior |
|---|---|
| **Patrol Mode** | Moves along zone edges, uses cover, makes one Perception (PER) check per round (listen/look) |
| **Target Spotted** | Observes first (1-2 rounds), assesses the other's equipment and intent before deciding action |
| **Encounter Response** | If the other is dominant → conceal and wait for them to pass; if the other is weak → keep distance and continue observing; if the other holds high-value items → may track them |
| **Combat Tactics** | Rarely initiates combat. If forced, uses sniping/long-range, hit-and-run, terrain advantage. Aims at weak points with precision. |
| **Retreat Condition** | Retreats when HP drops below 50%; if the other side has more than 2 people, retreats at below 70% HP |
| **Special Behavior** | May leave small loot as bait; ambushing near an Extraction Point is the most dangerous situation |

**Interaction Trigger Conditions**:
- Player does not fire first + distance over 20m → may call out
- Player actively shows a non-hostile posture (lowers weapon, raises hands) → may briefly converse

**Sample Lines**:
- "Don't come closer. I won't bother you, you don't bother me."
- (seeing the player holding a high-value item) "That radio module… where'd you find it?"
- "My advice: don't go east. The Brotherhood just passed through."
- (during negotiation) "I want that blue thing in your backpack. One for one — an intel trade."

### 4.6.2 Newbie Team

**Basic Info**:
| Item | Content |
|---|---|
| **Squad Size** | 2-4 people |
| **Equipment Tier** | Mostly Junk~Common (Gray/White), may have one Rare item |
| **Operating Area** | Mostly Edge Zone, occasionally enters Old Town |
| **Appearance Rate** | 20% (Edge Zone) / 10% (Old Town) |

**AI Behavior Script**:

| Phase | Behavior |
|---|---|
| **Patrol Mode** | Disorganized, talks in low voices while walking (easy to spot with a Perception check), hesitates between buildings |
| **Target Spotted** | May panic — roll a Courage (CRG) check to decide whether to stay calm. On failure may scream or open fire |
| **Encounter Response** | Extremely variable: may try to flee (40%), may try to negotiate (35%), may fire first out of fear (25%) |
| **Combat Tactics** | Poor coordination, easily ambushed. Tend to focus-fire one target but with low hit rate. Limited ammo (may run out after 3-5 rounds). |
| **Retreat Condition** | Any member below 50% HP → whole team retreats (may abandon a Prone teammate). Ammo depleted → retreat immediately. Seeing a mutated creature → 50% chance to flee directly. |
| **Special Behavior** | When injured and Prone, may beg for mercy; may be willing to trade intel or items for safe passage |

**Interaction Trigger Conditions**:
- Almost always willing to negotiate (unless they fire first out of fear)
- Easily intimidated when seeing a fully-armed player

**Sample Lines**:
- "Wait—wait! We're just here for medical supplies! Don't shoot!"
- "Are you veterans? Please… our teammate is hurt…"
- (when intimidated) "O-okay, we'll put the stuff down, just don't kill us…"
- (seeing a mutated creature) "What is that — run! Run!"

### 4.6.3 Bounty Hunter

**Basic Info**:
| Item | Content |
|---|---|
| **Squad Size** | 1-3 people |
| **Equipment Tier** | Rare~Military (Blue/Purple), equipment specialized for the target |
| **Operating Area** | Depends on target location, often near Boss zones |
| **Appearance Rate** | 5% (regular) / 15% (when a Boss Commission is active) |

**AI Behavior Script**:

| Phase | Behavior |
|---|---|
| **Patrol Mode** | Highly purposeful, heads straight to the target zone, stays alert to non-target units along the way but doesn't engage |
| **Target Spotted** | Quickly determines if it's the target. If not → assesses whether it's a threat. If yes → enters Hunting Mode. |
| **Encounter Response** | Probes first — observes equipment, asks intent. If player is not the target and doesn't interfere → ignored. If player interferes with the hunt → fires after warning. If player is the target → fires or sets ambush immediately. |
| **Combat Tactics** | Professional level — coordinated attacks, uses tactical equipment (flashbangs, smoke), prioritizes taking down the greatest threat |
| **Retreat Condition** | Target eliminated → extract immediately. Below 40% HP and target not eliminated → decides after assessment (50% retreat / 50% continue). |
| **Special Behavior** | May propose "cooperative hunt" — "The target's loot is mine, the loot on the way is yours" |

**Interaction Trigger Conditions**:
- Player not the target + keeps distance → may have brief conversation
- Player asks about the target → may share intel (for a fee)
- Player tries to help → accepts cautiously

**Sample Lines**:
- "I'm looking for someone. You wouldn't want to be in my way."
- (after assessing the player) "Nice gear. But not who I'm looking for."
- "That Mutant is my mark. You can help, but the loot is mine."
- "Seen a lone guy carrying a long rifle?"

### 4.6.4 Scav Gang

**Basic Info**:
| Item | Content |
|---|---|
| **Squad Size** | 3-6 people |
| **Equipment Tier** | Junk~Common (Gray/White), 1-2 may carry Rare weapons |
| **Operating Area** | Old Town (main), Edge Zone (secondary), Industrial Zone (rare) |
| **Appearance Rate** | 25% (Old Town) / 15% (Edge Zone) |

**AI Behavior Script**:

| Phase | Behavior |
|---|---|
| **Patrol Mode** | Numbers advantage — loud, occupies main passages, loots building by building. Doesn't care about exposure. |
| **Target Spotted** | First assesses numbers and equipment. If clearly outnumber the other → Greed Mode. If the other looks strong → Protection Money Mode. |
| **Encounter Response** | First choice → demand "toll" or "leave your backpack and go." If the other shows weakness → pushes further. If the other fights back hard → may back down. |
| **Combat Tactics** | Human-wave tactics but poor coordination. Tend to surround the threat then fire together. Easily picked off one by one. If the leader falls → morale drops sharply (whole team makes a Courage (CRG) check). |
| **Retreat Condition** | Casualties over 50% → scatters. Leader falls → whole team makes a Courage (CRG) check, flees on failure. Encountering a Warlord Patrol → flees immediately. |
| **Special Behavior** | May "raise the price" after taking money — not trustworthy. May track players to a high-value loot point then rob them. |

**Interaction Trigger Conditions**:
- Almost certainly attempts interaction (usually negative — extortion or threats)
- Bribes are very effective — but only buy temporary safety

**Sample Lines**:
- "Hey! New here? This street is ours. Wanna pass? Leave something behind."
- "Your backpacks look pretty heavy. Need help… lightening the load?"
- (after the boss is down) "Boss is down — retreat, retreat, retreat!"
- (after taking money) "Alright, you're lucky today. Now scram."

### 4.6.5 Warlord Patrol (Iron Brotherhood)

**Basic Info**:
| Item | Content |
|---|---|
| **Squad Size** | 3-5 people |
| **Equipment Tier** | Military (Purple), some Legendary (Gold)-tier weapons |
| **Operating Area** | Industrial Zone (main), Old Town edge (rare) |
| **Appearance Rate** | 40% (Industrial Zone) / 5% (Old Town edge) / 0% (Edge Zone) |

**AI Behavior Script**:

| Phase | Behavior |
|---|---|
| **Patrol Mode** | Two abreast, fixed route, timed radio reports. Fixed spacing (tactical formation). Equipped with flashlights and night-vision gear. |
| **Target Spotted** | Radio report to base → determine if target is on the wanted list → regardless of result, eliminate on sight (within Industrial Zone) |
| **Encounter Response** | Password warning (once) → open fire. No negotiation. No bribes (unless the GM designs a specific corrupt officer). |
| **Combat Tactics** | Suppressing fire + flanking. Uses cover and crossfire. Calls reinforcements (another team arrives after d6 rounds). Prioritizes eliminating targets with heavy weapons. |
| **Retreat Condition** | Almost never retreats — unless facing overwhelming fire (e.g. a Boss-level Mutant). Strict discipline means they fight until casualties pass half. |
| **Special Behavior** | If players are captured → confiscate all equipment → dumped at the Industrial Zone boundary (alive but with nothing). Second capture → execution. |

**Interaction Trigger Conditions**:
- **None**. Encountering a Brotherhood patrol in the Industrial Zone = combat (or extremely lucky hiding).
- Only exception: if the player holds the Brotherhood's "pass" (extremely rare quest reward), they may pass safely once.

**Sample Lines**:
- "This is Iron Brotherhood jurisdiction. Drop your weapons, get on the ground — you have five seconds."
- (radio report) "Command, Team B spotted unauthorized personnel at the freight yard. Requesting engagement permission."
- "Fire! Don't let them reach the pipeline entrance!"
- (after arrest) "You're lucky today. Next time I see you, I won't be so polite."

## 4.7 Extraction System Design

### 4.7.1 Extraction Point Types

| Type | Description | Advantage | Disadvantage |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Fixed Extraction Point** | 1-2 known locations per map, always available | Predictable, route can be planned | Most crowded, most likely to be ambushed, most likely to be occupied by NPCs |
| **Random Extraction Point** | 1-2 randomly activated each sortie | Element of surprise, may be safer | Need to find and confirm, may be far from main routes |
| **Hidden Extraction Point** | Requires specific conditions to use (key, intel, signal flare) | Safest, may be near high-value areas | Requires advance preparation and resource expenditure |
| **Conditional Extraction Point** | Opens after conditions met (clear the zone, defeat guards, activate equipment) | Unique narrative experience, may yield extra rewards | High risk, unpredictable |

### 4.7.2 Extraction Point Conflict Scenarios

**Scenario One: Occupied**
> "You approach the Extraction Point and hear voices from ahead. Through cover you observe — a Scav Gang is waiting at the Extraction Point for the vehicle, their backpacks full. The countdown still has 8 minutes left."

Options:
- Wait for them to leave (but the countdown may not be enough)
- Negotiate sharing the extraction (credibility questionable)
- Take it by force (they outnumber you)
- Find another Extraction Point (time pressure)

**Scenario Two: Ambushed**
> "The Extraction Point looks quiet. But your instincts are screaming — too quiet. Make a Perception (PER) check. Success? Good — you notice the shadow angle in the ruins to the left is unnatural; someone is waiting behind cover."

Options:
- Counter-ambush (requires tactical advantage)
- Detour and approach from the other side
- Wait for the ambushers to lose patience (but the countdown is running)
- Abandon this point and find another Extraction Point

**Scenario Three: Time Pressure**
> "A radio warning comes through — the Pulse Surge is expected to arrive in 12 minutes. All electronic devices will fail during the Pulse Surge. The nearest available Extraction Point is a 10-minute walk away — if you move at full speed."

Options:
- Go at full speed (abandon loot along the way, may be ambushed)
- Risk grabbing one more item (backpack space vs time)
- Use the Hidden Extraction Point (if players have unlocked it)

**Scenario Four: Extraction Point Closed**
> "You reach the Extraction Point, but find the beacon has been sabotaged — someone passed through before you and deliberately destroyed the extraction equipment. The countdown still has 15 minutes, but you need to find another Extraction Point."

Options:
- Search for a Random Extraction Point (requires Perception and Navigation checks)
- Use a Conditional Extraction Point (if you have a backup plan)
- Risk crossing a dangerous area to another Fixed Extraction Point

### 4.7.3 Narrative Handling of Extraction Failure

Extraction failure (failing to reach the Extraction Point in time) does not necessarily mean character death. The GM has the following narrative options:

**Option A: Swallowed by the Pulse Surge** (no death but lose equipment)
> "The Pulse Surge arrives when you're still three hundred meters short. The world turns white — not light, but a kind of… hollow roar. When you regain consciousness, your backpack and weapons are gone, all electronic devices on you destroyed. But you're alive. Four hours later, you stagger back to the Hideout."

**Option B: Forced to Hide** (extended sortie becomes a survival scenario)
> "The extraction vehicle leaves. You're trapped inside the Quarantine Zone. The next 24 hours become a completely different game — not scavenging, but survival. You hide in the sewer, listening to the warlord's footsteps on the ground above and the distant howls of Mutants. Tomorrow, there will be a new extraction window."

**Option C: Captured** (opens a new narrative thread)
> "As you rush toward the Extraction Point, the Brotherhood is already there waiting. 'Drop your weapons — your extraction permit has been revoked.' You're marched deep into the Industrial Zone. Equipment stripped, but at least… you're alive. The story from here is about escape."

**Option D: Death** (total loss)
> "Gunfire rings out in the final second. A sniper. You don't even see who. Character death. All equipment lost."

> **GM Guide**: Option D should be a real threat but not the default outcome. Each time you use Options A-C, ensure the players pay a real price (equipment loss, time loss, new predicament), otherwise the death-deterrence system fails. Recommended ratio: about 40% of extraction failures lead to death, 60% lead to other consequences.

## 4.8 Encounter Design Guide

### 4.8.1 Encounter Difficulty Balance

Search, Attack, Retreat TRPG does not pursue "fair combat" — asymmetry is its core appeal. The goal of balance is to ensure players have enough information to make choices.

**Three Elements of Encounter Design**:

| Element | Explanation |
|---|---|
| **Warning** | Can players obtain information before the encounter? (sound, light, NPC behavior hints, environmental clues) |
| **Choice** | Do players have options other than "start fighting"? (detour, negotiate, wait, set ambush) |
| **Consequence** | Is the outcome of combat interesting? (resource expenditure, reputation change, new intel, altered retreat route) |

**Encounter Classification**:

| Type | Warning | Choice | Consequence | Example |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Environmental Tell** | High | Many | Low | Distant gunfire, bloodstains in the ruins |
| **Optional Encounter** | Medium | Many | Medium | Patrol ahead — detour or engage? |
| **Corner Encounter** | Low | Few | High | Open the door — the room is full of Residuals |
| **Boss Encounter** | High | Medium | Extreme | Zone Boss, with prior intel |

**Usage Limits for Corner Encounters**:
- At most 1-2 Corner Encounters per sortie
- Never use when the player's backpack is full and they're about to extract
- Always provide a high-quality breathing-room scene after a Corner Encounter

### 4.8.2 Ratio of Loot Points to Danger

| Region | Loot Abundance | Danger Density | Loot/Danger Ratio | Design Intent |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Edge Zone** | Low (mostly Gray/White) | Low | Balanced | Let rookies learn the basic loop |
| **Old Town** | Medium (White/Blue) | Medium | Slightly more loot | Let players experience greedy choices |
| **Industrial Zone** | High (Blue/Purple) | High | Slightly more danger | High risk high reward — veteran paradise |
| **Core Zone** | Extremely High (Purple/Gold) | Extremely High | Danger clearly exceeds loot | Ultimate challenge — needs a team and a plan |

### 4.8.3 Recommended Encounter Density per Zone

| Region | Recommended Encounters (3-hour sortie) | Of which combat | Of which NPC | Of which environmental |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Edge Zone** | 3-5 | 1-2 | 1-2 | 0-1 |
| **Old Town** | 4-7 | 1-3 | 2-3 | 1-2 |
| **Industrial Zone** | 5-8 | 2-4 | 2-3 | 1-2 |
| **Core Zone** | 6-10 | 3-5 | 1-2 | 2-3 |

## 4.9 GM Running Guide

### 4.9.1 Suggested First-Run Procedure

**Preparation Checklist**:

- [ ] Choose a sample map (recommended: Ash Mall — Edge Zone)
- [ ] Prepare 3 Commissions (at least 1 Simple, 1 Regular)
- [ ] Pre-roll the first 3 results of the encounter table (for a smooth opening)
- [ ] Prepare 5-8 loot point locations and contents
- [ ] Decide Extraction Point configuration (at least 2 usable Extraction Points)
- [ ] Prepare full data for 1-2 NPC squads (recommended: 1 Newbie Team + 1 Scav Gang)
- [ ] Familiarize with basic rule dice (Attribute checks, combat flow)
- [ ] Prepare a simple map (hand-drawn is fine)

**Opening Flow** (60-minute intro version):

| Time | Segment | Content |
|---|---|---|
| 0-5 min | Rules Intro | Quick explanation of Attributes, the 3d6 dice system, six-part HP (Head/Chest/Abdomen/Left Arm/Right Arm/Left Leg/Right Leg), Carry Capacity |
| 5-10 min | Hideout | Choose Commission + confirm equipment (default equipment cards issued) |
| 10-15 min | Entry | Environmental description + Entry Point choice |
| 15-45 min | In-Zone Action | 2-3 loot points + 1 NPC encounter + 1 combat |
| 45-55 min | Extraction Choice | Countdown + Extraction Point conflict |
| 55-60 min | Settlement | Loot + Experience + Reputation change |

### 4.9.2 How to Create a Tense Atmosphere

**Concrete Techniques**:

1. **Use silence, not noise**: The most terrifying moment is not after the gunshot, but the silence before it. Leave 3-5 seconds of silence before an encounter.

2. **Use the players' imagination**: "You hear the sound of metal clashing from down the corridor — then it stops. Complete silence. Your flashlight beam sways on the wall." — don't tell them what it is right away.

3. **Ritualize resource counting**: When a player says "I use my last magazine," pause for a second. Let everyone realize the fact.

4. **Describe physical reactions**: Not "you are afraid," but "you notice your hands trembling slightly — the sweat on the grip blurs your sight picture."

5. **Incomplete information**: Never give the full picture on first description. "Something is moving in the corner — you didn't see clearly, but your body is already stepping back."

6. **Visualize time**: Put a countdown timer on the table (a phone is fine), so players see time passing.

7. **Make loot temptation concrete**: "The crate opens — inside is a fully maintained military sniper rifle. Complete accessories. Could sell for at least 8,000 on the black market. But it weighs 7 kg, and your backpack is already 80% full."

### 4.9.3 GM Response When All Equipment Is Lost

This is the most sensitive part of the Search, Attack, Retreat system. The following are rules of thumb:

**Preventive Measures** (before death occurs):

- **Insurance Education**: Before the first sortie, ensure players know the Insurance mechanism exists and how it works
- **Commission Variety**: Always provide Commissions within the players' capability; don't let "only the Industrial Zone is an option" be the only choice
- **Warning Culture**: Build trust that "the GM will give clues" — players should know that if they pay attention to environmental details, they can spot danger
- **Retreat Window**: At the start of an extremely dangerous encounter, clearly state "you realize this may be beyond your ability — retreat is still an option"

**When Death Occurs**:

1. **Acknowledge the loss**: "Mark is dead. At the Rusted Factory railway freight yard, shot in the head by a Brotherhood sniper. His equipment — that modified M4, the Level 3 Body Armor, and the radio module in his backpack — all left there."

2. **Don't offer compensation immediately**: Give the loss some space to settle. Don't immediately say "but you found a better gun."

3. **Provide narrative closure**: Describe the scene of teammates bringing Mark's keepsakes (the safe contents) back to the Hideout. If possible, let NPCs react to the dead character ("Old Ke was silent for a long time. 'He was a good Scavenger.'")

4. **New Character Inheritance**:
   - The new character inherits the Hideout's facilities and Vault contents
   - Starting funds are 50% of the original character's (minus insurance deduction)
   - Basic equipment (pistol + simple gear)
   - Optional: the new character is a contact/friend of the original — this provides a reasonable motive to enter

5. **Legacy Mechanism** (optional rule):
   - Unlocked Hideout facilities are retained
   - Reputation with factions is inherited at half
   - The Intelligence Center retains discovered Extraction Point info
   - The previous character's "keepsakes" (safe contents) can be inherited

**Key to Preventing Players from Quitting**:
- Death is not an end, but a narrative turn — let the new character enter with the motive of "revenge" or "finishing unfinished business"
- The Hideout's continued existence means the players' investment is not entirely wiped out
- Build the narrative satisfaction of "rising from the ashes" — starting from zero but this time knowing what you're doing

### 4.9.4 Pace Control: Interleaving Scavenging and Combat

**Golden Ratio** (using 3 hours as example):
- Exploration/Scavenging: 40-50%
- NPC Interaction (non-combat): 15-20%
- Combat: 20-25%
- Extraction: 10-15%

**Pace Signals**:

| When players… | The GM should… |
|---|---|
| Scavenge 3+ loot points in a row with no encounter | Insert an environmental event or distant threat signal |
| Fight 2 combats in a row | Provide a safe area to breathe, let players process loot and resupply |
| Discuss too long (over 5 minutes with no action) | Advance the environment — "you hear something approaching from down the corridor" |
| Start to feel frustrated/fatigued | Shorten the remaining flow, accelerate the extraction phase |
| Are clearly excited/engaged | Provide one more loot point or optional encounter (don't force it) |

### 4.9.5 One-Shot vs Long Campaign

| Aspect | One-Shot (3-4 hours) | Long Campaign |
|---|---|---|
| **Hideout** | Quick resolution, default facilities | Deep management, facility upgrades are the core driver |
| **Commissions** | 1-2, linearly related | 3-5 in parallel, faction networks interwoven |
| **Maps** | One map, no switching | Multiple maps, zone unlocking |
| **Equipment** | Default starting equipment + in-zone pickup | Long-term accumulation, modification, insurance management |
| **Death** | Character death = player observes until the end | Death = new character inherits (see 4.9.3) |
| **NPC** | Single encounter, not tracked | Recurring, building relationships/hatred |
| **Faction Reputation** | Simplified (Friendly/Neutral/Hostile) | Full Reputation system |
| **Narrative Arc** | One complete "enter → scavenge → exit" | Cross-sortie continuous narrative (revenge, uncovering truth, faction war) |
| **Each Sortie** | The whole session is one sortie | Each session equals one sortie |

**Long Campaign Specific Advice**:
- **Recap**: Spend 2 minutes before each session reviewing the key points of the last sortie
- **Faction Clock**: Track faction dynamics behind the scenes (the Brotherhood is expanding into Old Town, etc.), so the world changes even when players aren't sortieing
- **NPC Memory**: Record key NPCs' names and attitudes toward players — NPCs remember players on next meeting
- **Hideout Development**: Unlock one facility upgrade every 3-5 sorties
- **Main Plot Foreshadowing**: Gradually reveal the truth of the Quarantine Zone across several sorties (the nature of the Curtain experiment, the secrets of the Core Zone)

## 4.10 Sample Module Framework: "A Rookie's First Contract"

### Module Overview

| Item | Content |
|---|---|
| **Module Name** | A Rookie's First Contract |
| **Suitable Level** | Level 1 characters × 2-4 people |
| **Estimated Time** | 3-4 hours |
| **Map Used** | Ash Mall (Edge Zone) |
| **Difficulty** | Intro ★☆☆☆☆ |
| **Core Gameplay** | Scavenge → NPC Encounter → Extraction Decision |
| **GM Prep Time** | 20-30 minutes |

### Adventure Structure

```
Act One: Hideout (15 min)
  └─ Old Ke's Commission → Equipment Confirmation → Entry

Act Two: Mall Exploration (90-120 min)
  ├─ Scene 1: North Bus Stop Entry → Environmental Perception
  ├─ Scene 2: Food Court Scavenging → Discover Newbie Team
  ├─ Scene 3: Electronics Store Warehouse → Optional High-Value Target
  ├─ Scene 4: Underground Parking Garage → Mutant Combat
  └─ Scene 5: Management Office → Discover Hidden Extraction Point Intel

Act Three: Extraction (30-45 min)
  ├─ Countdown Begins
  ├─ Extraction Point Conflict (Scav Gang occupies Fixed Extraction Point)
  └─ Final Choice: Assault / Negotiate / Use Hidden Extraction Point

Settlement (10 min)
  └─ Loot → Commission Complete → Experience → Hideout Upgrade Hint
```

### Key Scenes

#### Scene 0: Hideout — Old Ke's Commission

**Narration**:
> "Your Hideout is still an empty room. A bed, a workbench, a chair. A Quarantine Zone map torn from an old newspaper is pinned to the wall. A note has been slipped under the door — the handwriting is Old Ke's. It says only one thing: 'Stop by the Trading Post. Got work.'"

**Commission Details**:
- **"Basic Supplies"** (Simple, Regular): Bring back 3 medical supplies (Common quality is fine) — reward 600
- **"Missing Persons"** (Regular, optional): The Newbie Team "Greenleaf" went missing in Ash Mall — find their trail — reward 1,200 (+ extra 500 if you escort them to extraction)

**Old Ke's Extra Intel**:
- "Ash Mall just had a Pulse Surge, loot should have refreshed."
- "There's something in the underground parking garage. We're not sure what. Avoid that place."
- "The East Side parking lot Extraction Point is the most stable. But I hear the rooftop helipad is occasionally usable — if you can find the Management Office key."

#### Scene 1: North Bus Stop Entry

**Environmental Description**:
> "The bus stop shelter's roof has collapsed halfway. An abandoned bus lies on its side by the road, its body covered in bullet holes and rust. The mall's main entrance is fifty meters ahead — the glass doors long shattered, pitch black inside. The only sound you hear is the low moan of wind through broken windows."

**Optional Perception (PER) Check** (Difficulty: Medium — the GM may set the specific Target Number (TN) per the TN table in Chapter Two of the core rules):
- Success → "Flickering lights deep in the mall's first-floor corridor — maybe emergency lights, maybe something else."
- Critical Success → "You notice a set of fresh footprints beside the underground parking garage entrance — about four to five people, heading inward. Different sizes, chaotic steps — not the Brotherhood."

#### Scene 2: Food Court — Encounter Newbie Team

**Environmental Description**:
> "The open space of the Food Court is lit by a few dim light shafts from the broken skylight above. Overturned tables and chairs are scattered everywhere. The air carries the sour smell of rotting food, and… voices. Lowered voices. Coming from behind the corner café counter."

**Encounter**: Newbie Team "Greenleaf" (3 people)
- Status: One has a leg injury (50% HP), out of bandages. The other two are nervous.
- Equipment: Basic pistol ×2, shotgun ×1 (low on ammo), simple backpack
- Attitude: Hope in despair — pleads for help

**Dialogue Trigger** (player approaches within 10m):
> "Don't shoot! We're not a threat — our teammate is hurt. We've been trapped here for three hours, the Extraction Point is occupied by a bunch of Scavengers… please, do you have any bandages?"

**Possible Developments**:
- Player provides medical assistance → Newbie Team is grateful, may share intel (good stuff in the underground parking garage but there's a monster inside)
- Player intimidates → Newbie Team hands over a small amount of loot (2 Common-quality items)
- Player ignores → Newbie Team may reappear in later scenes, in worse shape
- Player attacks → easy victory but no valuable loot, and a Reputation penalty (Scavengers' Compact -5)

#### Scene 3: Electronics Store Warehouse (Optional)

**Environmental Description**:
> "The front of the shop has been looted empty, empty display racks askew. But the rear warehouse door is closed — a rusty iron lock hanging on it."

**Required Check**: Tech (TEC) check (Lockpicking, specific TN see Chapter Two of core rules) or Physique (PHY) check (force the door).

**Loot** (on successful lockpick):
- Electronic parts ×2 (Rare/Blue)
- Basic tool kit ×1 (Common/White)
- Old radio ×1 (Rare/Blue — repairable)

**Risk**:
- Noise from breaking the door may draw AI Residuals (d6: 4+ draws 2)
- A small gravity anomaly inside the warehouse — items float slightly, requires a Tech (TEC) check (TN see Chapter Two of core rules) to stabilize the grab

#### Scene 4: Underground Parking Garage — Mutant Combat

**Environmental Description**:
> "The ramp goes down; light is completely swallowed by darkness ten meters ahead. The flashlight beam sways between the concrete pillars. There are black marks on the ground — not oil stains. You smell a metallic stench. Then you hear it — a wet, multi-legged crawling sound. Coming from all directions."

**Encounter**: Mutant Hound ×2

> ※ The following are placeholder stats; complete Mutant combat data (including part HP, attack rolls, special abilities) is in Chapter Three [Combat Rules].

- HP: 15 each (Medium size)
- Attack: Bite (Melee, moderate damage) + possible knockdown
- Special: Light-sensitive — flashlight can temporarily blind (easy check)
- Tactics: Ambush from the dark, prioritize attacking isolated targets

**Loot** (after defeat):
- One of them wears a collar with a keyring — Management Office key
- In the trunk of an abandoned vehicle nearby: Military-grade first-aid kit ×1 (Rare/Blue)

**Difficulty Adjustment** (for new GMs):
- If players look strained → one Mutant Hound retreats deeper
- If too easy → a third Mutant Hound appears from the entrance (player retreat blocked)

#### Scene 5: Management Office

**Environmental Description**:
> "The office is locked. But the key is in your hands. Behind the door is a room disturbingly tidy — as if no one had been there on the day of the Collapse. The coffee cup on the desk is still in place, the liquid long evaporated leaving a dark stain. The surveillance screens on the wall are all black. The filing cabinet is half open."

**Loot**:
- Mall map (complete version, including Hidden Extraction Point markers)
- Signal flare ×1 (3 flares — for the rooftop Extraction Point)
- Cash 200 (scattered bills)
- Optional for GM: Manager's diary — records the chaos of Collapse Day, adds worldbuilding depth

**Discovery**:
> "Among a stack of documents on the desk, you find a handwritten extraction procedure — 'The rooftop helipad may be used in emergencies. The signal flare is stored in the Management Office. Password: the mall name reversed.'"

### Extraction Phase

**Countdown Begins**:
> "Your radio crackles — 'Warning: the next Pulse Surge is expected to arrive within 30 minutes. All Scavengers in the zone proceed to Extraction Points immediately.'"

**Conflict: Scav Gang Occupies Fixed Extraction Point**
- East Parking Lot Extraction Point occupied by a Scav Gang (4 people)
- They are waiting for the vehicle, backpacks full
- Option A: Negotiate — they want 500 cash or 2 Rare items as a "ride-share fee"
- Option B: Wait (but the countdown is running, and the Pulse Surge approaches)
- Option C: Use the rooftop Extraction Point (requires Management Office key + signal flare — if players have obtained them)
- Option D: Assault (4v4, but the opponents have mixed gear and unstable morale)

**Rooftop Extraction Point** (if players unlocked):
> "You climb to the third floor, cross through the broken ceiling maintenance ladder, and reach the rooftop. The yellow haze layer churns overhead. The helipad marking is faded but still there. You pull out the signal flare. A red flare shoots into the sky. Five minutes later, the sound of an engine comes from afar — the extraction vehicle is approaching. Looking down from the rooftop, you can see the Scav Gang still noisy in the parking lot. You ascended safely before they noticed."

### GM Memo — Module Adjustments

| If… | Then… |
|---|---|
| Players too smooth (no sense of crisis) | Add a second wave of encounter in the extraction phase — another Scav Gang also arrives at the same Extraction Point |
| Players too strained (low HP, little ammo) | Reduce the number of enemies in the Extraction Point conflict, or have the extraction vehicle arrive early |
| Not enough time (approaching 4 hours but still exploring) | Skip Scenes 3 and 4, trigger the extraction countdown directly |
| Time to spare (main plot done in 2.5 hours) | Insert an extra encounter — a Lone Wolf Veteran appears near the Management Office, offering an intel trade |
| Players miss the Management Office key | Auto-obtain after defeating the Mutants in the parking garage, or have the Newbie Team say "we saw a keyring on the dog" |

## Appendix A: Worldbuilding Narrative Hooks — Improvisation Material Library

The following provides narrative elements the GM can improvise during exploration:

### A.1 Stories in the Ruins

| d10 | Discovery | Implication |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | A child's backpack, with intact textbooks and a insulated lunchbox inside | How chaotic the evacuation was |
| 2 | Bloody handprint on the wall — but only four fingers | Something is wrong in this area |
| 3 | A family photo on the desk, glass frame intact | The original owner never came back |
| 4 | A pile of burned documents, only one line legible: "…no one is allowed into the Deep…" | What the officials are hiding |
| 5 | An answering machine still running, playing the last call of Day 0 | The personal perspective of the Collapse |
| 6 | Drag marks on the ground — from inside the building outward | Something was dragged away |
| 7 | A sealed military crate, empty — but the label reads "Experimental Sample #47" | The contents are somewhere outside |
| 8 | Graffiti on the wall: "They're still here" — dated two years after the Collapse | Who wrote it? Where are they? |
| 9 | Words written in lipstick on the bathroom mirror: "I'm sorry" | Fragments of a personal tragedy |
| 10 | Scattered personal diary, the last page's handwriting grows more and more scrawled, the final line illegible | Slow madness |

### A.2 Environmental Events

| d8 | Event | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | A non-human howl from afar — then dead silence | All NPC squads become nervous, Perception (PER) check +2 |
| 2 | Ground trembles slightly, haze layer darkens | A minor Pulse Surge is coming, all electronic devices unstable |
| 3 | Sudden heavy rain — rainwater with faint anomalous energy | Visibility greatly reduced, tracks untraceable |
| 4 | All Residuals stop moving at the same instant, turn the same direction — resume after 3 seconds | Implies something in the Core Zone is commanding them |
| 5 | Radio cuts out — all channels filled with a low hum | Communications fail, team must maintain line of sight |
| 6 | Signal flare seen rising in the distance — someone is extracting | Hints at the location of other Scavengers |
| 7 | Automatic broadcast from before the Collapse suddenly sounds inside a building ("Please stay calm, follow evacuation routes…") | Creepy contrast |
| 8 | Fresh blood trailing down the corridor — not yet dry | Something just passed — and it's injured |

### A.3 Optional Rule: Fear and Greed

> **Core Mechanic Reference**: Chapter Two [Dice System] — the dual mechanism of Courage (CRG) (Fear Resistance + Greed Suppression) defines the specific DC and dice procedure in the core rules. The following is a narrative-level application guide, complementing the core mechanic.

**Fear Roll** (narrative application):
- When a character experiences an extremely terrifying situation (a suddenly appearing Residual, a teammate's death, entering a deep anomaly zone), the GM may require a Courage (CRG) check
- Specific DC see Chapter Two of the core rules — set by threat level
- Failure → gain 1 "Fear Point." Accumulate 3 Fear Points → all checks -1, until returning to the Hideout
- Critical Failure → character briefly loses control (GM decides — freezes, screams and exposes position, or fires indiscriminately)

**Greed Roll** (narrative application):
- When a character finds high-value loot but the backpack is full, and knows staying longer is dangerous — the GM may require a Courage (CRG) check
- Specific DC see Chapter Two of the core rules — set by item temptation level
- Failure → character cannot resist the temptation, must attempt to take it (discard existing items or become overloaded)
- This rule simulates "knowing you should leave but just can't let go" — can be triggered voluntarily by the player, or required by the GM based on the character's traits
