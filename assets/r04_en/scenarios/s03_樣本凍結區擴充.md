# Sample Freeze Zone Expansion — Frozen Wasteland TRPG

> This document contains 12 complete Freeze Zone samples, covering the full difficulty gradient from novice to legendary. Each Freeze Zone can be used independently, or strung together within a campaign. The first 3 (the supermarket, the hospital, and the military checkpoint) are the basic samples; the remaining 9 are newly added expansions.

---

# I. Novice Freeze Zones (×3)

---

### Sample 01: Supermarket "Frostfall Supermarket" (Novice, Slumber Type)

**Overview**: A medium-sized chain supermarket, frozen at the height of a weekend shopping rush. Frost slowly settled from the ceiling, wrapping the entire store in tranquil white. The aisles are filled with the Frozen holding their shopping poses — a mother pushing a cart, a child hesitating in the snack aisle, an elder queuing at the register. Thanks to its structural stability and gentle thaw rhythm, this is the ideal introductory venue for novice Cold Walkers.

**Thaw Parameters**:

| Parameter | Setting |
|---|---|
| Rhythm Mode | Slumber Type |
| Starting Threshold / Cycle | Starting Threshold 19, −1 every 4 rounds, minimum 14 |
| Omen Difficulty (DC) | 10 (Perception (PER) check to notice faint tremors) |
| Thaw Duration | 2 rounds (generous evacuation time after the omen) |
| Thaw Range | Single area (30 ft × 30 ft) |
| Special Rules | Some supermarket shelves can serve as cover (Defense (AC) +2); the refrigerated section is abnormally cold — staying more than 3 rounds requires a Guts (GUT) Difficulty (DC) 12 to avoid Level 1 Cold Fatigue |

**Map Description**:
- **Entrance Area**: The automatic doors are frozen half-open, with carts scattered about. There is a crack in the ceiling, through which light filters in.
- **Fresh Produce Area (Zone A)**: Vegetables and fruit encased in ice crystals; the freezers still hum faintly. The floor is slippery (Speed (SPD) Difficulty (DC) 10 to avoid slipping).
- **Dry Goods Area (Zone B)**: Canned food and dry-goods shelves are relatively intact. This is where supplies are most plentiful.
- **Cold Storage (Zone C)**: A large freezer containing employee remains and some high-value supplies. The door is frozen shut and requires a Tech (TEC) Difficulty (DC) 14 or a combined push to open.
- **Checkout Area (Zone D)**: 8 checkout lanes; some registers are still usable (requires a Tech (TEC) Difficulty (DC) 12 to read transaction logs for intel).

**In-Zone Threats**:
- **Frost Mist Blob**: A semi-transparent mist creature formed from condensed water vapor and Time Crystal Shards. Health (HP) 15, Defense (AC) 10, Speed 20 ft. Attack is "Cold Touch" (+3 to hit, 1d4+1 cold damage; target must make a Guts (GUT) Difficulty (DC) 11 or have its Speed halved for 1 round). Immune to cold damage; vulnerable to fire.
- **Freezing Collapse**: After the thaw omen appears, a random area's ceiling may collapse within 1d4 rounds. Area 10 ft × 10 ft; characters within must make a Speed (SPD) Difficulty (DC) 13 to dodge, or take 2d6 bludgeoning damage and be pinned.
- **Starving Scavenger**: A survivor not yet fully frozen, mentally broken, who attacks anyone approaching the supplies. Health (HP) 12, Defense (AC) 12, uses a broken blade (+3 to hit, 1d6+1 piercing). If calmed with a Negotiation (NEG) Difficulty (DC) 14, he can provide intel about the supermarket's rear warehouse.

**Supply Distribution**:

| Area | Supply Type | Expected Yield | Risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| Dry Goods Area (Zone B) | Canned food, preserved supplies | 3d6 Rations, 2d4 units of Purified Water | Low — possible Frost Mist Blob |
| Cold Storage (Zone C) | Frozen food, emergency medicine | 2d6 Rations, 1d4 medkits, 1d3 Crystal Shards | Medium — frozen door blocks, possibly 2× Frost Mist Blob inside |
| Checkout Area (Zone D) | Currency substitutes, transaction records | 1d6 silver coins, intel "Supplier List" (can lead to the harbor/docks) | Low |
| Rear Warehouse | Bulk supplies, tools | 2d8 Rations, 1d4 toolkits, 1 Time Crystal | High — requires a key (held by the frozen manager inside the manager's office) |

**Encounter with the Frozen**:
- **"The Mother with the Cart"**: A woman in her thirties pushing a cart, with a roughly 4-year-old child seated inside. Both are in a Full Freeze state. The mother's diary (retrievable from the shopping bag) records her fear of "the world suddenly going quiet."
- **Type**: Full Freeze (mother) + Critical Freeze (child — pupils still faintly glowing)
- **Ethical Dilemma**: The child's Thaw Threshold is only 12 (far below the supermarket's environment Threshold), so in theory a rescue attempt can be made without triggering a large-scale thaw. But it requires spending 1 precious unit of Defrost Agent. Rescue, or not?

**GM Tips**:
- Difficulty: Novice — suitable for 2-4 level-1 characters, first-time play
- Estimated Playtime: 2-3 hours
- Teaching Focus: Basic exploration, supply management, intro to the thaw mechanic
- Omen Difficulty (DC) 10 is extremely low, ensuring players have enough reaction time
- Main campaign clues can be buried in the receipts at the checkout (e.g., donation records of the "Church of the Silent Hand")

### Sample 02: Residential Community "Frostbell Community" (Novice, Slumber Type)

**Overview**: A middle-class residential community of about 50 households, frozen in the early morning. Most residents were still asleep; a few early risers held poses of watering plants, walking dogs, and fetching the newspaper. The freeze here came especially gently — as if time had decided to let this community sleep peacefully. Yet, "anomalies" in certain houses hint that the freeze was not entirely random. At the center of the community is a small park whose fountain froze into a perfect ice sculpture.

**Thaw Parameters**:

| Parameter | Setting |
|---|---|
| Rhythm Mode | Slumber Type |
| Starting Threshold / Cycle | Starting Threshold 20, −1 every 5 rounds, minimum 15 |
| Omen Difficulty (DC) | 12 (Perception (PER) check to notice accelerated window frosting) |
| Thaw Duration | 3 rounds (after the omen) |
| Thaw Range | Single residence (thaw affects only the current building) |
| Special Rules | Each residence calculates thaw progress independently; the community park is a "permanently stable zone" (the central fountain emits a stable time field — resting here additionally recovers 1 level of Cold Fatigue) |

**Map Description**:
- **Community Entrance**: The arch reads "Frostbell Community"; the guard in the booth is frozen mid-sip of tea. Vehicles are frozen in the parking lot.
- **Row A Residences (1-15)**: Smaller apartment units, mostly single occupants. Fewer supplies but faster to explore.
- **Row B Residences (16-35)**: Medium family homes with garages and backyards. The richest supply area.
- **Row C Residences (36-50)**: Larger detached houses, mostly wealthy families. Possibly valuables, but higher thaw risk.
- **Community Park**: Central fountain (frozen ice sculpture), children's playground, gazebo. Several Frozen sit on the park benches, including a tightly embracing couple.
- **Community Center**: A two-story building with gym, meeting room, and storage. May contain community supplies.

**In-Zone Threats**:
- **Frozen Pets**: Some household pets mutated after freezing, becoming aggressive frost creatures. Uses "Frost Hound" stats: Health (HP) 18, Defense (AC) 13, Speed 40 ft, Bite (+4 to hit, 1d8+2 piercing +1 cold); those hit must make a Guts (GUT) Difficulty (DC) 12 or have movement −10 ft for 1 round.
- **Unstable Time Echo**: In some residences, the moment of freezing replays repeatedly (the creak of an opening door, the smell of breakfast, the first half of a sentence). Characters staying in such an area more than 2 rounds need a Perception (PER) Difficulty (DC) 13 or become entranced by the echo, losing 1 action.
- **Collapsing Building**: Residence B-22 is structurally unstable, with floor collapse risk. If more than 3 characters enter the second floor at once, a Tech (TEC) Difficulty (DC) 14 check for structural stability is required, or part of the floor collapses.

**Supply Distribution**:

| Area | Supply Type | Expected Yield | Risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| Row A Residences | Basic household goods, personal diaries | 1d6 Rations, 1d4 intel fragments | Low |
| Row B Residences | Family reserves, tools, medicine | 2d8 Rations, 1d6 medical supplies, 1d3 tools | Medium — some residences have Frozen Pets |
| Row C Residences | Valuables, rare supplies | 1d6 valuables, 1d3 rare trinkets, 2d4 Rations | Medium-High — Thaw Threshold fluctuates more |
| Community Center | Emergency supplies, collective reserves | 3d6 Rations, 2d4 medkits, 1 Time Crystal | Medium — guarded by 2× Frozen Pets |
| Community Park | No supplies | Rest point — fully recovers Cold Fatigue | None |

**Encounter with the Frozen**:
- **"The Old Man in the Garden"**: In the back garden of residence B-8, an old man was watering roses when frozen. Beside him lies an open notebook recording strange dreams in the days before the freeze — in the dream he saw "a giant clock, its hands moving backward." This is an important clue to the Genesis Hypothesis.
- **Type**: Partial Freeze (the old man — right arm and face have thawed, allowing extremely slow communication)
- **Ethical Dilemma**: The old man seems to "want" to stay frozen — his notebook reads "the world in the dream is more real than reality." Players may forcibly thaw him (spending Defrost Agent), or respect his wishes. But the notebook's information is crucial to the main plot — can the full notebook be read without thawing him?

**GM Tips**:
- Difficulty: Novice — suitable for 2-4 level 1-2 characters
- Estimated Playtime: 2.5-3.5 hours
- Teaching Focus: Multi-building exploration management, time-resource allocation, social ethical dilemmas
- The fountain's stabilizing effect can serve as a "safety anchor" for novice players
- The B-8 notebook is a key main-plot clue — ensure players have a chance to discover it

### Sample 03: School "Daybreak Elementary School" (Novice, Pulse Type)

**Overview**: A combined elementary and middle school, frozen during the second morning class. Teachers in classrooms hold chalkboard-writing poses; students are frozen at their desks to varying degrees. In the gym, a basketball game was suspended at the score 47:42. The library is the most unsettling place — the contents of every book have become the same sentence: "Time Remembers Everything." The school basement has a small boiler room, still running, the only heat source in the whole building.

**Thaw Parameters**:

| Parameter | Setting |
|---|---|
| Rhythm Mode | Pulse Type |
| Starting Threshold / Cycle | Starting Threshold 17, −1 every 3 rounds, minimum 10 |
| Omen Difficulty (DC) | 13 (Perception (PER) check to notice faint floor pulsation) |
| Thaw Duration | 2 rounds |
| Thaw Range | Two adjacent classrooms (thaw "spreads" to the next room) |
| Special Rules | The boiler room provides heating protection (thaw timer paused inside, but every 3 rounds of stay requires a Tech (TEC) Difficulty (DC) 14 maintenance or it overheats and explodes — 30 ft range, 3d8 fire damage); all books in the library have become "Time Memory" text (collecting 3 yields one main-plot clue) |

**Map Description**:
- **School Gate & Guard Booth**: The guard is frozen before the monitors. The monitors still record the moment of freezing — viewable (Tech (TEC) Difficulty (DC) 12).
- **Teaching Building (3 floors)**: 6 classrooms and 2 offices per floor. Frozen students' seats form obstacle terrain.
- **Library (2nd floor, east wing)**: ~5000 volumes, all rewritten as "Time Memory" text. Open books lie on reading desks — curated fragments of "Time Memory."
- **Gymnasium**: Open space with a frozen basketball game. The scoreboard still flickers — numbers flashing back and forth between 47:42 and 42:47.
- **Boiler Room (basement)**: A warm refuge. A frozen janitor sits by the boiler, holding a wrench and a maintenance log.
- **Rooftop**: Reachable via stairs. Good visibility over the surrounding wasteland. But the wind is fierce; every 5 rounds requires a Guts (GUT) Difficulty (DC) 14 or gain Level 1 Cold Fatigue.

**In-Zone Threats**:
- **Time Echo — "The Eternal Dismissal Bell"**: The school bell rings on its own without thawing (every 2d4 rounds). Characters hearing the bell need WILL DC 13 (Guts (GUT) may be substituted); failure causes a 1-round "trance" state (cannot take reactions, movement halved). The bell also triggers partial thawing in nearby areas.
- **Frozen Student Mass**: In some classrooms, the student group exists as a "group frozen entity" — acts as a single unit but counts as one large creature. Health (HP) 30 (representing group stability), Defense (AC) 8. When disturbed, releases a "collective fear wave" — 15 ft cone, Perception (PER) Difficulty (DC) 13, failure takes 2d4 mental damage and is confused for 1 round. Cannot be destroyed by normal attacks — only calmed (Negotiation (NEG) Difficulty (DC) 15) or stabilized with Defrost Agent.
- **Boiler Overload**: If boiler maintenance fails, boiler pressure overloads. If not repaired within 3 rounds (Tech (TEC) Difficulty (DC) 16) it explodes.

**Supply Distribution**:

| Area | Supply Type | Expected Yield | Risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| Faculty Offices | Documents, personal items,small amount of cash | 1d6 intel, 1d4 misc | Low |
| Infirmary | Medical supplies | 2d6 medkits, 1d4 medicine | Low — possible wandering Frost Mist Blob |
| Library | "Time Memory" text | 3d4 clue fragments (every 3 assemble one main-plot piece of info) | Medium — prolonged reading requires Perception (PER) Difficulty (DC) 14 or becomes confused |
| Gym Storage | Sports equipment, emergency supplies | 1d6 tools (can be modded into weapons/armor), 1d4 Rations | Low |
| Boiler Room | Tools, janitor's log | 1d4 tools, intel "The Janitor's Discovery" (main-plot clue) | Medium — boiler maintenance risk |
| Kitchen/Cafeteria | Food reserves | 3d8 Rations, 2d6 Purified Water | Medium — 2× Frost Mist Blob in the freezer |

**Encounter with the Frozen**:
- **"The Teacher at the Blackboard"**: A third-grade math teacher, frozen before a half-written equation. The blackboard holds not a math problem — but a precise star chart, marking some celestial coordinates and a line: "It watches us from there." The teacher is in a Partial Freeze state, fingers trembling slightly, as if wanting to finish the last character.
- **Type**: Partial Freeze (foggy consciousness but slight physical response)
- **Ethical Dilemma**: Thawing the teacher may yield more info (the meaning of the star chart), but it triggers the entire classroom's student mass to thaw — they may panic collectively, causing irreversible psychological trauma. Alternatively, one can only copy the blackboard, but lacking the teacher's explanation, the star-chart info may be incomplete.

**GM Tips**:
- Difficulty: Novice (leaning hard) — suitable for 3-5 level 1-2 characters
- Estimated Playtime: 3-4 hours
- Teaching Focus: Environmental threat management, non-combat handling of group frozen entities, info gathering
- The star-chart clue can link to the harbor/docks (observation station) or the laboratory
- The library's "Time Memory" text is a puzzle element running throughout the whole campaign

### Sample 04: Hospital "St. Mary's Memorial Hospital" (Intermediate, Pulse Type)

**Overview**: An eight-floor general hospital, frozen at the height of the ER rush. The freeze here is extremely uneven — the ER is almost fully frozen, while some wards are merely "room-temperature stagnation." Deep in the hospital, in an operating room, a heart surgery was frozen at a critical moment — the surgeon's scalpel hovering half a centimeter from the heart. Most eerie is the morgue: its freeze did not come from outside, but spread outward from within. The hospital is strewn with large amounts of medicine, medical equipment, and patient records — extremely valuable, but equally risky.

**Thaw Parameters**:

| Parameter | Setting |
|---|---|
| Rhythm Mode | Pulse Type |
| Starting Threshold / Cycle | Starting Threshold 16, −1 every 3 rounds, minimum 9 |
| Omen Difficulty (DC) | 14 (Perception (PER) check to notice an ECG monitor pulsing on its own) |
| Thaw Duration | 2 rounds |
| Thaw Range | Single floor (thaw spreads horizontally along the floor) |
| Special Rules | The operating room's time field is extremely unstable — all actions within require an extra Minor Action (time flows abnormally slowly); the morgue has an "anti-thaw" effect — characters entering the morgue need a Guts (GUT) Difficulty (DC) 15 every 3 rounds or begin to freeze themselves (gain Freeze Level 1, accumulating 3 levels becomes Full Freeze); the pharmacy is rich in supplies but its drugs are chaotically categorized (requires Tech (TEC) Difficulty (DC) 15 or Perception (PER) Difficulty (DC) 14 to correctly identify drugs, failure may use the wrong drug) |

**Map Description**:
- **1st Floor Lobby / ER**: A chaotic frozen scene — waiting patients, nurses, gurney patients. The registration machine still runs, screen flickering. Several high-value medical carts in the ER.
- **2nd Floor Outpatient**: Various specialty clinics; some instruments still usable (TEC check required). The eye clinic's eye chart has been replaced with rows of "Time Memory" text.
- **3rd Floor Wards**: 30 wards, patients in varied states. Some wards' flowers still bloom (time anomaly).
- **4th Floor ORs**: 4 operating rooms, of which OR 3 has the most famous frozen scene (heart surgery). The OR's surgical light still glows faintly.
- **5th Floor Maternity**: In the nursery, the infants seem to be... smiling? The most unsettling area.
- **6th Floor Admin Offices**: Medical record archive, director's office. Records may contain key info (records of patients showing abnormal symptoms before the freeze).
- **7th Floor Pharmacy & Lab**: Drug warehouse, testing lab. The richest supply area.
- **8th Floor Morgue (basement extension)**: An independent cold-storage facility, the source of the "anti-thaw." Deep within is a metal cabinet that cannot be opened by any means — engraved with "Seed of Time."

**In-Zone Threats**:
- **Thawed Patient**: Some patients behave abnormally when thawed — their bodies thaw, but their consciousness remains trapped in the frozen moment. They repeat pre-freeze actions (pressing the nurse call button, trying to get up, muttering), unresponsive to outside stimuli but instinctively grasping those who approach. Health (HP) 8 (fragile), Defense (AC) 8. Those grabbed need a Speed (SPD) Difficulty (DC) 13 to break free or become restrained. Cannot be treated as enemies — they are victims. Negotiation (NEG) Difficulty (DC) 16 can calm a single individual.
- **Time Parasite**: A special threat bred in the morgue. A semi-transparent human silhouette formed from Time Crystal Shards and the time-energy of the moment of death. Health (HP) 25, Defense (AC) 14, Speed 30 ft. Attack is "Time Drain" (+5 to hit, 2d6+2 mental damage; target's age temporarily advances 1d4 years — purely narrative effect, but being hit 3+ times in a row produces permanent narrative consequences). Has a strong urge to attack Time Crystal items. Weakness: cannot leave the morgue area.
- **Drug Confusion**: Pharmacy drug labels are blurred by the freeze. Using an unidentified drug requires a 1d6: 1-2 wrong drug (1d4 poison damage + nausea for 1 hour), 3-4 expired but effective drug (effect halved), 5-6 correct drug (normal effect).

**Supply Distribution**:

| Area | Supply Type | Expected Yield | Risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| ER | First-aid supplies, medical tools | 2d6 medkits, 1d6 first-aid supplies | Medium — 3× Thawed Patients |
| Wards | Personal items, medical-record clues | 1d6 intel, 1d4 misc | Low |
| ORs | Surgical instruments (can be modded into precision tools or weapons), anesthetic | 1d4 surgical toolkits, 1d6 anesthetics (usable for special arrows or traps) | High — OR 3 time anomaly |
| Pharmacy | Various drugs | 3d8 drug units (require identification), 1d4 rare drugs | High — drug confusion + possible Frost Mist Blob |
| Lab | Research equipment, experiment data | 2d4 intel (incl. Genesis Hypothesis clues), 1d3 lab equipment | Medium |
| Admin Offices | Medical record archive | Intel "Abnormal Case List" — pointing to the power plant and the laboratory | Low |
| Morgue | Time Crystals, special items | 2d4 Time Crystal Shards, 1 Time Crystal, "Seed of Time" (legendary item) | Extreme — 2× Time Parasite + anti-thaw effect |

**Encounter with the Frozen**:
- **"The Boy on the Operating Table"**: In OR 3, a 12-year-old boy is undergoing heart surgery. At the moment of freezing the scalpel was only 0.5 cm from his heart. The boy's eyes are slightly open beneath the ice — he is a Critical Freeze, conscious but completely unable to move. His heart rate beats extremely slowly on the monitor (once every 30 seconds).
- **Type**: Critical Freeze
- **Ethical Dilemma**: If the boy is thawed without moving the scalpel, the scalpel will cut his heart (fatal). If the scalpel is removed first, it may trigger the OR's time field to collapse — the entire floor's Thaw Threshold immediately drops to minimum (9), triggering a large-scale thaw. A third solution must be found (hint: anesthetic can relax the boy's muscles — anesthetize first, then carefully remove the scalpel — Tech (TEC) Difficulty (DC) 18, failure then...).

**GM Tips**:
- Difficulty: Intermediate — suitable for 4-5 level 3-5 characters
- Estimated Playtime: 4-6 hours
- Focus: Resource management, complex ethical dilemmas, multi-area exploration strategy
- The morgue is recommended as an optional challenge — extremely high reward but also extremely high risk
- "Seed of Time" is one of the campaign's core items

### Sample 05: Subway System "Frozen Vein Line" (Intermediate, Chain Type)

**Overview**: The city's subway system before the freeze, about 40 km long, deepest point 60 m underground. At the moment of freezing, all trains stopped simultaneously — some in tunnels, some beside platforms. Because the underground environment is enclosed, the freeze is preserved unusually well. Passengers in the cars hold their commuting poses: office workers reading newspapers, students with headphones, dozing elders. But what's special about the subway is that time here shows a strange "stratification": different tunnel segments flow at inconsistent rates. The deepest transfer station, "Zero Point Station," is said to hide one of the Frozen Wasteland's oldest secrets.

**Thaw Parameters**:

| Parameter | Setting |
|---|---|
| Rhythm Mode | Chain Type |
| Starting Threshold / Cycle | Starting Threshold 18, −1 every 2 rounds, −2 when adjacent area thaws, minimum 6 |
| Omen Difficulty (DC) | 15 (Perception (PER) check to notice a low-frequency rumble echoing in the tunnel) |
| Thaw Duration | 1 round (Chain Type thaw is extremely fast) |
| Thaw Range | Current tunnel segment + all adjacent segments |
| Special Rules | Time Stratification effect — different tunnel segments have independent "time density" (decided by 1d4: 1 = time-accelerated zone — +1 move action per round; 2 = time-slowed zone — −1 move action per round; 3 = time-loop zone — repeats the previous round's action effect every 3 rounds; 4 = normal zone); trains can be repaired (Tech (TEC) Difficulty (DC) 18) and used as transport — but a moving train triggers thaw in all segments along its route; Zero Point Station has "Eternal Darkness" — all light sources halved in brightness here, darkvision is ineffective |

**Map Description**:
- **Entrance Station "Frostbell Plaza Station"**: Surface entrance hall, frozen commuters. Ticket machines still operate, selling one-way tickets to "Zero Point Station" (requires 1 Time Crystal Shard).
- **Line A Tunnel (E-W)**: 4 stations, beneath the commercial district. Station billboards rewritten as "Time Memory" text.
- **Line B Tunnel (N-S)**: 3 stations, beneath the residential district. A frozen train sits in the tunnel — its cars rich in supplies.
- **Transfer Hall "Clock Hand Station"**: Where Line A and Line B intersect, a large underground space. At its center is a giant frozen clock sculpture — its hands moving extremely slowly.
- **Maintenance Passage**: A narrow maintenance tunnel network connecting the main tunnels. Some passages have collapsed, requiring Tech (TEC) Difficulty (DC) 16 to clear.
- **Zero Point Station (deepest)**: The subway's oldest station, built in the previous civilization era before the freeze. Its walls are engraved with unrecognizable writing and a giant mural — depicting a world surrounded by time. At the station's center is a stone platform holding a fist-sized black crystal — the "Zero Point Core."

**In-Zone Threats**:
- **Tunnel Walker**: A special group living in the subway — they happened to be underground when the freeze occurred, gained a semi-immortal state after being "partially frozen." They look pale, move slowly, but are fully conscious. Tunnel Walkers are not necessarily hostile — they crave contact with the outside world, but their language ability has greatly degraded. If enraged: Health (HP) 20, Defense (AC) 12, uses "Time Slow Aura" (passive, all creatures within 10 ft have Speed −10 ft). Negotiation (NEG) Difficulty (DC) 18 can establish initial communication.
- **Time Rift**: Some tunnel segments have unstable Time Rifts — semi-transparent cracks floating in the air, showing frozen-pre-freeze scene fragments inside. Approaching a Time Rift (within 5 ft) requires a Guts (GUT) Difficulty (DC) 15 or be drawn to it, taking 1d8 mental damage and being teleported to a random adjacent segment. A Time Rift can be stabilized with Tech (TEC) Difficulty (DC) 20 (consuming 2 Time Crystal Shards).
- **Frozen Train**: A frozen train in the Line B tunnel. Some of its cars have begun to thaw — but what thaws is not ice, but "time." Time flows extremely fast inside the cars; staying one round inside equals one hour passing outside (effect on characters: each round requires CON DC 13 to avoid Level 1 Fatigue, food and water consumed faster). Inside the cars is a group of scavengers driven mad by time acceleration — Health (HP) 15×3, Defense (AC) 11, use broken weapons (+3 to hit, 1d8+1 damage), but can make 2 attacks per round (time-acceleration effect).

**Supply Distribution**:

| Area | Supply Type | Expected Yield | Risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| Station shops | Preserved food, daily goods | 2d6 Rations, 1d8 misc (per station) | Low — scattered distribution |
| Line B Frozen Train | Passenger luggage, valuables | 3d6 personal items, 2d4 valuables, 1d3 intel | Extreme — time acceleration + mad scavengers |
| Maintenance Passage | Tools, emergency equipment | 2d4 tools, 1d6 emergency lighting, 1d4 comms equipment | Medium — collapse risk |
| Clock Hand Station | Time Crystals, antiquities | 2d4 Time Crystal Shards, intel "Clockmaker's Notes" | Medium — 4× Tunnel Walker (negotiable) |
| Zero Point Station | "Zero Point Core," ancient knowledge | 1 Zero Point Core (core plot item), 3d4 Time Memory texts | Extreme — Zero Point Guardian (see below) |

**Encounter with the Frozen**:
- **"The Clockmaker of Clock Hand Station"**: An elderly Tunnel Walker, sitting beside the clock sculpture at the center of Clock Hand Station. He claims to have been the city's chief clockmaker before the freeze, studying "the flow of time" in the subway system. He is in a Loop Freeze state — thaws for 30 minutes every 24 hours, fully frozen the rest of the time. His notes (found at the sculpture's base) record that "time does not come from outside — it spreads outward from the earth's core."
- **Type**: Loop Freeze
- **Ethical Dilemma**: The clockmaker wants players to help him "fully thaw" — but that means his remaining lifespan will run out within days (he has already maintained beyond a normal lifespan in the freeze). On the other hand, the knowledge he holds is crucial to understanding the truth of the freeze. Gain all info at once (thaw him, but he dies), or accept the limit of 30 minutes of communication every 24 hours?

**GM Tips**:
- Difficulty: Intermediate (leaning hard) — suitable for 4-5 level 4-6 characters
- Estimated Playtime: 5-7 hours
- Focus: Complex environment management, strategic use of the time-stratification mechanic, diplomacy with Tunnel Walkers
- Zero Point Station is recommended as the climax scene — the Zero Point Guardian is an intermediate boss fight
- The Zero Point Core is a campaign core item — collecting it triggers a major main-plot turn

### Sample 06: Power Plant "Everfrost Power Plant" (Intermediate, Breath Type)

**Overview**: A medium geothermal power plant that powered half the city before the freeze. Because it used geothermal + experimental Time Crystal hybrid generation, the plant did not stop at the freeze — it entered a kind of "time closed loop" state. The turbines still turn, the control panels still run, the whole plant sits in an eternal "operating" state. But the problem is: the plant's core reactor is slowly drawing energy from Time Crystals, and this process is accelerating the freeze in the surrounding area. If the reactor is not shut down or stabilized in time, the freeze will spread outward from the plant, devouring tens of kilometers of area.

**Thaw Parameters**:

| Parameter | Setting |
|---|---|
| Rhythm Mode | Breath Type |
| Starting Threshold / Cycle | Starting Threshold 16, −1/round ×3 then +3 reset (but each reset expands thaw range by 5 ft) |
| Omen Difficulty (DC) | 14 (Perception (PER) check to notice abnormal light-flicker frequency) |
| Thaw Duration | Instant (Breath Type thaw needs no prep time) |
| Thaw Range | 30 ft around the reactor core (expands 5 ft each reset) |
| Special Rules | Reactor time closed loop — all mechanical equipment in the plant is forever operating, cannot be normally shut down (requires Tech (TEC) Difficulty (DC) 19 in the control room to execute forced cooling); "Eternal Operation" effect — completing a long rest inside the plant does not heal Cold Fatigue (machinery noise and time-field vibration disturb sleep), but a short rest additionally recovers 2 Health (HP); high-voltage areas — some areas have unprotected high-voltage wires; contact or proximity (within 5 ft) requires a Speed (SPD) Difficulty (DC) 15 to dodge or take 2d8 lightning damage |

**Map Description**:
- **Entrance Security Check**: Frozen guards and security equipment. The security gate still beeps (Eternal Operation effect). The visitor log has the last visitor's signature — including a representative of the "Church of the Silent Hand."
- **Control Room (2nd floor)**: Overlooks the whole generation hall. Panels still run, screens showing data. The main console can execute forced cooling (Tech (TEC) Difficulty (DC) 19, takes 5 rounds, during which threats must be withstood).
- **Generation Hall**: Giant turbines and generator sets, deafening noise (auditory-related checks at Disadvantage here). Narrow maintenance walkways around the turbines — only one person wide.

- **Tower Plaza**: Within a 500 ft radius of the Clock Tower is an "Absolute Freeze Zone" — no thaw possible, time is fully static here. The Frozen here are not ice sculptures, but "Time Sculptures" — they look like paused video frames. The plaza is strewn with frozen bodies of pilgrims, researchers, and explorers — frozen while trying to approach the Clock Tower.
- **1st Floor — Gate of Time**: The Clock Tower's entrance hall. A giant pendulum swings at the hall's center. Inscriptions in four languages are carved on the walls: "Time is not a river — time is an ocean. You swim within it, but the ocean is also within you." A door at the entrance — only those carrying "the three shards of the Genesis Hypothesis" may pass (the three shards are at: the school library's star chart, the port's observation-station data, the lab's theory document — or other item combinations at the GM's discretion).
- **2nd Floor — Hall of Memories (Slumber Type, Starting Threshold 22, −1 every 5 rounds, minimum 16)**: A long corridor lined with countless mirrors. Each mirror shows not the present image, but a past moment of the viewer — usually an important memory. Characters lingering in the corridor are gradually surrounded by their own memories. The threat here is not combat, but that characters may be lost in memory and unwilling to advance.
- **3rd Floor — Hall of Pulses (Pulse Type, Starting Threshold 19, −1 every 3 rounds, minimum 12)**: At the hall's center is a giant "Time Heart" — a pulsing organ made of pure Time Crystal. Every beat (every 3 rounds), the hall's reality briefly distorts. Here characters must synchronize with the Time Heart — act at the right moment to pass.
- **4th Floor — Chain Corridor (Chain Type, Starting Threshold 20, −1 every 2 rounds, −3 when adjacent area thaws, minimum 6)**: A maze of countless interconnected rooms. Each room represents a "possible future" — different timelines born from different choices. Taking a wrong turn in the maze causes thaw to spread in a chain across the whole maze. Finding the correct path requires understanding "the chain nature of time."
- **5th Floor — Abyss of Spasms (Spasm Type, Starting Threshold 16, 1d6−1 per round, minimum 2)**: The most dangerous floor. Reality here is extremely unstable — space constantly tears, reassembles, tears again. Gravity direction changes randomly each round. Here exist "Time Shards" — reality fragments peeled from different timelines, each containing independent threats.
- **6th Floor — Peak of Breath (Breath Type, Starting Threshold 18, −1/round ×2 then +4 reset)**: The second-to-top floor, closest to the summit. Space here expands and contracts like breath — the hall's size constantly changes. At its center is a giant hourglass, sand flowing between top and bottom — then flipping, starting over. This is the ultimate symbol of the time loop.
- **7th Floor — Top of the Clock Face**: The Clock Tower's top floor. The giant clock face is right overhead, hands moving slowly. At the center of the face is a socket — exactly fitting the "Zero Point Core" and the "Perfect Crystal." Around the socket is carved a line: "When time begins is also where time ends." This is where the final choice is made.

**In-Zone Threats (common to all floors + special)**:
- **Time Guardian**: The Clock Tower's ultimate guardian, appearing in every floor's challenge. Appears as a humanoid entity of rotating clock gears and glowing crystal. Health (HP) 80, Defense (AC) 20, Speed 40 ft. Attacks are "Blade of Time" (+12 to hit, 4d8+6 force damage; those hit need a Guts (GUT) Difficulty (DC) 20 or be time-stopped 1 round — lose all actions) and "Bell Toll Shock" (30 ft circle, +8 to hit, 3d8 thunder damage + stun 1 round). Special ability "Time Rewind" — once per encounter, can restore its Health to its state 3 rounds prior. "Time Acceleration" — every 3 rounds gains an extra full round of actions. At the tower summit, the Time Guardian's power peaks — Health (HP) 120, all Difficulty (DC) +2.
- **Time Shadow**: The character's own "shadow" in a wrong timeline — if the character makes a major wrong choice inside the tower, a Time Shadow appears and attacks. Stats identical to the character, but possessing all abilities and equipment the character did not choose. This is a battle against "the self one might have become."
- **Memory Trap (2nd Floor)**: The character must pass through their most important memory — but the memory tries to hold them. Each memory layer deeper requires a Guts (GUT) Difficulty (DC) 18 or stay in the memory 1 round (cannot move). Accumulating 3 stays makes advance impossible (needs rescue by another character).
- **Time Shard (5th Floor)**: In the Abyss of Spasms, Time Shards may produce any threat encountered before — enemies from all past Freeze Zones in the campaign may reappear here (GM picks the 1d3 past enemies most fitting the current plot).

**Supply Distribution**:

| Area | Supply Type | Expected Yield | Risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tower Plaza | Predecessors' relics, Time Crystals | 3d6 Time Crystal Shards, 1d8 predecessors' relics (may include intel or gear) | Extreme — Absolute Freeze Zone (each round Guts (GUT) Difficulty (DC) 22 or begin to freeze) |
| Each Floor (common) | Time Crystal Shards | Scattered across floors, total 5d8 Time Crystal Shards | Varies by floor |
| 3rd Floor (Time Heart) | Time Heart Shard | 1 "Heart Shard" — can craft legendary gear or consumables | Extreme — approaching the heart requires enduring time pulses |
| 6th Floor (Hourglass) | "Sand of Time" | 1d4 "Sand of Time" — single use reverses everything that happened within one round (legendary consumable) | Extreme — Hourglass Guardian |
| 7th Floor (Top of the Clock Face) | Ultimate Choice | See "Final Choice" below | Ultimate — Time Guardian (final form) |

**Encounter with the Frozen**:
- **"The Pilgrim in the Tower"**: On various floors of the Clock Tower, characters meet explorers who entered before — frozen at different points in time. Most important is the "First Pilgrim" — an old man found on the 3rd floor (beside the Time Heart). His body is fully frozen, but his consciousness is projected somehow, allowing telepathic communication with characters. He claims to have spent "eternity" in the tower, witnessing countless freeze-and-thaw cycles. He gives characters warnings and advice — but his motive is unclear. Does he truly want to help, or want characters to follow in his footsteps?
- **Type**: Full Freeze (body) + consciousness projection (special)
- **Ethical Dilemma**: The First Pilgrim offers a "shortcut" — he knows how to bypass the 4th and 5th floors to reach the 6th directly. But the price is that the character must transfer a "part of their time" (i.e., lifespan) to him, letting him briefly regain physical form. Accepting means the character's lifespan shortens (specific years at GM's discretion — but should have actual play impact); refusing means must fully experience the 4th and 5th floor challenges.

**GM Tips (Important)**:
- Difficulty: Legendary — suitable for 4-5 level 9-10 characters
- Estimated Playtime: 10-14 hours (recommended in 2-3 sessions)
- Focus: Epic challenge, deep philosophical themes, ultimate choice of character fate
- The Clock Tower is the climax of the whole campaign — each floor should have a unique experience and challenge
- The "three shards" threshold ensures players have done enough exploration before entering
- The Final Choice should change the wasteland's world order — and the characters' own fate
- Recommend preparing detailed maps and a time-tracking table for each floor before play
- The bell tolls once every hour, an important timing mechanic

### Sample 11: Wasteland Abyss "Genesis Corridor" (Legendary, Breath Type)

**Overview**: If the Clock Tower is the "heart" of the freeze, then the Genesis Corridor is its "memory." This is a cave tunnel system at the wasteland's deepest point, said to have formed at the world's birth. The tunnel walls are covered with unrecognizable ancient writing and patterns — but they were not carved, they "grew": mineral crystals naturally arranged as text. At the corridor's deepest point is a place called the "Genesis Wall" — a giant crystal wall displaying this world's history in moving images, from birth to... the future not yet happened. Those who successfully reach the Genesis Wall are said to be able to "see the full picture of time." But most never return — not because of death, but because they chose to stay there, watching time's flow forever.

**Thaw Parameters**:

| Parameter | Setting |
|---|---|
| Rhythm Mode | Breath Type (but each reset expands thaw range by 10 ft, and randomly changes thaw mode) |
| Starting Threshold / Cycle | Starting Threshold 20, −1/round ×3 then +5 reset, but after each reset 1d4 decides the next round'sadditional mode (1 = Slumber variant — omen time +1 round; 2 = Pulse variant — cycle accelerated 1 round; 3 = Chain variant — adjacent-area thaw−1 extra; 4 = Spasm variant — thaw range becomes whole area) |
| Omen Difficulty (DC) | 20 (Perception (PER) check to notice crystal walls beginning to glow — Time Memory about to release) |
| Thaw Duration | 1 round |
| Thaw Range | Current cave segment (expands 10 ft each reset) |
| Special Rules | "Time Memory" — characters near crystal walls (within 5 ft) see fragments of the world's history. Every minute of viewing requires a Guts (GUT) Difficulty (DC) 18 or fall into "Time Immersion" (character unwilling to leave, each round needs persuasion or forcible removal); "Crystal Echo" — Time Crystals used in the corridor release their stored "Time Memory," providing extra history fragments but possibly also releasing the danger within; "Whisper of the Genesis Wall" — characters near the Genesis Wall hear whispers. The whisper's content varies by person — usually the character's deepest question. Answering the whisper's question (Negotiation (NEG) Difficulty (DC) 22 or Guts (GUT) Difficulty (DC) 22) may yield revelation or incur mental trauma |

**Map Description**:
- **Entrance — Throat of Oblivion**: A giant cave entrance hidden deep in the wasteland. The rock at the entrance shows unnatural spiral patterns — as if time itself left traces in the rock. Near the entrance is a small camp ruin — a temporary base left by the previous expedition.
- **Segment 1 — Corridor of History**: Tunnel walls display images of the distant past — world birth, life origin, civilization rise. These images move, but silently. Touching the wall lets you hear the era's sounds (Tech (TEC) Difficulty (DC) 16 to record sound samples).
- **Segment 2 — Beginning of the Freeze**: The walls here display the whole process of the freeze occurring — but the viewpoint is not from the ground, but from... above the sky? Images show a giant energy wave spreading outward from the Clock Tower's position, instantly covering the whole world. But the energy wave's "source" seems to be beyond the sky — pointing at a point in the starry sky.
- **Segment 3 — Sea of Possibilities**: The wall here splits into countless branches, each showing a possible future. Some show the freeze permanently continuing, civilization perishing; some show the freeze reversed, the world restored; some show completely incomprehensible scenes. Branches constantly split, merge, vanish — time's countless possibilities exist here simultaneously.
- **Segment 4 — Void Rift**: The corridor is interrupted. Ahead is a giant abyssal cleft — ~50 ft wide, bottomless. The cleft has no ice, no light, not even time — a realm of absolute nothingness. Crossing requires a special "Bridge of Time" — by using the Perfect Crystal or large amounts of Time Crystal Shards at the cleft's edge, you can temporarily create a time bridge in the nothingness (lasts 3 rounds).
- **Segment 5 — Genesis Wall**: The corridor's end. A giant crystal wall ~100 ft high, ~200 ft wide, its surface flowing with gorgeous light. The wall displays not the past, but the "now" — a live panorama of the whole wasteland. Every settlement, every Freeze Zone, every still-active person — all on this wall. At the wall's center is a raised crystal — touching it lets you "enter" any scene on the wall, witnessing what is happening as an observer. But most unsettling is — it shows what is beyond the border. At the wasteland's edge, a giant dark silhouette is slowly approaching...

**In-Zone Threats**:
- **Crystal Guardian**: Native creature of the Genesis Corridor — made of pure Time Crystal, appearing as polyhedral geometric forms with memory-light flowing inside. They guard the corridor, attacking any existence trying to go deeper. Health (HP) 60, Defense (AC) 19, Speed 25 ft (fly). Attacks are "Memory Projection" (+11 to hit, 3d8+5 mental damage; those hit need a Guts (GUT) Difficulty (DC) 19 or randomly lose proficiency in one skill — until a long rest) and "Time Freeze Ray" (ranged 40 ft, +9 to hit, 2d8+4 cold damage + target time-slowed — Speed −15 ft for 1 round). Special ability "Memory Drain" — each round can steal a memory fragment from a character (GM describes a past scene of the character "played" by the guardian), the character needs a Guts (GUT) Difficulty (DC) 18 or lose the emotional weight of that memory (game effect: Disadvantage on checks related to that memory, until leaving the corridor). Count: 2d2 per segment.
- **Void Abyss**: The void cleft of Segment 4. This is not a threat, but the purest "non-existence." Anything that falls into the void ceases to exist — not death, but erasure from time. No one remembers they existed. Characters can try to jump the cleft (Speed (SPD) Difficulty (DC) 24 — nearly impossible), but once failed, no rescue is possible. The Bridge of Time is the only safe passage.
- **Future Self**: In Segment 3 (Sea of Possibilities), characters may meet a "possible future" version of themselves. This future self may be friendly or hostile — depending on the character's choices in the campaign. Stats: identical to the character, but level +1, possessing gear and abilities the character might gain in the future. This is not a mandatory fight — the future self may offer warnings, advice, or... a plea for help.

**Supply Distribution**:

| Area | Supply Type | Expected Yield | Risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| Entrance Camp | Predecessors' relics, map fragments | 2d4 intel, 1d6 Rations, predecessors' leftover notes (may include corridor clues) | Low |
| Corridor of History | Knowledge, ancient-tech clues | 3d6 intel (incl. ancient-tech knowledge, unlocks special crafting recipes) | Medium — 2× Crystal Guardian |
| Beginning of the Freeze | Visual record of freeze origin | Key intel "Energy Wave Source of the Freeze" — pointing at a point in the starry sky (matches the school star chart and port observation data) | Medium — 3× Crystal Guardian |
| Sea of Possibilities | Future foresight, time-branch knowledge | 1d4 "Future Shards" — use to gain foresight once (Advantage on next important check) | High — complexity of meeting the Future Self |
| Genesis Wall | Live panorama of the world, the existence beyond the border | Ultimate intel — see the dark silhouette beyond the wasteland's border (reveals "the true cause of the freeze"), optional entry into any scene to observe | Extreme — 4× Crystal Guardian + Whisper of the Genesis Wall |

**Encounter with the Frozen**:
- **"Those Who Stayed Before the Genesis Wall"**: Before the Genesis Wall sit dozens of fully frozen people — explorers of past ages who reached here. They did not die — they chose to stay before the Genesis Wall, watching time's flow forever. Some have sat here for centuries. Their bodies are fully frozen, but their eyes are open — irises reflecting the Genesis Wall's light. Among them, characters may recognize familiar faces.
- **Type**: Full Freeze (voluntary — they chose eternal watching)
- **Ethical Dilemma**: The Genesis Wall's whisper asks each character the same question: "Are you willing to see everything?" Accepting means gaining the Genesis Wall's full knowledge — but also, like predecessors, being drawn by eternal watching, hard to leave. Each character must choose independently. Accepting characters gain "Genesis Sight" (permanent ability — can sense time's flow anywhere, gaining foresight), but each use requires a Guts (GUT) Difficulty (DC) 20 or fall into a 1-hour "Time Immersion" state. Refusing characters are unaffected, but also cannot gain the Genesis Wall's full knowledge. Additionally, characters may try to awaken a specific person who stayed before the Genesis Wall — this requires Negotiation (NEG) Difficulty (DC) 24 or using the Perfect Crystal; on success that person wakes from eternal watching, but may be mentally broken.

**GM Tips**:
- Difficulty: Legendary — suitable for 4-5 level 10 characters (or near level 10)
- Estimated Playtime: 8-12 hours (recommended in 2 sessions)
- Focus: Philosophical exploration over combat, ultimate themes of time and existence, personalized character journeys
- The Genesis Corridor and Clock Tower can be done in either order — depending on the campaign's narrative needs
- The "dark silhouette beyond the border" shown by the Genesis Wall is the campaign's ultimate reveal — handle this moment with care
- The "Future Self" in the Sea of Possibilities needs careful design by the GM based on each character's story line
- This scene's emotional tone should be "awe" rather than "fear" — players should feel the grandeur and beauty of time
