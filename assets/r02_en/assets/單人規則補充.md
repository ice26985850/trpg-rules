# SCP: CONTAINMENT PROTOCOL — Solo Mode Rules Supplement v1.0

**Reference Baseline**: SCP: CONTAINMENT PROTOCOL Rulebook v1.2
**Applicable Scenario**: A two-player game of one Player (PC) + one Game Master (GM)
**Design Goal**: Preserve the core experience of the original rules, adjust the multiplayer-dependent mechanics, and add an NPC Companion system

---

## I. NPC Companion System (Sidekick System)

A solo player cannot cover the combat, investigation, and technical tracks simultaneously. This system provides **1–2 simplified NPC Companions**, played by the GM but issuable commands by the player during combat.

### 1.1 Companion Creation (30 Seconds)

Use the following streamlined template; do not build a full character sheet:

```
Name: __________  Role: __________  Level: Same as PC
HP: END + Class Bonus + HP per Level × (Level−1) [auto-calculated by formula]
Core Skills: 2 (value = 40 + Level×3)
Weakness: 1 (value = 20)
Resource Pool: Fixed 2 points (does not grow with level)
Equipment: Issued at start based on role
```

### 1.2 Companion Action Rules

- **In Combat**: Each round, the player may use one quick action to issue a command to one companion (e.g., "Attack that target," "Heal me," "Use the equipment"). The companion performs the action on its own initiative turn
- **Out of Combat**: A companion may perform its core skill checks (proposed by the player, adjudicated by the GM)
- **SAN/SP**: Companions have no SAN/SP system—they are not the subject of horror. But a PC who witnesses a companion's death/transformation must still make a normal SAN Check
- **Near-Death**: Companion HP=0 → immediately collapses; stabilization requires first aid within 3 rounds (d100 ≤ Medicine), otherwise dies
- **Permanent Loss**: A fallen companion cannot be revived. A new companion must be obtained through "Recruitment" at the Site (see §1.4)

### 1.3 Recommended Companion Combinations

| PC Role | Recommended Companion A | Recommended Companion B | Coverage Gap |
|---------|-------------------------|-------------------------|--------------|
| Mobile Task Force Operative | Researcher (Anomalistics + Cryptography) | Medical Specialist (Battle Triage) | Knowledge + Healing |
| Field Agent | MTF (Firearms + Melee) | Technical Specialist (Engineering + Computers) | Combat + Technical |
| Researcher | Security Agent (Melee + Observation) | Medical Specialist (Treatment) | Combat + Protection |
| D-Class Personnel | Field Agent (Stealth + Deception) | Technical Specialist (Scrap Repair) | Infiltration + Tools |
| Technical Specialist | Security Agent (Protection) | Field Agent (Observation) | Security + Intelligence |
| Medical Specialist | MTF (Firepower) | Researcher (Analysis) | Combat + Knowledge |
| Security Agent | Researcher (Weakness Discovery) | Medical Specialist (First Aid) | Knowledge + Healing |

### 1.4 Companion Recruitment & Replacement

After returning to the Site, the PC may attempt one `d100 ≤ PRE + Leadership` check (Standard difficulty ±0):

- Success → Gain a new companion (chosen from existing Site personnel)
- Failure → Wait and retry after the next mission
- Catastrophic (00) → The Site Director refuses—"The people you took out last time didn't come back."

## II. Encounter Difficulty Adjustment

### 2.1 Enemy Count Scaling

The enemy count in the original scenario is adjusted by the following formula:

| Original Design (4 Players) | Solo Mode | Notes |
|-----------------------------|-----------|-------|
| 1 Elite Enemy | 1 (Attributes −10, HP −20%) | Concede but do not weaken to the point of no threat |
| 2–3 Standard Enemies | 1 (unchanged) | Companion can handle the other target |
| 4+ Mooks | 1–2 (unchanged) + appear in waves | Will not be overwhelmed |
| Boss | Attributes −5, HP −30%, but retains all Special Abilities | Defeatable but still stressful |

### 2.2 SCP Difficulty Adjustment

- **Safe Class**: No adjustment. Solo + 1 companion should be able to handle it.
- **Euclid Class**: Cognitohazard SAN Damage −1 tier (Severe → Moderate); physical SCP HP −20%.
- **Keter Class**: Containment Success Points requirement reduced from 4 to 3; the GM provides more environmental utilization options.
- **Unbeatable SCP**: Difficulty not adjusted—the core of this type is survival and containment rather than combat.

### 2.3 Reward Adjustment

In Solo Mode, all CR/SA/XP rewards ×**1.5** (due to concentrated risk). The PC is indivisible.

## III. Rule Modifications

### 3.1 Action Economy

Original rule (§3.1): `1 Standard + 1 Move + 1 Quick + 1 Reaction`

Solo Mode: The PC retains the original action quota. Each companion gains `1 Standard + 1 Move` on its own turn (no quick, no reaction). In non-combat scenes, companions have no action quota limit—they are reasonable assistants.

### 3.2 Squad Command (§3.5 Modified)

The original rule requires "the holder of command authority uses a Standard action to issue commands." In Solo Mode:

- The PC may use a **Quick Action** to issue a single command to one companion ("Shoot that," "Heal me," "Activate the equipment")
- The companion executes at the start of the next round
- No command authority needed—the PC is the squad leader
- Of the original squad commands, only "Focus Fire" and "Retreat" are retained; the rest do not apply

### 3.3 Assist Rules (§1.4 Modified)

Original rule: The helper succeeds on d100 ≤ own TN → the actor +10 TN (max +20).

Solo Mode:

- Companion core skills can provide assistance (+10 TN)
- Companion weaknesses cannot assist
- The GM may play companions to provide intel—but the GM should not make decisions for the player

### 3.4 Morale System (§3.5 Modified)

Original rule: Panic spread is based on the squad's presence.

Solo Mode:

- Remove "Panic Spread" (since there is no squad to spread to)
- **Morale = 40 + PC's WIL/2** (no longer uses the leader value; the PC is the reference point)
- Companion morale is calculated independently (using NPC morale presets: Civilian 30 / Security 45 / MTF 55)
- When a companion panics → the PC witnesses a teammate's breakdown (SAN −1d4)

### 3.5 SAN/SP Recovery (§1.5 Modified)

The original rule has several recovery methods that depend on teammates.

Solo Mode modifications:

| Original Rule | Solo Modification |
|---------------|-------------------|
| Teammate "Soothe" (PRE+Leadership) −2d4 SP | Companion can soothe the PC (using its own PRE/2 as TN, success −1d4 SP) |
| Psychological Counseling −1d6 SAN/session | Retained; the PC has priority booking rights |
| Complete a major containment mission −1d8 SAN | **Solo Bonus**: −2d6 SAN (the reward for bearing all risk alone) |

**New: Solo Recovery Within the Site**
A PC spending 1 hour alone in a safe zone of the Site (reading, organizing gear, writing reports) → SP −1d4. Limited to 1 use per mission.

## IV. GM Guide Supplement

### 4.1 Pacing Control

A solo game's pacing is 2–3 times faster than a squad game—no inter-player discussion, disagreement, or strategy meetings. The GM should:

- Reduce lengthy descriptions; quickly move to decision points
- Prepare 3–5 direct choices per scene (rather than open-ended exploration)
- Use companions to provide hints ("Sir, I notice anomalous readings on the left side of the corridor")
- Advance the plot immediately after each die roll—do not wait

### 4.2 Tension Management

The horror in a solo game is more concentrated—because there are no teammates to share it. The GM should:

- Keep SAN/SP tracking visible (let the player see stress accumulate)
- Amplify environmental details when the PC is alone (footsteps, strange noises from ventilation ducts, flickering lights)
- A companion's presence must not diminish the horror—they are equally fragile and equally mortal
- At key decisions, companions should not provide the "correct answer"—their advice may contradict each other

### 4.3 "Lone Wolf" Trait (Optional Rule)

When creating a character, the PC may choose to gain the **"Lone Wolf" Trait**:

```
Lone Wolf: When you act without teammates, you gain the following effects:
· When acting solo, all checks gain ADV (d100 take lower)
· When a companion falls, you gain the "Adrenaline" status—for the next 2 rounds attacks gain ADV + damage +1 die
· Cost: Relationship value with the department −2 (you are difficult to work with)
· Cost: SAN recovery halved (you are not good at accepting help)
```

## V. Quick Start

### 5.1 Create a Solo Character

1. Create a standard PC per original rules §2.1–§2.8
2. Choose 1–2 companions (see §1.3 Recommended Combinations)
3. Fill in using the companion streamlined template (30 seconds each)
4. Choose whether to have the "Lone Wolf" Trait

### 5.2 Choose a Scenario

Among the original four campaign scenarios, the following scenes are adapted for Solo Mode (see Solo Scenario Supplement):

- **Recommended Start**: "Lone Wolf Protocol"—a 3-scene intro module designed specifically for solo play
- Series B "Crimson Dossier" is best suited for solo investigative play
- Series A "Daybreak Protocol" scenes A-1, A-2 are adapted
- In Series D "The Cost," the D-Class PC path is naturally suited for solo
