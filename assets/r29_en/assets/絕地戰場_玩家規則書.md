# Chapter One: Game Overview

## 1-1 What Kind of Game Is This?

**Last Stand TRPG** is a tabletop role-playing game (TRPG) themed around near-future battle-royale competition. You play a Contestant dropped onto a sealed battlefield. No magic, no superpowers—only the gun you scavenged, the armor you looted, and that one extra second of composure over your opponent.

One hundred people airdrop onto a deserted island; only the last one standing (or the last squad) walks away with glory and prize money.

## 1-2 The Core Loop

```
Drop-point Selection → Loot Scavenging → Combat Encounter → Zone Contraction & Relocation → Final Showdown
```

Every match is a unique survival adventure. The map, the Safe Zone (the Circle), the weather, and enemy squads are all generated by the Game Master (GM) before the game begins—unknowable to the players.

## 1-3 Player Count and Modes

| Mode | Players | GMs | Description |
|------|:---:|:---:|------|
| **Solo Mode** | 1 | 1 | One player plays a Lone Wolf Contestant; the GM controls all other enemy Contestants |
| **Squad Mode** | 2–4 | 1 | Players form a squad and cooperate; the GM controls enemy squads |

**This rulebook covers the complete rules for both modes.**

# Chapter Two: Core Rules

## 2-1 Core Resolution: the d20 System

Nearly every uncertain action in the game is resolved by the following formula:

```
d20 + Attribute Modifier (AM) + Proficiency Bonus (PB) ≥ Difficulty Class (DC)
```

- **d20**: A twenty-sided die, rolling a random result from 1–20
- **Attribute Modifier (AM)**: The modifier of the attribute relevant to your action (see 2-2)
- **Proficiency Bonus (PB)**: If you are proficient in the relevant skill, add this bonus (see 2-4)
- **Difficulty Class (DC)**: The target number set by the GM, determined by the situation

### Special Roll Results

| Result | Effect |
|------|------|
| **Natural 1** (d20 shows 1) | Critical failure—automatic failure regardless of modifiers. When firing, may jam |
| **Natural 20** (d20 shows 20) | Automatic hit. When firing, triggers a "Precision Hit"—damage die takes maximum value |
| **Critical Success** (final result ≥ DC+5) | Effect exceeds expectations—the GM may grant an extra reward (e.g., bullet penetration, dropping two targets with one shot) |

### Advantage and Disadvantage

| State | Rule |
|------|------|
| **Advantage** | Roll two d20s, take the higher result |
| **Disadvantage** | Roll two d20s, take the lower result |

Advantage and Disadvantage do not stack—if you have both Advantage and Disadvantage, they cancel each other out and you roll a single d20 normally.

### Contest Check

When you directly oppose another person (e.g., you are hidden while they try to spot you), each side rolls **d20 + Attribute Modifier + Proficiency Bonus**, and **the higher result wins**. On a tie, the status quo is maintained (no change).

Example: You attempt to hide (d20 + TACmod + PB), the enemy attempts to spot you (d20 + TACmod + PB). Your result 18 > their 14—you successfully hide.

### Cooperative Check

When two or more characters cooperate on the same task:
- The **Lead** rolls the check (as usual: d20 + Attribute Modifier + PB)
- The **Helper** makes a relevant attribute check at DC 10—on success, grants the Lead **Advantage**
- Any number of helpers is allowed, but at most 1 Advantage is granted (no stacking)

Example: While you pick a lock, a teammate keeps watch. You roll d20 + DRVmod + PB as the Lead; the teammate makes a Perception check (DC 10). On success → your lockpicking gains Advantage.

## 2-2 The Five Core Attributes

| Attribute | Eng. Abbr. | Meaning | Affects |
|------|:---:|------|------|
| **Aim (AIM)** | AIM | Ranged weapon accuracy, ballistic control | Hits with all firearms and crossbows |

## 3-4 Step Three: Allocate Attributes

You have **27 attribute points**, allocated to the five core attributes using the following **point-buy method**.

### Point-Buy Cost Table

Each attribute starts at **8** (cost 0 points). The point cost to raise an attribute score is as follows:

| Score | Cost | Score | Cost |
|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| 8 | 0 | 12 | 4 |
| 9 | 1 | 13 | 5 |
| 10 | 2 | 14 | 7 |
| 11 | 3 | 15 | 9 |

**Rules:**
- Each attribute may be as low as 8 (cost 0); you are not forced to spend points
- Total point-buy cost must not exceed **27 points**
- Add the background's attribute bonuses only after point-buying is complete
- Background bonuses may raise an attribute above 15 (up to 22)
- Example: To reach Aim (AIM) 14 costs 7 points; to reach 15 costs 9 points

### Quick Allocation: Standard Array

If you don't want to calculate point by point, you may directly use this standard array allocated across the five attributes:

```
15, 14, 13, 12, 10
```

Place these values into your five attributes (order is your choice), then add your background bonuses.

# Chapter Four: Equipment Details

Last Stand TRPG's equipment system is vast—from pistols to airdropped sniper rifles, from helmets to Lv.3 armor, from scopes to vehicles, dozens of items together form the tactical choices. The **complete stat tables** for these items (damage, range, magazine, weight, rarity, price, special attributes, attachment effects, consumable effects, ammunition caliber, vehicle performance) are collected in a separate compendium.

▶ **For complete equipment data, see `assets/物品圖鑑.md`** (structured data also in `物品圖鑑.yaml`): covering weapons, armor, attachments, consumables, ammunition, and vehicles. This book keeps only the core mechanic explanations that affect rule resolution.

## 4-1 Equipment and Resolution Overview

- **Hit DC**: A weapon's effective range, and attachments such as scopes/muzzles/grips/stocks, directly modify the Hit DC (see Chapter Five, Hit Resolution).
- **Damage Formula**: `Final Damage = (Weapon Damage Die + Proficiency Damage Bonus) × Hit-Location Multiplier − effective Damage Reduction (DR)`.
- **Armor Penetration**: Your level reduces the enemy's effective DR (Penetration = ⌊Level ÷ 2⌋).
- **Attachment Slots**: Most firearms can equip scopes, muzzles, magazines, grips, stocks, etc.; see the compendium for specific effects and compatible weapons.

## 4-2 Armor Durability Rules

- Each hit on the corresponding body part: armor durability -1
- Hit by a penetrating attack (e.g., Desert Eagle, AKM): durability -2
- Armor durability ≤ 50%: DR -1
- Armor durability ≤ 25%: DR halved (round down)
- Armor durability reaches zero: broken, cannot be repaired, must be replaced

## 4-3 Ammunition Rules

- **Same caliber is interchangeable**: Weapons using the same caliber share ammunition (e.g., P92 and MP5K share 9mm)
- **Magazines are independent**: Each magazine is a separate item. Swapping a magazine costs 1 Tactical Action
- **Combine partial mags**: During a short rest, bullets from multiple half-empty magazines may be merged into full magazines
- **Starting ammunition**: At character creation, each starting weapon comes with 2 full magazines. Extra ammunition may be purchased (30 rounds price = base caliber price ×1)

## 4-4 Starting Gear and Purchase

At character creation you receive base gear by background (usually a pistol + Lv.1 helmet/armor + some bandages), and have a sum of starting funds to buy extra supplies. For each item's price, weight, and full attributes, see `assets/物品圖鑑.md`.


## 5-1 Combat Round Structure

Each round is about 6 seconds. The combat flow is:

```
① Encounter Phase → ② Initiative Roll → ③ Action Phase → ④ Reaction Phase → ⑤ Round Settlement
```

### What You Can Do Each Round (choose one of three)

| Action Config | Move | Fire | Tactical Action | Best For |
|------|:---:|:---:|:---:|------|
| **Tactical Shot** | Half-speed move (15m) | ✓ | 1 | Normal firefight |
| **Aimed Shot** | No move | Aim then fire (+3) | 1 | Needs precise hit |
| **Suppressing Shot** | No move | Burst ×2 | None | LMG suppression |

### Tactical Action List

| Action | Description |
|------|------|
| Reload | Swap weapon magazine (LMG takes a full round) |
| Switch Weapon | Switch between primary/secondary weapon (pistol switch is a Free Action) |
| Change Cover | Move to another cover within 10m |
| Use Consumable | Use bandage, energy drink, etc. (first-aid kit takes 2 rounds) |
| Revive Ally | Touch a Downed teammate, takes a full round |
| Throw Item | Throw frag grenade, smoke grenade, etc. |
| Go Prone / Crouch | Change posture, affects DC to be hit |

### Other Action Types

Beyond the main action configuration each round, you also have the following actions:

| Action Type | Usage Limit | Description |
|------|:---:|------|
| **Free Action** | Unlimited | Does not consume the main action—speaking, gestures, dropping an item, switching between pistol and primary weapon |
| **Bonus Action** | 1 per round | A special quick action allowed by certain feats or abilities (e.g., "Quick-Draw Pistol" drawing and firing, "Squad Command" commanding a teammate). If you have no ability granting a Bonus Action, you have none available this round |
| **Reaction** | 1 per round | Triggers on another's turn—e.g., emergency dodge (avoid a bullet), suppressive counter. After use, must wait until the start of your next round to use again |

## 5-2 Hit Resolution

### Steps

1. **Declare target**: Who are you aiming at? With what weapon? What firing mode?
2. **GM confirms DC**: Determine base DC based on distance and target state
3. **Apply modifiers**: Scope, own movement, target cover, burst fire, etc.
4. **Roll hit die**: `d20 + Aim modifier + weapon modifier` vs DC
5. **Resolve result**: Hit → roll hit location and damage; miss → stray bullet may hit a nearby target (5% chance)

### Base Hit DC

| DC | Condition | Example |
|:---:|------|------|
| 8 | Stationary target, within 50m | Enemy currently scavenging |
| 10 | Slow-moving, within 50m | Crouching enemy |
| 12 | Moving target, 50–150m | Standard combat movement |
| 14 | Moving + partial cover | Peeking from behind cover to fire |
| 16 | Fast-moving, 150–300m | Running or inside a vehicle |
| 18 | Fast-moving + cover | Peeking from inside a vehicle |
| 20 | Extreme sniper (300m+) | Ultimate sniper trial |

### DC Modifier Overview

| Modifier Source | DC Change |
|------|:---:|
| Red Dot / Holographic (within 50m) | -1 |
| 2x Scope (within 100m) | -1 |
| 4x Scope (within 150m) | -2 |
| 8x Scope (within 300m) | -3 |
| Suppressor | Gunshot distance halved |
| Self moving | +2 |
| Target half cover | +3 |
| Target head only exposed | +5 |
| Each extra round of Burst Fire | +1 |
| Aim one round (sniper) | -3 |
| Target prone / crawling | +5 |
| Self crouching / prone | -1 (more stable firing platform) |

## 5-3 Damage Calculation

### Base Formula

```
Final Damage = (Weapon Damage Die + Proficiency Damage Bonus) × Hit-Location Multiplier − effective Damage Reduction (DR)
```

### Proficiency Damage Bonus

When using a **proficient weapon class**, add your **Proficiency Bonus (PB)** to the weapon damage die. This represents how, with experience, you better hit enemy weak points.

| Level | Proficiency Bonus (PB) | Damage Bonus |
|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| 1–4 | +2 | +2 |
| 5–6 | +3 | +3 |
| 7–8 | +3 | +3 |
| 9–10 | +4 | +4 |

### Armor Penetration

Your combat experience lets you better bypass armor protection. Against enemies, your level reduces their effective DR:

```
Effective DR = Enemy's original DR − ⌊Your Level ÷ 2⌋ (minimum 0)
```

| Level | Armor Penetration | Effect |
|:---:|:---:|------|
| 1–2 | 1 | DR -1 |
| 3–4 | 2 | DR -2 |
| 5–6 | 3 | DR -3 |
| 7–8 | 4 | DR -4 |
| 9–10 | 5 | DR -5 (Lv.3 armor fully penetrated) |

### Hit Location (roll d20)

| d20 Result | Location | Damage Multiplier |
|:---:|------|:---:|
| 1–8 | Torso | ×1.0 |
| 9–14 | Limbs | ×0.5 (leg = Slowed) |
| 15–18 | Head | ×2.0 (×1.5 with helmet) |
| 19–20 | Vital | ×2.5 (helmet cannot defend) |

### Armor DR Application

- Minimum final damage is 1 point (a hit always deals at least 1 damage)
- Armor DR applies only to the armor of the hit location
- Apply hit-location multiplier first, then subtract armor DR

### Example Calculation

> Lv3 character (PB +2, Armor Penetration 2) hits an enemy wearing Lv.2 Armor (Torso DR 3) with an AKM (1d12).
> Weapon die = 8 + PB(+2) = 10; effective DR = 3 - 2 = 1; torso multiplier = ×1.0
> Final Damage = 10 × 1.0 - 1 = **9 HP damage**

> Lv8 character (PB +3, Armor Penetration 4) headshots an enemy in Lv.3 Helmet (Head DR 3) with an AWM (2d12).
> Weapon die = 9+7+3(PB) = 19; effective DR = 3 - 4 = 0 (penetrated!); helmet multiplier = ×1.5
> Final Damage = 19 × 1.5 - 0 = **28 HP damage** (instant kill—headshot with no armor protection)

## 5-4 Downed and Revival

### Multiplayer Mode (Squad)

| Situation | Rule |
|------|------|
| **HP reaches zero** | Enter Downed state—cannot move or attack |
| **While Downed** | Each round make a Calm check (DC 12) to stay conscious. Failure → unconscious (cannot be revived, dies directly) |
| **Countdown** | 3 rounds. After it reaches zero, the character dies |
| **Teammate revive** | Requires contact, costs a full round. While reviving, reviver is automatically hit on a hit |
| **Revive success** | Downed character restored to HP = 1, may act immediately |
| **HP zero with no teammate** | Dies directly (no Downed phase) |

### Solo Mode

| Situation | Rule |
|------|------|
| **HP reaches zero** | Self-Revive Syringe auto-triggers (if not yet used) → restores 1 HP |
| **Self-Revive Syringe already used** | Dies directly |
| **After self-revive** | Enters "Weakened" state—half speed, attack Disadvantage, lasts until short rest |

## 5-5 Cover Rules

| Cover Situation | DC to be Hit |
|------|:---:|
| No cover (standing still) | +0 |
| Half cover (peeking from behind wall) | +3 |
| Head only exposed (sniper hole / car window) | +5 |
| Full cover (fully behind wall) | Cannot be aimed at |
| In smoke | Attack at Disadvantage |

# Chapter Seven: Zone Contraction and Stress System

## 7-1 Safe Zone (the Circle) Structure

The core mechanic of the battle royale—the Safe Zone shrinks over time, forcing all Contestants closer together.

```
First Circle (game start)  →  Safe Zone: 2 km diameter
        │ Contraction countdown: 5 rounds
        ▼
Second Circle             →  Safe Zone: 1.2 km diameter
        │ Contraction countdown: 4 rounds
        ▼
Third Circle              →  Safe Zone: 600 m diameter
        │ Contraction countdown: 3 rounds
        ▼
Fourth Circle             →  Safe Zone: 200 m diameter
        │ Contraction countdown: 2 rounds
        ▼
Fifth Circle              →  Safe Zone: 50 m diameter
        │ Contraction countdown: 1 round
        ▼
Final Circle              →  Safe Zone disappears—entire map is danger zone
```

## 6-2 Out-of-Zone Damage (Electric Field Damage)

Out-of-Zone Damage **ignores armor DR** and deducts HP directly.

| Zone Layer | Damage per Round |
|:---:|:---:|
| Outside First Circle | 1d4 |
| Outside Second Circle | 2d4 |
| Outside Third Circle | 3d4 |
| Outside Fourth Circle | 4d4 |
| Outside Fifth Circle | 5d4 |
| Final Circle (no Safe Zone) | 6d4/round |

> ⚠️ **Survival Tip**: The 1d4 outside the first circle doesn't look like much, but if you stay outside for 3 rounds you'll average 7–8 HP lost. Don't overcommit to a fight—when it's time to run, run.

## 6-3 Stress System

Stress is the soul mechanic of this game. It's not just a number—it actually affects your dice results.

### Stress Accumulation

| Stress Source | Stress Change |
|------|:---:|
| Encounter enemy squad (each time) | +1 |
| Teammate downed | +2 |
| Contraction countdown (each round outside the zone) | +1 |
| Enter top 10 | +3 + PB (one-time) |
| Enter top 5 | +2 + PB (one-time) |
| Enter final circle | +3 + PB (one-time) |
| Acting alone (no teammate within 50m, per round) | +1 |

> Proficiency Bonus (PB) grows with level (Lv1-4=+2, Lv5-6=+3, Lv7-8=+3, Lv9-10=+4). Higher-level characters face greater stress—because they understand the cost of failure better.

### Stress Recovery

| Recovery Method | Stress Change |
|------|:---:|
| Successfully kill an enemy target | -2 |
| Use energy drink | -1 |
| Teammate by your side (within 10m) for a full round | -1 |
| Enter Safe Zone | -3 |
| Successfully revive a Downed teammate | -5 |
| Use sedative | -5 |

### Stress Stage Effects

| Stage Name | Stress Value Range | Game Effect |
|------|:---:|------|
| **Calm** | < 50% Stress Threshold | No negative effects |
| **Tense** | 50–74% Stress Threshold | Disadvantage on Aim checks |
| **Panic** | 75–99% Stress Threshold | Disadvantage on Aim + Tactics checks, may misjudge enemy position |
| **Breakdown** | ≥ 100% Stress Threshold | Each round roll d6 for a random action:<br>1-2: Freeze in place (cannot act)<br>3-4: Fire wildly, exposing position<br>5-6: Flee in a random direction |

> **Stress Threshold = Calm (CLM) × 2 + 5**
>
> Example: A character with CLM 14 has Stress Threshold = 14 × 2 + 5 = 33
> - Calm stage: stress 0–16
> - Tense stage: stress 17–24
> - Panic stage: stress 25–32
> - Breakdown stage: stress ≥ 33

# Chapter Ten: Rest and Recovery

## 9-1 Short Rest

A short rest is a brief respite lasting at least **5 rounds** (about 30 seconds). You must hide in a safe place (behind cover, inside a building), and may not take any combat actions during it.

**During a short rest you may:**
- Use one first-aid kit or medical item
- Recover 1d6 + Physique modifier HP (natural recovery, 1 per encounter)
- Clear the "Fatigued" state
- Stress -2 (calm down)
- Swap armor (pick up a Downed enemy's helmet/armor)

**Short rest limit:** At most 1 short rest between each encounter. If there is no short rest opportunity between two consecutive encounters, the second encounter starts with the "Fatigued" state.

## 9-2 Between-Games Recovery

After a game ends (win or lose), before the next game begins, the character fully recovers:

| Recovery Item | Effect |
|------|------|
| HP | Restore to full |
| Stress | Reset to zero |
| Consumables | Not restored (must repurchase with prize money) |
| Armor durability | May spend CR to repair (20 CR per durability point) or replace |
| Ammunition | Not restored (consumed ammo is not auto-refilled) |

## 9-3 Leveling and Rest

When you gain enough XP to level up in a game, the level-up effect takes effect **after the game ends** (not immediately). This means you cannot suddenly gain a new feat mid-combat.
