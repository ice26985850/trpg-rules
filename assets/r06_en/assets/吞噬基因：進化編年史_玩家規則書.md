## I. System Overview

### 1-1 What Is "Devour Gene: Chronicle of Evolution"?

"Devour Gene: Chronicle of Evolution" is a tabletop role-playing game (TRPG) whose core mechanic is "evolution through absorption." In this world, living beings possess the ability to absorb defeated enemies and acquire their traits. You are not "leveling up" — you are "becoming something entirely new."

### 1-2 Core Features

| Feature | Description |
|---|---|
| **No Class System** | No "warrior," "mage," or "thief." Your abilities come from evolution choices, not class labels. |
| **No Level System** | No "level 1 to level 20." Your strength depends on the quantity and quality of accumulated evolution nodes. |
| **No Fixed Skill Tree** | No preset skill paths. Every evolution tree is unique. |
| **Growth Through Absorption** | After defeating enemies, you may choose to absorb their traits, unlocking entirely new evolution directions. |
| **Identity** | Evolution has a cost — the more powerful the evolution, the further you drift from your initial form, and your Humanity Value gradually slips away. |

### 1-3 Player Count

This system is designed by default to support a **1 Player + 1 Game Master (GM)** solo mode, while also being fully compatible with a 2–5 player multiplayer mode. All mechanics are designed to ensure solo playability.

### 1-4 What You Need to Prepare?

| Item | Description |
|---|---|
| **This Rulebook** | The document you are currently reading |
| **Character Sheet** | A blank character sheet (printable or Excel version) |

| **Several d6 (six-sided dice)** | Damage dice |
| **d4, d8, d10, d12** | May be needed for evolution skill damage |
| **Pencil and Eraser** | For recording attribute changes and evolution nodes |

## II. Core Dice System

### 2-1 Basic Check

Devour Gene: Chronicle of Evolution uses "**d20 + Attribute Value vs Difficulty Level (DC)**" as its core resolution mechanic.

**Roll Formula:** d20 + Attribute Value ≥ Difficulty Level (DC) → Success

Your attribute value is used directly as a bonus (no need to calculate a derived modifier). For example: a character with Strength (LIL) of 6 making a Strength check rolls d20 + 6.

### 2-2 Difficulty Level Table

| Difficulty | DC Range | Description | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Trivial** | 5–9 | Almost impossible to fail | Running on flat ground, lifting a light object, remembering a familiar route |
| **Normal** | 10–14 | An ordinary person has about a 50% chance of success | Climbing a rope, persuading a neutral NPC, tracking obvious footprints |
| **Hard** | 15–19 | Requires some expertise or talent | Disarming a trap, tracking in a storm, negotiating a major deal |
| **Very Hard** | 20–24 | Tricky even for experts | Climbing a cliff face, breaking a magical seal, persuading a sworn enemy |
| **Epic** | 25–29 | A realm beyond ordinary humans | Catching an arrow mid-flight, walking on lava, persuading a king to abdicate |
| **Legendary** | 30+ | A near-mythical achievement | Tearing down a city wall bare-handed, conversing with deities, altering the laws of nature |

### 2-3 Critical Success and Critical Failure

| Roll Result | Name | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| **Natural 20** | Critical Success | Automatic success, plus an additional benefit (GM's call, e.g.: double damage, gain extra information, skip subsequent checks) |
| **Natural 1** | Critical Failure | Automatic failure, and may be accompanied by negative consequences (GM's call, e.g.: weapon drops, alarm raised, friendly fire) |

> **Note:** Critical Success and Critical Failure only look at the raw d20 roll result, not the total after adding the attribute bonus.

### 2-4 Advantage and Disadvantage

Certain situations grant you Advantage or Disadvantage:

| State | Rule | Example Conditions for Gaining |
|---|---|---|
| **Advantage** | Roll 2d20, take the higher | Attacking from above, target is blinded, having ample preparation time |
| **Disadvantage** | Roll 2d20, take the lower | Attacking in darkness, under fear, acting hastily |

**Advantage/Disadvantage Interaction Rules:**
- When you have both Advantage and Disadvantage simultaneously, they cancel each other out (regardless of quantity), reverting to a normal roll (1d20).
- Multiple sources of Advantage do not stack (still 2d20 take higher).
- Multiple sources of Disadvantage do not stack (still 2d20 take lower).

### 2-5 Contest Check

When two characters oppose each other (e.g., wrestling, stealth vs perception), each rolls d20 + their corresponding attribute value; the higher result wins. On a tie, the status quo is maintained.

### 2-6 Team Check

When a team needs to accomplish a task together (such as jointly pushing a boulder), designate one lead performer to make the check. Each assisting teammate whose relevant attribute ≥ 4 grants the lead performer a +2 bonus (maximum +6).

## III. Five Attributes

### 3-1 Attribute Overview

| Attribute Name | Abbrev. | Meaning | Main Influence |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Strength** | LIL | Physical attack power and carrying capacity | Melee damage, encumbrance limit, breaking obstacles |
| **Agility** | MJ | Speed and flexibility | Initiative, evasion, movement distance, precise manipulation |
| **Constitution** | TZ | Vitality and resistance | Max HP, poison resistance, fatigue recovery |
| **Perception** | GZ | Observation and reaction ability | Detecting traps, tracking, preemptive strike, identifying weaknesses |
| **Will** | YZ | Mental power and evolution control | Controlling evolution direction, resisting mental attacks, maintaining self-awareness |

### 3-2 Attribute Values and Derived Values

| Derived Value | Formula | Description |
|---|---|---|
| **HP (Hit Points)** | Constitution × 5 | Your total life. When HP drops to 0, the character enters Near Death. |
| **Max Energy** | Constitution + Will | Resource pool for using evolution skills. Recover 2 Energy per round. |
| **Evolution Capacity Limit** | 10 + Constitution + Will | Total number of evolution nodes your body can hold. Each node takes 1 capacity (the base 10 gives even offense-focused builds enough room). |
| **Initiative Value** | Agility + 1d20 | Determines action order in combat. |
| **Movement Speed** | See table below | Number of squares moved per Action Point spent. |

## IV. Base Forms

### 4-1 Form Overview

Your starting form determines your starting attributes, innate talents, and evolution direction. Choose a base form that best fits your character concept.

| Base Form | Strength | Agility | Constitution | Perception | Will | Total |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Human** | 3 | 3 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 18 |
| **Beast** | 5 | 4 | 4 | 3 | 2 | 18 |
| **Insect** | 1 | 5 | 2 | 5 | 3 | 16 |
| **Plant** | 2 | 1 | 6 | 3 | 4 | 16 |
| **Inorganic** | 4 | 1 | 7 | 2 | 3 | 17 |
| **Hatchling Dragon** | 6 | 3 | 5 | 4 | 4 | 22 |

### 4-2 Per-Form Talents

| Base Form | Talent Name | Talent Effect |
|---|---|---|
| **Human** | Adaptability | Cost of any evolution node −1 Evolution Point (minimum 1) |
| **Beast** | Wild Instinct | Gain Advantage on Perception (GZ) checks in combat |
| **Insect** | Multiple Organs | Can activate two Organ Node effects simultaneously (other forms can only activate one) |
| **Plant** | Photosynthesis | At the end of a turn while stationary and exposed to a light source, recover 1d4 HP |
| **Inorganic** | Painless | Immune to poison and pain-related status effects |
| **Hatchling Dragon** | Higher Origin | All evolution node effects +20% (rounded up), but evolution cost ×1.5 (rounded up) |

### 4-3 Form Descriptions

**Human**
The most balanced base form. No outstanding talent, but possesses the strongest adaptability — any evolution path is easier for Humans to achieve than for other forms. Human is also the only form that can maintain humanity relatively well while evolving.

**Beast**
Survives through keen intuition and a strong body. Beast-form characters have a great perceptual advantage in combat, able to strike first and see through weaknesses. But Will is weaker, making evolution control and resisting mental attacks more difficult.

**Insect**
Small in size but possessing astonishing perception and speed. The Insect form's greatest advantage is "Multiple Organs" — other forms can only activate one Organ Node at a time, but Insects can activate two simultaneously. This means Insect characters can combine ability setups impossible for other forms.

**Plant**
Seemingly fragile but actually the most vital. The Plant form has extremely high Constitution, with far more HP than other forms. Photosynthesis provides unique sustainability — resting in a safe zone allows quick recovery without consuming resources.

**Inorganic**
A life form made of stone, metal, or crystal. Possesses the highest Constitution and complete immunity to poison/pain. But Perception and Agility are extremely low, and movement is clumsy.

**Hatchling Dragon**
Highest starting attribute total (22), with overwhelming talent advantage. But the double-edged sword of "Higher Origin" — the ×1.5 evolution cost means more resources are needed to grow than other forms. The Hatchling Dragon suits players pursuing the ultimate final form.

## V. Character Creation

Creating your Evolver requires the following six steps:

### Step 1: Choose an Origin Form

Choose one of the six base forms. This determines your starting attribute values, talent abilities, and evolution starting point.

### Step 2: Determine Background

Why does your character possess evolution abilities? Choose or customize a background:

| Background | Starting Bonus | Description |
|---|---|---|
| **Natural Prodigy** | +1 starting Evolution Point | You were born with evolution abilities. This is your gift — or your curse. |
| **Test Subject** | Starting Perception (GZ) +1 | You are the product of some laboratory. Your evolution was artificially induced. |
| **Cursed One** | Starting Will (YZ) +1 | Your evolution stems from an ancient curse. You must evolve to survive — but the curse is devouring you too. |
| **Mutation by Contamination** | Starting Constitution (TZ) +1 | You were exposed to some contaminant — perhaps radiation, toxin, or magical residue. |
| **Chosen by the Divine** | Starting Humanity Value +10 | Some deity or higher being granted you evolution abilities. But you must fulfill Their mission. |
| **Atavist** | Starting Strength (LIL) +1 | Your ancestors were once mighty evolving creatures; the bloodline has awakened in you. |
| **Symbiote** | Starting Agility (MJ) +1 | You fused with another life form — it is your companion and the catalyst for your evolution. |
| **Orphan** | May additionally choose a "second Root Node" at start (choose two in Step 4) | You don't know where you came from. Your past is a blank — but your body remembers how to evolve. |
| **Scholar** | Starts with +2 Evolution Points, but starting Humanity Value −5 | You buried yourself in evolution research for years, growing distant from people. Knowledge gives you a faster start, at the cost of weaker ties to human society. |
| **Fugitive** | Starting Movement Speed +1 square | You escaped from somewhere. Your body has adapted to running. |

### Step 3: Attribute Allocation

On top of your base form attributes, you have **8 free points** to distribute. You may allocate points to any attribute. No single attribute may exceed 10 at character creation.

> **Example:** A player choosing the "Human" form (Strength 3, Agility 3, Constitution 3, Perception 4, Will 5) could allocate the 8 points as: Strength +2 → 5, Constitution +2 → 5, Perception +1 → 5, Will +3 → 8.

### Step 4: Choose Root Node

A Root Node is your first evolution — a mutation that occurred before your story began. Choose one from the Root Node list corresponding to your base form (one for a normal character; if the background is "Orphan," you may additionally choose a second Root Node):

**Human Root Nodes:**
| Root Node | Type | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Reinforced Bones | Attribute Node | Strength (LIL) +1, Constitution (TZ) +1 |
| Keen Intuition | Passive Node | Gain Advantage on Perception (GZ) checks outside of combat |
| Will Training | Passive Node | When Humanity Value decreases, reduce by 1 less each time (minimum 0) |

**Beast Root Nodes:**
| Root Node | Type | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Sharp Claws | Active Node | Melee attack deals 1d8 + Strength (LIL) damage, costs 1 Energy |
| Thick Hide | Passive Node | Physical damage taken −1 |
| Night Vision | Organ Node | No Disadvantage in darkness, increased vision range |

**Insect Root Nodes:**
| Root Node | Type | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Exoskeleton | Passive Node | HP +5 |
| Compound Eye Prototype | Organ Node | Gain Advantage on Perception (GZ) checks, but Disadvantage on Will (YZ) checks |
| Wing Buds | Passive Node | Movement Speed +1 square |

**Plant Root Nodes:**
| Root Node | Type | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Reinforced Root System | Passive Node | Recover 1 HP each round while stationary (stacks with Photosynthesis) |
| Thorns | Active Node | When attacked in melee, may use a reaction to deal 1d4 piercing damage |
| Vine Tentacle | Active Node | Grapple a target at medium range (3 squares), Agility (MJ) contest, costs 1 Energy |

**Inorganic Root Nodes:**
| Root Node | Type | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Metallization | Passive Node | Physical damage taken −2 |
| Shock Impact | Active Node | All enemies within melee range take 1d6 damage, costs 2 Energy |
| Magnetic Sense | Passive Node | Can sense nearby metal objects and metal structures |

**Hatchling Dragon Root Nodes:**
| Root Node | Type | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Dragon Scales | Passive Node | All damage resistance +1 |
| Dragon Breath Prototype | Active Node | Cone area (3 squares) deals 1d10 fire damage, costs 3 Energy, cooldown 2 rounds |
| Dragon Prestige | Passive Node | When low-tier enemies make contact, must make a Will (YZ) check (DC 12); on failure they become frightened |

### Step 5: Set Your Evolution Vision

Answer this question: **"What do you ultimately want to become?"**

Not a numerical goal, but a vision — what kind of being does your character want to be at the end of the evolution path? This affects how the GM designs the enemies you encounter and the absorbable traits.

> **Example:** "I want to become the ultimate adaptor who can survive in any environment — desert, deep sea, the void; no place can stop me."

### Step 6: Your Forbidden Evolution

Answer this question: **"There is one evolution direction you will never take — what is it, and why?"**

This defines your character's bottom line. In return, when you refuse an evolution related to this taboo, you gain 1 extra Evolution Point (once per scenario).

> **Example:** "I will never evolve into a spirit. I've seen those evolvers who lost their flesh — they became wandering ghosts who can't even maintain their self."

## VI. Evolution Tree System

### 6-1 Tree Structure

Every character possesses a dedicated evolution tree. The tree starts from the "Root Node," and a new node can be added each time conditions are met.

```
               [Venom Fang (Active)]
              ↗
[Sharp Fang (Passive)] → [Petrifying Fang (Active)]
              ↘
               [Vampiric Fang (Active)]

[Origin: Reinforced Bones] → [Reinforced Muscle (Attribute)] → [Steel Muscle (Passive)] → [Titan Body (Form)]
                      ↘
                  [Elastic Muscle (Passive)] → [Leaping Tendon (Organ)]
                                            ↘
                                        [Ejecting Tendon (Organ)]
```

### 6-2 Five Node Types

| Node Type | Description | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Attribute Node** | Permanently increases an attribute value | Reinforced Muscle → Strength (LIL) +2 |
| **Passive Node** | A passive ability in permanent effect | Regeneration → recover 2 HP at the start of each round |
| **Active Node** | A skill usable in combat | Poison Mist Spray → area poison attack, costs 3 Energy |
| **Organ Node** | Unlocks a new body part or organ, usually with multiple abilities | Compound Eye → doubles vision + Advantage on Perception (GZ) checks + can observe multiple targets simultaneously |
| **Form Node** | Changes the base form — the most significant evolution | Half-Dragon Form: overall attribute boost + gain dragon traits + Humanity Value drops sharply |

### 6-3 Evolution Requirements

Each node has unlock conditions that must all be satisfied:

| Condition Type | Description | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Prerequisite Node** | Must already possess a specific node | "Venom Fang" requires first possessing "Poison Gland" |
| **Attribute Threshold** | A certain attribute reaches a specific value | "Flight" requires Agility (MJ) 8+ |
| **Absorption Requirement** | Must first absorb a specific type of enemy | "Dragon Breath" requires having absorbed dragon-type creatures |
| **Environmental Trigger** | Triggered in a specific environment | "Heat-Resistant Skin" requires surviving 3 rounds in a volcanic zone |
| **Story Unlock** | Unlocked by the GM during the story | "Angel Form" requires completing a sacred quest |

### 6-4 Evolution Conflicts

Not all nodes can coexist:

| Conflict Rule | Description |
|---|---|
| **Type Conflict** | Within the same "element series," only one branch may be chosen. For example, choosing "Fire Breath" prevents choosing "Frost Breath" — unless a special node allows dual attributes. |
| **Form Conflict** | A Form Node resets old nodes that conflict with the new form. For example, after becoming an aquatic creature, the "Climbing Claws" node becomes invalid (the Evolution Points of that node are not refunded). |
| **Capacity Limit** | The total number of evolution nodes a character may possess cannot exceed the "Evolution Capacity Limit" (Constitution + Will). Once at the limit, you must first raise Constitution or Will to add new nodes. You may also choose to discard old nodes to free up space (discarded nodes' Evolution Points are not refunded). |

## VIII. Humanity Value System

### 8-1 What Is the Humanity Value?

The Humanity Value represents the degree of your connection to your initial form. Every major evolution causes you to drift away from your original self. The Humanity Value affects not only your appearance — but also how the world perceives you.

**Initial Humanity Value:** 100 (some backgrounds may increase or decrease this)

### 8-2 Humanity Value Reduction

Whenever you add an Evolution Node, your Humanity Value decreases based on the node type:

| Node Type | Humanity Value Lost |
|---|---|
| Attribute Node | 0 (purely strengthens existing traits, does not affect form) |
| Passive Node | -1 |
| Active Node | -1 |
| Organ Node | -4 |
| Form Node | -8 |

> **Humanity Value Deduction Adjustment (v1.1 balance fix):** Organ Nodes were adjusted from -3 to -4, and Form Nodes from -5 to -8, so that the "Abomination (10-40%)" and "Monster (<10%)" stages are reachable in normal play, making the corresponding NPC reactions and trade penalties meaningful content. For example, a 20-node character (roughly 5 Attribute + 5 Passive + 5 Active + 3 Organ + 2 Form) ≈ -29 → Humanity Value 71 (Humanoid); but if they choose more organ/form paths (e.g., 4 Organ + 4 Form), it can drop to 100-(16+32)=52 (Hybrid), or even lower.

### 8-3 Humanity Value Stages

| Humanity Value Range | Stage Name | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| **> 70%** | Humanoid | Appearance is basically normal. Can socialize normally and use all human items and equipment. NPCs will not react abnormally to your appearance |
| **40-70%** | Hybrid | Appearance begins to show obvious mutations (scales, antennae, discolored skin, etc.). Social checks suffer Disadvantage. Some NPCs will display distrust or curiosity. Some human-exclusive equipment cannot be used |
| **10-40%** | Abomination | Appearance has greatly deviated from the initial form. Most human NPCs will feel fear or hostility upon seeing you. Most human settlements refuse you entry. Cannot use human-exclusive equipment. But you gain Advantage on "Intimidation" checks |
| **< 10%** | Monster | You are almost unacceptable to human society. But you are also freed from the constraints of human society — no longer bound by laws, morals, or social norms. All social checks automatically fail (against humans), but all Intimidation checks automatically succeed (against humans) |

### 8-4 Humanity Value Recovery

The Humanity Value can be recovered through the following methods (but cannot exceed 100):

| Method | Recovery Amount | Description |
|---|---|---|
| **Shapeshift Disguise** | Depends on node | Gaining the "Color-Changing Skin" or "Disguise Skin" node can make you visually more human-like (does not restore actual Humanity Value, but offsets social Disadvantage) |
| **Therapeutic Rest** | +2 per week | With long-term rest without performing any evolution, the body gradually adapts to its current mutations |
| **Humanity Ritual** | 1d10 | Certain special story events (such as reuniting with loved ones or completing a Humanity trial) may trigger Humanity Value recovery |
| **Abandoning Evolution Node** | +3/node | Voluntarily discarding an Evolution Node can restore Humanity Value (but Evolution Points are not refunded) |

### 8-5 Humanity Value Check

When you face extreme situations that may shake your self-identity (such as devouring your own kind, witnessing mass death, or making inhuman choices), the GM may require you to make a Humanity Value Check:

**Humanity Value Check = d20 + Will (YZ) vs DC (set by GM based on the situation, usually 12-18)**

- Success: Maintain self-identity
- Failure: Temporarily lose self-control (1d4 rounds), during which your actions are described by the GM based on "your darkest impulses"
- Critical Failure: Permanently lose 1d4 Humanity Value

## IX. Combat Rules (Player Perspective)

### 9-1 Action Point System

Each round you have **3 Action Points (AP)**. You can freely combine the following actions:

| Action | Action Point Cost | Description |
|---|---|---|
| **Basic Attack** | 1 | Use a body part (fists, claws, etc.) or a weapon to perform a basic attack |
| **Use Evolution Skill** | 1-3 | Use an Active Node skill from the Evolution Tree; cost depends on skill level |
| **Move** | 1 | Move up to a number of squares equal to your movement speed |
| **Defend** | 1 | All damage taken this round is halved (rounded down) |
| **Observe (Identify Weakness)** | 1 | Make a Perception (GZ) check (DC per target); upon success, your next attack against that target this round gains Advantage |
| **Absorption Attempt** | 2 | Attempt to absorb a Near Death or Paralyzed enemy |
| **Forced Evolution** | 3 | Temporarily activate an unlocked Evolution Node in combat (requires rest before it can be used again after use) |
| **Use Item** | 1 | Use a consumable from your backpack (potions, catalysts, etc.) |
| **Standby** | 0 | Take no action, retaining remaining Action Points (Action Points cannot be carried over between rounds; unused ones disappear) |

### 9-2 Energy System

Energy is the resource used to activate Evolution Skills. It is not as limited as spell slots — you can continuously recover it during combat.

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| **Energy Maximum** | Constitution (TZ) + Will (YZ) |
| **Recovery per Round** | 2 Energy |
| **At Combat Start** | Energy is full |

**Skill Energy Cost:**

| Skill Level | Energy Cost | Cooldown |
|---|---|---|
| Low-tier | 1-2 | No cooldown (or 1 round) |
| Mid-tier | 3 | 1-2 rounds |
| High-tier | 5 | 3 rounds |
| Ultimate | All remaining Energy (min 5) | Once per entire battle only |

### 9-3 Attack Roll

**Melee Attack:** d20 + Strength (LIL) vs target's Defense Value
**Ranged Attack:** d20 + Agility (MJ) vs target's Defense Value

### 9-4 Defense Value Calculation

**Player Base Defense Value = 10 + Agility (MJ)**

Some Passive Nodes or equipment may increase the Defense Value.

**Monster Defense Value (GM-controlled):** Monsters do not use the player's fixed formula, but are set according to the "suggested tier" of their scenario, ensuring hit rates at different stages remain within a reasonable range (see the "Tier Defense and Attack Table" in Section IX of the GM Rulebook). General principle: Minion 14-22, Elite 16-24, Boss 19-27, Ultimate Boss 28-32, scaling up with the player's tier. The GM may also dynamically fine-tune during combat (see GM Rulebook 15-4).

### 9-5 Damage Calculation

| Attack Type | Base Damage |
|---|---|
| Unarmed Melee (fists/claws/teeth) | 1d6 + Strength (LIL) |
| Light Weapon (dagger, short sword) | 1d6 + Strength (LIL) or Agility (MJ) (whichever is higher) |
| Medium Weapon (longsword, axe) | 1d8 + Strength (LIL) |
| Heavy Weapon (greatsword, war hammer) | 1d10 + Strength (LIL) (Strength requirement 6+) |
| Simple Ranged (slingshot, darts) | 1d4 + Agility (MJ) |
| Standard Ranged (bow, crossbow) | 1d8 + Agility (MJ) |
| Evolution Skill | See each skill description (Low/Mid-tier + corresponding attribute; High/Ultimate-tier + corresponding attribute ×2) |

### 9-6 Damage Types

| Damage Type | Description | Common Resistance Source |
|---|---|---|
| **Piercing** | Damage caused by sharp objects (teeth, arrows, spikes) | Hardened skin, carapace |
| **Bludgeoning** | Damage caused by heavy impacts (fists, hammers, falls) | Steel skin, reinforced bones |
| **Slashing** | Damage caused by cutting (claws, blades) | Hardened skin, carapace |
| **Fire** | High-temperature burns | Heat resistance, dragon scales |
| **Frost** | Low-temperature frostbite | Cold resistance |
| **Lightning** | Electric shock damage | Special organs, grounding ability |
| **Poison** | Venom or toxic gas | Poison resistance, inorganic form |
| **Acid** | Corrosive damage | Special skin |
| **Mental** | Direct attack on the mind | Psionic shield, iron will |
| **Pure** | Damage that cannot be reduced by any resistance | None |

### 9-7 Spatial Combat and Area Rules

This rule uses a square-grid map for combat. A normal character occupies 1 square (Gigantic Form occupies 2×2 squares). All skills marked with "Cone / Circle / Radius / Line / Burst" determine their coverage area according to the following rules.

**Distance Classification:**

| Distance | Squares | Description |
|---|---|---|
| Melee | 1 square | Can attack from adjacent square |
| Mid-range | 2-3 squares | Short-range projectiles, breath attacks |
| Long-range | 4-6 squares | Bows, crossbows, mental attacks |
| Extreme-range | 7-10 squares | Long-range sniping, gigantic breath attacks |

**Area Templates (geometric shapes marked for skill range):**

- **Cone N squares**: A fan-shaped area extending N squares from the direction you face (approximately 60°). All squares within the cone are affected. Example: Flame Breath "Cone 3 squares" affects the fan-shaped area within 3 squares in front of you.
- **Circle N squares / Area Circle N squares**: A circle centered on a designated square (usually you or a specified center point) with radius N squares. Affects all targets within the circle.
- **Radius N squares**: Synonymous with Circle, centered on yourself with radius N squares (e.g., Earthquake Slam "self-centered, radius 5 squares").
- **Line**: A strip extending in a straight line from you (1 square wide), with length per skill description (e.g., Flame Stone Golem "in a line forward").
- **Burst**: Instantly spreads outward one ring from the center point (a circle of radius 1 square), usually used for traps or contact explosions.

**Opportunity Attack:** When an enemy is within your melee range (1 square) and it voluntarily leaves your adjacent square (without using an evasion action such as "Defend" or "Sprint"), you may immediately make a normal melee attack against it. This attack does not consume your Action Points, limited to 1 per round. If you are in the "Defend" state, no Opportunity Attack is triggered against you.

**Difficult Terrain:** Terrain such as swamps, rubble, deep snow, and steep slopes requires 2 squares of movement per 1 square moved (i.e., movement speed halved). Moving through difficult terrain does not consume extra Action Points.

**Passing Through Squares:** You may freely pass through squares occupied by friendly characters (but may not end movement on a friendly square). Passing through a square occupied by an enemy requires passing an Agility (MJ) check DC 14; on failure, you stop in place and take 1d4 damage. Squares occupied by a "Gigantic"-sized character cannot be passed through.

**Stealth and Detection:** A character in the "Hidden" state requires enemies to pass a Perception (GZ) check contested against their Agility (MJ) to detect them; while undetected, that enemy's attacks against the Hidden character suffer Disadvantage, while the Hidden character's attacks against them gain Advantage. Stealth is broken in the following situations: initiating an attack, being attacked, using a skill that requires sound, or moving into an enemy's line of sight without cover.

### 9-8 Status Effects (Common for Players)

| Status | Effect | Common Removal Method |
|---|---|---|
| **Poisoned** | Takes 1d4 Poison damage at the start of each round, lasting 3 rounds | Antidote, Constitution (TZ) check DC 14 can be attempted each round to remove |
| **Burning** | Takes 1d6 Fire damage at the start of each round, lasting 2 rounds | Spend 1 Action Point to extinguish, water |
| **Paralyzed** | Loses all actions for 1 round (can still speak) | Automatically removed at end of round, special potion |
| **Frozen** | Movement speed halved, lasting 2 rounds | Fire damage can remove it early |
| **Stunned** | All checks suffer Disadvantage, lasting 1 round | Automatically removed at end of round |
| **Bleeding** | Takes 1d4 damage at the start of each round, lasting until treated (spend 1 Action Point to bandage, Perception check DC 10) | Bandaging, regeneration |
| **Petrified** | Cannot act, but damage taken -5, lasting 1 round | Automatically removed at end of round |
| **Feared** | Cannot voluntarily approach the source of fear, attacks against that source suffer Disadvantage | Will (YZ) check DC 14 can be attempted each round to remove |
| **Blinded** | Attacks suffer Disadvantage, Perception (GZ) checks suffer Disadvantage | Automatically removed at end of round (temporary), treatment |
| **Silenced** | Cannot use active skills that require sound | Automatically removed at end of round, special potion |
| **Hidden** | Enemies need to pass a Perception (GZ) check contested against your Agility (MJ) to detect you; while undetected, your attacks against them gain Advantage, and their attacks against you suffer Disadvantage. Initiating an attack, being attacked, using a skill requiring sound, or moving into an enemy's unobstructed line of sight all break Stealth | Move out of enemy line of sight, or succeed on the contested Perception (GZ) check |

### 9-9 Combat Flow

1. **Declare Initiative**: All participants roll d20 + Agility (MJ), sorted from highest to lowest
2. **First Round Begins**: The highest initiative acts first
3. **Your Turn**: You gain 3 Action Points and may freely combine actions
4. **End of Round**: Unused Action Points disappear; recover 2 Energy; resolve ongoing effects (status damage/recovery)
5. **Next to Act**: Proceed to the next in initiative order
6. **Repeat until combat ends**

### 9-10 Near Death and Death

When your HP drops to 0:
- You enter the **Near Death state**
- You can no longer take any action
- At the end of each round, make a Constitution (TZ) check DC 10: success → stabilized (no further deterioration), failure → HP drops another -5
- Three successes → you stabilize, recovering 1 HP after 1 hour
- When HP drops to -(Constitution × 5) → you truly die
- Other characters may spend 1 Action Point to perform first aid on you (Perception GZ check DC 12); on success, you stabilize

### 9-11 Forced Evolution in Combat

By spending 3 Action Points, you may forcibly activate an Evolution Node that is not yet unlocked but whose unlock conditions are known. This is a measure for emergency situations:

- The node's effect lasts only for this battle
- After combat ends, the node disappears (not retained)
- Forced Evolution can only be used once per scenario (or each use requires accepting a permanent Humanity Value -2 cost)
- On the round after Forced Evolution, all your checks suffer Disadvantage (side effect of bodily adaptation)

## X. Absorption System

### 10-1 Kill vs Absorb

| Choice | Effect |
|---|---|
| **Kill** | Gain Evolution Points (Minion 1 / Elite 2 / Boss 3) + Remnant Coins (Minion 5-10 / Elite 15-30 / Boss 50-100) + collectible Biomass. Fast and reliable |
| **Absorb** | Spend 2 Action Points, gain no ready-made Evolution Points, but obtain a "Trait Sample" — unlocking a unique evolution branch for that enemy type. The "Evolution Crystals" refined during absorption can be exchanged for an additional 50% Remnant Coins. Higher long-term returns |

### 10-2 Absorption Difficulty

| Target State | Absorption DC (Will YZ) |
|---|---|
| Already Dead | 15 (extract residual traits from the corpse, lower success rate) |
| Near Death (HP < 10%) | 10 |
| Paralyzed/Paralysis | 8 |
| Voluntarily Absorbed | Automatic success |

### 10-3 Absorption Process

1. Choose a target in an absorbable state
2. Spend 2 Action Points
3. Make a Will (YZ) check vs Absorption DC
4. Success → gain that target's "Trait Sample"
5. Failure → failed to extract traits; the target cannot be attempted for absorption again. But you may instead kill it to gain Evolution Points (halved)

**Defense During Absorption:** During absorption you are in a vulnerable state — all attacks against you gain Advantage.

### 10-4 Trait Samples

A Trait Sample is not a ready-made skill — it is an "Evolution Direction Choice." You choose one direction from it, and the other options are permanently closed.

**Examples:**

|---|---|

| **Fire Elemental** | ①Heat Resistance (Passive) ②Flame Breath (Mid-tier Active) ③Thermal Vision (Passive) |
| **Ghost** | ①Etherealization (Form Node — Warning: permanently lose physical form) ②Psionic Shield (Passive) ③Mental Attack (Mid-tier Active) |
| **Petrifying Lizard** | ①Hardened Skin (Passive) ②Petrifying Gaze (Mid-tier Active) ③Regeneration Lv.1 (Passive) |

| Abomination (10-40%) | Only simple weapons (requiring no precise operation), some accessories. Armor cannot be used (too much size difference) |
| Monster (<10%) | Almost all artificial equipment cannot be used (but your body itself is the strongest weapon and armor) |

### 11-2 Weapon Table

> ▶ For complete weapon data (including v1.1 flat bonuses), see Chapter I of the "Item Compendium" in this rule set. Key rules:

- Weapon damage = base die + corresponding attribute (melee +Strength[LIL] / ranged +Agility[MJ]), with flat bonuses added per weapon tier (Heavy/Ranged +2~+3, Medium +1).
- **Weapon Gap Explanation (v1.1 balance fix):** Heavy weapons additionally carry flat bonuses (two-handed greatsword, war hammer +3; battle axe +2; longbow, crossbow +2; longsword, spear +1), ensuring heavy weapons still clearly outclass light weapons at high attributes. Strength (LIL)/Agility (MJ) thresholds remain unchanged.

### 11-3 Armor Table

> ▶ For complete armor data, see Chapter II of the "Item Compendium" in this rule set. Key rules: armor and shields provide a "Defense Bonus" (leather armor +1 to plate armor +4); heavy armor reduces movement speed and is limited by Humanity Value stage (see 11-1).

### 11-4 Consumables

> ▶ For the complete consumables list (including rare/special potions), see Chapter III of the "Item Compendium" in this rule set.

- **Healing Amount Design (v1.1 balance fix):** Healing potions are changed to a "percentage + minimum die roll" dual guarantee — healing potions restore 25% of max HP (minimum 2d6), strong potions 50% (minimum 4d6), ultimate potions 80% (minimum 6d6), making them useful in both early and late game.
- **Carry Limit (v1.1 added):** Healing-type potions may not exceed 5 bottles carried at once (including all tiers), energy potions no more than 5 bottles; excess must be stored in a safe place. This limit prevents infinite sustain.

### 11-5 Currency and Economy

This work uses a universal trading currency — **Remnant Coins (Coins)**. It is accepted by both Evolvers and ordinary people, sourced from crystals refined from ancient creature remains. Most equipment, consumables, and services are priced in Remnant Coins.

| Rarity | Typical Price (Remnant Coins) |
|---|---|
| Common | 5-25 coins |
| Uncommon | 30-80 coins |
| Rare | 100-300 coins |
| Very Rare | 400-800 coins |
| Legendary | 1000+ coins (usually cannot be purchased directly, only obtainable through story or high-difficulty rewards) |

**Price Examples:** Dagger 8 coins, longsword 20 coins, leather armor 15 coins, plate armor 120 coins, healing potion 10 coins, strong healing potion 40 coins, ultimate healing potion 120 coins, energy potion 8 coins, Evolution Catalyst 150 coins, Humanity Stabilizer 200 coins, Strength Charm 25 coins.

> **Note:** When Humanity Value is too low (<40%), most merchants refuse to trade or raise prices to ×3 (see Section VIII Humanity Value Stages and the GM Rulebook's NPC Reaction Table). Players who wish to grow purely through absorption may also not use currency at all — items are obtained solely through scenario rewards and absorption.
>
> **Income Sources (v1.1 supplement):** Remnant Coins mainly come from enemy drops (see 7-1), selling surplus Biomass (3 coins/unit) and equipment, story rewards, and exploration discoveries. A standard scenario (3-4 hours) can accumulate about 80-200 coins, enough to resupply 1-2 Uncommon pieces of equipment or a batch of consumables in town.

### 11-6 Accessory Table

Accessories do not occupy weapon or armor slots, and can be worn stacked with other equipment (usually multiple can be worn at once, but effects of the same type of accessory do not stack).

## XII. Growth and Advancement

### 12-1 Growth Curve

Devour Gene: Chronicle of Evolution does not use a level system, but instead measures character strength by "total number of Evolution Nodes":

| Stage | Node Count | Character State |
|---|---|---|
| **Prototype** | 1-3 nodes | Just beginning to evolve. Abilities are close to ordinary people, but already possess special talents |
| **Growth Stage** | 4-8 nodes | Evolution begins to manifest. Possesses various passives and low-tier skills |
| **Maturity Stage** | 9-15 nodes | The backbone among Evolvers. Possesses mid-tier skills and organs |
| **Pinnacle Stage** | 16-22 nodes | Close to the apex of evolution. Possesses high-tier skills and multiple organ combinations |
| **Transcendence** | 18+ nodes | Surpasses ordinary Evolvers. May have achieved Form Evolution, approaching the "Ultimate Form" |

### 12-2 Suggested Evolution Pacing

GMs usually distribute evolution opportunities with reference to the following pacing:

| Game Stage | Evolution Point Acquisition Pace |
|---|---|
| Prologue (character introduction, initial exploration) | About 3-5 points after completion |
| Each battle | Minion battle 1 point, Elite battle 2 points, Boss battle 3 points (excluding absorption) |
| End of each scenario | Story reward 3-5 points + settlement of all battle and absorption gains |
| Entire campaign (5 scenarios) | Accumulates about 20-30 Evolution Nodes |

### 12-3 Solo Play Adjustments

This system fully supports Solo Mode (1 Player + 1 GM). In solo mode:

- Enemy count is usually smaller (1-3 instead of 3-5)
- Encounter difficulty reduced by about 20% (GM may adjust)
- More absorption opportunities (since you are the only absorber)
- GM may provide an NPC companion (does not participate in evolution) to assist in combat
- Environmental adaptation rewards increased (since no teammates share resources)
