# Chapter 1: Lore

## 1.1 The World's Name: Ynserghard

Ynserghard is a medieval-fantasy continent of the "Post-Heroic Age." Roughly twenty years have passed since the last great monster war (the "Rift War"). The Demon Lord is sealed, and adventurers are in surplus — of the thirty-thousand-plus sword-bearers, fewer than a thousand subjugation commissions appear each year.

This world is undergoing a quiet revolution: **forging swords into spatulas, turning combat instinct into quality-control eyes, redefining an adventurer's resilience as a crafter's focus.**

---

## 1.2 Creation Myth

> They say that before time began, Ynserghard was a blank dining table. The gods sat around it, each offering a dish. The last to arrive was the Wanderer God, empty-handed, yet He produced a blank recipe book: "My dish needs time to be finished."
>
> The Wanderer God tore the recipe book to shreds and scattered them across the land. Each page fragment that fell into the mortal world became a recipe — hidden in monsters' flesh, in the depths of mineral veins, in the wind of the seasons.

Now the crafters of Ynserghard all believe: every recipe discovered is a page of the Wanderer God's recipe book. And every retired adventurer who walks into a shop is merely changing their method, continuing the search for the next page.

---

## 1.3 Four Reasons for the Adventurer Retirement Wave

| Reason | Note |
|------|------|
| **Economic Surplus** | Thirty thousand postwar adventurers vs. ~thousand commissions needed yearly. The guild launched the "Hoes for Swords" program |
| **Dungeon Economy** | Dungeons seen as ecological resources, needing those who understand monsters but need not fight all day |
| **Settlement Decree** | King Edwin III offered tax breaks and initial loans |
| **Personal Burnout** | Seen too many comrades fall, disillusioned with glory — baking bread feels safer than swinging a sword |

## 1.6 Four Seasons System

| Season | Effect |
|------|------|
| **Spring** (Awakening) | Plants abundant but spoil easily; best time to sow & cultivate |
| **Summer** (Active) | More adventurer customers; high-heat zones need extra Stamina Checks |
| **Autumn** (Harvest) | Full harvest, price −20%; autumn fog raises foraging Check difficulty |
| **Winter** (Dormancy) | Scarce but special materials appear (ice-type / hibernating monsters); mining geothermal strengthens |

About 12–15 game turns per season.

# Chapter 2: GM Running Guide

## 2.1 Game Loop (Six Weekly Stages)

| Stage | Suggested Time | GM Focus |
|------|----------|---------|
| **1. Business Prep** | 5–10 min | Roll Market Fluctuation (1d6), announce the week's state, players decide direction |
| **2. Material Acquisition** | 15–25 min | Guide foraging/purchasing strategy; use sensory language to describe discoveries — don't just say "you found 3 mushrooms" |
| **3. Crafting Production** | 15–25 min | Describe the sounds/smells/feel of crafting; build suspense when revealing quality |
| **4. Business & Sales** | 10–20 min | Play customer NPCs, calculate income, intersperse small events (regulars / complaints / surprise orders) |
| **5. Event Stage** | 10–20 min | Trigger Random Event or advance main plot |
| **6. Growth Settlement** | 5–10 min | Experience (XP) / Reputation / Funds tally, update character-sheet ticks |

---

## 2.2 Pacing Techniques

### Narrative Packaging of Crafting Checks

❌ Bad example: *"You rolled a Craft Check, 18, success, Superior quality."*

✅ Good example: *"You plunge the iron ingot into the forge-fire. The memory of a thousand hammer-strikes awakens in your arm — at the first blow you hear the metal answer in resonance. As you quench the blade in oil, the steam clears and you see natural ripples surface on the steel. You didn't just forge a sword — you awakened the soul within the metal."*

### Handling Critical Failure

Don't just say "failed, materials wasted." Add a small event:

*"As you mix the potion your hand twitches, adding two extra drops of Dragon Blood extract — the liquid in the vial begins to boil, change color, then explode. Acrid smoke stings your eyes shut. When the smoke clears, you find a fist-sized glowing mushroom has grown on the workbench. It seems to be looking at you."*

That is the next plot hook.

### Scene Sense for Foraging / Purchasing

- Foraging-leaning: Have players describe foraging strategy → 1–2 combined Checks → describe the discovery
- Purchasing-leaning: Make haggling a small scene. Play the supplier's personality — the stubborn old Dwarf, the crooked broker, the nervous new supplier

---

## 2.3 Three-Act Narrative Pacing

```
Stable Period (30%) → Turbulent Period (40%) → Blooming Period (30%)
```

| Stage | Content | What GM Does |
|------|------|-----------|
| **Stable Period** | Build routine, meet NPCs, accumulate resources | Let players fall in love with their shop and neighbors |
| **Turbulent Period** | Rivals, supply-chain problems, past coming knocking | Create the crisis feeling that "this shop might not survive" |
| **Blooming Period** | Overcome hardship, shop upgrades, legendary commissions | Let players' effort earn grand reward |

## 2.4 "Yes, And" in Practice

| Player Action | Example Response |
|----------|----------|
| Wants to make a cake from dragon scale | "Yes, you can try — and if you succeed, it might be history's first dragon-scale mille-feuille. But how will you grind it to powder?" |
| Critical Failure: bread burned | "Yes, it's burned — and the burnt smell drew the baker next door, who knocks asking if you need help" |
| Critical Success: forging vision | "Yes, you didn't just forge a Superior sword — and the fiery vision during forging attracted a passing retired old smith, who stood at the door watching for a long time" |

## 2.6 Customer Satisfaction System

Satisfaction is not a number scribbled at will. The following rules complete the chain "Quality → Satisfaction multiplier → Income."

### Per-Transaction Satisfaction Determination

```
Base Satisfaction = Base satisfaction of the product's quality + service modifier + decor modifier + special modifier
Final Satisfaction Multiplier = looked up by total satisfaction value
```

### Quality Base Satisfaction

| Quality | Base Satisfaction |
|------|-----------|
| Crude | −2 |
| Standard | +0 |
| Fine | +1 |
| Superior | +3 |
| Legendary | +5 |

### Service Modifier

| Check Result | Modifier |
|----------|------|
| Hospitality skill 0 | −1 |
| Hospitality skill 1 | +0 |
| Hospitality skill 2 | +1 |
| Hospitality skill 3 | +2 |
| Matches customer preference | +1 (Cook's "Custom Seasoning" can add +1 more) |
| Violates customer taboo | −3 (e.g. noble allergic to Dragon Blood but Dragon Blood ore was used) |

### Decor Modifier

| Decor Score | Modifier |
|----------|------|
| 0–2 | +0 |
| 3–5 | +1 |
| 6–10 | +2 |
| 11–15 | +3 |
| 16+ | +4 |

### Satisfaction → Multiplier Conversion Table

| Total Satisfaction | Multiplier | Customer Reaction |
|-----------|------|----------|
| Below −3 | ×0.3 | Refund, bad review, Reputation −1 |
| −2 ∼ −1 | ×0.5 | Unsatisfied but grudgingly accepts. May not return |
| 0 ∼ +1 | ×1.0 | Normal transaction, no special reaction |
| +2 ∼ +3 | ×1.2 | Satisfied. Return rate up |
| +4 ∼ +5 | ×1.5 | Very satisfied. May refer new customers |
| +6 ∼ +7 | ×1.8 | Amazed. Actively promotes shop (Reputation +1) |
| +8 or more | ×2.0 | Unforgettable. Surely triggers a special event (GM choice or event roll) |

### Regulars & Word of Mouth

Whenever Satisfaction reaches +4 or above, that customer becomes a "regular" — recorded in the shop's regulars list. Every 3 regulars → base Foot Traffic permanently +1 (word-of-mouth effect).

### Collective Satisfaction (Banquet / Bulk Orders)

For multi-dish occasions like banquets: take the **median** of all dishes' Satisfaction (not average — one bad dish lowers the whole but won't ruin everything, one divine dish can save the day).

## 2.7 Travel & Cross-Region Rules

### Distance Table (unit: weeks)

Travel time between Ynserghard's Five Major Regions. Values already include reasonable rest.

| From \ To | Royal Capital | Seabreeze | Anvilhearth | Verdantvale | Watchkeep |
|-------------|------|--------|--------|--------|--------|
| **Royal Capital** | — | 1 | 2 | 1 | 3 |
| **Seabreeze** | 1 | — | 3 | 2 | 4 |
| **Anvilhearth** | 2 | 3 | — | 1 | 1 |
| **Verdantvale** | 1 | 2 | 1 | — | 2 |
| **Watchkeep** | 3 | 4 | 1 | 2 | — |

> Reading: Royal Capital → Watchkeep takes 3 weeks. Watchkeep ↔ Anvilhearth are close, only 1 week.

### Travel Cost

Each cross-region trip consumes **Action Points = travel weeks × 2** (representing journey fatigue, not just movement). During travel the shop cannot do business (unless an Employee stays behind — Employees can run basic business but Quality Cap −1 level, and no player skill bonus).

### Travel Risk (roll 1d6 per leg)

| 1d6 | Encounter | Effect |
|-----|------|------|
| 1 | Nothing happens | Arrive safely |
| 2–3 | Minor delay | Travel time +1 week (weather / road condition) |
| 4 | Small event | Minor trouble (bandit harassment / beast / road damage), Stamina Difficulty (DC) 10 to handle |
| 5 | Moderate event | Material loss or light injury — lose 1d3×10G of goods or light injury |
| 6 | Major event | GM draws a Random Event or triggers a journey-exclusive plot |

### Trade Route Blockage

When a specific route is blocked by events (war, disaster, blockade), all materials via that route cost +50% and Rare-or-above materials cannot be obtained. Merchants may use "Sense the Opportunity" to learn of blockage warnings early.

### Fast Travel Means

| Means | Effect | Cost |
|------|------|------|
| Relay carriage | Travel time −1 week (minimum 1 week) | 50G/person/leg |
| Caravan companion | Travel risk −2 (roll 1d6−2) | Free — but must help guard (Stamina Difficulty (DC) 12 or combat) |
| Teleport Circle (Royal Capital↔Seabreeze) | Arrive instantly | 200G/person or royal permission |

## 2.8 Long-Term Projects

Some activities cannot be finished in one week and need cross-turn investment. The following are unified long-term project rules.

### Long-Term Project Table

| Project | Weeks Needed | Weekly Cost | Check | Interruption Rule |
|------|---------|----------|------|----------|
| **Recipe Research (Custom)** | 2–4 weeks | 1 Action Point (AP) + materials ×2/week | Weekly Inspiration Difficulty (DC) 12 | Interruption recoverable, no penalty |
| **Shop Decor Renovation** | LV×2 weeks | 1 Action Point (AP) + 50G/week | Final Inspiration Difficulty (DC) 10+LV | Interruption → decor score −1 |
| **Large Equipment Install** | 2–3 weeks | 1 Action Point (AP) + equipment fee/week | Final Craft Difficulty (DC) 12 | Interruption → cost +20% |
| **Employee Training** | 4 weeks | 1 Action Point (AP)/week (mentor) | Every 2 weeks trainee Inspiration Difficulty (DC) 9 | Interruption → training extended 1 week |
| **Teaching Recipe** | 1 week | 1 Action Point (AP) (each side) | Learner Inspiration Difficulty (DC) per Integrity table | Failure → wait 2 weeks to retry |
| **Apply Patent / Certification** | 2–4 weeks | 50–200G | Final Social Difficulty (DC) 14 | Interruption → re-apply |
| **Shop Upgrade Construction** | See upgrade table | Upgrade fee + 1 Action Point (AP)/week | None (automatic) | Insufficient funds → pause (progress not lost) |

### Progress Tracking

GM gives each long-term project a "progress bar": `■□□□`. Fill one cell after each week's requirement is met. Players may choose to accelerate (spend 2 Action Points (AP)/week → progress ×2), but the accelerated week's Check Difficulty (DC) +2.

## 2.9 GM Balance Reference

### Event Income Pacing (Key)

> **Weekly business income is the survival line. What truly drives upgrades are commissions and events.**

| Reputation Stage | Suggested Event Frequency | Single Commission Reward | Note |
|----------|-------------|-------------|------|
| 0–9 (Unknown) | Once per 5–8 weeks | 80–200G | Scenario 1 level (wedding / festival orders) |
| 10–29 (Neighborhood) | Once per 5–8 weeks | 200–500G | Advanced commissions, guild orders |
| 30–59 (City) | Once per 6–10 weeks | 500–1,500G | Royal / noble commissions |
| 60–99 (Kingdom) | Once per 8–12 weeks | 1,500–3,000G | Legendary commissions |
| 100+ (Legendary) | Plot-determined | No cap | Wanderer God-level events |

> A pure business model without event income makes upgrades painfully slow. GM should treat commissions as "chapter climaxes" — each module equals one large commission.

### Newbie Group (Reputation 0–9, 3–4 players)

| Parameter | Suggested Value |
|------|--------|
| Starting Funds | 300–400G |
| First-week market | Auto "Stable" (no roll — let players find footing) |
| First transaction | Suggest GM let it succeed (tutorial-friendly) |
| First Random Event | Warm-leaning (#1–#15), not dark |
| Shop Lv.1 expected weekly net profit | 15–40G (incl. 5G Base Revenue safety net) |
| First upgrade (Lv.1→2) | Naturally reached in ~6–10 turns |

### Mid-Tier Group (Reputation 10–40)

| Parameter | Suggested Value |
|------|--------|
| Market Fluctuation | Normal rolling |
| Rival appears when | Reputation 15+ |
| Noble customer first appears | Reputation 20+ and has Fine-or-above work |
| T2 Skill Tree unlock | ~10–15 turns (Reputation 10 + EXP 15) |
| Lv.2→3 expected time | ~18–22 turns |

### High-Tier Group (Reputation 40+)

| Parameter | Suggested Value |
|------|--------|
| Legendary material appearance rate | One opportunity per 8–12 turns (not guaranteed) |
| T3 Skill Tree unlock | ~20–30 turns (Reputation 20 + EXP 35) |
| Cross-city trade unlock | Shop Lv.4+ |
| Lv.3→4 expected time | ~25–35 turns |
| Lv.4→5 expected time | ~35–50 turns |
| Shop Lv.5 expected total time | ~80–110 turns (long-campaign endgame goal) |

### Difficulty Adjustment Levers

The simplest balance tools for the GM (in priority order):
1. **Adjust Market Fluctuation**: consecutive Stable = easy mode; consecutive Depression = hardcore mode
2. **Adjust Starting Funds**: ±50G changes early pressure. Don't go below 200G (else first month may run dry)
3. **Adjust Random Event trigger rate**: change to 1d6 result 5–6 triggers = more events
4. **Adjust material prices**: global ±20% = inflation / deflation
5. **Give / withdraw NPC aid**: Hearthside Circle help is the strongest safety net

## 3.2 Risk & Conflict System

### Outdoor Risk Flow

```
Decide scope → Spend Action Points (AP) → Encounter Check (GM hidden roll) → Response Check (player Stamina) → Settle
```

### Risk Difficulty

| Level | Difficulty (DC) | Stamina 3 Success Rate | Typical Scene |
|------|-----|-----------|----------|
| Safe | 7 | 99.5% | Town outskirts |
| Normal | 10 | 90.7% | Forest foraging |
| Dangerous | 13 | 62.5% | Deep mountains / large monsters |
| Lethal | 16 | 25.9% | Dragon nest / cursed land |
| Cataclysm | 19 | 9.3% | Natural disaster / legendary monster |

**Encounter Check (GM hidden roll)**: 3d6 + zone risk modifier, vs Difficulty (DC) 10 + party's highest Stamina value.

### Simplified Hunting (2 Checks)

| Step1 | Step2 | Result |
|-------|-------|------|
| Success | Success | Perfect: complete materials + rare drop |
| Success | Failure | Escape |
| Failure | Success | Barely: quality −1 + light injury |
| Failure | Failure | Empty-handed + possible light injury |

Prey Difficulty (DC): Small 10, Medium 14, Large 18, Legendary 22.

## 3.3 Fatigue & Injury

### Fatigue (0–10)

| Value | State | Effect |
|----|------|------|
| 0–2 | Energized | None |
| 3–4 | Light | Stamina −1 |
| 5–6 | Moderate | Physical −2, Action Points (AP) −1 |
| 7–8 | Severe | All −3, cannot craft long |

## 3.9 Tool & Equipment Durability

### Tool Durability System

Crafting tools don't just wear — they are a crafter's second pair of hands. Simplified durability rules below:

| Tool Type | Base Durability | Per-use Consumption | Critical Failure Extra | Repair Difficulty (DC) |
|----------|---------|-------------|-------------|---------|
| Chef's Knife Set | 20 | −1 | −2 | 9 |
| Smithing Tool Set | 30 | −1 | −2 | 9 |
| Alchemy Tool Set | 25 | −1 | −2 | 9 |
| Foraging Tool Set | 15 | −1 (foraging) | −2 | 8 |
| Research Tool Set | 30 | −1 (research) | −2 | 9 |
| Weighing Scale | No durability | — | — | — |

### Durability to Zero

| Durability | State | Effect |
|------|------|------|
| 10+ | Good | None |
| 5–9 | Worn | Check −1 when used |
| 1–4 | Severely Worn | Check −2 when used, Critical Failure chance +1 (nat 3–6 counts as Critical Failure) |
| 0 | Broken | Unusable, needs repair (spend materials ×1 + Craft Difficulty (DC) per table above) |
| −5 | Destroyed | Unrepairable, must repurchase / recraft |

### Repair

Successful repair restores 1d3 + Craft value durability. Smith repair Difficulty (DC) −3 and recovery +2.

### No-Tool Penalty

Penalties for crafting without the corresponding tool (defined per class, unified here):
- Cooking without knife → Difficulty (DC) +3
- Smithing without tools → cannot smith
- Alchemy without tools → Difficulty (DC) +3
- Foraging without tools → yield −1
- Research without tools → Difficulty (DC) +2

## 3.10 Personal Inventory Rules

### Character Carry Cap

Shops have inventory capacity (Lv.1 is 20 units), but characters also have personal carry limits — especially when foraging outdoors.

| Stamina Value | Personal Carry Cap (material units) |
|--------|------------------------|
| 1 | 4 |
| 2 | 6 |
| 3 | 8 |
| 4 | 10 |
| 5 | 14 |

Over the cap → Stamina Check −1 when moving (each 2 extra units −1 more).

### Inventory Management While Foraging

After each foraging trip, if yield exceeds personal carry cap:
- **Option A**: Discard excess materials
- **Option B**: Spend extra 1 Action Point (AP) on "Staggered Transport" (cap ×1.5, but no other Stamina activities that week)
- **Option C**: Have a pack horse (Gatherer T2 skill) → cap ×2

### Shop Inventory Cap

| Shop Level | Inventory Capacity | Overflow Handling |
|----------|---------|----------|
| Lv.1 | 20 | Overflow → Freshness decay ×2 (poor management from piling up) |
| Lv.2 | 30 | |
| Lv.3 | 50 | Each Space Ring +5 |

The Settlement Decree tax exemption expires — must raise enough Funds within 3 weeks.

## 4.2 Module 2: "The Royal Wedding Banquet" (Special Commission, 3–5 turns)

| Stage | Content |
|------|------|
| **Bidding** (Week 1) | Multiple shops compete; need to submit a tasting + Social (DC 15) |
| **Preparation** (Weeks 2–3) | Gather designated rare ingredients, design menu, test dishes |

## 4.3 Module 3: "A New Shop Next Door" (Rival Conflict, 5–8 turns)

Four-stage structure:

| Stage | Turn | Content |
|------|------|------|
| **Coexistence** | 1–2 | New shop opens, initial contact — friendly or cold? |
| **Friction** | 3–4 | Customer loss / supplier stolen / small moves → first Contest Check |
| **Crisis** | 5–6 | Rival plays killing move (price war / rumor / legal means) |
| **Ending** | 7–8 | Reconcile (win-win cooperation) / Defeat (rival closes) / Collaborate (merge brands) / Acquire |

**Four Hidden Truths** (GM chooses):
1. The rival is a pawn sent by the Myriad Guild
2. The rival is also a retired adventurer, and your past competitor
3. The rival's shop is actually near bankruptcy, bluffing
4. The rival just admires you too much, over-imitating
