## Chapter One: GM Basics

### 1.1 The GM's Three Core Principles

**Principle One: "Yes, and…" — Beastman Warring States Edition**

When a player proposes an action, affirm their creativity ("Yes"), then add the unique flavor of Beastman Warring States ("and").

> Player: "I want to use Intimidation to make the Ox Clan gate guard step aside."
> GM: "Yes — roll an Intimidation Check. And if you succeed, the Rat Clan spies nearby will notice your 'Tiger Majesty,' which could become a useful source of information… or a complication."

**Principle Two: Yokai as Narrative Resource**

Yokai Value is not just a combat number — it is the thermometer of narrative tension. As a player's Yokai Value rises, describe the changes in their body (ears twitching, tail lashing uncontrollably, fur standing on end, pupils dilating). Let the players "feel" themselves losing control.

**Principle Three: The Blade Is a Character**

Blades of Masterwork grade and above are not equipment — they are NPCs. When players interact with a famous blade, portray it as you would an NPC: the blade will resist an unworthy wielder, remember betrayals, and "speak" at key moments (through vibration, resonance, or letting the wielder feel an emotion).

### 1.2 Single-Player Section

This game is designed to be compatible with 1 player by default. The following are special guidelines for single-player GMs.

#### Combat Balance Adjustments

| Adjustment | Rule |
|---|---|
| Mob count | Halved (enemies in a normal encounter ÷2, minimum 1) |
| Boss Hit Points (HP) | −25% |
| NPC companions available to the player | Provide 1–2 NPC companions (using a simplified stat card) |
| Status duration cap | Statuses applied to the player last at most 2 rounds |
| Enemy critical rate | Only natural 20; critical range is not expanded by techniques or weaknesses |

#### NPC Companion System (single-player only)

The GM provides 1–2 NPC companions for the single player, using simplified stats:

**NPC Companion Simplified Stat Template**:

| Item | Content |
|---|---|
| Name | (named by GM) |
| Clan / School | Complementary to the player (e.g., if the player is front-line, choose ranged or healer) |
| Hit Points (HP) | 70% of player's HP |
| Attack bonus | Player's attack bonus −2 |
| Damage | 1d8 + 3 |
| Special ability | 1 (chosen by GM, such as healing, ranged attack, support technique) |

**Four Combat Commands** (the player may issue these as an incidental action on their turn):

| Command | Effect |
|---|---|
| "Cover me!" | NPC moves to the player's side and takes one attack for the player this round |
| "Attack that!" | NPC makes one attack against the designated target |
| "Use your technique!" | NPC uses its special ability |
| "Stand down!" | NPC retreats to a safe distance and assumes a defense stance |

**Three NPC Companion Templates**:

| Template | Clan | Ability | Personality |
|---|---|---|---|
| Loyal Retainer | Dog Clan | Protect companion, raise morale | Taciturn and loyal, guards silently |
| Mysterious Rōnin | Monkey Clan | Ambush, mimic enemy techniques | Flippant and whimsical, hides real strength |
| Defecting Enemy | Snake Clan | Poison attack, provide information | Gloomy and suspicious, currently atoning |

#### Single-Player Unique Advantages

Single-player games have unique narrative advantages:
- **Deep character development**: Can focus entirely on one character's inner world
- **Flexible pacing**: No need to coordinate multiple players' schedules; can speed up or slow down per the player's preference
- **Private Giri dilemmas**: No other players' opinions to interfere; Giri predicaments become more personal
- **Rōnin route fits best**: The Rōnin (masterless samurai) route is especially tense in single-player mode

### 1.3 Pacing Control

The ideal game loop:

```
Cultivation scenes (dōjō practice, meditation, test-cutting) —— 20%
    ↓
Exploration / diplomacy scenes (Daimyō audience, information gathering, travel) —— 30%
    ↓
Combat scenes (encounters, mass battles, single combat) —— 30%
    ↓
Giri dilemma scenes (major moral decisions) —— 20%
    ↓
Consequences and feedback (the world changes from choices, return to cultivation)
```

## Chapter Two: Complete Checks and Difficulty Guide

### 2.1 Check Types and Formulas

| Check Type | Formula | Description |
|---|---|---|
| Standard Check | d20 + Attribute value + skill proficiency bonus ≥ DC | General action resolution |
| Contest Check | Active side total > Passive side total (tie goes to passive) | Direct opposition between characters |
| Saving Throw | d20 + Attribute value ≥ DC | Resist a passive effect |
| Group Check | More than half succeed → overall success | Collective action |
| Assist | Assisting side makes a DC 10 Check; on success grants the assisted side Advantage | Teamwork |

### 2.2 Difficulty Class (DC) Setting Guide

| DC | Name | When to Use |
|---|---|---|
| 5 | Trivial | Everyday actions. E.g.: climbing a low wall, remembering common information |
| 10 | Simple | Achievable with basic training. E.g.: cutting a training straw dummy, identifying common herbs |
| 13 | Ordinary | Requires focus and competence. E.g.: keeping balance in the rain, spotting a forged document |
| 18 | Difficult | Only reliably achieved by the skilled. E.g.: scaling a castle wall in combat, deciphering ancient Dragon-tongue inscriptions |
| 22 | Very Hard | Master-level challenge. E.g.: blocking arrows while blind, hitting a target 50 squares away in heavy fog |
| 26 | Legendary | Worthy of the chronicles. E.g.: cutting a waterfall in one stroke, achieving a Soul Pact with a famous blade in silence |
| 30 | Mythic | Beyond beastman limits. E.g.: sealing the Great Yokai Calamity single-handedly, forging a Five-Swords-Under-Heaven-grade blade |

### 2.3 Hidden DC Principle

The following DCs should be set privately by the GM, without telling the players the specific numbers:

| Situation | Principle |
|---|---|
| Noticing an ambush / trap | DC depends on the ambusher's Stealth Check result (Contest Check) |
| Seeing through a lie | DC depends on the liar's Deception Check result (Contest Check) |
| Identifying a Cursed Blade's true name | DC 20–30, depending on the blade's legend status |
| Sensing the presence of Yokai | DC 15, lower DC the closer the distance |
| Resisting a Cursed Blade's temptation | DC 18–25, depending on the Cursed Blade's grade |

### 2.4 Situation Definition and Advantage / Disadvantage

| Situations granting Advantage | Situations granting Disadvantage |
|---|---|
| Flanking an enemy (2+ companions surround the same target) | Attacking in darkness (for those without night vision) |
| Attacking a lower target from high ground | Any Check while Stunned |
| Target is Prone (melee attack) | Fighting in water (for non-aquatic clans) |
| Using Yokai that counters the target's clan | Fatigue state (over 24 hours without a long rest) |
| School technique text explicitly grants Advantage | Wounded state (HP < 30%) on physical Checks |
| Other favorable situations adjudicated by the GM | Other unfavorable situations adjudicated by the GM |

> **Stacking Rule**: Multiple sources of Advantage do not stack (still only roll 2d20 take higher). The same for multiple Disadvantages. Advantage and Disadvantage cancel out.

### 2.5 Application of Margin of Success

Margin of Success (MoS) is not only used to determine school technique effects. The following are general guidelines:

| Margin of Success | Example of extra effect the GM should grant |
|---|---|
| 5–9 | Gain extra information, save time, grant an ally Advantage, discover hidden details |
| 10+ | Unlock hidden options, gain unexpected allies, enemy Stunned or surrenders, item quality raised one grade |

### 2.6 Attribute Check Quick Reference

| What the player wants to do | Corresponding Check |
|---|---|
| Cut a rope in one stroke | Swordsmanship (Sword Arts) Check DC 15 |
| Lift a collapsed beam | Physique Check DC 18 |
| Sense the residual Yokai here | Spirit (Onmyōdō) Check DC 15 |
| Leap between rooftops | Agility (Climbing) Check DC 13 |
| Intimidate a defeated enemy captive | Spirit (Intimidation) Check DC 15 |

## Chapter Three: Complete Combat Rules

### 3.1 Combat Management Flow (for the GM)

```
【Combat Start】
  1. Confirm the battlefield map and terrain
  2. Decide initial positions of all participants
  3. All participants make Initiative Checks
  4. Build the Initiative order table

【Each Round】
  1. Round Start Phase
     ├── Process ongoing effects (burning damage, poison damage, Yokai fade)
     └── Trigger "at round start" effects

  2. Action Phase (processed one by one in Initiative order)
     └── Each character performs: main action + move action + incidental action + possible reaction action

  3. Round End Phase
     ├── Ongoing effect durations decrement
     ├── Natural Yokai fade (each character −2 Yokai Value)
     ├── Process "at round end" effects
     └── Judge dying characters' state (those at HP 0 make death saves)

【Combat End】
  ├── Calculate Cultivation Points rewards
  ├── Distribute loot (enemy commander's blade, information, captives, etc.)
  └── RP consequences (Giri dilemmas, Reputation changes, etc.)
```

### 3.2 Death and Dying

When a character's Hit Points (HP) drop to 0:

| Round | Rule |
|---|---|
| The instant HP drops to 0 | Character falls prone, loses consciousness. Yokai Value automatically resets to 0 |
| Start of each subsequent round | Make a death save: d20 (no Attribute added), ≥10 succeeds, <10 fails |
| 3 cumulative successes | Character stabilizes (HP stays 0, but no longer dying; regains consciousness after 1 hour) |
| 3 cumulative failures | Character dies |
| Natural 20 | Immediately regain 1 HP, wake |
| Natural 1 | Counts as 2 failures |
| Taking damage | Automatically counts as 1 failure (Critical Hit counts as 2) |
| Healed by a companion | Regain HP, dying state ends |

### 3.3 Enemy Behavior AI (GM Quick Reference)

#### Beastman Enemy Commander Behavior Logic

| Yokai Stage | Behavior Pattern |
|---|---|
| Calm (0–30) | Tactical action: use techniques, hold formation, prioritize the most threatening target |
| Restless (31–60) | Half-bestial: begins using innate skills, abandons defense stance, prioritizes nearest target |
| Bestial (61–85) | Bestial dominance: each round must make a Spirit Saving Throw; on failure attacks the nearest target (friend or foe). Uses strongest techniques ignoring Yokai cost |
| Loss of Control (86+) | Full Beastification: GM directly controls, attacks nearest target, double attack, indiscriminate AOE |

#### Mob Behavior

- Ashigaru: Hold formation, 2–3 ganging up on one target (flanking grants Advantage)
- Archers: Prioritize targets without cover, keep distance
- Ninja: Stealth → appear and attack → smoke bomb retreat → stealth again
- Warrior Monks: Prioritize healing the enemy commander, then use wards

### 3.5 Environment and Special Rules

#### Full-Moon Scene (all Rabbit Clan related)

- All clans' Yokai gain +50%
- Rabbit Clan extra +5 Yokai / round
- Rabbit Clan Moon Madness triggers (requires Spirit Saving Throw)
- All Moon Shadow Style techniques +1

#### Dragon-Vein Node

- Yokai gain +1 / round (all present)
- School technique effect +1
- Sword Aura-related Checks gain Advantage
- Forging a famous blade here: +20% success rate

#### Wailing Plains (ancient battlefield)

- Those with Spirit below 3 must make a Spirit Saving Throw DC 15 each hour, on failure gain 5 Yokai (battlefield grudge)
- Undead-type enemies' Hit Points (HP) maximum +20% here

## Chapter Four: Mass Battle System

### 4.1 Mass Battle Overview

A Mass Battle is a large-scale military conflict between Daimyō. Unlike personal combat, the Mass Battle uses macro-scale rules.

**Time and Space Scale**:
- 1 round = about 10 minutes (battlefield time)
- Distance unit "formation" = about 50 meters

### 4.2 Combat Power (CP)

The core value of a Mass Battle is "Combat Power (CP)," representing a unit's overall combat strength.

**Combat Power (CP) = Scale × Troop-Type Coefficient × Morale Modifier**

### 4.3 Ten Troop Types

| Troop Type | Troop Coefficient | Clans Suited | Trait |
|---|---|---|---|
| Scout | ×1.0 | Rat, Monkey | First-strike intel: Advantage on the first Mass Battle round |
| Light Infantry | ×1.2 | Dog, Monkey | High mobility: may adjust formation each round |
| Heavy Infantry | ×1.5 | Ox, Boar | Frontal breakthrough: ×1.5 CP vs. light infantry |
| Cavalry | ×1.8 | Horse | Flanking raid: ×2.0 CP vs. archers |
| Archers | ×1.3 | Rooster | Ranged suppression: may attack any enemy formation on the first round |
| Warrior Monks | ×1.0 | Sheep | Morale recovery: restore 1 Morale per round |
| Ninja Corps | ×0.8 | Rat, Snake | Assassinate enemy commander: 1 assassination Check per Mass Battle |
| Dragon Cavalry | ×2.0 | Dragon | Air superiority: −1 Morale to all enemy formations per round |
| Shrine Maidens | ×0.5 | Rabbit | Yokai purification: reduce whole-army Yokai gain |
| Siege Unit | ×1.5 | Boar, Ox | ×3.0 CP vs. castles |

### 4.4 Morale System

Morale ranges 0–20, starting at 10.

| Morale | Stage | CP Modifier | Effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| 16–20 | High | ×1.2 | Whole army gains Advantage on attacks |
| 11–15 | Stable | ×1.0 | Normal performance |
| 6–10 | Shaken | ×0.8 | May retreat at start of next round (Spirit Saving Throw DC 12) |
| 1–5 | Rout | ×0.5 | Automatically lose 1d4 Scale per round |
| 0 | Collapse | — | Whole army scatters, Mass Battle ends |

**Morale Changes**:
- Breaking an enemy unit: +2
- Enemy commander killed or loses a Single Combat: −3 (enemy whole army)
- Hit by fire strategy or ambush: −2
- Reinforcements arrive: +3

### 4.5 The Player's Role in Mass Battles

The player may choose one of two roles in a Mass Battle:

#### Commander Type (strategic command)

Use "Command Value" to deploy units:

**Command Value = Spirit value + floor(Swordsmanship value / 2) + floor(Giri Value / 3)**

Command actions (once per Mass Battle round):

| Command Action | Command Value DC | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Full Assault | 15 | This round CP ×1.5, next round CP ×0.7 |
| Hold Position | 12 | This round enemy CP ×0.7 |
| Flank Envelopment | 18 | Designate one enemy unit; this round ×2 CP against it |
| Fire Strategy | 20 | Enemy whole army −3 Morale, but 30% chance of also affecting own side |
| Rally Morale | 10 | Own side Morale +1d4 |
| Cover Retreat | 15 | Withdraw from Mass Battle, losses halved |

#### Assault Captain Type (action-oriented)

Cut directly into the enemy formation, influencing the battle with personal might.

| Assault Action | Rule |
|---|---|
| Charge into Formation | Make three combat Checks (continuous attacks on mobs), tally total kills. Every 5 kills reduces enemy Scale by 1 |
| Mob Combat | Automatically kill 1d6 mobs per round. Enemy Scale −1 |
| Single Combat | Seek out an enemy commander for a duel (see Chapter Five). The outcome directly affects the Mass Battle's direction |

### 4.6 Battlefield Event Table (d20)

At the end of each Mass Battle round, the GM rolls d20 to determine a battlefield event:

| d20 | Event | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Yokai Storm | All beastmen on the field +10 Yokai Value. Great risk of loss of control |
| 2–3 | Heavy Rain | Ranged attacks Disadvantage, fire strategy ineffective |
| 4–5 | Dense Fog | All units Scale −1 (lost / scattered) |
| 6–7 | Earthquake | Cavalry Scale −2, Siege Unit Scale +2 (walls collapse) |
| 8–9 | Lightning Strike | A random unit Scale −1d4 |
| 10–11 | No Event | — |
| 12–13 | Reinforcements Arrive | A random side gains Scale +3 |
| 14–15 | Tsukuyomi Appears | Rabbit Clan and Moon Shadow Style related characters' effects doubled |
| 16–17 | Wind Shifts | Fire strategy effect doubled or reversed |
| 18–19 | Amaterasu's Light | Rooster Clan and Phoenix Style effects doubled |
| 20 | Hundred Beasts Roar | All-field Yokai +20. All Beastification Check DCs −5. A legend is happening |

## Chapter Five: Single Combat System

### 5.1 Single Combat Overview

Single Combat is the core climax of a Mass Battle — the duel between the commanders of both sides. The outcome of a Single Combat can reverse the entire Mass Battle situation.

### 5.2 Pre-Battle Etiquette

Before Single Combat, both sides must perform a "Name Declaration" — declaring their school and blade name.

- Each side declares their school and the name of their blade
- After the declaration, each side gains +1 Giri Value (respect for the opponent's etiquette)
- If one side refuses the Name Declaration, that side suffers Disadvantage on all Single Combat Checks (dishonorable)

### 5.3 Three-Round System and the Three-Cycle Counter

Single Combat is fixed at three rounds. Each round both sides choose "Momentum" — a rock-paper-scissors-like psychological gamble:

```
Attack Momentum ──→ beats ──→ Skill Momentum ──→ beats ──→ Guard Momentum ──→ beats ──→ Attack Momentum
   (assault)              (technique)              (defense)
```

| You Choose | Opponent Chooses | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Attack Momentum | Skill Momentum | You Advantage, opponent Disadvantage |
| Skill Momentum | Guard Momentum | You Advantage, opponent Disadvantage |
| Guard Momentum | Attack Momentum | You Advantage, opponent Disadvantage |
| Same | Same | Both side normal contest |
| (unfavorable) | (favorable) | You Disadvantage, opponent Advantage |

### 5.4 Per-Round Resolution

Both sides roll "Momentum Contest":

**Momentum Value = d20 + Swordsmanship value + school technique Momentum bonus**

- Same Momentum chosen: normal contest
- Counter opponent: Advantage
- Countered by opponent: Disadvantage

**Gap Effect**:

| Momentum Gap | Effect |
|---|---|
| 0–3 | Narrow win: deal weapon base damage |
| 4–6 | Moderate win: deal weapon base damage + Swordsmanship value |
| 7–9 | Major win: deal weapon damage + Swordsmanship ×1.5, opponent Disadvantage on next round's Momentum |
| 10+ | Total dominance: deal maximum weapon damage + Swordsmanship ×2, opponent Stunned 1 round |

### 5.5 Famous Blade Bonus

Holding a famous blade grants extra bonuses in Single Combat:

| Blade Grade | Momentum Bonus | Extra Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Nameless | +0 | — |
| Fine Blade | +1 | — |
| Superior Blade | +1 | Damage +1d4 |
| Masterwork Blade | +2 | Damage +1d6 |
| Supreme Masterwork | +2 | May ignore one of the opponent's Momentum counters |
| Five Swords Under Heaven | +3 | Damage +1d8, may ignore one of the opponent's Momentum counters, holder gains Advantage on all Momentum Contests |

### 5.6 Clan Innate Influence on Single Combat

| Clan | Single Combat Special Effect |
|---|---|
| Tiger | +1 Momentum Value when choosing Attack Momentum |
| Dragon | Opponent's first Momentum Contest suffers Disadvantage |
| Horse | May cancel first-strike disadvantage (if opponent chooses first and counters you, becomes normal contest) |
| Boar | +3 attack damage on the third round |
| Dog | If this fight is to protect a companion, all Momentum Contests +1 |
| Rabbit | Under full-moon scenes, opponent must make Spirit Saving Throw at round start or be bewildered (chooses Momentum randomly) |
| Snake | On hit, adds 1d4 poison continuous damage |
| Rat | May observe the opponent's choice first, then decide own Momentum on the first round |
| Ox | +3 Damage Reduction (DR) when choosing Guard Momentum |
| Monkey | May change the chosen Momentum on the second round (but before both reveal) |
| Rooster | All Momentum Contests gain Advantage in dawn time slots |
| Sheep | Automatically recover 1d8 Hit Points (HP) after three rounds end |

### 5.7 Post-Battle Results

#### Winner's Choice:

| Choice | Effect |
|---|---|
| **Slay** | Gain the loser's blade (famous blade). Giri Value −5. Loser permanently dies. Loser's faction whole army Morale −5 |
| **Release** | Giri Value +5. Loser owes you a "Debt of Gratitude" (may demand repayment in the future). Loser's faction Morale −2 |
| **Recruit** | Requires a Spirit Contest (your Spirit vs. loser's Spirit, you gain Advantage). On success the loser joins your faction (becomes an NPC companion). Giri Value +3. Loser's loyalty is "Watching" |

### 7.2 Challenge Rating Calculation Formula

**CR = (Attack Score + Defense Score + Special Ability Score) / 3 (rounded down)**

| Score Item | Calculation |
|---|---|
| Attack Score | Expected damage per round / 3. Expected damage = hit rate × average damage |
| Defense Score | (HP / 15) + (DV − 10) / 2 + DR |
| Special Ability Score | 1–3 points per special ability (by GM judgment) |

> **Simple Rule**: CR value ≈ the level a single player character can challenge. A CR X enemy suits a 4-person party of level X.

**Batch Processing Rule** (when facing large numbers of mobs):

- Merge 4–5 identical mobs into one "cluster"
- Cluster HP = single × count × 0.7
- Cluster attack: roll hit once; for every 3 points over DV, deal one extra base damage
- When cluster HP drops below 50%, attack −1 (personnel loss)

### 7.5 Boss Design Guide

#### Three-Phase Boss Mechanism

Strong Bosses (CR 10+) are recommended to have three combat phases:

| Phase | HP Threshold | Trait |
|---|---|---|
| First Phase | 100%–66% | Standard mode. Uses 2–3 basic attacks and techniques |
| Second Phase | 66%–33% | Bestial arousal. Gains new techniques, attack pattern changes. May summon reinforcements or alter the battlefield |
| Third Phase | 33%–0% | Dying outburst. Strongest techniques unlocked, passive effects activated. Yokai uncontrolled (may attack allies) |

#### Boss Design Checklist

- [ ] Clear clan and school tags
- [ ] At least one famous blade (Fine Blade or above)
- [ ] 2–3 different types of attacks
- [ ] At least 1 area attack (AOE)
- [ ] At least 1 special mechanic (summon, terrain change, phase transition, etc.)
- [ ] Giri-related backstory (why is he the villain?)
- [ ] Meaningful post-defeat choices (slay / release / recruit)

## Chapter Eight: Worldview Overview

### 8.1 Creation Myth: The Divine-Age Chronicle of the Twelve Sword Saints

Before time began, the Beast God created the Twelve Sword Saints from its own twelve ribs — each corresponding to an archetype of the twelve zodiac signs. Each Sword Saint was granted an "understanding of the blade," and these twelve understandings later became the roots of the Twelve Zodiac Schools.

The Sword Saints did four things:
1. **Opened the Hundred Beasts Archipelago** — each split the chaos with their own Soul Blade, creating the archipelago fit for beastmen to inhabit
2. **Created their respective beast clans** — each Sword Saint created a beast clan from their own flesh and blood
3. **Sealed the Great Yokai Calamity** — together they sealed the Great Yokai Calamity born of chaos beneath Heaven Pillar Mountain
4. **Became Soul Blades** — the sealing exhausted the Sword Saints' power. They transformed themselves into twelve "Soul Blades," sleeping across the archipelago, awaiting the day of rebirth

> **Prophecy**: "When the hundred beasts again fight for Giri, the twelve Soul Blades shall awaken together, and a new Sword Saint shall be born from the flames of war — or the world shall be devoured by the Yokai Calamity."

### 8.2 Historical Timeline

| **Ancient Era** | Epoch 1–500 | Beast clans each multiply and thrive. School dōjō established. Each clan forms primitive tribes |
| **Dynasty Era** | Epoch 501–1200 | The Dragon Clan Dragon Emperor unifies the Hundred Beasts Archipelago, founding the Hundred Beasts Dynasty — the only time all clans were unified. The Dragon Emperor Mausoleum on Heaven Pillar Mountain built |
| **Warring Era** | Epoch 1201–1500 | The Dragon Emperor falls, the Hundred Beasts Dynasty collapses. The Twelve Provinces gradually form. The Blade-Smith Guild established. Four of the Five Swords Under Heaven forged in this era |
| **Warring States Era (current)** | Epoch 1501–present | Tiger Clan Daimyō Tora Arashi-zai declares "Tenka Fubu." The seal begins to loosen. Soul Blades awaken one by one. The Twelve Schools seek successors |

**Act One Giri Dilemma**:
> Your mission objective is to arrest a villager stealing province supplies. But you discover he stole to feed his hungry family. Do you carry out the order (loyal to your lord), or let him go (loyal to your own Giri)?

**Key NPCs First Appearance**:
- Your school grandmaster
- Your future main companion (NPC or player)

### Act Four: The Path of Legend (Levels 16–20)

**Theme**: Ultimate choice, the Five Swords Under Heaven, seal or liberate

#### Inter-Act Nodes

| Node | Suggested Level | Content |
|---|---|---|
| **Kaikyoku Self-Created Technique** | 16 | Create your own school technique. The school recognizes you as a future grandmaster candidate |
| **Dragon Clan Trial** | 17 | Go to Heaven Pillar Mountain. Ryūjinshi reveals the truth of the seal. You may need to choose: help the seal or exploit it? |
| **Seek the Five Swords Under Heaven** | 18 | The locations of all Five Swords Under Heaven are revealed. Head to the final trial ground. Negotiate or fight with each holder |
| **Black Moon Society Truth** | 19 | Discover the Black Moon Society's true purpose — not to release the Great Yokai Calamity, but to fuse with its power to "evolve" all beastmen. Their leader is a fallen Dragon Clan member |
| **Final Battle** | 20 | The final battle atop Heaven Pillar Mountain. Three ending routes available |

#### Final Endings (three routes)

**Route One: The Path of Sealing (canonical ending)**
- Sacrifice your own soul to strengthen the seal
- The Great Yokai Calamity is re-subdued
- The Hundred Beasts Archipelago returns to peace
- You become the legendary "Twelve Sword Saints Reborn"
- Your blade (Soul Blade) becomes a new sealing wedge, guarding the archipelago forever
- **Cost**: Your character may die or lose all power, becoming mortal (but leaving an immortal name)

**Route Two: The Path of Liberation (dark ending)**
- Accept the Black Moon Society's logic: beastmen should not suppress their nature
- Actively undo the seal, fuse with the Great Yokai Calamity's power
- All beast clans evolve into full Yokai beasts — losing the identity of "samurai," gaining unparalleled power
- The Hundred Beasts Archipelago becomes a paradise (or hell) of Yokai beasts
- You become the "god of the new world" or the "demon king who destroyed the world"
- **Cost**: Lose all Giri, Blade Bond resets to zero, all bonds severed

**Route Three: The Path of Transcendence (hidden ending, requires specific conditions)**
- **Condition**: Hold all five of the Five Swords Under Heaven, Blade Bond 100, Giri Value 20+, school grandmaster qualification
- You find the third path: neither sealing nor liberation — but "transformation"
- Transform the Great Yokai Calamity's power into pure Sword Aura, pour it back into the Hundred Beasts Archipelago
- The Great Yokai Calamity vanishes, all beast clans' Yokai becomes Sword Aura — beastmen are no longer beastmen, but "Blade Men"
- The Hundred Beasts Archipelago enters a new era — "the Age of the Blade"
- You are honored as "the one who transcended the Twelve Sword Saints"
- **Cost**: You must completely abandon your beast clan identity and all innate skills — becoming "clanless"

## Chapter Eleven: Optional Rules and Variants

### 11.1 Dragon Clan Exclusive Rules

#### Dragon Blood Awakening (plot-triggered)

Dragon Clan characters may undergo Dragon Blood Awakening at specific plot nodes (GM adjudicates):

| Before Awakening | After Awakening |
|---|---|
| No flight ability | Gain flight (Movement 8 squares, requires main action to maintain) |
| Dragon Scales DR 1 | Dragon Scales DR 3 |
| Dragon Breath 2d6 | Dragon Breath 3d6 |

**Cost of Dragon Blood**: After awakening, each use of an innate skill doubles the Yokai gain. The stronger the dragon blood, the harder the bestial nature to control.

#### The Dragon's Solitude

Dragon Clan cannot use the "Assist" action. In multiplayer games, when other player characters make a joint Check with a Dragon Clan character, they suffer Disadvantage (the dragon's aura repels cooperation).

### 11.2 Rōnin Rules

A player may become a Rōnin (masterless samurai) during play, declaring it at any time or being one from origin.

| Rōnin Advantage | Rōnin Disadvantage |
|---|---|
| Free body: no Giri bond constraints, may switch factions freely | Masterless dog: Disadvantage on negotiation Checks with any Daimyō faction |
| Lone Wolf Soul: +1 attack when fighting alone, +50% Giri Value gain | Masterless blade: Blade Bond growth speed halved |
| Rōnin Network: may contact other Rōnin for information | Cannot receive province stipends, financially troubled |

### 11.3 Dual-School Cultivation

#### Cultivation Conditions
- Main school reaches Okuden (Cultivation Points 60+)
- Obtain the second school grandmaster's consent
- The two schools' conflict level ≤ 2 (conflict level 3 combinations cannot be cultivated simultaneously)

#### Cultivation Rules
- The second school starts from Shoden (Cultivation Points counted from 0)
- Cannot use two schools' active techniques in the same round
- Fusion techniques: after Kaikyoku, may create a unique technique fusing both schools' traits

### 11.4 Fallen Samurai and Cursed Blades (dark route)

#### Three Stages of Fall

| Stage | Trigger Condition | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Shadow of Giri | Accumulate 2 Giri-Breaches | Disadvantage on Spirit Checks. Yokai fade halved |
| Bestial Liberation | Accumulate 2 more Giri-Breaches (total 4) | Swordsmanship +2, Yokai Power +2, but Spirit −4. Yokai Value never drops below 30 |
| The Fallen | One more major Giri-Breach (total 5+) | Irreversible. Character becomes an NPC, GM takes over. Becomes the scenario's villain |

#### Known Cursed Blades

| Cursed Blade | Effect | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| Bloodstain | Restore HP on killing an enemy (50% of the slain target's HP) | +10 Yokai per enemy killed |
| Mad Bone | Critical range 17–20 | Cannot assume a defense stance |
| Lament | Holder immune to all mental attacks | Holder cannot perceive any being's emotions (all social Checks Disadvantage) |
| Black Moon (newly forged) | Blade of the Black Moon Society leader. Effect unknown | Presumed: manipulates the holder's will |

> **Path of Redemption**: Before the third stage (The Fallen), a character may reverse the fall by completing an Atonement task designed by the GM. After entering The Fallen stage it is irreversible — a new character can only be created in a new campaign.

## Chapter Thirteen: Forging System

### 13.1 Forging Overview

Forging is one of the most revered arts in Beastman Warring States. Only characters with the "Forging" skill may forge famous blades. The Ox Clan Blade-Smith Guild (Takumi-ryō) monopolizes Soul-Steel smelting tech, but individual smiths may still master basic forging through cultivation.

### 13.2 Forging Requirements

| Forging Target | Required Skill | DC | Required Materials | Time Needed | Failure Consequence |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Repair a damaged Nameless blade | Forging Shoden | 12 | Iron ore ×1 | 1 day | Materials damaged |
| Forge a Nameless blade | Forging Chūden | 15 | Iron ore ×3 | 3 days | Materials damaged, product is damaged quality |
| Forge a Fine Blade | Forging Okuden | 20 | Soul-Steel Shard ×1, iron ore ×5 | 7 days | Soul-Steel Shard damaged, ordinary materials recoverable |
| Forge a Superior Blade | Forging Kaiden | 24 | Soul-Steel Shard ×2, iron ore ×5 | 14 days | Half the Soul-Steel Shards damaged |
| Forge a Masterwork Blade | Forging Kaiden | 24 | Soul-Steel Shard ×3, Dragon-Vein Stone ×1 | 30 days | All materials damaged, smith takes 3d6 mental damage |
| Forge a Supreme Masterwork | Forging Kaiden | 30+ | Soul-Steel Shard ×5, Dragon-Vein Stone ×2 | 90 days | All materials damaged, smith takes 5d6 mental damage, lifespan reduced by 1 year |

### 13.3 Gaining Soul-Steel Shards

Soul-Steel Shards cannot be bought, only obtained through the following:
- Scenario rewards (e.g., S1 Shadow of the Wailing Plains, defeating a Yokai-beast)
- After defeating an enemy commander holding a Fine Blade or above, may disassemble their blade for 1d3 shards
- 7 days of meditation at a Dragon-Vein node (Spirit DC 22), success reveals a nearby Soul-Steel vein
- Quests from the Ox Clan Blade-Smith Guild

### 13.4 Forging Rite

Forging a Fine Blade or above requires a rite:

1. **Material Selection**: Prepare sufficient Soul-Steel Shards
2. **Soul Fire Kindling**: At a Dragon-Vein node or using a Dragon-Vein Stone, light the soul fire (requires Spirit DC 15)
3. **Seven Days and Nights of Hammering**: Make a Forging Check daily; cumulative successes must be ≥ half the required days
4. **Blood Quench**: The smith drips their own blood (takes 1d6 damage), granting the blade initial Blade Bond
5. **Blade Inscription Naming**: Name the blade. At this point the GM may determine the blade's Blade Nature based on the forging process

### 13.5 Bestowing Blade Inscriptions

Blade Inscriptions may be bestowed during forging, or awaken naturally later at key events (see Player Rulebook Chapter Seven). Bestowing an inscription during forging requires meeting the trigger condition and consuming one extra Soul-Steel Shard.

## Chapter Fourteen: School Trial Framework

### 14.1 Trial Overview

Key nodes in school cultivation require passing a trial. The following is a framework for the GM to design trials:

### 14.2 Trial Guide by Tier

| Tier | Trial Type | Suggested Difficulty | Typical Content |
|---|---|---|---|
| Okuden Trial (Cultivation 60+) | Skill Trial | Medium | Demonstrate understanding of the school's philosophy before the grandmaster. E.g., Tiger Might Style: defeat a same-level opponent's simulated battle within 3 rounds |
| Gokuden Trial (Cultivation 100+) | Real Combat Trial | Hard | In real combat, use a secret technique to defeat a strong foe of CR ≥ own level + 2. The enemy must be a real threat (not practice) |
| Grandmaster Trial (Cultivation 200+) | Lineage Trial | Very Hard | Make a contribution to the school worthy of its history. E.g.: defeat a foe threatening the school, recover lost school secrets, create a new technique recognized by the grandmaster |

### 14.3 Trial Design Principles

- **Trials should reflect the school's philosophy**: Tiger Might Style's trial should be an open frontal duel, Shadowmeld Style's trial may be a silent assassination
- **Failure should not be the end**: After failing a trial, may challenge again after +20 Cultivation Points
- **Giri involvement**: Meaningful trials should involve a Giri dilemma (e.g., the trial target is an NPC the player knows)

## Chapter Fifteen: Custom Technique Framework

### 15.1 Custom Technique Rules

When a character reaches Kaikyoku (Cultivation Points 150+), they may create their own school technique. The GM and player negotiate the design.

### 15.2 Design Parameters

| Technique Tier | Yokai Pool Cost Range | Damage Range (single target) | Attachable Effects |
|---|---|---|---|
| Shoden-tier (basic) | 1–2 | Weapon damage + 1d6 | 1 small support effect (e.g., move +5 squares) |
| Chūden-tier (advanced) | 3–4 | Weapon damage + 2d6 | 1 medium effect (e.g., Stun, Advantage, DR reduction) |
| Okuden-tier (secret) | 5–7 | Weapon damage + 3d6 | 2 effects or 1 large effect (e.g., AOE, multi-attack) |
| Grandmaster-tier (final secret) | 8–12 | Weapon damage ×2 + 3d8 | 2 large effects + 1 plot-tier effect |

### 15.3 Balance Checklist

- [ ] Does the Yokai Pool cost match the effect? (Compare with existing same-tier techniques)
- [ ] Are there clear usage limits? (once per battle / must meet conditions)
- [ ] Does it preserve the school's philosophical traits?
- [ ] Is there room for clan variants?
- [ ] Is it over- or under-powered vs. existing same-tier techniques? (allow within ±20%)

## Chapter Sixteen: Mounted Combat Consolidation

### 16.1 Mount Requirements

- Requires a warhorse (500 ryō) or famous horse (2000 ryō), or obtained in plot
- Requires Riding skill at Shoden or above
- Requires sufficiently open space (cavalry cannot perform indoors or in narrow caves)

### 16.2 Rules While Mounted

| Item | Rule |
|---|---|
| Movement | Use the mount's Movement (warhorse 12 squares, famous horse 14 squares) in place of your own |
| Attack | Using a tachi while mounted gains Advantage; using a Japanese bow does not (requires standing to shoot); long spear deals +1d6 damage vs. foot targets |
| Being attacked | Enemies may choose to attack the rider or the mount. Mount HP = 20 (warhorse) / 30 (famous horse); when the mount falls, the rider must make Agility Saving Throw DC 15 or be knocked prone |
| Mounted Charge | After moving in a straight line at least 6 squares then attacking, damage +1d8 (requires Riding Chūden or above) |
| Mounted Techniques | Gale Style techniques +1 while mounted; Immovable Style techniques unusable while mounted (requires firm footing) |

### 16.3 Horse Clan Mount Advantage

Horse Clan beastmen gain the following when mounted:
- May fight mounted without the Riding skill (innate ability)
- +2 Initiative while mounted
- Mount HP +10 (Horse Clan and horses have natural resonance)

## Chapter Seventeen: Reputation System

### 17.1 Reputation Overview

Reputation represents a character's fame and influence within a specific faction. Reputation ranges from −10 (sworn enemy) to +10 (legendary).

### 17.2 Reputation Tiers

| Reputation Value | Tier | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| −10～−6 | Sworn Enemy | That faction will actively hunt you |
| −5～−1 | Disliked | Members of that faction treat you poorly, transaction prices +50% |
| 0 | Neutral | Default state |
| 1–3 | Friendly | May receive basic hospitality within that faction's territory |
| 4–6 | Respected | May request minor assistance (e.g., information, supplies); transaction prices −20% |
| 7–9 | Revered | May request medium assistance (e.g., military support, special equipment) |
| 10 | Legendary | May request major assistance (e.g., mobilizing armies); that faction may fight for you |

### 17.3 Gaining and Losing Reputation

| Action | Reputation Change |
|---|---|
| Complete that faction's missions | +1～+3 |
| Win a victory for that faction in Mass Battle | +2～+5 |
| Save that faction's important NPC | +3 |
| Attack that faction's members | −1～−5 |
| Betray a pact with that faction | −3～−7 |
| Publicly insult that faction's Daimyō | −5 |

### 17.4 Multi-Faction Reputation

A player may hold Reputation with multiple factions simultaneously. But note:
- Reputation with opposing factions (e.g., Tiger Clan vs. Dog Clan) influences each other — raising one may lower the other
- The GM should record the player's Reputation changes in major factions
- Reputation with neutral factions (Rat, Monkey, Boar Clans) is calculated independently
