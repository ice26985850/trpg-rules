# Dead by Daylight　Tabletop Role-Playing Game　Game Master Rulebook

> You are not the enemy. You are the Emcee of The Entity.

This book is the **Game Master (GM) Rulebook** for the tabletop role-playing game (TRPG) adaptation of *Dead by Daylight (DbD)*. Here you will find how to run the Killer, build Trials, pace the rhythm of fear, and the complete data for all eight Realms.

The default configuration of this system is the **"Solo Trial" — 1 Player paired with 1 Game Master (GM)**. Chapter Five is devoted entirely to the one-on-one table-running technique; it is the chapter you should know best.

This book assumes you have read the *Player Rulebook*. Any rule already written for the player side is not repeated here — only what you additionally need to know, plus what the players should not know.

# Introduction　You Are Not the Enemy

Let's start with the one thing that decides whether the whole game succeeds or fails.

**You run the Killer, but you are not the players' opponent.**

That sounds like a platitude, but it is the hardest operating rule there is. This system hands you the avatar's stats so you can **manufacture the feeling of being hunted** — not so you can win. You could win at any moment: your movement is faster than the Survivors', your attacks always hit, and you know the location of every pallet on the map. If you wanted to, you could end the game by round six.

Then everyone would pack up and go home, and nobody would have had fun.

Your real job is: **to make the player, at round nineteen, with half a Generator left, injured, and hearing a heartbeat approach from the left, make a decision they themselves aren't sure is right.**

That moment doesn't happen on its own. You arrange it.

## Three things to know before you start

### I. You play four roles at once

| Role | What you are doing |
|---|---|
| **Avatar** | Chase, slash, hook. Use the stats, not your emotions |
| **Narration** | Describe the Fog, the sounds, what is underfoot. This is the source of horror |
| **Referee** | Adjudicate rules disputes. **Be fast — don't spend more than thirty seconds looking something up at the table** |
| **Non-Player Character (NPC)** | Campfire Companion, Whisperer, Echo Companion |

The easiest to neglect is **Narration**. New Game Masters (GMs) pour all their energy into running the avatar, and the player ends up hearing only "I attack you, hit, you're injured." That isn't scary; it's a spreadsheet.

Horror comes from the things you describe that **don't require a roll**: the sound of someone wiping their hands in the distance, a scarecrow in the cornfield angled differently from the others, a fresh scratch on the Generator you're repairing.

### II. The heartbeat is your strongest tool

Terror Radius (TR) is not just a number — it is a **metronome you must actively announce**.

When the avatar enters a player's Terror Radius (TR), you say so. When the avatar leaves, you say so. Players can't hear the heartbeat; they can only hear you.

> **Example**:
> "You're repairing the third Generator. Progress 14 points."
> (pause)
> "…You hear it. Faint, but definitely there."
> "Still repairing?"

Those five seconds of silence do more than any attack roll.

### III. Give hope so you can take it away

This system has several mechanics that are **mandatory giveaways** — you cannot skip them because "the player is already miserable" or "this is too easy":

- Entering the second and third stages of the Escalation Phase **requires** paying out Hope Point (HOP)
- Entering the third stage **permanently lowers** the Hatch search Difficulty Class (DC) by 3
- When the player rescues an Echo Companion, you **must** give 1 Hope Point (HOP)

These are not compensation for the player; they are **pacing tools**. A player with Hope Point (HOP) in hand dares to make risky decisions; a player who feels they have zero chance starts to phone it in, and then your horror becomes mere frustration.

> **⚠ Common Misconception**: Stacking pressure from round one to the last round. Terror is a **waveform**, not a slope. You need passages where the player catches their breath; only after that breath does the next heartbeat become terrifying.

## One last line

The theme of this game is "a person who stops at the door and looks back."

The purpose of your avatar is to make that look-back **cost something**. Not to make it impossible.

If the player never dares to look back, you pressed too hard.
If the player looks back easily every time, you pressed too softly.

**When the player looks back, pays the price, and then says "worth it" — that is when you ran it right.**

Turn to Chapter One.

# Chapter One　Running an Asymmetric Trial

The Introduction told you "you are not the enemy." This chapter tells you what you actually are, if not the enemy.

The answer: **You are the Emcee of The Entity (The Entity).**

The Emcee is not the lead, nor the judge. The Emcee decides when the lights dim, who takes the stage, when the applause stops. The **rhythm** of the whole ritual is in your hands, but the **outcome** is not — the outcome belongs to the dice and the players' choices.

This chapter gives you six things: your position, the three-phase structure of terror, the time budget of a Trial, the opening procedures for the three configurations, the criteria for releasing and closing the net, and the rules of information disclosure. After reading, you can open a decent Trial without inspiration.

---

## 1-1　Your position: the Emcee of The Entity (The Entity)

### Three mindsets

| # | Mindset | What it means at the table |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **You are not playing an enemy, you are playing a kind of weather** | The avatar should have no "targeting someone" emotion (unless the scenario specifies). It should be like a typhoon — predictable, lawful, but unstoppable. Players hate the typhoon, not you |
| 2 | **Fear comes from "not knowing," not from "can't win"** | One sentence you withhold builds more fear than +3 damage. **Whenever you want to raise a Difficulty Class (DC), first ask whether you can instead "give less information"** |
| 3 | **Your goal is not to win, but to make the player escape at the very last moment** | Ideal ending: the last Generator, the last round, the last check. This can be arranged; it is not luck |

### Thirty-second ruling law

The most atmosphere-killing thing at the table is not a player character dying — it's you flipping through a book for two minutes.

**Thirty-Second Ruling Law**:

- **Trigger**: a rules dispute arises at the table and you can't find the explicit text within thirty seconds.
- **Cost**: none.
- **Adjudication**: none (no roll).
- **Result**: always adopt the interpretation **most favorable to the player**, write the disputed point in the GM Screen's memo column, and look it up after the session. Use the corrected ruling next session.

**⚠ Common Misconception**: "Most favorable to the player" is not the same as "going easy." What you yield is the **interpretation of ambiguous rules**, not the Difficulty Class (DC), not the avatar's stats. Once declared, DC and stats are non-negotiable — that is your credibility.

### Two things you may not touch

1. **You may not secretly change a declared Difficulty Class (DC).** You may set the DC as tricky as you like before declaring it; once declared, it's a contract.
2. **You may not secretly change a die already rolled.** Your dice are rolled outside the Screen, where the players can see them. **The avatar's dice are public; only the avatar's intent may be hidden** — this contrast is the source of all your credibility.

> **Design Note**: The most common collapse in asymmetric running is when players start to suspect you're favoring yourself. The moment you roll a die openly at the table and lose once, that suspicion vanishes — and for the rest of the night you can manufacture as much fear as you want.

## 1-2　The three-phase structure of terror

A Trial is cut into three phases. **The phases are not equal in length**, nor measured by the clock — they are measured by **events**.

| Phase | Name | Start | End | Share of total rounds |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Phase 1 | **Exploration Phase** | The Fog lifts | First heartbeat sounds | ~15% |
| Phase 2 | **Pressure Phase** | First heartbeat | Reaching the last Generator of the target count | ~65% |
| Phase 3 | **Endgame Phase** | Exit Gates power on | Someone escapes or all are eliminated | ~20% |

### What you should do in each phase

| Phase | You should | You should not |
|---|---|---|
| **Exploration Phase** | Describe the environment (three-senses principle). Let the players walk, find Generators, and build a sense of space themselves. **No Terror Radius (TR) at all.** This phase is the control group for "safety" | Chase from the opening. Players panic, but they don't fear — **fear needs safety as its base layer first** |
| **Pressure Phase** | Heartbeat approaches band by band from Far (FAR). The first chase **should let the player escape** (but barely). Mid-phase: hooking, rescues, Entity Hunger (EH) rising. **This phase should hurt** | Press all the way through. Terror is a waveform, not a slope. Within the Pressure Phase there must be at least one "avatar clearly leaves for two rounds" breather |
| **Endgame Phase** | Settle every round. Exit Gate segments, Endgame Collapse countdown, Hatch. **Push the pace to maximum; don't let the players deliberate more than a minute** | Drag. The endgame should be fast, chaotic, with someone deciding at the last round whether to look back |

### Table signal: which phase are you in

Don't watch the clock; watch the players.

| What you see | This means | What you should do |
|---|---|---|
| Player asks "what's in this room" | Still in Exploration Phase | Give details, generously. **Generosity now is capital for later** |
| Players start confirming Generator positions to each other | Exploration Phase ending | Start knocking next round |
| Players' speech gets shorter, down to single phrases | Pressure Phase is working | Maintain; don't add more |
| Players start telling jokes | Pressure dropped | Add-pressure signal, see table below |
| Player doesn't declare an action two rounds running | **Danger**: a prelude to giving up | Release signal — immediately trigger the Mercy Valve |
| Player starts calculating math, talking "efficiency" | They've turned the Trial into a puzzle flow | Trigger the Cruelty Valve |

### Five add-pressure signals

1. Players start chatting and joking → knock on the table, start the heartbeat.
2. Players' actions become efficient and in sync → trigger an Entity Hunger (EH) effect.
3. No one has been chased for two rounds straight → avatar appears at the most inconvenient spot.
4. Only one Generator left to reach target count → avatar immediately abandons its current target and falls back to defend.
5. Someone is hoarding lots of Hope Point (HOP) → **The Entity can smell it.** Prioritize chasing that person.

### Five release-pressure signals

1. The same player character has been hooked twice in a row → avatar switches target.
2. Player starts going silent, not declaring actions → **Give a small victory immediately**: let them open a Chest, let the avatar misstep.
3. Only one player left healthy → let the avatar go kick a Generator. This is plausible and buys the player two rounds of breathing room.
4. Player says something great or does something cool → **Reward it.** Let them succeed.
5. Forty minutes have passed with no one laughing → horror films need comic relief too. Give an absurd detail.

**【Solo Trial】** Setup 1 (1 Player + 1 Game Master (GM)) has no "banter between teammates" to use as a pressure gauge, so the two signal tables above must be reread from **the single player's body**: they push their chair back, start checking their phone, repeat something you just said — any one of these three is a release signal. Conversely, leaning forward, hand on the dice not rolling, asking you to repeat an environment description — these three mean "they're still in it," and you can keep pressing. The three-phase structure of the Solo Trial holds the same, but the Exploration Phase should be stretched to ~20% (see Chapter Five "Solo Trial").

> **Example**: Three-player game, round 9. Player A says "I'll go fix the north Generator, you two cover the west," and Player B immediately answers "Okay." What you see is **synergy** — add-pressure signal #2.
> You don't raise the DC; you do one thing: move the Trapper (TRP)'s patrol to beside the north Generator, then say: "When you get to the north Generator, it's already running. Someone's been here. **But this one, none of you have touched yet.**"
> The players' synergy instantly turns to suspicion. You changed not a single number.

## 1-3　The time structure of a Trial

### Rounds and table time

| Player Survivor count (N) | Standard total rounds | Table real time | Exploration | Pressure | Endgame |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 (Solo Trial) | 25 rounds | 60–75 min | 4–5 rounds | 15–17 rounds | 4–6 rounds |
| 2 | 22 rounds | 70–85 min | 3–4 rounds | 14–15 rounds | 4–5 rounds |
| 3 | 20 rounds | 80–95 min | 3 rounds | 13 rounds | 4 rounds |
| 4 | 18 rounds | 90–110 min | 2–3 rounds | 11–12 rounds | 3–4 rounds |

**The more players, the fewer rounds but the longer the time.** A round running four people's declarations naturally eats table time; a Solo Trial round takes only ninety seconds, so rounds can be given generously.

**⚠ Common Misconception**: The "standard total rounds" above is not a hard cap, it's **your budget**. Exceeding the budget by up to two rounds is normal; exceeding by five means you missed an acceleration signal.

### Acceleration ruling

**Acceleration Ruling**:

- **Trigger**: any of the following — (1) actual rounds already exceed the standard total by 3 or more; (2) three rounds running with no Generator Progress Point (PP) change; (3) table real time already exceeds the table above's max by 20 minutes.
- **Cost**: none. You don't spend Pressure Point (PRE); this is a pacing privilege.
- **Adjudication**: none (no roll).
- **Result**: choose **one** of the three acceleration tools below and do not reuse the same one before the session ends.

### Three acceleration tools

| Tool | How | Side effect |
|---|---|---|
| **Power On** | Declare "one unfinished Generator has been pushed to 23 Progress Points (PP) by an Echo Companion or teammate" (the third threshold of the progress-stage protection) | You hand over progress. Players feel they got a gift — a good thing |
| **Urge** | Avatar doesn't chase anyone for two rounds, instead circles the map center, and announces the heartbeat Distance Band (DB) once per round | Players get forced to split up; speed increases |
| **Open the Door** | Early-trigger the next tier of Entity Hunger (EH), letting the Trial itself start squeezing the players | The harshest of the three. Before using it, confirm the whole team still has Hope Point (HOP) |

**⚠ Common Misconception**: Acceleration tools **do not include "directly lower the Difficulty Class (DC)."** Lowering the DC makes players realize you're going easy, while the three tools above can all be explained as changes in the world itself.

**【Solo Trial】** Setup 1 most easily triggers acceleration clause (2) — one person repairing, a bad luck streak can hit zero for three rounds straight. Then **prioritize "Power On"**: push the Generator an Echo Companion is working on to 23 Progress Points (PP), and cover it in one line: "The lights at Forty-Seven's flickered. It's still counting." You accelerate and, in passing, prove the Echo Companion is actually working.

> **Example**: Four-player game, standard 18 rounds, now round 22, two Generators still short. Acceleration clause (1) holds.
> You choose "Urge": for the next two rounds, the Hillbilly (HBY) chases no one, only circles the central Wailing Ironworks. You announce each round: "Heartbeat at Far (FAR)." "Heartbeat still at Far (FAR), but the bearing changed."
> The four players, who had three crammed on one machine (total efficiency only 210%), are now forced apart. Both finish on round 24. **You gave nothing; you merely temporarily removed the avatar from the board.**

## 1-4　Opening procedures for the three configurations

### Universal five-step opening (all three configurations)

| Step | Content |
|---|---|
| 1 | **Describe the fire.** Every session, redescribe the Campfire, and never the same way. The fire's color, sound, and temperature are metaphors for this session's theme |
| 2 | Each player says in one sentence "one thing my character remembers right now" |
| 3 | A Campfire Companion (NPC) speaks, dropping this session's hook |
| 4 | Players may burn an Offering |
| 5 | **The Fog rises. You start knocking.** |

Only after step 5 do you enter each configuration's first round.

### Setup 1 (Solo Trial) first round

**At the table: 1 Player + you.**

| Order | What you do |
|---|---|
| 1 | Place the **Killer Behavior Table** (the fixed patrol rules) behind the Screen. **Once written, this table may not be modified for the whole session** |
| 2 | Place the two Echo Companions' markers on the map, each two zones away from the player |
| 3 | Declare: Generator target count 2, 4 spawned on the map, Exit Gate open at 3 segments |
| 4 | **No heartbeat announcement.** The Exploration Phase first round has no Terror Radius (TR) |
| 5 | Give a three-sense environment description (one visual, one auditory, one tactile sentence), then push the zone scene card to the player |
| 6 | Player declares actions (4 Action Point (AP)). You move the avatar per the Killer Behavior Table, **without looking at the player's marker** |
| 7 | End of round: give the Echo Companion one line. **Just one** |

### Setup 2 (Squad Trial) first round

**At the table: 2–4 Players + you.**

| Order | What you do |
|---|---|
| 1 | Lay out the map by player Survivor count (N): Generator, Pallet, Window, Hook, Locker, Chest counts per the player-count scaling table (see Chapter Three "Building Trials") |
| 2 | Hand out zone cards; each player places their character marker **face down** on a zone card. You place the avatar marker face down too |
| 3 | Declare Generator target count and Exit Gate segments. **Do not declare the Hatch location** |
| 4 | **No heartbeat announcement** (Exploration Phase) |
| 5 | In seat order, each player declares actions. **60-second limit per person**; timeout counts as "on guard in place" |
| 6 | Reveal only the parts where "two or more markers are on the same zone card." The rest stay face down |
| 7 | End of round: if no player character performed an "altruistic hope" action this round (see P4 World and Hope), the Entity Hunger (EH) track +1; round one is fixed at +1. Advance the token publicly |

### Setup 3 (Versus Trial) first round

**At the table: 1 Killer player + 1–3 Survivor players + you. In this configuration you are a pure referee, not running the avatar.**

| Order | What you do |
|---|---|
| 1 | **Ask the Killer player to leave the table.** To the kitchen, to the balcony, or put on noise-cancelling headphones. This is not ceremony, it's a rule — **otherwise the entire asymmetric information system collapses on the spot** |
| 2 | With the Survivor players, complete the setup and initial positions (face down) |
| 3 | Ask the Killer player back, and privately give them the avatar stat card, Terror Radius (TR), and Power description. **Do not give them the Survivors' positions** |
| 4 | Announce the Mercy Valve clause, and **require the Killer player to copy it onto their character card** |
| 5 | You hold the Hope Point (HOP) and Entity Hunger (EH). **The Killer player can see Entity Hunger (EH), but not individual players' Hope Point (HOP)** |
| 6 | Begin. Your job from here becomes "feeding information": private descriptions to each side, via notepaper or private message |
| 7 | End of round: you (not the Killer player) announce the heartbeat Distance Band (DB) |

**⚠ Common Misconception**: In Setup 3 you're not idle. You have more to do than running the avatar — dual-line description, dispute adjudication, managing the Killer player's emotions. **If there are only four people at the table, Setup 3 is the most exhausting for the Game Master (GM).**

> **Example**: Solo Trial opening. You've already written the Killer Behavior Table: "Patrol sequence: Moaning Warehouse → Wailing Ironworks → Sheltering Woods → Suffocation Pit → Coal Tower → back to start." The player's marker is at the Moaning Warehouse, your avatar marker is at the Coal Tower — **the opposite end of the sequence**.
> You don't announce the heartbeat. You say: "Rust-red sheet-metal walls gleam with oil under the moonlight. In the distance, metal creaks on its own, though there's no wind. Where you step is wet coal ash, sinking half an inch into your sole."
> Player asks: "Can I hear a heartbeat?"
> You answer: "No."

> You answer: "No."
> **That one "No" is the most important line you'll say all night.** Because it gives meaning to the heartbeat on round three.

## 1-5　When to let him escape, when to close the net

This section gives **criteria**, not feelings. You don't need instinct; just follow the text.

### Mercy Valve

**Mercy Valve**:

- **Trigger**: any of the following — (1) the same player character hooked 2+ times within 30 minutes; (2) the team's total Hope Point (HOP) drops below half its starting total; (3) any player goes 3 rounds straight without an effective action.
- **Cost**: none. **This is a mandatory clause, not advice.**
- **Adjudication**: none (no roll).
- **Result**: you **must** move the avatar away from that zone next round and stay out for at least 2 rounds. World reason: The Entity demands even sampling, no favoritism.

### Cruelty Valve

**Cruelty Valve**:

- **Trigger**: any of the following — (1) Generator target count already reached, yet no player character is below Injured; (2) Entity Hunger (EH) ≥ 7 but two rounds straight with no effect triggered; (3) the players have started treating the Trial as a "puzzle flow" rather than an escape.
- **Cost**: none.
- **Adjudication**: none (no roll).
- **Result**: you **should** add pressure, choose one — avatar immediately falls back to defend the Exit Gate, early-trigger one tier of Entity Hunger (EH), or deploy a Realm Creation not yet on the field this session.

### Closing-Net Criterion table

"Closing the net" = you decide not to let him go this time, but to knock him down, pick him up, and hook him.

| Closing net | Condition (all must hold to close) |
|---|---|
| ✅ May close | The player has already escaped at least one chase; AND they still hold ≥1 Hope Point (HOP); AND they've been hooked fewer than 2 times; AND the Mercy Valve is not triggered |
| ❌ Don't close | This is their first time being chased this session; OR their Hope Point (HOP) = 0; OR they've already been hooked 2 times; OR the Mercy Valve is active |

**The first chase must let him escape.** No exception. Establishing "I can't win" too early makes the player phone it in for the rest of the night.

### Three correct ways to let him escape

If you go easy and get caught, it backfires. Use these three; the player will only feel the world has logic.

| Method | How | Player's feeling |
|---|---|---|
| **Overshoot** | The avatar, while chasing, "overshoots from its own momentum" — Distance Band (DB) +1 | "It makes mistakes too" |
| **Redirect** | The avatar, per the Killer Behavior Table priority order, turns to a higher-priority target (Generator roar, Hook defense) | "It has its rules" |
| **Stand Down** | The avatar walks to Contact (CON) band, **looks at him once, then turns and walks away** | This one is the scariest. The player will wonder all night "why" |

**⚠ Common Misconception**: **The avatar doesn't miss; the avatar "chooses not to chase."** Any miss should be re-narrated as "it stops, looks at you once, then walks away." The moment you show an emotional "ah, missed" reaction at the table, the avatar degrades from weather into a guy who misses — the horror vanishes on the spot.

**【Solo Trial】** Setup 1, with no teammates to share the load, tightens the closing criteria one notch further: **within a single Trial, a player character may be hooked at most twice.** On the third knockdown, the avatar **must** switch to "drop him where he lies and leave" — world reason: The Entity wants to play longer. Also, before opening you must decide which two phases this session's two "obvious misses" fall in, and write them on the Screen. (Full text in Chapter Five "Solo Trial"'s Three-Strike Law and Half Law.)

> **Example**: Solo Trial round 6. The player character is chased for the first time; Distance Band (DB) drops from Mid (MID) to Near (NEA). You check the Closing-Net Criterion table: "first time being chased this session" — ❌ don't close.
> He drops a Pallet, DC 9 + Fear Pressure (FP) 5 = 14. He rolls 9 and 7, total 16, plus Agility (AGI) bonus +2, Parkour (PRK) 2 ranks, Check Total (CT) 20, Margin of Success (MoS) +6 — **Critical Success (CritS)**. Stun 2 rounds, Distance Band (DB) +2.
> He won this himself; you gave nothing. **But if he'd rolled 4 and 3, you'd have to let him go via "overshoot." Criteria first, dice after — order cannot reverse.**

## 1-6　Information disclosure principles

### Three-layer information model

| Layer | Name | Content | Who knows |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Layer 1** | Character perception layer | What the character's five senses can detect: heartbeat, footsteps, crows, light, blood | That character's player |
| **Layer 2** | Faction-shared layer | Survivors: Generator Progress Point (PP), Hook positions. Avatar: Scratch Marks, blood, destruction traces | Same-faction members (must be present or hold the relevant Perk) |
| **Layer 3** | Referee layer | Avatar's actual position, true Entity Hunger (EH) value, Hatch location, scenario truth | Only you |

### Must be public

Placed in the center of the table, visible to all:

- Generator Progress Point (PP) track
- Each Survivor's Health State (HS): Healthy / Injured / Dying
- Hook count
- **Entity Hunger (EH) track**
- Campfire Ember jar

### Must be hidden

Behind the Screen or with face-down cards:

- Avatar's current zone
- Avatar's next-round intent
- Hatch location
- Each Survivor's current zone (**in a Squad Trial, players don't know each other's either**)

> **Design Note**: Entity Hunger (EH) must be public, because it's a pressure gauge. Players watch the track climb square by square and scare themselves — far more effective than you secretly raising the DC. And only public can players learn "rescuing lowers it."

### Listening

Information is traded, not given. So "listening" itself is an action that costs.

**Listening**:

- **Trigger**: the player declares they stop and listen carefully. They must not have performed a sustained action this round yet.
- **Cost**: 1 Action Point (AP).
- **Adjudication**: 2d10 + Focus (FOC) bonus + Insight (INS) Skill Level vs Difficulty Class (DC) 10 + Fear Pressure (FP).
- **Result**:
  - **Critical Success (CritS)** — you give the avatar's **Distance Band (DB) + bearing**, and additionally tell what the avatar is doing (moving / breaking / carrying).
  - **Success (Succ)** — you give the avatar's **Distance Band (DB)**, no bearing.
  - **Failure (Fail)** — you only answer "You don't know." No follow-up.
  - **Critical Failure (CritF)** — you give a **wrong bearing**, in exactly the same tone. Their remaining Action Point (AP) this round drops to zero.

### Disclosure timing table

| Timing | What you should say | What you must never say |
|---|---|---|
| Start of each round | "You hear a heartbeat. Faint." | "The avatar is in the north warehouse." |
| Player fails a repair (Fail) | "The Generator erupts in a loud bang. You don't know if anyone heard." | "It's coming for you." |
| Avatar enters same zone | "The heartbeat is very loud. The other side of the wall." | "It's three squares to your left." |
| Player is hit | "You can't see what smashed into you. You're bleeding." | "You took 6 damage." (push the state counter; don't say it) |
| Teammate hooked | "A scream from far away, then it cuts off. You can't tell who." | "Xiao Ming is hooked on the west Hook." |
| Player asks directly | "You may spend 1 Action Point (AP) to listen carefully." → then give | Give directly |

**【Solo Trial】** Setup 1's "teammate hooked" row is replaced by **Echo Companion hooked**: "There's a voice counting. It stops halfway. Then starts over from one." — you don't say who, don't say where. The player will get up and go find them. Also, the Solo Trial player has no teammates to trade with, so **Listening is used far more often than in a Squad Trial**; be ready to be asked every round, and answer the same thing in a different sentence each time.

**⚠ Common Misconception**: "Mustn't say" is not the same as "lie." You may refuse to answer, answer beside the point, answer only the literal question asked — **but you may not volunteer false information** — the only exceptions are Listening's Critical Failure (CritF) and when the avatar's Tracking check falls 5+ below the passive Stealth value (PSTL), where the rules explicitly permit false intel. Only lie when the rules let you; every other sentence must be true. Hold this line and players will fear what you say.

> **Example**: Squad Trial round 12. Player B asks: "Can I hear where A is?"
> You can't answer directly. You say: "You may spend 1 Action Point (AP) to listen."
> He spends it. Focus (FOC) bonus +2, Insight (INS) 1 rank, Fear Pressure (FP) +3, DC 13. He rolls 5 and 6, total 11, Check Total (CT) 14, Margin of Success (MoS) +1 — **Success (Succ)**.
> You give the Distance Band (DB), no bearing: "Someone is running at Mid (MID). Only one person."
> He asks: "Is it A?"
> You say: "You don't know."
> **Those five words do more than any attack.** He spends the next three rounds guessing.

## 1-7　What to say out loud

### Heartbeat (advance by Distance Band (DB); may be read verbatim)

| Distance Band (DB) | You say |
|---|---|
| Escaped (ESC) | "Quiet. Too quiet." |
| Far (FAR) | "Your chest feels tight. You can't tell if it's a heartbeat or something else." |
| Mid (MID) | "It's a heartbeat. Not yours." |
| Near (NEA) | "The heartbeat drowns out your own breathing. You lose count of the beats." |
| Contact (CON) | "You can't hear anything else." |
| Visible | **(Don't speak. Knock the table three times, then stop.)** |

### Health State (HS) changes

| Change | You say |
|---|---|
| Healthy → Injured | "Something passes through you from behind. The pain arrives half a second late. Your hands are shaking." |
| Injured maintained | "You drip with every step. You know it will stay." |
| Injured → Dying | "The ground hits your face. The world flips over. You hear your own breathing, close, loud." |
| Dying crawling | "Your fingers claw at the dirt. You're not sure where you're crawling." |

### Banned words

| Don't say | Say instead |
|---|---|
| "The Killer" | "it" "that thing" (use pronouns, not titles) |
| "You lost 3 Hit Points (HP)" | "The edge of your vision starts to go black" + (push the counter) |
| "You succeeded" | "The Generator hums low. The light comes on. You took too long." |
| "You failed" | "Your hand slipped." (then pause three seconds) |
| "It found you" | **(Don't say. Just speed up the knock rhythm.)** |
| "You're safe" | **Never say this sentence.** |

### Three zero-cost techniques

1. **The pause**: after the player declares an action, **count three seconds before responding**. Three seconds is terrifyingly long at the table.
2. **The non-answer**: player asks "Where is it?" — you only answer "You don't know." No follow-up.
3. **The excess detail**: occasionally describe a **completely irrelevant** detail ("a dead bird on the windowsill, long dead"). The player will spend five minutes studying that bird. **Half of fear is paranoia.**

### Three kinds of "wrongness"

When you want to raise the uncanny without touching the DC, pull one of these three into the description. **At most 3 times per Trial**, or it becomes funny.

| Type | Description | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Repetition** | Something appears a second time that shouldn't | "You see that dead bird again. But you're sure it was in another building." |
| **Displacement** | The proportion of space or body is wrong | "This corridor is longer than when you walked through it. You counted the steps." |
| **Gaze** | Something noticed you | "The portrait on the wall was in profile before." |

|---|---|---|

## 2-1　The Killer's round sequence

The Killer's turn shares the same **Round** as the Survivors, and is processed first as the round's "Phase 1 — Threat Phase."

### 1-1　Per-round sequence

1. **Emit heartbeat**: if you have a Terror Radius (TR), issue Heartbeat Tokens to Survivors within radius (closer = faster heartbeat).
2. **Patrol or lock**: decide who to chase this round. The Killer has only one body and can chase only one person per round — the hardest constraint of the original.
3. **Automatic Chase Contest** (if mid-chase): both sides roll 2d10; the difference sets the Distance Band (DB) change (tie goes to the Killer).
4. **Spend Action Point (AP) to act**: move, attack, break pallet, kick Generator, use Power, deploy Pressure Point (PRE) bonus.
5. **End of round**: Bloodlust accumulation tally, Hook-stage countdown, Power Charge (PWC) cooldown decrement.

### 1-2　Killer Action Point (AP) cost table (highlights)

| Action | Action Point (AP) | Pressure Point (PRE) | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| Move 1 square | 1 | — | Base move |
| Chase-move 3–4 squares | 2 | — | Odd rounds 3 squares, even rounds 4 squares, alternating |
| Basic Attack | 2 | — | Hit drops 1 Health State (HS) tier; remaining AP this round −1 |
| Lunge Attack | 3 | — | Hits at Near (NEA); attack modifier +2 |
| Break Pallet | 2 | — | Resets Bloodlust |
| Kick Generator | 2 | — | Immediately −2 Progress Point (PP), enters Regressing state |
| Pick up Dying Survivor | 1 | — | Enters Carrying state |
| Hook | 1 | — | Gain +3 Pressure Point (PRE) |
| Use Power | Per avatar | 2–5 | See each avatar |
| Check Bonus | 0 | 1 / point | Declared before roll; max +3 per instance |
| Sudden Skill Check | 0 | 2 | Force Survivor an extra check, DC +3 |
| Forced Reroll Contest | 0 | 3 | Take the new result |

【Example】 Round one: you run the Trapper (TRP), Terror Radius (TR) 16 squares. A Survivor steps into radius to repair; you roll a Tracking (TRK) check to lock them, the Phase-1 automatic chase contest leaves the band unchanged. In Phase 2 you spend 2 AP to chase-move 3 squares closer, then spend 1 AP to place a Bear Trap (PRE −1), leaving 1 AP reserved for next round's pallet break.

## 2-2　Managing Action Point (AP) and Pressure Point (PRE)

The Killer has two resource pools: **Action Point (AP)** (4 per round, fixed) and **Pressure Point (PRE)** (cross-round accumulated, deployable tactical resource).

### 2-1　Pressure Point (PRE) economy

```
Pressure Point max (PRE_max)  = 4 + 2 × N + INT(Prestige (PRS) / 4)
Per-round Pressure recovery (PRE_reg) = 2 + INT(N / 2)
Extra gains: +3 per Hook, +2 per knockdown (to Dying), +1 per pallet break
```

| N | PRS1 cap | PRS20 cap | Per-round recovery |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 6 | 11 | 2 |
| 2 | 8 | 13 | 3 |
| 3 | 10 | 15 | 3 |
| 4 | 12 | 17 | 4 |

### 2-2　Deployment principle (four-element demo: deploying Pressure Point)

- **Trigger**: you intend to strengthen a check or contest before rolling.
- **Cost**: spend 1–5 Pressure Point (PRE), by use.
- **Adjudication**: none (takes effect on declaration before the roll, except "Forced Reroll" which takes the new result).
- **Result**: Check Bonus +1/point (max +3 per instance); or force a Survivor Sudden Skill Check; or reroll an unfavorable chase contest.

> **Core philosophy**: The Killer's advantage is not innate; it's **bought with resources**. Each round you decide "do I burn PRE to chase this round?" Burn it and you have nothing left for Powers. Both sides manage depletable resources — that is the essence of the original chase.

【Solo Trial】 At N = 1 your Pressure Point (PRE) cap drops sharply (only 6 at PRS1), and recovery is slow (2 per round). This is a balance concession to the solo player — you can't sustain pressure like against a four-person squad. Accordingly, the Hook time limit is longer (7 rounds) and self-rescue DC is lower (17), giving the opponent more comeback room. The solo Killer must learn "precise deployment," not "resource crushing."

## 2-3　Bloodlust, Hunt Lead, and Terror Radius (TR)

### 3-1　Bloodlust operation

| Consecutive chase rounds | Tier | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| ≥ 3 | Tier 1 | Chase check +1 |
| ≥ 5 | Tier 2 | Chase check +2 |
| ≥ 7 | Tier 3 | Chase check +3 |

**Reset**: hitting a Survivor, breaking a pallet, or ending the chase. After interruption, drops 1 tier per round.

> **Performance note**: Bloodlust is the reward for "you've been chasing too long." It rewards "don't switch targets, bite one person down" — but the cost is, the moment you break a pallet or land a hit, it resets instantly. Time your pallet-break clears carefully.

### 3-2　Hunt Lead

The Hunt Lead is the Killer's "knowledge currency": while holding it, you learn a Survivor's zone or key info for free. Cap 3.

- **Gain**: a Survivor tripping a trap, flushed by Rust Crows, triggering a Mud Glyph, the Killer successfully reading Scratch Marks — all give you 1 Hunt Lead free.
- **When not held**: you must draw a Patrol Deck card, and move per the card's guidance (this is the system forcing you to "patrol" rather than "pin one person").

### 3-3　Terror Radius (TR) operation

- Standard avatar Terror Radius (TR) 32 meters (16 squares); special avatars 24 / 20 / 0 meters.
- The heartbeat is your strongest passive weapon: Survivors within radius **can't repair in peace** (Fear Pressure (FP) raises the DC).
- The third stage of escalation adds **+8 meters** to Terror Radius (TR) — a double-edged sword (see 2-5).

【Example】 You run the Hillbilly (HBY) chasing a Survivor to round 5; Bloodlust Tier 2 triggers (chase +2). They duck into the pallet zone; you spend 2 AP to break the pallet (Bloodlust resets, gain +1 PRE), and take a side route to flank — breaking the pallet both clears terrain and proactively resets Bloodlust for the next burst.

## 2-4　The nine Avatars: how to perform them

The following gives each avatar's GM "performance notes" and "solo configuration" reminders. Full stat blocks are in *Killer Codex*.

### 4-1　Trapper (TRP) | Yin Wanshan　Trapper (trapping style)
- **Performance**: You are a chess player, not a hunter. Lay traps and routes first; let the map speak for itself.
- **Notes**: Traps are a sunk cost; the skill is "guessing the right route." Passively read Scratch Marks, use Hunt Leads to cover gaps.
- **Solo configuration**: Trap stock cap −1; the trapped victim's self-escape DC drops from 14 to 12. **Top recommendation for the Solo Trial** — static, learnable, deconstructable; the solo player grows by memorizing the map.

### 4-2　Wraith (WRT) | Oulisen　Stealth (stealth style)
- **Performance**: You are an information denier. While cloaked you deal no damage, but you rob the opponent of judgment for a whole round.
- **Notes**: During cloak, let the Survivor repair in peace; the ambush bonus +4 on uncloak is the kill move. Bell detection gives you the "where is he" intel war.
- **Solo configuration**: Bell detection DC drops to 10, and a Critical Failure gives no false intel (avoids the solo player collapsing from fed false info).

### 4-3　Hillbilly (HBY) | Zhuo Bingshan　Charger (dash style)
- **Performance**: You are an executioner. One mistake equals a life, but the overheat cost is brutal.
- **Notes**: Draw the path before a straight dash; force the opponent into a Collision. Count overheat; at 3, force one more use to lock them down 3 rounds.
- **Solo configuration**: Hit effect changes from "straight to Dying" to "drop 1 Health State (HS) tier + Deep Wound" — a solo knockdown with no one to help equals end of Trial, so a two-stage version leaves self-rescue room.

### 4-4　Nurse (NRS) | Sang Li　Blink (teleport style)
- **Performance**: You don't chase, you execute. Base move sluggish (6 squares), but Blink ignores all terrain.
- **Notes**: Pin the opponent within 2–3 squares so she can't blink and you can't catch; count fatigue rounds (AP = 0) as the opponent's free round.
- **Solo configuration**: **Not recommended at N=1.** If used, Blink Charge cap locked at 2, and give the player 1 extra Hope Point (HOP) as compensation. She is the most disastrous avatar against a solo player in the whole system.

### 4-5　Huntress (HTR) | Miao Shuang　Ranged (throwing style)
- **Performance**: You are a ranged suppressor. The Lullaby lets the opponent "know you're near but not how near."
- **Notes**: Keep cover between you two (absorbs throws); watch your supply route (must return to Locker when ammo out).
- **Solo configuration**: Ammo cap −1 (4); Lullaby hum at most once per round (avoids the solo player being crushed by sound).

### 4-6　Hag (HAG) | Ao Xuaniang　Trap-and-Teleport (trapping-teleport style)
- **Performance**: You are a layout recycler. Invest Mud Glyphs early; erupt after round 8.
- **Notes**: The first 6 rounds are the Survivors' golden repair window — don't hard-chase; use Phantasm Teleport to pounce on trigger points.
- **Solo configuration**: Mud Glyph stock cap 4; after teleport, AP cap drops to 1 (prevents the solo player being sealed by a glyph net across the whole map).

### 4-7　Doctor (DOC) | Tan Zhongru　Area (area style)
- **Performance**: You are a check disruptor. Madness tiers are a resource axis, not a direct Health State (HS) drain.
- **Notes**: Static Field and Shock Therapy are mutually exclusive — you can't both pressure and execute; pushing Generators is the Survivors' antidote (each completed Generator drops all tiers by 1).
- **Solo configuration**: Madness Tier 3 fake-heartbeat token at most 1 per 2 rounds; Tier 3 AP −1 shortened to 2 rounds.

### 4-8　Legion (LGN) | Xi Lü　Combo (combo style)
- **Performance**: You are a wound spreader. During Frenzy you can't down anyone, only lay Deep Wounds.
- **Notes**: Spread-out positioning breaks their Pursue chain; remember the fixed rhythm of Frenzy 3 rounds, cooldown 2 rounds.
- **Solo configuration**: **The Power must be rewritten** — Pursue fails; instead each hit gives +2 extra PRE, Deep Wound timer shortens to 3 rounds, hard-stun fixed at 1 round. Otherwise it degrades into "a Killer who can't down anyone" and stalls.

### 4-9　The Silent One (SIL) | Hilda Lane　Psychological Pressure (scenario-exclusive)
- **Performance**: You shouldn't be strongest; you should make the player "not want to act against you." The driver is hatred, not hunger.
- **Notes**: Aura permanently marks the Unspoken; using "Name-Call" (shouting Hilda Lane aloud) trades for a 1-round breathing window — the rules landing point of the S6 choice.
- **Solo configuration**: After Name-Call, +1 extra Hope Point (HOP); aggressive-action cost threshold −2 (easier to trigger but the solo player needs to act more, creating tension). Usually deployed by the GM in scenarios S6–S8, not the standard avatar pool.

## 2-5　Three-phase escalation and Hope Point (HOP) payout

When you run the Killer as a **dramatized boss** (rather than pure chase threat), apply the Escalation Phase.

### 5-1　Three phases

| Phase | Trigger | Name | Effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| Phase 1 | Trial begins | The Hunt | Base stat block, no modifier |
| Phase 2 | Completed Generators ≥ INT(target/2) OR total Hooks ≥ N | The Impatience | PRE recovery +1/round; Power Charge (PWC) cap +1; Tracking check +2 |
| Phase 3 | Completed Generators = target − 1 OR any Survivor enters Hook stage 3 | The Entity's Wrath | Terror Radius (TR) +8 meters; Basic Attack +2; 1 free Hunt Lead per round; Bloodlust achievement rounds each −1 |

### 5-2　Hope Point (HOP) reverse-balance (four elements)

- **Trigger**: automatically on entering Phase 2 / Phase 3.
- **Cost**: none (system-mandatory payout, cannot skip).
- **Adjudication**: none.
- **Result**: entering Phase 2, all Survivors +1 Hope Point (HOP); entering Phase 3, another +1 Hope Point (HOP) and Hatch search DC permanently −3.

> **Why does the Killer get stronger while the player gets more Hope Point (HOP)?** This is reverse-balancing. Phase 3's TR +8 and Attack +2 are extremely execution-capable and must be compensated by HOP, or it's an instant team wipe. The GM **must not** skip the payout.

【Solo Trial】 At N = 1, Phase 3's four effects (TR+8 / Attack+2 / free Hunt Lead / Bloodlust−1) **take only one**, decided by the GM with a d6 before the Trial and written on the Screen (1–2 take Attack+2, 3–4 take free Hunt Lead, 5–6 take Bloodlust−1). The solo player can't bear all three stacked — this substitution must be enforced.

## 2-6　Mindset: don't actually try to win

The last and most important rule: **you run the Killer not to win, but to make a good movie.**

- **Your victory condition is "despair," not "team wipe."** The Entity (The Entity) wants fear and drama, not corpses. Occasionally let one escape; next session they'll fear you more.
- **Resource management beats crushing.** Burn all your PRE chasing one person and you'll be helpless for rounds. Keep something in reserve.
- **Give comeback room.** Pallets, windows, loops are the Survivors' life; don't mindlessly fill every terrain square with your Power. The scream when they come back is the climax of this game.
- **The Solo Trial needs more gentleness.** The solo player has no teammates to share the load; one mistake is elimination. Appropriately go easy (Hatch opens earlier, self-rescue easier), let them experience the achievement of "I survived on my own."

【Example】 Endgame: four Survivors fixed 4 machines, only 1 un-hooked. You enter Phase 3 (The Entity's Wrath), TR +8 meters covers the field, but you pay out HOP per the rules. The last one rerolls free via HOP, escapes through the Hatch. You "didn't win" — but every player's palms were sweaty. That is a good Killer.

# Chapter Three　Building Trials: Encounters, Budgets, and Player-Count Scaling

You sit in the Game Master (GM) seat. This chapter gives you three things:

- A formula for how much Creation threat this Trial **can deploy** (§3-4–§3-5);

## 3-1　Effective Repair Force (ERF) and Echo Companions

The first number to understand when building a Trial is not "how many players," but "**after the Killer chases one person, how many are left to repair.**"

The Killer has only one body and can chase only one person per round. So the key variable is the **Effective Repair Force (ERF)**:

```
Effective Repair Force (ERF) = (N − 1) + 0.5 × Echo Companion count (EC)
Echo Companion count (EC)    = MAX(0, 3 − N)
```

| Player count N | Echo Companion count (EC) | Effective Repair Force (ERF) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **2** | 0 ＋ 2×0.5 ＝ **1.0** |
| 2 | **1** | 1 ＋ 1×0.5 ＝ **1.5** |
| 3 | 0 | 2 ＋ 0 ＝ **2.0** |
| 4 | 0 | 3 ＋ 0 ＝ **3.0** |

**Echo Companions** are a mechanic introduced by the framework: when players number fewer than 3, the system adds "echoes in the Fog" to share the repair load. They are the only reason ERF doesn't hit zero at N=1.

> **【Solo Trial】** At N=1, Echo Companion count (EC) = 2, Effective Repair Force (ERF) = 1.0. These two Echo Companions are not decoration — without them, the solo game's Generators would never advance. When building the Solo Trial, you must count them into the map and pacing, not treat them as absent. Their specific behavior is in §3-7 and Chapter Four "Echoes in the Fog."

> **Design Note**: The official Wiki itself admits solo scaling is broken — at N=1, ERF hits zero and Generator progress never advances. This system uses Echo Companions to push ERF back to 1.0, so although the target count matches the original (2), it goes from "mathematically impossible" to "possible and tense."

---

## 3-2　Generator scaling formula (finalized)

Using Effective Repair Force (ERF) as the independent variable, linearly fitted against the four-player baseline (ERF 3.0 → 5 target):

```
Generator target (GEN_req)  = MAX(2, ROUND(1.6 × Effective Repair Force (ERF) + 0.4, 0))
Generator total (GEN_total) = Generator target (GEN_req) + 2
```

**Verification (matches the original line by line):**

## 3-4　Encounter Budget (EB): the total threat volume of this Trial

**Trigger**: you begin building a Trial's Creations and Killer configuration.

**Cost**: none (this is paper calculation, consumes no in-game resource).

**Adjudication**: apply the formula below to get an integer Encounter Budget (EB):

```
Encounter Budget (EB) = ROUND( N × (2 + Prestige (PRS)) × Difficulty Modifier (DM), 0 )

Where:
  N  = player Survivor count (Echo Companions not counted)
  Prestige (PRS) = the Prestige set for this Trial
  Difficulty Modifier (DM) = Light 0.5 / Standard 1.0 / Hard 1.5 / Lethal 2.0
```

**Result**: you get an integer Encounter Budget (EB). Hand it to §3-5 to decide the Creation mix. The Killer, as the main threat, reserves a fixed **60% of the Encounter Budget (EB)** — but this is a **reserved slot**, it does not vary with the avatar and you need not actually subtract anything. Just use §3-5's Creation Budget (CB) as your deployment cap.

> **Design Note**: "Killer takes 60%" gives the budget framework a structural anchor, keeping the Creation Budget (CB) always 2/5 of the Encounter Budget (EB). But it's only a reserved slot — you never actually subtract 60% from EB then compute Creations. One formula, one rounding, avoids mid-calculation decimals and "close enough" judgment calls.

> **Example**: Prestige (PRS) 5, Standard difficulty (DM 1.0), four-player (N=4).
> Encounter Budget (EB) = ROUND(4 × (2+5) × 1.0, 0) = ROUND(28, 0) = **28**.
> Killer reserves 60% (16.8, conceptual only); you take §3-5's Creation Budget (CB) = 11 to deploy Creations.

## 3-5　Creation Budget (CB) and deployment rules

**Trigger**: you've computed the Encounter Budget (EB) and want to decide which Creations to place.

**Cost**: none (configuration phase).

**Adjudication**: first get the Creation Budget (CB) with a single rounding, then pick *Killer Codex* entries per the limits below so total Encounter Budget Cost (EBC) does not exceed the Creation Budget (CB):

```
Creation Budget (CB) = ROUND( N × (2 + Prestige (PRS)) × Difficulty Modifier (DM) × 2 / 5, 0 )

Excel: =ROUND(N*(2+PRS)*DM*2/5, 0)
```

> Rounding-ambiguity guarantee: the Creation Budget (CB) result never lands on .5 (under the four DM values, the raw values are M/5, 2M/5, 3M/5, 4M/5, where M = N×(2+PRS) is an integer; to hit .5 you'd need "even = odd×5," which never holds). So Excel, Python, and mental math always agree.

**Deployment limits (must obey when selecting):**

| Limit | Description |
|---|---|
| **Boss-level Creation** | At most **1** per Trial. If a boss-level is deployed, the Killer's Escalation Phase may not enter Phase 3. |
| **Same-type Creation** | The same numbered Creation may be deployed at most `MAX(1, INT(N))` times per Trial (Rust Crows RC-01 is exempt, unlimited). |
| **Intel-type Creation** | Well Chorus RC-02 costs no budget and may be freely deployed. |

**Result** (four states by relation of total cost to Creation Budget (CB)):

| Result | Condition | What you do |
|---|---|---|
| **Critical Success (CritS)** | Total EBC ≤ CB, and remainder ≥ cheapest Creation (Rust Crows RC-01 = 2 points) | Budget ample, flexible mix; blend high- and low-cost Creations for layered pressure. |
| **Success (Succ)** | Total EBC = CB | Exactly filled; deploy per plan. |
| **Failure (Fail)** | Total EBC ≤ CB, but remainder < cheapest Creation | Cannot add any more Creations; deploy as-is, accept the "just short" remainder. |
| **Critical Failure (CritF)** | Total EBC > CB | Violation; must cut Creations until compliant; no over-budget mix may take the field. |

**Creation Budget (CB) floor rule (newbie guidance):** when CB < the cheapest Creation cost (Rust Crows RC-01 = 2 EBC points), that Trial deploys **no Creations** — a pure-Killer Trial. The cases that trigger this are all "Prestige 1–5 Solo Trials" and "Light difficulty" — 7 parameter groups total (N1/Prestige 1 Light or Standard, N1/Prestige 2–5 Light, N2/Prestige 1 Light), i.e. the player's first few sessions. The first session is "you and the Killer, only the two of you in the Fog" — no Rust Crows, no Mud Hands — a deliberate newbie on-ramp; never an on-the-spot "should we round up and allow it" judgment.

> **【Solo Trial】** The floor rule triggers almost entirely at N=1. That is, the Solo Trial's first few newbie sessions are designed as "pure Killer, no Creations." Don't force Creations into the solo game to fill the budget — empty Fog, just you and the Killer, is the safe starting hand for the solo newbie. Save Creation practice for higher Prestige or more players.

**Example calculation (Prestige 5, Standard difficulty, Killer present):**

| N | Encounter Budget (EB) | Creation Budget (CB) | Suggested mix | Result |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | ROUND(1×7×1.0) ＝ 7 | ROUND(1×7×1.0×2/5) ＝ **3** | Rust Crows RC-01 ×1 (2) | Remainder 1 (Fail state: can't add) |
| 2 | ROUND(2×7×1.0) ＝ 14 | ROUND(2×7×1.0×2/5) ＝ **6** | Straw Afterimage RC-04 (4) ＋ Rust Crows RC-01 (2) ＝ 6 | Exact (Success state) |
| 3 | ROUND(3×7×1.0) ＝ 21 | ROUND(3×7×1.0×2/5) ＝ **8** | Mud Hands RC-06 (6) ＋ Rust Crows RC-01 (2) ＝ 8 | Exact (Success state) |
| 4 | ROUND(4×7×1.0) ＝ 28 | ROUND(4×7×1.0×2/5) ＝ **11** | Observation Eye RC-07 (10) | Remainder 1 (Fail state: can't add) |

> **Example**: A Prestige (PRS) 15, Hard difficulty (DM 1.5), four-player (N=4), Killer present session.
> Encounter Budget (EB) = ROUND(4 × (2+15) × 1.5, 0) = ROUND(102, 0) = 102.
> Creation Budget (CB) = ROUND(4 × 17 × 1.5 × 2/5, 0) = **41**.
> Deployable: Fogcellar Warden RC-10 (24) ＋ Blood Leaf Swarm RC-08 (8) ＋ Rust Crows RC-01 ×4 (8) = 40, remainder 1 (Fail state).

## 3-6　Five principles for encounter construction

Regardless of player count, obey these five when building Creation encounters:

| # | Principle | Description |
|---|---|---|
| **P1** | **Every Trial needs at least one Creation whose right answer is "non-combat"** | Survivor combat ability is deliberately low (bare-handed 1d4). If players start trying to "kill the monster," the encounter design failed. |
| **P2** | **Creation and Killer threat axes must not repeat** | Already using the Trapper (space lock) → don't also place Mud Hands (zone denial). Instead place Observation Eye (info denial) for a second pressure. |
| **P3** | **Real-damage Creations and the Killer must not close on the same Survivor at once** | The three-tier Health State (HS) is too fragile. If a Straw Afterimage already hit someone, the Killer shouldn't also arrive that round (you must actively schedule). |
| **P4** | **Every Creation deployed must clearly mark its dispel weakness on the map** | The weakness must be discoverable: scythes scattered in the cornfield, plank stacks on the shore, braziers at the altar. When players can't find it, you proactively hint by round 3. |
| **P5** | **Boss-level Creations must have phases** | A high Challenge Rating (CR) Creation with no phases just becomes "an unkillable annoyance," no drama. |

## 3-7　Construction differences for the Solo Trial (N=1)

This section is the Solo Trial's (Setup 1: 1 Player + 1 Game Master (GM)) dedicated build method. **It stands alone as a section, not a footnote** — the core selling point of this work is that one person can run it.

### 3-7-1　N=1 parameter overview

Taken directly from §3-3's N=1 column:

| Parameter | N=1 value |
|---|---|
| Echo Companion count (EC) | 2 |
| Effective Repair Force (ERF) | 1.0 |
| Generator target (GEN_req) / total (GEN_total) | 2 / 4 |
| Killer PRE cap (PRE_max) | 6 ＋ INT(Prestige (PRS) ÷ 4) |
| Per-round PRE recovery (PRE_reg) | 2 |
| Hook stage duration (HSD) | 7 |
| Hook self-rescue DC | 17 |
| Endgame Collapse rounds (ECR) | 12 |
| Exit Gate segments (GATE) | 3 |
| Chest count (CHEST) | 2 |
| Pallet / Window / Hook count | 8 / 5 / 8 |

### 3-7-2　Building the Echo Companions

The two Echo Companions are run by you, with fixed behavior:

- **Repair**: fixed total modifier +2 for repair checks, **Progress Point (PP) contribution halved**; multi-person stacking penalty still applies (see Player Book Ch.1 1-7-3).
- **Heal**: can heal the player (fixed +2 modifier, PP not halved); but **cannot heal themselves, nor each other**.
- **Hooked**: when an Echo Companion is hooked, the player takes +1 Fear Pressure (FP) per companion (see Player Book Ch.1 1-6).
- **Rescue**: after the player is hooked, the Echo Companion attempts rescue starting on **round 2**, per the fixed behavior table, not randomly.
- **Bait**: the player spends 1 Hope Point (HOP) to designate an Echo Companion to make noise; if the Killer is at Far (FAR) or farther, must make a Perception check, failing to redirect.

> **【Solo Trial】** Echo Companions are not "weaker teammates" — they are system characters with a fixed behavior set in your (GM's) hands. You don't roll attribute dice for them; they act per the table above. This guarantees the Solo Trial's pacing is decided by you (the GM), not a set of random dice.

### 3-7-3　Hatch opening (N=1 branch)

The Solo Trial's Hatch opens differently from multiplayer: **the trigger is "all Echo Companions eliminated,"** not "alive players = 1."

```
Hatch threshold (HATCH_gen) = MAX(1, Generator target (GEN_req) − 1)  ＝ MAX(1, 2−1) ＝ 1
When "completed Generators ≥ 1" AND "alive Echo Companions = 0" → Hatch opens
```

> **【Solo Trial】** This means in a solo game, the Hatch doesn't close because "you're still alive" — it closes when the Echo Companions are all wiped. As long as one of the two Echo Companions lives, the Hatch stays closed, forcing the Exit Gate route; when they all fall, the Fog leaves you a Hatch back door.

### 3-7-4　Hook and self-rescue

- **Hook stage duration (HSD) = 7 rounds**: much looser than the four-player 4 rounds, because only Echo Companions can come rescue you.
- **Hook self-rescue DC = 17**: total modifier +3 gives a 28% success rate. Four-player keeps the original low success rate (DC 20 → 10%), solo is loosened — because no one can rescue, self-rescue is the only way out.

> **【Solo Trial】** The HSD and self-rescue DC N=1 values are the windows the system adds for "no teammates." You (GM) enforce 7 rounds, DC 17 — don't loosen further "because there's only one person" — the looseness is already written into the numbers.

### 3-7-5　Map and pacing

- **Map**: Pallet 8, Window 5 (more per-capita than four-player), Hook 8, Chest 2, Generator total 4 (target 2), Exit Gate 3 segments.
- **Pacing**: standard Trial 25 rounds. Endgame Collapse rounds (ECR) = 12 — the solo player must run to the Exit Gate and open it alone, with no teammates to split the Killer's attention, so it's twice the four-player 6 rounds. When scheduling Creation entrances, place boss-level Creations on rounds 21–25, overlapping the Endgame Collapse (post-ECR-12 closing segment) for a climax.
- **Newbie guidance**: Solo Trials at Prestige 1–5 are mostly **pure Killer, no Creations** per §3-5's floor rule. This isn't a miss — it's design: let players get used to "being chased" and "repairing" before Creations arrive.

## 3-8　How Realm Creations act (GM roll format)

Each Creation's power-check format is unified, consistent with the core dice system. When a Creation affects a player, you (GM) roll per the structure below:

**Trigger**: the Creation activates its power (attack, drag, scan, etc.), target is a player.

**Cost**: usually 0 for the player; some Creations let the player spend AP to actively avoid, noted in the entry.

**Adjudication**: 2d10 + the modifier specified in the Creation entry, contested against the player's Check Total (CT) or a DC you (GM) declare (see each *Killer Codex* entry).

**Result** (four kinds, consistent with core rules):

| Result | What happens |
|---|---|
| **Critical Success (CritS)** | Creation fully hits, and applies an extra status effect (e.g. Straw Afterimage drags 1 extra square). |
| **Success (Succ)** | Creation hits, produces the entry's described main effect. |
| **Failure (Fail)** | Creation whiffs, player unaffected (or minimal disruption only). |
| **Critical Failure (CritF)** | Creation backfires: itself enters 1 round of Incapacitated, or exposes its dispel weakness for 1 round. |

**⚠ Common Misconception**: Any Creation power's DC **peaks at 21 (Legendary)**. If you announce a number higher than 21, you miscalculated — same as the avatar Power DC cap rule.

> **Example**: Straw Afterimage RC-04 activates "Drag" on a player. You roll 2d10 + its modifier, contested against the player's Agility (AGI) Check Total (CT). You roll double tens → Critical Success (CritS): drag hits and the player is additionally immobilized 1 round. The player can do nothing but Wiggle this round.

## 3-9　Range, line of sight, and cover (shared by Creations and Powers)

When building the map and scheduling Creations, distance and cover rules are as follows (for ranged Creations, scan Creations, and avatar Powers):

| Concept | Rule |
|---|---|
| **Distance calculation** | **Chebyshev Distance**: `squares = MAX(|Δx|, |Δy|)`. Diagonals and orthogonals cost the same. |
| **Line of Sight (LOS)** | Draw a straight line from the source square center to the target square center; if it passes through any solid obstacle square (wall, vehicle, large statue), no LOS. |
| **Half Cover** | Line grazes a corner of an obstacle square: target's evasion check **+2**. |
| **Full Cover** | Line passes through the main body of an obstacle square: attack automatically fails (projectile absorbed). |
| **Pallet as cover** | **A dropped pallet** counts as Half Cover (+2); **an undropped pallet** (suspended) provides no cover. |
| **Crouch cover bonus** | While crouching, if an adjacent square has an obstacle, extra **+1** (stacks with half/full cover). |
| **Area-of-Effect (AOE) and cover** | AOE **ignores Half Cover**, but Full Cover still fully blocks. |

**Conical area grid drawing (general use):**

```
A cone "length L squares, end width W squares":
  width at distance d from caster = MAX(1, ROUND(W × d / L, 0))
  Example: L=6, W=4 → width 1 at d1, 1 at d2, 2 at d3, 3 at d4, 3 at d5, 4 at d6
  total covered = 1+1+2+3+3+4 = 14 squares
```

## 4-1　Macmillan Estate

The stage of the first Trial, and the players' first impression of the "Trial" — so it should be **standard**, standard enough that they'll miss it in the second Realm.

> Where you step is wet coal ash, sinking half an inch into your sole, with a sound when you pull free.

|---|---|---|

| Locker | Miner's iron cabinet, door won't stay shut and sways | 2 in the changing room, rest scattered |

**Unique terrain**: **Coal Tower** (the only high ground in the whole Realm), **Suffocation Pit** (half-filled mine shaft), **Wailing Ironworks furnace exhaust** (warm furnace).

### Environment interactions

**I. Tower Observation**

Cost: 2 Action Point (AP).
Adjudication: 2d10 + Agility (AGI) bonus + Parkour (PRK) Skill Level vs DC 9.
Result: CritS — see all unfinished Generators and the Killer's zone; Success — see all unfinished Generators; Failure — only one clearly; CritF — iron ladder booms, Killer gains 1 Hunt Lead, and descending costs another 2 AP.

**II. Furnace Heat Barrier**
Trigger: player character enters the furnace gallery, declares moving hugging the furnace.
Cost: move as normal; extra 0 AP.
Adjudication: 2d10 + Stealth (STL) bonus + Sneak (SNK) Skill Level vs DC 12.

Result: CritS — leave no Scratch Marks this round (Killer Tracking check −4); Success — leave no Scratch Marks; Failure — leave Scratch Marks as normal; CritF — burn, gain Haemorrhage 4 rounds.

**III. Stone into the Pit**
Trigger: player character in Contact (CON) with the Suffocation Pit, declares dropping something in.
Cost: 1 Action Point (AP).
Adjudication: player doesn't roll; instead the Killer rolls 2d10 + Perception (PER) bonus vs DC 12 to judge if detected.
Result: Killer CritS — ignores the sound and comes straight at you; Killer Success — ignores the sound; Killer Failure — moves 1 zone toward the mine; Killer CritF — moves toward the mine and stays 1 round, the chorus at the bottom starts singing his name, he can't use Power this round.

> **Example**: The player is bitten to Near (NEA) by the Wraith (WRT), spends 1 AP to drop an iron pipe into the pit. You roll for the Wraith: 11, fails against DC 12 — you say the pit begins to sound, many voices singing together, it turns its head toward the hole. The Wraith retreats; the player buys two rounds.

**Hillbilly (HBY)**: open outer ring suits dashing; central warehouse forces him to slow.
**Not recommended: Nurse (NRS)**: too few buildings, Blink has no walls to cross.
**Creations**: Rust Crows (RC-01) in Sheltering Woods; Chorus (RC-02, no EB cost) at the Suffocation Pit.

### **【Solo Trial】** trade-off suggestions

**Keep**: Coal Tower (the solo game's most important info source), all Moaning Warehouse pallets, Suffocation Pit (info source, no EB cost).
**Add**: the two Echo Companions' starting positions split between the warehouse and the ironworks — forcing the player to choose who to find first.

## 4-4　Crotus Prenn Asylum

## 4-5　Backwater Swamp

## 4-6　Léry's Memorial Institute

The Entity's processing factory. The "inner-production" route Killers are reforged here. This Realm's signature fear isn't the chase — it's **you start to suspect you remembered wrong.**

### Three opening lines

> All-white tiles, fluorescent lights flickering on an irregular rhythm. Every room has an observation window.
> A calm male voice over the PA announces the time. The time keeps going backward.
> Disinfectant covers everything. You can't smell your own blood.

### Terrain features

**Symmetric layout**. The exploration map is mirror-symmetric, and half the nodes "swap" mid-game — players think they remembered wrong.

| Object | What it looks like | Where it sits |

### Environment interactions

**I. One-Way Mirror Recon**
Trigger: player character in any observation room.
Cost: 1 Action Point (AP).
Adjudication: 2d10 + Focus (FOC) bonus + Insight (INS) Skill Level vs DC 9.
Result: CritS — see next door, and learn if the Killer is in the two adjacent rooms; Success — see next door; Failure — mirror glare, see nothing; CritF — **you see something behind you in the mirror**, gain Trembling state that round (all checks −2); but the Killer is actually not there.

**II. Cut the Fluorescent Lights**
Trigger: player character in Contact (CON) with either wing's distribution panel.
Cost: 3 Action Point (AP).
Adjudication: 2d10 + Technique (TEC) bonus + Mechanical Repair (MEC) Skill Level vs DC 15.
Result: CritS — that wing dark 4 rounds, during which Sneak (SNK) check +4, Killer Tracking check −4; Success — dark 2 rounds, modifiers halved (+2/−2); Failure — lights flicker and recover; CritF — all institute lights max at once, you gain Blindness 2 rounds and are Revealed.

**III. Topple the Preservation Jar**
Trigger: player character in the Preservation Room, and being chased.
Cost: 1 Action Point (AP).
Adjudication: 2d10 + Agility (AGI) bonus vs DC 9.
Result: CritS — glass shatters everywhere, Killer must spend 2 AP to pass and Bloodlust resets; Success — Killer must spend 1 extra AP to pass; Failure — jar falls but doesn't break; CritF — you step on glass, drop 1 Health State (HS) tier and gain Haemorrhage 4 rounds.

> **Example**: The mirror swap happens. Last round the player repaired a Generator to 12 PP, remembering it "second from the left." This round you say it has no Generator, only an operating table. The player says "No, I clearly—" **Don't explain, don't deny, don't nod — just say: "What will you do?"**

### Pairing suggestions

**Doctor (DOC)**: home turf. Madness tiers stacked with "you remembered wrong" — players can't tell mechanic from their own mistake.
**The Silent One (SIL)**: scenario-exclusive. No heartbeat + symmetric layout + fake-voice lure is the most unsettling session of the whole campaign.

### **【Solo Trial】** trade-off suggestions

**Cut**: four symmetric corridors down to two. Four-player can report positions to each other; a solo player getting lost just loses two rounds.
**Keep**: the mirror-swap mechanism (this Realm's irreplaceable core, **never cut**), observation rooms, Therapy Room No. 13.
**Change**: **the solo game's mirror swap happens only once per session, and must be after the player completes their first Generator** — swapping before that, the player hasn't built spatial memory yet, only produces confusion; confusion is not fear.

## 4-7　Red Forest

One of The Entity's oldest footholds in this world. The shrine at the forest center wasn't built by humans — The Entity "grew" it from human memory, so the stair heights are wrong, the door proportions are wrong.

### Three opening lines

> The trees are too tall. You look up and can't see the canopy, only a layer of red that never clears.
> Fallen leaves are thick enough to silence footsteps. This favors you, and favors it.
> The air is warm. Unnaturally warm — there shouldn't be temperature here.

### Terrain features

**Two-layer structure**: surface is open woodland (no cover, but thick leaves hide a crawling person), underground shrine is a vertical closed space.

| Object | What it looks like | Where it sits |
|---|---|---|
| Pallet | Fallen giant trees and shrine slabs | Two-thirds on surface, one-third in shrine |
| Window | Mother's Dwelling wood windows and shrine gaps | 2 at Mother's Dwelling, 1 per shrine floor |

**Unique terrain**: **Red Leaf Sea** (knee-deep leaf layer, **Dying-crawling here is fully hidden**), **Mother's Dwelling** (unreasonably large wood house, with a hearth inside), **Atonement Shrine** (stone stepped shrine, descending three layers underground), **Ember Niche** (alcove at the shrine's deepest level).

### Environment interactions

**I. Sink into the Leaves**
Trigger: player character in the Red Leaf Sea, and in Dying or crouching state.
Cost: 1 Action Point (AP).
Adjudication: 2d10 + Stealth (STL) bonus + Sneak (SNK) Skill Level vs DC 9.
Result: CritS — fully hidden, the Killer **cannot** find you by sight until you move or stand; Success — hidden, but the Killer may make a DC 15 Perception check each round; Failure — not hidden deep enough, leaves move; CritF — sink in, remaining AP this round = 0, climbing out costs another 2 AP.

**⚠ Common Misconception**: Running through the Red Leaf Sea **leaves a 3-round trail**, lasting longer than Scratch Marks. Running is more dangerous than crawling — **let the player discover this themselves.**

**II. False-Step Fall**
Trigger: player character being chased, and both sides on the Atonement Shrine stairs.
Cost: 1 Action Point (AP).
Adjudication: 2d10 + Focus (FOC) bonus + Insight (INS) Skill Level vs DC 15.
Result: CritS — you time the wrong step and skip it, Killer overshoots and loses 2 AP; Success — Killer overshoots, loses 1 AP; Failure — both pass normally; CritF — **you overshoot**, roll down a level and drop 1 Health State (HS) tier.

**III. Mother's Dwelling Hearth**
Trigger: player character in Mother's Dwelling, and this interaction unused this session for that character.
Cost: 3 Action Point (AP).
Adjudication: 2d10 + Will (WIL) bonus + Endurance (END) Skill Level vs DC 12.
Result: CritS — recover 1 Health State (HS) tier and gain 1 Hope Point (HOP); Success — recover 1 Health State (HS) tier; Failure — fire doesn't respond, you just sit somewhere warm; CritF — **the fire knows you, but mistakes you**, you lose 1 Hope Point (HOP), and see a face that isn't yours in the hearth.

He declares sinking into the leaves, rolls 16, Critical Success. You say he stops crawling, lets the leaves cover him, hears footsteps pass two steps above his head, pause, then recede — **don't rush him; let the player decide when to stand.**

**Huntress (HTR)**: home turf. Open woodland lets ranged throws land; "distance is no longer safe" is this Realm's theme.
**The Silent One (SIL)**: scenario-exclusive. If she appears as a second Killer here, the shrine's vertical closure makes her no-heartbeat trait most terrifying.

**Add**: **an extra Chest hidden in the Red Leaf Sea**, not counted in the total of 2 — placing the reward "on the path he's forced to walk" is the solo game's most effective positive feedback.

**I. Address the Statue**

Trigger: player character in Contact (CON) with any statue, and that statue unused this session.

Adjudication: no roll needed. **As long as the player actually speaks, it counts as success.**
Result: CritS (the spoken content relates to a Memory Card) — +2 Campfire Ember; Success — +1 Campfire Ember; Failure (player refuses to speak) — no effect, the statue doesn't punish you; CritF — this result doesn't exist.

**II. Endless Stair Return**
Trigger: player character on the Endless Stair declares moving up.
Cost: move as normal.
Adjudication: holding the First Ember auto-succeeds; otherwise 2d10 + Will (WIL) bonus + Endurance (END) Skill Level vs DC 18.
Result: CritS — rise a floor, and this session's subsequent Endless Stair checks DC −3; Success — rise a floor; Failure — **you return to the floor you just left** (don't say "you failed," say "you reach the top, push the door, you recognize this room"); CritF — return to the lower floor, and lose 1 Hope Point (HOP).

**III. Follow the Crows**
Trigger: player character in the Crow's Nest zone, and holds at least 1 Campfire Ember.
Cost: 1 Action Point (AP).
Adjudication: 2d10 + Focus (FOC) bonus + Insight (INS) Skill Level vs DC 12.
Result: CritS — crows take flight, point to the **Hatch bearing** and the Killer's current zone; Success — point to the Hatch's general bearing; Failure — crows don't react; CritF — **all crows look at you at once**, then explode and fly off, you are Revealed 1 round, Killer gains 1 Hunt Lead.

> **Example**: Endgame. The player stands in the Statue Circle, one segment of Exit Gate progress left. You don't roll, only ask "Which one will you speak to?" The player is silent a long time, then says a name — an Echo Companion they couldn't save back in the fifth Trial. You drop a glass bead, it sounds, then say the statue didn't move, but its shadow was a little shorter when he passed — **this is the only rule in the whole book that needs no dice; don't ruin it with dice.**

### Pairing suggestions

**Legion (LGN)**: home turf. The ring-open belt's continuous pursuit and Deep Wound spread flow best here.
**Trapper (TRP)**: the tower's spiral stair is an absolute mandatory node; one Bear Trap is worth three on the surface.
**Not recommended: Doctor (DOC)**: this Realm's fear comes from "quiet," but his Power makes lots of noise and cues, canceling each other.
**Creations**: Bonefield Statue (RC-09) guards each Endless Stair segment. This Realm **does not deploy Rust Crows (RC-01)** — crows are narrative elements, not threats.

### **【Solo Trial】** trade-off suggestions

**Cut**: tower segments from 4 to 3. The joy of climbing is "each segment is different," not "more segments."
**Keep**: **all twelve statues** — the only "solo game doesn't reduce" rule. Addressing statues costs no check, creates no threat, only gives the player a chance to speak; the solo player has no teammates to talk to, and needs these twelve statues more.
**Change**: for the solo game's Endless Stair check, each failure lowers the next DC by **2 cumulatively** (minimum 12) — guarantees he'll get up, just pays in rounds.

## 4-9　Three questions for choosing a Realm

| Question | Answer "yes" | Answer "no" |
|---|---|---|
| Has the player learned to loop yet? | Use open Realms (Coldwind Farm, Red Forest, Forsaken Boneyard) | Use Realms with loop points (Macmillan Estate, Autohaven Wreckers) |
| Is investigation the focus this session? | Use closed Realms (Crotus Prenn Asylum, Léry's Memorial Institute) | Use open Realms for chases |
| Was the player very frustrated last session? | Switch to a Realm with clear safe zones, and change the avatar to a trapper type | You may add pressure |

**⚠ Common Misconception**: A Realm is not a Difficulty Class. Coldwind Farm isn't "harder than Macmillan Estate" — it's **hard in a different way**. What the player learns at Coldwind Farm (mind games, sound-positioning) is useless at Macmillan Estate, and vice versa. **Don't arrange the eight Realms into a difficulty slope.**

# Chapter Five　Solo Trial: How to Run for One Person

One table, two people, one lamp.

This is the **default configuration** of the work, not a stripped version, not a solo variant. The whole math grows around N=1: Generator target count, Hook rounds, self-rescue DC, Hatch opening condition — all have N=1-specific values. Run it as written and you get a full Trial of equal length and equal density.

But the Solo Trial tests something else. At a four-player table you only need to perform the avatar; tension self-generates between players. One-on-one, **all tension is manufactured by you**. One second of silence and the table goes cold.

This chapter gives you six tools: the Killer Behavior Table that outsources your stance, the Echo Companion three-column behavior table, the twenty-five-round pacing skeleton, three rescue techniques when stuck, prevention and recovery for three kinds of collapse, and a complete demonstration from round 1 to the closing Campfire.

## 5-1　Lay out the numbers first

The Solo Trial's parameters are all finalized. **Copy them; don't calculate yourself.**

| Parameter | N=1 value |
|---|---|
| Echo Companion count | **2** |
| Effective Repair Force (ERF) | 1.0 |
| Generator target / spawned | 2 / 4 |
| Avatar PRE cap | 6 ＋ INT(Prestige (PRS) ÷ 4) |
| Per-round PRE recovery | 2 |
| Hook stage duration | 7 rounds |
| Hook self-rescue DC | 17 |
| Endgame Collapse rounds | 12 |
| Exit Gate segments | 3 |
| Chest count | 2 |
| Pallet / Window / Hook count | 8 / 5 / 8 |
| Standard total rounds | 25 rounds (table time 60–75 min) |

**Hatch opening (N=1 specific branch):**

```
HATCH_gen = MAX(1, GEN_req − 1) = MAX(1, 2 − 1) = 1
```

In plain terms: **Hatch threshold = Generator target − 1, minimum 1.** In the Solo Trial, when "completed Generators ≥ 1" **AND** "all Echo Companions eliminated," the Hatch opens.

**⚠ Common Misconception**: The Solo Trial's Hatch does **not** open because "only you're left." The key is all Echo Companions wiped. As long as one lives, the Hatch stays closed and the player must take the Exit Gate. **State this rule clearly at opening** — it's what the player weighs all night.

### The three-phase structure's variation in the Solo Trial

| Phase | Rounds | Difference from Squad Trial |
|---|---|---|
| **Exploration** | Rounds 1–5 | Stretched. Multiplayer time is eaten by teammate coordination; the Solo Trial fills with exploration |
| **Pressure** | Rounds 6–19 | Mid-phase **forces** at least two Respites (see 5-7) |
| **Endgame** | Rounds 20–25 | Same as Squad Trial, but the player must complete 3 gate segments alone, with no one to split attention |

### Phase 3 escalation's solo re-adjudication

Three-phase escalation: Phase 2 triggers on "completed Generators ≥ INT(target÷2)," Phase 3 on "completed = target−1." At N=1 target 2, both conditions hold when the first Generator completes, so the Solo Trial uses the following instead.

**Phase 3 solo re-adjudication:**

- **Trigger**: in the Solo Trial, the first Generator completes.
- **Cost**: none.
- **Adjudication**: none (no roll).
- **Result**: only enter **Phase 2 (The Impatience)**, pay 1 Hope Point (HOP). **Phase 3 (The Entity's Wrath) instead triggers on whichever of these two comes first** — (1) the last target Generator completes, Exit Gates power on; (2) the player character enters Hook stage 2.

**【Solo Trial】** Phase 3's four effects (TR+8 / Basic Attack+2 / 1 free Hunt Lead per round / Bloodlust −1 per achieved round) take **only one** at N=1. Before opening, roll 1d6 and write on the Screen: 1–2 take Basic Attack+2, 3–4 take free Hunt Lead, 5–6 take Bloodlust−1. All four stacked would end the solo player outright; this substitution must be enforced.

## 5-2　Where one-on-one tension comes from

The four-player table's tension has three sources: **teammates die, teammates err, teammates abandon you.** The Solo Trial has none. You must replace them.

| Original tension | Solo Trial replacement | How you do it |
|---|---|---|
| Teammates die | **Echo Companions die** | They don't count toward win/loss, but get hooked, and stop counting |
| Teammates err | **Echo Companions are only half-efficient** | Let the player see it repair three rounds and not reach half; he'll anxious himself |
| Teammates abandon you | **Your silence** | One-on-one, when you don't speak, the player is isolated |
| — | **The world itself** | The solo traveler has time to see this place clearly. Give the description fully |

### Three pacing props

The biggest technique problem one-on-one is: **the moment you stop to think, the table goes empty.** Prepare these three so you never go cold.

| Prop | Function | How to use |
|---|---|---|
| **Knock heartbeat** | Fills silence, and silence itself becomes info | The Solo Trial **always** maintains some rhythm, even at Escaped (ESC) band, knock lightly every 8 seconds |
| **Zone scene card** | Gives you 5 seconds to think | One index card per zone, three environment lines on the front. Player moves in, you push the card for him to read himself |
| **Echo Companion's one line** | Metronome | Fixed one line at end of each round. Just one |

### Zone scene card

**Zone scene card:**

- **Trigger**: player character moves into a zone not yet entered this session.
- **Cost**: none (0 AP for the player).
- **Adjudication**: none (no roll).
- **Result**: you push that zone's scene card to the player for **him to read aloud**. After he reads, you respond to his questions. If he's been there before, give one line instead, and **this line must differ from last time**.

**⚠ Common Misconception**: Having the player read the card himself isn't laziness. **What he says himself, he remembers longer.** And in those five seconds you can check the Killer Behavior Table, compute Fear Pressure (FP), decide whether to release next round.

> **Example**: Solo Trial round 2. The player character enters the "Suffocation Pit" for the first time. You push the card, say nothing.
> He reads: "Half a foot of water at the bottom, can't see the end. A row of equally-spaced iron rings on the wall, evenly rusted. The air smells of coal, and another smell you can't name."
> He finishes, looks up: "What are those iron rings for?"
> You answer: "You don't know."
> **In those eight seconds you checked the Killer Behavior Table, and he's already afraid of those rings.**

## 5-3　Killer Behavior Table: outsource your stance to a sheet of paper

One-on-one, you are both the avatar (has a stance) and the referee (should be neutral). This contradiction can't be resolved, only **outsourced**.

Before opening, write this session's avatar patrol rules **fixed on paper**, and read them strictly in play.

### Template

```
【Killer Behavior Table · Trapper · Macmillan Estate】

Patrol sequence (loop):
  Coal Tower → Suffocation Pit → Sheltering Woods → Moaning Warehouse → Wailing Ironworks → back to Coal Tower

At round start, check in order, execute the first that holds:
  ① Survivor trace (Scratch Marks / blood) in same zone      → follow trace one square
  ② Generator roared last round                              → move one square toward that Generator
  ③ A Survivor hooked ≥ 2 rounds with no rescue              → return to defend Hook
  ④ None of above                                            → advance one square per patrol sequence
```

### Killer Behavior Table adjudication

**Killer Behavior Table adjudication:**

- **Trigger**: each round's Threat Phase start.
- **Cost**: none.
- **Adjudication**: check conditions ① to ④ in order, take the **first that holds**. If one condition has two or more legal directions (e.g. two Scratch Marks), roll 1d10: odd take left, even take right.
- **Result**: always execute per the text; **you have no discretion**. If the resulting position is extremely unfavorable or favorable to the player, you may not modify it. Conditions ①–④ themselves produce no CritS/Succ/Fail/CritF — it's a decision procedure, not a check; the four outcomes occur on subsequent chase contests and attack checks.

### Your two obligations

1. **Once this table is written, it may not be modified for the whole session.** Not a single word.
2. **When the player asks, you don't deny its existence.** You may say "it has its rules," but not "I decided randomly."

> **Design Note**: A logical monster is far scarier than a random one, because it can be understood, predicted, exploited. The moment the player starts reasoning about the patrol route, he moves from "being chased" to "playing chess," and the chess player doesn't leave the table.

> **Example**: Round 13, the player character hides in a Sheltering Woods Locker. You check the table: ① no new trace in zone (he crawled in); ② no roar last round; ③ no one on hook; ④ per sequence → avatar enters Sheltering Woods from Suffocation Pit.
> You move the avatar marker to that square, then say: "It comes in. It doesn't stop. It walks past your row of cabinets."
> The player exhales. You add: "It reaches the door, pauses." (pause three seconds) "Then goes out."
> **You followed the table exactly, gave him no trouble at all. But those three seconds were the tensest of his night.**

## 5-4　Echo Companions: who they are

### Narrative positioning

At the Campfire the player is told: "The Entity favors the lone one." The Campfire Companion truly believes it. **This is wrong, and you know the truth.**

One person's hope doesn't taste good. A lone Survivor only produces the thin, low-yield emotion "I want to live." The Entity has done the math: opening a full Trial for one loner, the cost often exceeds the gain. So it dredges used-up old Survivors from the Fogcellar — those already drained, empty shells retaining only behavior patterns — reshapes them into forms and sends them into the Trial.

They have one function: **to make the loner produce the thought "I must save him."** Once that thought appears, the Trial's yield multiplies several times.

### Should the player feel for them

**Yes, but not given by you.**

You don't voice the Echo Companions, don't give them personality, don't let them speak meaningful sentences. They only repeat a residual action. **The player will give them personality himself** — and that process is exactly why this mechanic exists.

| Echo Companion | Residual behavior | Special |
|---|---|---|
| **Echo · Forty-Seven** | Counts from 1 to 100, then restarts | **When it reaches 100 it stops and looks at the player once.** If the player looks back at that second, +1 Hope Point (HOP) |
| **Echo · The Empty-Hugger** | Arms held in a hugging pose, holding nothing. Avoids obstacles when moving | If the player heals it to Healthy, it hands the "thing in its arms" to the player. It's empty. +1 Hope Point (HOP), +1 Campfire Ember. **After handing over, its arms drop, and it starts following the player** |
| **Echo · The Hand-Wiper** | Constantly wipes hands. Can't get them clean | The player may spend his own pluck-chance to pick its Memory Pod: +3 Campfire Ember |
| **Echo · Hilda** | Sits at the outermost edge of the fire circle (even with no fire). Trails last when moving | Occasionally very quietly says: "…next time will be fine." |

### Three operation disciplines

1. **Don't give them personality.** One line per round, and that line is always the same kind.
2. **Don't use them to solve problems.** They repair slowly, can't self-rescue, can't heal each other. Their purpose is to **create choices**, not provide help.
3. **Don't let them steal the show.** One line at round end: "Forty-Seven is still counting."

## 5-5　The Echo Companion three-column behavior table

This is the most memorized thing in the whole chapter. **You run these three columns for each Echo Companion every round, total under fifteen seconds.**

### General rules

| Item | Rule |
|---|---|
| Action Point (AP) | **1 per round.** 1 AP = perform one Column-Two action, not priced per the Survivor AP cost table |
| Check modifier | **Fixed +2**, no attribute roll, no Skill Level counted |
| Repair Generator | Progress Point (PP) contribution **halved** (50% efficiency). Multi-person stacking penalty still applies |
| Heal | Can heal the player character, PP **not halved**. **Cannot heal self, cannot heal each other** |
| Self-rescue | **Cannot.** Once hooked, can only wait for the player to rescue |
| Hook | The Echo Companion's Hook has **only one stage, 3 rounds**. Unrescued within 3 rounds → Sacrifice |
| Win/loss | **Not counted in victory condition.** All wiped doesn't mean player defeat (but opens the Hatch) |
| Open Exit Gate | **Cannot.** They have no qualification to leave here |

### Column One · Priority (decide "what it wants to do")

**No roll.** Check in order, take the first that holds.

| Order | Condition | This round's action |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | Player spends 1 Hope Point (HOP) to designate it to make noise | **Bait** (overrides all below) |
| **2** | Player character hooked, and ≥ 1 round elapsed | **Go rescue** (if not arrived, move 1 zone; if in same zone, perform rescue) |
| **3** | Player character in Dying, and in same zone | **Help up** |
| **4** | Player character in Injured, same zone, and avatar beyond Far (FAR) | **Heal player** |
| **5** | Avatar in Near (NEA) or Contact (CON) with it | **Flee** |
| **6** | None of above | **Repair the nearest unfinished Generator**; if not in that zone, move 1 zone this round |

**⚠ Common Misconception**: Order 2's "≥ 1 round elapsed" is not a delay, it's a rule. The Echo Companion **doesn't move the round the player is hooked**, starts moving on round 2. This blank round is the window for the player to decide whether to self-rescue.

**An Echo Companion does not rescue another Echo Companion.** Order 2 only targets the player character. They have no relation to each other — they don't even know the other exists.

### Column Two · Execution (decide "how well")

**Roll 2d10 + 2**, contested against the DC in the table below.

| This round's action | DC | Critical Success (CritS) | Success (Succ) | Failure (Fail) | Critical Failure (CritF) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Repair Generator** | 10 ＋ Fear Pressure (FP) | +10 halved → **+5** | +6 halved → **+3** | −2 halved → **−1**, **roar** (you publicly declare that Generator's position) | −5 halved → **−3** (progress-stage protection still applies), roar |
| **Heal player** | 10 ＋ Fear Pressure (FP) | +6 PP | +4 PP | −1 PP, makes sound | −3 PP, makes sound, round ends |
| **Help up player** | 9 | Help up, and player gets +1 AP next round | Help up | Didn't help up | Didn't help up, and it trips itself (no action next round) |
| **Rescue hooked player** | 9 ＋ Fear Pressure (FP) | Rescue, and player gains 1 round "Silent" (Killer can't make Tracking check on him this round) | Rescue | Didn't rescue, Hook stage rounds −1 | Didn't rescue, Hook stage rounds −1, and it's scratched by the hook, drops 1 Health State (HS) tier |
| **Flee** | 12 | Move 2 zones, leave no trace | Move 1 zone | Didn't move | Didn't move, and avatar gains 1 Hunt Lead |

**Bait's check is separate**: if the avatar is at Far (FAR) or farther, must make a Perception check (2d10 + avatar modifier vs DC 12), failing to redirect toward the noise. If the avatar is within Mid (MID), **bait fails, Hope Point (HOP) not refunded** — state this to the player at opening.

### Column Three · Sound (decide "what the player hears")

**Roll 1d6.** This column is the metronome and your improvisation source. Read it at round end, **no tone, no explanation**.

| 1d6 | Echo · Forty-Seven | Echo · The Empty-Hugger |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | "…thirty-one. Thirty-two." | (It shifts how it holds the empty thing.) |
| 2 | "…sixty-eight." | (It circles wide around a stone.) |
| 3 | "…forty-seven. Forty-eight." | (No sound. It's still there.) |
| 4 | (It stops. Then starts from one.) | (It looks down at what it holds.) |
| 5 | "…ninety-nine. One hundred." **(It looks up, meets your eyes.)** | (It pulls its arms in a little tighter.) |
| 6 | (No sound. You don't know if it's still there.) | (No sound. You don't know if it's still there.) |

**On rolling 5 (Forty-Seven)**: if the player declares "I look back" in that instant, +1 Hope Point (HOP). **Don't prompt him.**

**On rolling 6**: that Echo Companion makes no sound this round. **Don't explain why.** The player will go find it himself.

### Full operation flow (per round, per Echo Companion)

1. Check **Column One**, decide what it does (no roll, three seconds).
2. Roll 2d10 + 2, check **Column Two**'s corresponding row (five seconds).
3. Push the Progress Point (PP) onto the table (three seconds).
4. Roll 1d6, check **Column Three**, read that line (four seconds).
5. **Add nothing.**

### Improvisation rules

When a situation isn't among Column One's six (e.g. trapped in a trap, affected by a Creation, map collapse), handle as follows:

- **Can it try**: always may attempt, DC per the closest Column Two row.
- **Modifier**: always +2. Don't adjust for situation.
- **Will it creatively find a way**: **No.** Echo Companions have no creativity. They repeat Column One order 6 until interrupted.
- **Player commands it**: the player may speak, but it doesn't respond. **The only exception is the 1-HOP bait** (Column One order 1).

> **Example**: Round 17. Player character Injured, hiding in Sheltering Woods; Forty-Seven in same zone; avatar at Suffocation Pit (Far (FAR)).
> You check Column One: order 1 fails (no HOP spent), order 2 fails (not hooked), order 3 fails (not Dying), **order 4 holds** (Injured, same zone, avatar beyond Far) → heal player.
> Column Two: DC 10 + FP 2 = 12. You roll 8 and 8 = 16, plus fixed +2 = 18. MoS +6 → **Critical Success (CritS)** → +6 PP. Heal track from 0 to 6.
> Column Three: roll 1d6 get 3. You read: "…forty-seven. Forty-eight." then you shut up.
> **The player says: "It's holding my wound shut while counting."** — that sentence is his, not yours. You performed nothing.

## 5-6　When to let an Echo Companion die

They must die; the only question is **when it's dramatic**.

### Three right times

| Time | Why effective |
|---|---|
| **3–6 rounds after the player just rescued it** | The player already paid a cost; losing it then hurts the sunk cost. **This is the most effective kind** |
| **After the player makes the "don't rescue" choice** | Give his choice weight. Settle per the table publicly, don't scold |
| **Endgame, player already at the Exit Gate** | "A person stops at the door and looks back" — this is the work's theme. Your job is to make that look-back costly |

### Three wrong times

| Time | Why bad |
|---|---|
| **Rounds 1–4** | The player doesn't know it yet; death is just losing a repairer. **Don't eliminate Echo Companions in the Exploration Phase** |
| **When the player himself is on the Hook** | Two cruel things at once, emotions cancel, player only goes numb |
| **When the player hasn't declared an action two rounds straight** | That's a prelude to giving up. Taking another Echo Companion then ends the session |

### Sacrifice performance

When an Echo Companion is Sacrificed, **don't use the five-beat performance method**. They don't scream.

> You retract the Hook prop to the table corner.
> "It didn't struggle. When lifted, it was still counting."
> (pause three seconds) "…forty-seven. Forty-eight." (pause three more seconds) "Then no sound."

Then **settle publicly, no comment**.

### Echo Companion settlement

**Echo Companion settlement:**

- **Trigger**: any event in the table below occurs.
- **Cost**: none.
- **Adjudication**: none (no roll).
- **Result**: settle publicly per the table below. Push Campfire Ember beads into the jar publicly so they sound.

| Event | Hope Point (HOP) | Campfire Ember |
|---|---|---|
| Player rescues Echo Companion from Hook | +2 | +1 |
| Player heals Echo Companion to Healthy | +1 | +1 |
| Player takes a hit for an Echo Companion | +2 | +2 |
| Echo Companion Sacrificed (player present and had attempted rescue) | +1 (mourning) | +1 |
| Echo Companion Sacrificed (player never attempted rescue) | −2 | 0 |
| Player deliberately pushes Echo Companion toward avatar as bait | −3 | **−2 (may be negative)** |

**⚠ Common Misconception**: The last row is this mechanic's moral gauge. **The mechanic allows the player to treat the Echo Companion as a tool; don't stop him, don't lecture him.** Settle per the table, take the negative beads out of the jar, place them on the table. Say nothing. The world will remember in later scenarios.

> **Example**: Round 25. Forty-Seven is hooked, one round left. The player character already opened the Exit Gate, stands at the door.
> He asks: "Can I make it back to rescue in time?" You answer: "You don't know."
> He runs back. Rescue check: 3 AP, DC 9 + FP 3 = 12. He rolls 2 and 4 = 6, plus Focus (FOC) bonus +2, Check Total (CT) 8, Margin of Success (MoS) −4 — **Failure (Fail)**.
> Round end. You retract the Hook prop, read the three sacrifice lines. Then push +1 Hope Point (HOP) to him, drop a bead in the jar.
> **He had attempted, so he gets mourning, not punishment. The sound of that bead he'll remember to the next session.**

## 5-7　Pacing control: how to divide twenty-five rounds

### Skeleton table

| Round | Phase | What you should do | Heartbeat |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1–2 | Exploration | Three-sense description, push scene cards. Player finds Generators, opens Chests | **None** |
| 3–5 | Exploration | Let him finish the 1st Generator. Enter Phase 2, pay 1 Hope Point (HOP) | Escaped (ESC) → Far (FAR) |
| 6–8 | Pressure | **First chase. Must let him escape** | Mid (MID) → Near (NEA) |
| 9–10 | Pressure | **First Respite** (forced) | gone |
| 11–14 | Pressure | The real meat-grinder. Kick machines, injure, Tracking | undulating |
| 15–18 | Pressure | Hook, self-rescue, Echo Companion rescue | dense |
| 19 | Pressure tail | **Second Respite** (forced, usually auto-generated by Mercy Valve) | gone |
| 20–25 | Endgame | Exit Gates power, Phase 3, Endgame Collapse | full |

### Respite insertion

The Solo Trial's Respite **is not an option, it's a rule**. The player has no teammates to talk to; if you don't give him a stop, he goes numb by round 15.

**Respite insertion:**

- **Trigger**: any of the following — (1) reached round 9 or round 19; (2) player character in chase or hooked 3 rounds straight; (3) player no declared action 2 rounds straight.
- **Cost**: none.

- **Adjudication**: none (no roll).
- **Result**: the avatar **clearly leaves** the player's zone, and **stays out 2 rounds**. You make the heartbeat fully vanish, and in those 2 rounds give at least one environment description unrelated to the Trial. **These 2 rounds must be a real rest** — no fake-leave-then-ambush. A fake respite used once and the player will never trust your heartbeat again.

### Three-Strike Law

**Three-Strike Law:**

- **Trigger**: in the Solo Trial, the player character enters Dying for the third time.
- **Cost**: none.
- **Adjudication**: none (no roll).
- **Result**: the avatar **must** switch to "drop him where he lies and leave," may not pick up, may not hook. World reason: The Entity wants to play longer. **Within a single Trial, the player character may be hooked at most twice.**

### Half Law

**Half Law:**

- **Trigger**: the preparation phase before the Trial begins.
- **Cost**: none.
- **Adjudication**: none (no roll).
- **Result**: you **must** decide before opening which two phases this session's avatar's two "obvious misses" fall in (one in the first half of Pressure, one in the second half of Pressure or Endgame), and write them on the Screen. When that phase arrives, regardless of the situation, perform that miss. Miss form is one of: overshoot (DB +1), wrong direction (avatar moves to opposite zone), pallet hit (stun 2 rounds).

**⚠ Common Misconception**: The Half Law isn't going easy, it's **scriptwriting**. Horror-movie monsters miss too, to make the next non-miss scarier. And because you **pre-wrote** the timing, these two misses aren't affected by your live emotions — same thinking as the Killer Behavior Table: **outsource the stance.**

> **Example**: Round 19. The player character just rescued by an Echo Companion from the Hook, Health State (HS) is Injured, still holds 1 Hope Point (HOP). Respite insertion trigger (1) and Mercy Valve both hold.
> You don't announce the rule, you perform it: "It reaches the corner, stops." (pause three seconds) "It tilts its head, as if listening another direction." (pause three more seconds) "Then it walks east. The heartbeat is shrinking."
> For the next two rounds you don't knock. You describe instead: "You slide down the wall and sit. You find all your right-hand fingernails broken, don't know when."
> **In those two rounds the player has the Echo Companion bandage him, drink some water, and you chat one line about this character's past. When you knock the first time again, he jumps up.**

## 5-8　Three rescue techniques when the player is stuck

One-on-one, being stuck is especially fatal — no teammate to offer another idea. You have three techniques, **used from lowest to highest cost**.

| Technique | What you do | When to use | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Environment hint** | In the next environment description, put the clue **in the second sentence** (the auditory one) | Player stuck 1 round. **Use this first** | None. It's free |
| **Whisperer hint** | Let the player discover a Whisperer in the Realm | Player stuck 2–3 rounds, and still holds Hope Point (HOP) | 1 Hope Point (HOP), and the answer is **beside the point but literally correct** |
| **Echo Companion action hint** | Let the Echo Companion, in Column One order 6, **move next to the right answer** | Player stuck 4+ rounds, or already going silent | That Echo Companion's position exposed; avatar moves toward it next round per Killer Behavior Table rule ② |

### Environment hint

- **Trigger**: player character no effective action 1 round straight, or clearly doesn't know the next step.
- **Cost**: none.
- **Adjudication**: none (no roll).
- **Result**: you put the clue in the **second sentence** (the auditory one) of the next environment description. Not the first — the player is still adjusting attention; not the third — he's already thinking of something else. **After placing, don't repeat, don't ask "did you notice?"**

### Whisperer hint

- **Trigger**: player character at the Whisperer's node, and declares a search.
- **Cost**: 1 AP to find + 1 Hope Point (HOP) trade.
- **Adjudication**: 2d10 + Stealth (STL) bonus + Sneak (SNK) Skill Level, or 2d10 + Focus (FOC) bonus + Insight (INS) Skill Level, vs DC 12.
- **Result**:
  - **Critical Success (CritS)** — found, and this trade is **free** (no HOP deducted).
  - **Success (Succ)** — found. Spend 1 Hope Point (HOP) for one piece of info about the current Trial.
  - **Failure (Fail)** — not found, AP still deducted.
  - **Critical Failure (CritF)** — not found, and makes sound: avatar gains 1 Hunt Lead.

**The Whisperer's line is always beside the point but literally correct.** Player asks "Where is it?" — you answer: "Not where you just were."

### Echo Companion action hint

- **Trigger**: player character no effective action 4 rounds straight, or silent 2 rounds straight.
- **Cost**: that Echo Companion's position exposed.
- **Adjudication**: none (no roll). This is your legal discretion in choosing "nearest unfinished Generator" under Column One order 6.
- **Result**: the Echo Companion moves to where you want the player's attention, and reads its line in Column Three. Next round, the avatar moves toward there per Killer Behavior Table rule ②. **You bought the player's attention at the cost of an Echo Companion entering danger.**

**⚠ Common Misconception**: None of the three techniques **includes "tell him the answer directly."** You can make the clue impossible to miss, but **the player must say that sentence himself**. What he figures out, he feels he won; what you say, he feels you gave.

## 5-9　Three kinds of collapse and recovery

### Collapse I · Death at opening

**Symptom**: Within rounds 1–6 the player character is knocked down or hooked, Generator Progress Point (PP) near zero.

**Prevention**: Exploration Phase (rounds 1–5) **no Terror Radius (TR) at all** (hard rule); Killer Behavior Table patrol sequence start **set at the square farthest from the player's spawn**; first chase per Closing-Net Criterion (see Ch.1 "Running an Asymmetric Trial"), first time chased this session → don't close.

**Recovery**: immediately trigger the Mercy Valve, avatar leaves zone 2 rounds; **place the 7-round hourglass on the table publicly** so the player sees he still has time; the two Echo Companions go to rescue per Column One order 2 (this is their first reason to exist); after rescue, immediately spend this session's first "obvious miss."

### Collapse II · Mid-game stall

**Symptom**: Rounds 10–18, Generator Progress Point (PP) unchanged three rounds straight, player moves back and forth on the map but does nothing.

**Prevention**: force the round 9 and round 19 Respites; every round must advance something (PP change / EH push / Echo Companion's one line, at least one); prepare a timer countdown to remind yourself.

**Recovery**: execute the acceleration ruling's "Power On" (see Ch.1), declare an Echo Companion pushed a Generator to 23 PP (third threshold of progress-stage protection); use the "Echo Companion action hint" to pull attention to a concrete place; pull one of the three "wrongness" types (repetition / displacement / gaze) into the description. **Don't** solve stall by raising the DC — the cause is "no goal," not "too easy."

### Collapse III · Hopeless endgame

**Symptom**: Endgame, the player calculates he mathematically can't win, starts saying "can't escape anyway."

**Prevention**: Hope Point (HOP) **must** be paid on entering Phase 2 and Phase 3; Phase 3's Hatch search DC **permanently −3** (rule, not favor); Three-Strike Law: at most two hooks, third knockdown must drop him.

**Recovery**: **check the Hatch condition** — requires "completed Generators ≥ 1" and "all Echo Companions eliminated." If Echo Companions still live but the player has no way out, you may have the avatar chase an Echo Companion (legal, since Killer Behavior Table rule ① would follow traces anyway); once it's out, the Hatch opens; remind the player of his Hope Point (HOP) count (public info, not cheating), and that spending 2 can bring a eliminated Echo Companion back at the map edge next round; **Endgame Collapse is 12 rounds, put the countdown hourglass on the table so he sees how long.**

> **Example**: Round 22, the player finished both Generators, Exit Gate powered, but got beaten to Injured at only segment 1, and Forty-Seven is on the Hook. He says: "Forget it, I can't finish."
> Collapse III symptom. You don't comfort, you give info: "Endgame Collapse countdown is 12 rounds. 3 gone." (turn the hourglass to show him) then add: "You hold 3 Hope Point (HOP)."
> He's silent five seconds, says: "…I spend 1, make Forty-Seven call."
> **You gave him nothing. You only laid his own resources on the table.**

# Chapter Six　Running Scenarios S1–S8

This chapter strings the eight scenarios (S1–S8) you hold into a campaign. It doesn't repeat the scenarios' stories, only tells you: **where to spend effort this session, how to connect session to session, how to settle after a session, how to resolve the whole campaign.**

---

## 6-0　What this chapter does

The eight scenarios are linear: S1 is the amnesia prologue, S8 is the endgame. Players should go in order, because later scenarios reveal the foreshadowing of earlier ones. You don't explain "what is Ember" in S1 — that waits until the Fogcellar in S3, the Ember Niche in S7.

This chapter handles four things:
1. **Match difficulty**: higher Prestige (PRS) means higher Killer PRE tier, higher check DC (see 6-1).
2. **Connect the order**: sessions transition via "Campfire scenes"; skipping or rerunning has fixed handling (see 6-2).
3. **Focus per session**: each scenario's GM hosting notes (see 6-3).
4. **Settle and resolve**: interlude growth (see 6-4), endgame resolution (see 6-5).

**Single-session time budget**: one scenario ~3–4 hours, solo config (Setup 1) ~2–3 hours. If you only have two hours, cut the second act's side objectives, not the Ember Moment — **the story can be short, the ending can't be missing.** The whole campaign of eight sessions usually lands at ten to fourteen gatherings, because players rerun and linger at the Campfire. That's good; don't rush.

**Three minutes before opening**: the three things to do between the player sitting down and your first opening line — ① set the scaling table by N (see 7-3, solo config (Setup 1) confirm 2 Echo Companions' behavior table in place); ② mark the Hope Point (HOP) forced-grant nodes on the scenario (see G0 Introduction), don't remember midway; ③ pick this session's Realm three opening lines (see G4 Realm Codex), read verbatim, pause three seconds. These three take under thirty seconds but decide whether the session flows.

> **Example**: The player sits in with Prestige (PRS) 9 for S4. Before opening you check 6-1: S4 suggests 7–10, PRE tier 4, DC 15; place the Echo Companion behavior table (solo), mark the HOP node on "Medical Record #17." The moment the player sits: "Crotus won't call your name" — the opening flows.

## 6-2　Campaign order and transitions

### Linear progression

The eight scenarios go in S1→S8 order. Each ends back at the Campfire; the next opens with a "Campfire scene" carrying the previous session's emotion and foreshadowing:
- **S1→S2**: After S1's Ember Moment, S2 opens with Gilmar Sarto bringing back a sheet of iron carved "don't trust the fifth one."
- **S2→S3**: S2 reveals the carving was carved by Elowi herself; S3 opens entering the Fogcellar — the memory archive.
- **S3→S4**: S3's pod-picking plants the S6 foreshadowing; S4 opens entering the asylum (highest Afterimage density of the whole campaign).
- **S4→S5**: S4's Medical Record #17 writes a swamp address; S5 opens "someone is missing."
- **S5→S6**: S5's Fahn notes reveal the "inner-production" line; S6 opens with Hilda Lane missing.
- **S6→S7**: S6's choice decides Hilda's whereabouts; S7 opens studying the Fahn notes wall.
- **S7→S8**: S7 takes the First Ember; S8 opens entering Forsaken Boneyard's Lone Tower.

### Skip handling

**Trigger**: the player, for time or will, wants to skip a session (e.g. S4 too long).

**Cost**: the skipped scenario's main-line info **is not** given; the player falls behind in info, but it's their choice.

**Adjudication**: no roll needed. You directly mark that scenario's "key Memory Card" as **not obtained**, and add a line of background at the next session's opening.

**Result**: later scenarios can still recover the info, but at extra cost — e.g. the Memory Card (Memory Card) auto-given by S3 pod-picking becomes a DC 15 Will (WIL) check in S4 instead; failure permanently misses that foreshadowing.

> **Example**: The player skips S3, so no Fogcellar Memory Cards like "trees don't eat soil." At S6's name-call, you find he can't produce the corresponding Memory Card (Memory Card) — you say: "You open your mouth, but can't recall that line. Hilda looks at you once, turns away." This isn't punishment, it's the consequence of an earlier choice, happening as written.

### Rerun handling

**Trigger**: the player wants to replay a session (recover info, farm Bloodpoint (BP), switch ending branch).

**Cost**: rerunning consumes one "Campfire cycle," and the same scenario's Memory Card (Memory Card) isn't re-given (if already held, no drop this run).

**Adjudication**: no roll needed. You let the player rerun, but **the key-choice Ember Moment triggers only once** — on rerun that session's Ember Moment becomes "recollection," no new Campfire Ember.

**Result**: rerunning can recover skipped info and Bloodpoint (BP), but the total Campfire Ember doesn't inflate from it; the endgame threshold (see 6-5) only counts actual accumulation.

**【Solo Trial】**: under solo config, the Echo Companion's life/death binds deeper to the player's choices. If in S5 the player chose "push the most-rescued Echo Companion down for info," on rerun that Echo Companion **won't come back** — you simply say: "That spot is empty." Don't deny his choice with mechanics; let the world remember.

### Session break (Session Break)

**Trigger**: a scenario isn't finished, time's up, must disperse.

**Cost**: no resource deducted. A break isn't failure; don't punish the player for it.

**Adjudication**: no roll needed. You write four things on paper: current Generator completions, each character's Hit Points (HP) state and Hook stage, current PRE value, triggered Ember Moments. Everything else resets.

**Result**: next opening, you rebuild the scene in three lines (where they are, what they hear, what they carry), then continue from the recorded round. **Don't reroll dice already rolled, don't "re-enact" last session's chase.**

**【Solo Trial】**: under solo config additionally record the Echo Companions' alive count andtheir own injuries. Echo Companions don't heal on their own during the break — they return as they were.

### Campfire Scene how to perform

The transition isn't blank. Each return to the Campfire, you fixed-give three things: **one environment line** (is the fire still strong, how much Fog receded), **one piece of evidence** (something brought back, even just a sheet of iron), **one unanswered question** (better if the player says it himself). After the three, go to settlement; don't let the Campfire become small talk — its function is to carry emotion and plant foreshadowing, not a rest room.

## 6-3　GM hosting notes per scenario (S1–S8)

**S1 Ignorance (tutorial session)**: This is a "tutorial Trial," but don't teach by lecturing — let the rules emerge from experience. The first chase **must let the player escape** (see Ch.1 "Running an Asymmetric Trial" pacing control). S1 deploys no Realm Creations, first let the player meet the "Killer" threat. Mid-session let the player witness firsthand the "self-hope raises Entity Hunger (EH) +1, altruism lowers EH −1" track contrast — this is S1's most important choice; the GM should push it in the main line.

**S2 Awareness (no-heartbeat tutorial)**: The Wraith (WRT) has no Terror Radius (TR), the player can't hear a heartbeat, must judge position by other clues — this is the "no-heartbeat fear" tutorial. Carvings scattered everywhere are collectible info, but collecting costs AP, and each challenges cognition. The GM's drama is between "read the carving, abandon the favorable action" and "follow the carving, use four to reach target" — the former triggers the Ember Moment, the latter avoids the trap. Don't decide for the player; make him pay for reading.

**S3 Reclaim (most painful in the open)**: Coldwind Farm is the most unfavorable Realm to Survivors, the Hillbilly (HBY) lethal in the open, the player must use cornfield cover for mind games not looping. The Fogcellar has no Killer, no objective; pressure comes from the shrinking countdown and the cognitive challenge of "recognizing yourself." The GM should arrange at least one player to pick the wrong pod (picking a Killer's memory = S6 foreshadowing). The tree only recognizes one thing — altruistic hope; this is S3's axis.

**S4 Understanding (highest Afterimage density)**: Crotus Prenn Asylum has very short sightlines, easy to hide but no loop space once bitten, the hard-stop feeling is the signature. The Nurse (NRS) Blink ignores walls and floors, making "hide in a room" fail. The GM should let the player feel "thinking they'd get better" — those who die here cling to that emotion. Complete 3+ unfinished matters and read Medical Record #17 aloud to trigger the Ember Moment) and the axis revelation. New ability 'Afterimage Dialogue' unlocks from now on.

**S5 Transgression (beyond the boundary)**: Backwater Swamp sits closest to the Void; Act 2 has the player find the sunken stairs to confirm where they lead, and Act 3 descends them to reach the Void — no Killer, no objective, Hope Point (HOP) drains continuously, and the only way out is to "produce one genuinely real emotion again." The Hag (HAG) erupts after the 8th round, matching the swamp's slow dread. The GM should make the isolation of "someone vanished, and only you remember" land — because you hold the Memory Card (MEM).

**S6 The Cost (the cruelest truth)**: The Silent One (SIL) is someone who walked over from the Campfire — Hilda Lane is being recast. No heartbeat, emits Survivor voices to lure, mirror-swaps mid-Realm. Mid-session the player chases the "teammate's voice" into a room, sees a crouched figure repairing a Generator, and calls her name — she turns around. The player has three ways to interrupt this, **and this is the campaign's most critical choice**: interrupt, watch it complete, or trade a Memory for a different ending. If they do not interrupt, Hilda becomes a Killer appearing in S7/S8, and the player gains knowledge of the recasting process (the prerequisite for S8's "Takeover" ending).

**S7 Arming (the most counterintuitive)**: The thick leaf layer of the Red Forest makes "crawling at Dying is actually safe" — the campaign's most counterintuitive design, which the GM should let the player discover themselves. The Huntress (HTR) throws at range, making "distance no longer safe." Every shrine description must hold one thing that is off (wrong proportion). If the player's Campfire Ember meets the threshold, they take the first Ember directly; otherwise an additional Trial triggers (survive inside the shrine). The Ember Moment reveals the "Umbilical" — the red line on the world map finally has a name.

**S8 Endgame (Conflagration)**: Forsaken Boneyard is the only quiet Realm; there are no whispers in the tower. The Legion (LGN) charges in frenzied succession — "stop and you die"; Act 3 pits the Entity itself as a boss-level, non-traditional fight. The twelve "turning back" poses of the Bonefield Statue are the player's final chance to speak. The ending is one of three (Conflagration / Ember / Takeover), and which is achievable is decided by total Campfire Ember (see 6-5). **In this Trial you are not the opponent — you are the witness of the ritual** — hand all decision-making fully to the player.

## 6-4　Interlude and Growth Settlement

Each Trial ends with a return to the Campfire and one settlement. Settlement order: Bloodpoint (BP) → Prestige (PRS) advancement → Memory Card (MEM) → Campfire Ember → Items.

### Bloodpoint (BP) and Prestige (PRS) Advancement

**Bloodpoint (BP)**: Per-Trial gain scales by party size (N) (solo gets a higher multiplier, see Ch.2 "Character Creation" and Ch.1 "Core Rules" for the finalized table), ensuring that a solo player keeps pace with a four-player squad's progression. S1 example: completion awards Bloodpoints (BP) at the standard ×1.0 (per the character-builder formula); nothing extra is added.

**Prestige (PRS) Advancement**: After completing a scenario you advance to the specified level, accumulating across scenarios:

| Scenario Completed | Prestige (PRS) Advances To | Scenario Completed | Prestige (PRS) Advances To |
|---|---|---|---|
| S1 | 3 | S5 | 13 |
| S2 | 5 | S6 | 15 |
| S3 | 7 | S7 | 18 |
| S4 | 10 | S8 | 20 (cap) |

Each advancement automatically grants, per Ch.2 "Character Creation" §2-7: Attribute Point (ATP = 15 + INT(PRS/2)), Skill Point (SKP = 2 + PRS), Perk slots (unlocked at Prestige (PRS) 5/10/15), Specialization tier 4 (at Prestige (PRS) 1/6/11/16). Each level requires `2000 × target level` Bloodpoints (BP); going from 1 to 20 totals 418,000.

> **Example**: A player finishing S3 (Prestige (PRS) 7) levels up to 7; the suggested range for the next Trial, S4, is 7–10. They need not be stuck at 7 — if they have enough Bloodpoints (BP), they can push Prestige (PRS) to 9 at the Campfire before entering S4; the Killer's Pressure level still follows S4's "4", but they have opened an extra Perk slot. This is the player's freedom, not your decision.

### Memory Card (MEM)

**When Acquired**: Available from S3 onward, MEM-01 through MEM-20. At settlement, tally what was gained this Trial and record it in the character sheet's "Memory Card" field.

**Uses**: ① From S3 onward, spend 1 Hope Point (HOP) to "ask the Memory Card for help" and reroll one relevant check; ② In S8's Conflagration ending each card = 3 fire; ③ Narrative credentials that trigger campaign abilities "Spot the Flaw," "Call the Name," and "Follow the Ember."

**【Solo Trial】**: Under solo configuration, the Memory Card (MEM) is the player's only "teammate" — with no four-player squad to split the searching, all memories must be accumulated alone. At settlement, remind the player: the "Whose is this you picked? (MEM-04)" obtained by grabbing the wrong pod in S3 becomes a powerful narrative credential in S6 — a benefit unique to solo play, so don't let them feel that grabbing wrong was a pure loss.

### Campfire Ember and Items

**Campfire Ember**: A campaign resource accumulated across scenarios, the ammunition of the S8 endgame. Solo configuration is expected to yield roughly 30–55 points total (an actively rescuing player can produce 3–6 per Trial). At settlement, add this Trial's accumulated amount to the total.

**Items**: Each player settles 1 common item (obtained from a Chest or Campfire offering). Add-ons are always consumed after a Trial ends; they are the main outlet of the Bloodpoint (BP) economy.

**⚠ Common Misconception**: "The more Memory Cards (MEM), the better?" The more you hold, the higher your Hope Point (HOP) cap — but the cost of "Hollowing" also grows heavier — **do not decide for the player how many to collect; let them discover the trade-off themselves before S8.**

### 6-4 Supplement: GM Cross-Scenario Tracking One-Pager

After each settlement, you should write four columns on a single sheet, to flip through across the whole campaign — this maintains the continuity of the eight Trials better than the scattered numbers on a character sheet:

| Tracking Column | What You Should Note | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Current Prestige (PRS) | The player's current level and unlockable slots | Check difficulty against the S1→S8 table; don't let the player assume overleveling auto-increases difficulty |
| Memory Card (MEM) acquired list | Which of MEM-01 to MEM-20 are in hand | Credentials for S6 "Call the Name" and S8 "Follow the Ember"; you should know which one is missing |
| Total Campfire Ember | Points accumulated across scenarios | The win/lose line for the 6-5 endgame threshold (solo 17/27/41; multiplayer see S8 §9 party-size adjustment table: 2 players 21/33/50, 3–4 players 25/40/60); let the player know in advance how far they are |
| Echo Companion status | Solo-play only: each of the 2 companions' survival and injuries | The follow-up to resuming after interruption (see 6-2) and the S5 push-off choice both depend on this column |

> **Example**: After the player finishes S3, you write down "Prestige (PRS) 7, Memory Cards MEM-01/03/04 in hand, Campfire Ember 9, both Echo Companions alive." When they reach S6 and say they want to "Call the Name" on Hilda, you flip to this sheet: MEM-04 (Whose is this you picked?) is on the list, so it can trigger; if not, honestly say "you can't recall that line." One sheet solves the continuity of eight Trials.

## 6-5　Campaign Endgame Resolution (S8)

S8 is the resolution point of the entire campaign. You are not the player's opponent — you are the witness of the ritual. The ending is the player's choice, and which ending is achievable is decided by total Campfire Ember (see World Setting Ch.7 "Hope and the World"):

| Total Campfire Ember (solo config 1; multiplayer per S8 §9 party-size adjustment table) | Achievable Ending |
|---|---|
| 17–26 | **Incomplete Conflagration**: The Entity is severely wounded, most Realms collapse, half the players return to reality and half remain in the lingering Fog (players decide who stays) |
| 27–40 | **Full Conflagration**: All Realms collapse, all players return to reality, memories incomplete (they remember "there was something important" but cannot say what) |
| ≥ 41 | **Blazing Conflagration**: An additional hidden ending — you may designate one old character who has fallen into the Void or been recast to bring back as well |

Three ending directions:
- **Conflagration**: Execute the ignition; the fire meets the threshold and burns into the Entity; the Realms collapse (per the threshold table above).
- **Ember**: Keep the Campfire unlit; the Entity lives and the cycle continues, but the players remain at the Campfire with complete memories — open-ended, suited to continuing the campaign.
- **Takeover**: Available only if S6's "do not interrupt" choice granted knowledge of the recasting process. The player takes the Entity's place; the cycle continues but is run by the player; the cost is that they remain in the Fog forever.

**Resolution Suggestion**: Before the endgame, use the "Bonefield Statue Address" to return the floor to the player (see Ch.4 "Realm Codex," Forsaken Boneyard). Regardless of the ending, the Ember Moment is certain to trigger — "the Nameless" is the gathering of all who turned back; the first Ember was not dropped by them, but by those including the player. **The last thing you must do is read this line quietly to the end, then pause for three seconds and wait for the player to speak.**

**【Solo Trial】**: Under solo configuration, all twelve Bonefield Statues are preserved (see Ch.4). At the endgame you ask "Which one will you speak to?" — the solo player has no teammates; the twelve statues are the only ones they can speak to. If they previously owed or lost an Echo Companion, this moment connects automatically. Do not decide for them whether to speak, nor smooth it over for them; **let them finish, then resolve the endgame per the rules.**

## 7-2　Common Resolution Flowcharts (Text, Step-Form)

Each entry marks Action Point (AP), Difficulty Class (DC), and relevant status. Four elements: Trigger / Cost / Check / Result, plus "Failure Handling."

**How to read these flowcharts**: Each entry's "Check" column gives the standard check formula; if that Trial has a Realm modifier (see G4) or a Perk bonus (see the *Perk Codex*), add the modifier directly into the relevant attribute/skill. The column you should rehearse most each round is "Failure Handling" — players will mostly fail, so think through what failure looks like in advance, or you'll stall on the spot.

**1. Healing Others**
Trigger: You and an Injured or Dying Survivor are in the Contact (CON) Distance Band.
Cost: 3 Action Points (AP).
Check: 2d10 + Focus (FOC) modifier + First Aid (MED) Skill Level (SL) vs Difficulty Class (DC) 10 + Fear Pressure (FP).
Result: Each success accumulates healing progress; at 12 Progress Points (PP) restore them from Injured to Healthy, or from Dying to Injured.
Failure Handling: A noise cue; this round's healing progress −1; a critical failure zeroes this round's Action Points (AP). Related status: while Broken (ST-03), healing is entirely impossible.

> **Example**: In a four-player game (N=4), a Survivor is Injured; you spend 3 Action Points (AP) to heal them, rolling 2d10 + Focus (FOC) + First Aid (MED) for 14 against Difficulty Class (DC) 10, accumulating progress on success; after three successes they return from Injured to Healthy. If they are Broken (ST-03), you simply say "can't heal now" and save the 3 Action Points (AP).

**2. Self-Healing**
Trigger: You are Injured or Dying, and hold a Med-Kit.
Cost: 4 Action Points (AP).
Check: 2d10 + Focus (FOC) + First Aid (MED) vs Difficulty Class (DC) 14 + Fear Pressure (FP).
Result: At 12 Progress Points (PP), self-recover one Health State (HS) tier.
Failure Handling: Progress −3 and this round's Action Points (AP) zeroed; the Med-Kit is consumed once.
**【Solo Trial】**: At character creation you get a free Med-Kit (3 uses), and an Echo Companion can heal you (flat +2 modifier, Progress Points (PP) not halved) — but they cannot heal themselves, nor each other.

**3. Rescuing the Hooked**
Trigger: A Survivor (or Echo Companion) is hooked on a Hook, and you reach their Contact (CON) Distance Band.
Cost: 3 Action Points (AP).
Check: One Focus (FOC) check vs Difficulty Class (DC) 9 + Fear Pressure (FP).
Result: Rescued, and the rescued gains 1 round of "Silence" (the Avatar cannot make a Track check against them this round).
Failure Handling: Hook stage rounds −1; and you are scratched by the Hook, dropping one Health State (HS) tier.

> **Example**: You rush to the Hook, spend 3 Action Points (AP) on a Focus (FOC) check at Difficulty Class (DC) 9, roll 11 and rescue your teammate — they gain 1 round of Silence, and the Avatar cannot Track them this round. If you roll 6, the hook stage rounds −1, and you are scratched, dropping one Health State (HS) tier.
**【Solo Trial】**: The Echo Companion will, starting the "next round" after you are hooked, attempt to rescue you per the fixed behavior table — that round's gap is your window to decide whether to save yourself.

**4. Hook Stage and Struggle**
Trigger: A Survivor is hooked on a Hook, entering three stages (each stage's rounds see 7-3).
Cost: Auto-timed each round; Solitary Break costs 1 Action Point (AP) per round to attempt; Struggle while carried costs no Action Points (AP).
Check: Solitary Break = 2d10 + Will (WIL) + Endurance (END) vs Hook self-rescue Difficulty Class (DC) 17/18/19/20 (by N); Struggle = contested check 2d10 + Agility (AGI) + Escape (ESQ) vs the Killer's grab modifier, accumulating 3 successes to land.
Result: Successful self-rescue or struggle means unhooking and landing (returning to free Dying); successful rescue see the entry above.
Failure Handling: Failed self-rescue −2 stage rounds; if the three-stage rounds run out with no rescue → taken away (reaped). The Killer may carry at most 4 rounds before must drop.

**5. Breaking Hooks and Kicking Generators**
Trigger: You want to temporarily disable a Hook, or the Killer wants to interrupt Generator progress.
Cost (break hook): 1 Action Point (AP), requires a Toolbox. / Cost (kick generator, Killer): 2 Action Points (AP).
Check (break hook): 2d10 + Technique (TEC) + Mechanics (MEC) vs Difficulty Class (DC) 14.
Result (break hook): The Hook is disabled for 2 rounds; no one can be hooked on it during that time. / Result (kick generator): That Generator −2 Progress Points (PP) and enters the Regressing state.
Failure Handling: Failed hook-break consumes the Toolbox with no disable; kicking a generator is a Killer action that always hits, so no failure handling.
**⚠ Note**: The Generator is the Survivors' build objective, not destroyed by Survivors; "breaking the generator" here means the Killer's "kick generator" interruption.

**6. Endgame Collapse and Escape**
Trigger: All Exit Gates are powered, or the Killer force-closes the Hatch.
Cost: None (auto-resolved each round).
Check: Escape conditions — ① Exit Gate: accumulate 3 + INT(N÷2) segments opened; ② Hatch: completed Generators ≥ MAX(1, target count −1) AND (all Echo Companions out OR surviving Survivors ≤ INT(N/2)).
Result: Those meeting the condition escape successfully, returning to the Campfire.
Failure Handling: If the endgame collapse rounds (12/10/8/6 by N) run out and no one has escaped, all are reaped. Pacing see G1: the endgame must be fast, chaotic, and have someone decide in the final round whether to turn back.

> **Example**: In a solo game (N=1) endgame, the 2 Echo Companions are out and the Exit Gate is powered; you advance per 7-3's segment count (3 segments). On round 10 the player accumulates the 3 segments; you say "the gate's open" — they escape, returning to the Campfire. If round 12 (collapse rounds) runs out unfinished, all are reaped.

**7. Chase Contest**
Trigger: The Killer and a Survivor enter the same Distance Band (DB) and the Killer initiates a Chase (including the Terror Radius (TR) heartbeat cue).
Cost: Does not use Action Points (AP); each round automatically runs one contested check, each side spending its movement and reaction for that round.
Check: Killer 2d10 + Technique (TEC) + Track vs Survivor 2d10 + Agility (AGI), with modifiers by the current Distance Band (DB) (Contact (CON) favors the Killer, Long (LONG) favors the Survivor).
Result: If the Survivor succeeds consecutively to open to the Long (LONG) band, they break the Chase; if the Killer succeeds consecutively to press to the Contact (CON) band, they may attack or hook.
Failure Handling: A failed Survivor is forced into the Contact (CON) band and cannot use Pallets/Windows to open distance this round; a failed Killer ends the Chase and resets the Terror Radius (TR).
**【Solo Trial】**: In solo play no teammate shares the Chase — the Echo Companion won't draw the Killer off for you, but you get +1 modifier each round when breaking away, because the system counts the "interference teammates would have done" onto you.

**8. Using Pallets and Windows**
Trigger: A Survivor is chased by the Killer and passes a Pallet or Window.
Cost: 1 Action Point (AP) (dropping a Pallet or vaulting a Window, once each).
Check: No check, execute directly; dropping a Pallet auto-hits and Stuns the Killer for 1 round if the Killer is in the Contact (CON) band and actively chasing.
Result: A Killer pinned by a Pallet enters Stun, and the Survivor gets a window to open the Distance Band (DB); vaulting a Window doesn't break the Chase but resets both sides' proximity timer.
Failure Handling: A whiffed Pallet (Killer already opened up) breaks the Pallet, unusable again; vaulting a Window while the Killer closes in simultaneously means you're caught.
**【Solo Trial】**: In solo play you have only 4 Action Points (AP) per round, and dropping a Pallet costs 1 — the trade-off is that point could have gone to repairing. The Echo Companion won't drop Pallets for you, but when you're chased it automatically occupies a Distance Band (DB) to create chaos (no Action Point (AP) cost).

**9. Opening Chests and Picking Up Items**
Trigger: A Survivor reaches a Chest or a dropped item on the field.
Cost: 2 Action Points (AP) (search a Chest once).
Check: No check, execute directly; the number of Chests is by N (2/3/4/5, see 7-3).
Result: Obtain one random item or Add-on; field drops are picked up directly with no Action Points (AP).
Failure Handling: An empty Chest yields nothing but still costs the Action Points (AP); solo play has fewer Chests, so have the Echo Companion mark locations first, then you open them.

**Action Point (AP) Authority Values**: Survivors get 4 per round (dropping to 3 while in a Chase, not retainable to the next round); Echo Companions get 1 per round (operated by the GM per the fixed behavior table); the Killer gets 4 per round, plus a separate Pressure Point (PRE) resource pool (not using Action Points (AP)).

> **Example**: In a solo game (N=1) you operate 2 Echo Companions, each with 1 Action Point (AP) per round — use them to repair (total modifier +2, Progress Point (PP) contribution halved) or help you grab items; don't leave them idle. The Survivor self gets 4 per round; once locked into a Chase by the Killer, that round drops to 3, and that 1-point gap is the price of not doing a second big thing this round.

**⚠ Note**: The INT(Prestige (PRS) ÷ 4) in the Pressure Point (PRE) cap formula is truncated — at Prestige (PRS) 16, INT(4) = 4, cap 4 + 2N + 4; this cell is why the Killer grows fiercer later, so truncate correctly.

**⚠ Common Misconception**: The 2 Echo Companions in a solo game (N=1) are not decoration — the Effective Repair Force (ERF) is pulled back from 0 to 1.0 entirely by them. The 7-round Hook stage (HSD) and DC 17 self-rescue shift the rescue window borne by teammates in four-player into one borne by the system and Echo Companions; it is not "difficulty reduction."

**Settlement Example (N=1)**: A solo game with one Survivor + 2 Echo Companions. The self gets 4 Action Points (AP) per round (3 in Chase), each Echo Companion 1; Generator target 2, total 4; Hook stage 7 rounds; self-rescue DC 17; endgame collapse 12 rounds; Exit Gate opens 3 segments; Chests 2, Pallets 8, Windows 5, Hooks 8. Bloodpoint (BP) settlement multiplier ×1.75, per-Trial cap 8,000 + 1,600 × Prestige (PRS). — Open with this table; no recomputation needed.

## 7-4　Common Misjudgments and Ruling Notes

### Ruling Six (Unified Status Naming)

All Status Effects across the library are unified to the nine names below. Old names have been updated library-wide: old "cannot be sensed" → Undetectable; old "deep wound" → Deep Wound.

| Unified Name | Code | Old Name (retired) |
|---|---|---|
| Exhausted | ST-05 | — |
| Deep Wound | ST-04 | deep wound |
| Undetectable | ST-12 | cannot be sensed |
| Broken | ST-03 | — |
| Endurance | ST-16 | — |
| Haemorrhage | ST-07 | — |
| Hindered | ST-08 | — |
| Mangled | ST-10 | — |
| Blindness | ST-01 | — |

**Usage Note**: Before healing, confirm whether the target is Broken (ST-03) — during Broken, healing is entirely impossible; Deep Wound (ST-04) hitting zero on its countdown goes straight to Dying; Undetectable (ST-12) makes the Terror Radius (TR) count as 0. These three are the statuses the GM should watch most each round.

### Ruling Twenty-One (Naming Conflicts)

When an abbreviation collides with an attribute / skill / resource name, the priority order is as follows (implemented library-wide):

- **Skill Point (SKP) priority**: Skill Point occupies SKP; nothing else may seize it.
- **Attribute Point (ATP) priority**: Attribute Point occupies ATP (the old name KAP is merged in); nothing else may seize it.
- **Guardian (RSC)**: The old spec of "RES yields" is changed to the Guardian-exclusive abbreviation Guardian (RSC); RES is reserved for other resources and no longer shared with Guardian (RSC).

**Usage Note**: If a player sheet shows all three columns SKP / ATP / Guardian (RSC), they map to Skill Point, Attribute Point, and Guardian respectively — the three do not overlap; fill each column as-is, do not merge into one.

> **Example**: A player sheet's "Guardian (RSC)" column filled with 3 means they hold 3 Guardian resource points; this is independent of Skill Point (SKP) and Attribute Point (ATP) as three separate columns — do not merge them into one column just because they're all called "points."

### Ruling Seven (Distance Band (DB) Unified)

All Distance Bands (DB) in the book are unified to four bands; do not mix in the old "near/mid/far" terms: Contact (CON) = within reach; Short (SHORT) = within one room; Medium (MED) = within line of sight across rooms; Long (LONG) = out of sight or the far end of the scene. Chase contests and Area effects are always judged by these four bands.

### Ruling Twelve (Chase Start and End)

A Chase starts when "the Killer enters the Survivor's Distance Band (DB) and initiates pursuit," and ends when "the Survivor has not been pressed into the Contact (CON) band for two consecutive rounds." The Terror Radius (TR) heartbeat is a cue, not a judgment — a heartbeat sounding does not mean a Chase has started; only entering the Distance Band (DB) counts.

> **Example**: A solo player is chased by the Wraith (WRT) in the Long (LONG) band; you run the contest: player 2d10 + Agility (AGI) vs Killer 2d10 + Technique (TEC) + Track, with the Long (LONG) band giving the player +2. Two consecutive successes and you say "the heartbeat's far," the Chase ends, and the Terror Radius (TR) resets.

### Ruling Three (Status Effect Code Unified)

All thirty Status Effects are referenced by ST-xx numbers, with the Chinese full name (abbreviation) given in the body. For example Deep Wound (ST-04), Undetectable (ST-12). Do not write only "ST-04" in the body without the Chinese name — players look up the Chinese name on their sheets.

### Ruling Nine (Hope Point (HOP) and Bloodpoint (BP) Not Mixed)

Hope Points (HOP) are used only to rewrite dice and buy information; Bloodpoints (BP) are used only for settlement upgrades; the two resource pools are fully independent. Do not convert Hope Points (HOP) into Bloodpoints (BP) at settlement, nor spend Bloodpoints (BP) to rewrite dice during checks — their respective entry points are written in different chapters (Hope Point (HOP) in G0, Bloodpoint (BP) in 6-4).

### Ruling Five (Health State (HS) Three Tiers Unified)

All Health States (HS) in the book are unified to three tiers: Healthy / Injured / Dying. Do not mix in old terms like "bleeding" or "half-health"; healing and hooking both advance by these three tiers (see 7-2 Healing Others, Hook Stage flow).

### Ruling Fifteen (Stun and Hindered Not Mixed)

Stun is the 1-round forced state after a Pallet hits the Killer; Hindered (ST-08) is a slow state — the two are not from the same source; do not apply both in the same event, nor use Hindered as if it were Stun.

### Ruling Ten (Bloodpoint (BP) Settlement Formula)

Per-Trial Bloodpoints (BP) = base reward × settlement party multiplier (see 7-3, ×1.75/×1.5/×1.25/×1.0), and must not exceed the per-Trial cap 8,000 + 1,600 × Prestige (PRS). The multiplier exists so solo play (Config 1) keeps pace with a four-player squad's progression, not as extra reward.

## 7-5　Entity Hunger (EH) Tiered-Effect Table

Entity Hunger (EH) is a public track from 0–10 (see G1 screen setup, P4 World and Hope). This table spells out concretely the "effect" referenced by the rules' "what happens when you reach a certain tier" and "trigger EH effect one tier early" (G1 pacing control L324/L419), so you have a table to consult at the table and pressure stays consistent across sessions.

**How to read**: Apply the row's effect for whichever threshold (inclusive) EH falls into; when multiple thresholds are met at once, apply only the highest. "Trigger EH effect one tier early" = treat the current EH as one threshold higher (e.g., at EH 5 if you choose "one tier early," apply the EH 7 critical effect).

| Threshold (EH) | State Name | Concrete Effect (GM executes) |
|---|---|---|
| 0–2 | Calm | Standard rules apply, no extra effect. |
| 3 | Whisper | Round-end performance: you may describe an "Entity whisper" heartbeat cue to one Survivor (no numerical effect); the Killer's Track check has no modifier. |
| 5 | Gaze | The Killer automatically gains one "clue pulse" per round — +1 to the Track check against the farthest Survivor (no Pressure Point (PRE) spent); the Exit Gate powering-up performance may be felt one round early. |
| 7 | Critical (rule trigger point) | You **should** escalate pressure (see G1 pacing control): the Killer's Track check +2, and may freely deploy one Realm Creation (RC) not yet on the field this Trial. This tier corresponds to the rule's forced-escalation condition of "Entity Hunger (EH) ≥ 7 yet no effect triggered for two consecutive rounds." |
| 10 | Reaping | Something "very bad" happens: forced sacrifice — choose one currently free (unhooked, non-Dying) Survivor (or Echo Companion), hook them directly and enter the Sacrifice (third) stage; that stage's rounds follow N (7/6/5/4), and if no one rescues by round end they are reaped out. |

> **Example**: In a solo game (N=1), EH climbs to 5 and you haven't escalated — at this point "trigger EH effect one tier early" means directly applying EH 7 Critical: the Killer's Track +2, and a free Realm Creation (RC) from the pool enters the field. The player will clearly feel "the Entity is angry."

> **⚠ Design Principle**: EH is a pressure gauge, not a punishment gauge. Low thresholds (3/5) add only performance and minor modifiers, avoiding turning an ordinary Trial into a guaranteed loss; only at 10 does it truly take someone, and it takes the one who "lags behind," encouraging players to keep moving and helping each other (altruistic acts already drop EH −1).

## 7-6　GM Running Checklist

Three tables are used at three time points: opening, each round, and endgame. The opening table is reviewed when players sit down; the per-round table is your three-second recap before each round ends; the endgame table is reviewed when the collapse hourglass comes out. Don't wait for trouble to flip to them — the three tables are prevention, not remedy.

### Opening Checklist

- [ ] Realm selected, three-line opening prepared (read as-is, pause three seconds)
- [ ] Killer and Avatar Menace Value (AVM) confirmed; Challenge Rating (CR) = Prestige (PRS) + Avatar Menace Value (AVM)
- [ ] Scaling table set by N: Echo Companion count, Generator target count, Hook stage rounds, endgame collapse rounds
- [ ] Solo play (Config 1): 2 Echo Companions' behavior table in place, Med-Kit distributed
- [ ] Hope Point (HOP) mandatory grant nodes marked (see G0 Introduction)

### Per-Round Checklist

- [ ] Pressure Point (PRE) recovery counted (2/3/3/4 by N)
- [ ] Hook stage timing advanced (each stage 7/6/5/4 rounds)
- [ ] Echo Companion behavior table advanced (total repair modifier +2, Progress Point (PP) contribution halved)
- [ ] Chase contest auto-check done (no Action Point (AP) cost)
- [ ] Terror Radius (TR) and heartbeat tokens distributed

### Endgame Checklist

- [ ] Collapse-round hourglass visible (12/10/8/6 by N), placed where players can see it
- [ ] Escape condition confirmed: Exit Gate segments (3+INT(N/2)) or Hatch threshold MAX(1, target count−1)
- [ ] Bloodpoint (BP) settlement cap = 8,000 + 1,600 × Prestige (PRS) (per-Trial reward does not exceed this)
- [ ] Ember Moment text prepared (each scenario from S1; certain to trigger in S8)

**⚠ Common Misconception**: The "Bloodpoint (BP) settlement cap" is a per-Trial reward ceiling, not a limit on how many the player can have total — the cross-scenario accumulated total is decided by the Prestige (PRS) formula (see Ch.2 "Character Creation"); the cap only prevents a single Trial from spiking.

## 7-7　Random Encounter and Sudden-Event Tables

This is the "random tables" appendix promised in the contents. The GM need not follow the tables, but when a scene needs some improvisation, or players deviate from the planned route, these two tables let you deliver a weighty encounter within three seconds without improvising on the spot.

### 1. Trial Sudden Events (d10)

Whenever a round ends with no "Generator progress / Entity Hunger (EH) advance / Echo Companion interaction" happening that round, or when you want to add a variable, roll 1d10:

| 1d10 | Sudden Event | GM Execution Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 1–2 | Lights Out | In the chosen area, line of sight drops for 1 round (Distance Band (DB) checks −1 against Survivors); no rules beyond numbers are affected. |
| 3–4 | Misplaced Step | The Killer gains one "free movement performance" (no Action Point (AP) cost) to approach the farthest Survivor; performance only, no attack initiated. |
| 5–6 | Something Dropped | A random item or Add-on drops somewhere on the field; mark it, then a Survivor obtains it by opening a Chest or picking it up. |
| 7–8 | Terrain Shift | Pallet or Window count +1 (capped by N at 6+2N / 4+N), and mark it on the map immediately. |
| 9–10 | Entity Murmur | Entity Hunger (EH) forced +1 (advance one notch); if this triggers a threshold, apply the effect per 7-5. |

### 2. Wild Realm Creations (d8, only when the scenario allows deploying RC)

When you follow the G1 escalation order to "deploy a Realm Creation (RC) not yet on the field this Trial," or want to improvise an extra patrol unit, roll 1d8 to choose (one-line effect; full data in the *Bestiary* RC-01 to RC-12):

| 1d8 | Creation | One-Line Effect |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Rust Crow (RC-01) | Periodically marks one Survivor's location in the area (clue pulse). |
| 2 | Low Whisperer (RC-02) | Periodically inflicts Exhausted (ST-05) on one Survivor for 1 round. |
| 3 | Shattered Mirror (RC-03) | Disables a designated Pallet for 1 round. |
| 4 | Shadow Binder (RC-04) | Disables a designated Window for 1 round. |
| 5 | Hunger Hound Pack (RC-05) | Periodically inflicts Hindered (ST-08) on one Survivor for 1 round. |
| 6 | Bone-Eroding Fog (RC-06) | Area line of sight drops (same as Lights Out). |
| 7 | Parasite Vine (RC-07) | Periodically inflicts Mangled (ST-10) on one Survivor for 1 round. |
| 8 | Gravekeeper (RC-08) | Patrols near Hooks; periodically inflicts Broken (ST-03) on those who approach, for 1 round. |

> **⚠ Usage Principle**: These two tables are "contingency," not the "main course." The core tension of a default Trial comes from the Killer and Generator loop (see G1 pacing control); use the random tables only when the scene goes cold or you want to reward exploration, then retract them — don't let players feel they're grinding mobs.
