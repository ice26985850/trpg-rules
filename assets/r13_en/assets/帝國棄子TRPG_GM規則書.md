# Chapter One: Setting

## 1.1 The Vergessen Sector

The Vergessen Sector lies at the farthest outer edge of the Imperium's frontier. The core thesis: **The Imperium forgot it, but the Inquisition remembers.**

- **Capital:** Fortress-World **"Carcassonne"**
- **Population:** ~7 billion (7 major worlds)
- **Warp Routes:** Only a single unstable Mandeville Point; voyages take 3–6 months
- **Star:** "Rust-Sun" — a faintly red, aging star

> **Sector Proverb:** "By the time the light of the Emperor reaches here, it is already very weak."

### Historical Timeline

| Time | Event |
|------|------|
| M30.812 | The 947th Fleet of the Great Crusade arrives; three habitable worlds submit |
| M31.004 | The Horus Heresy breaks out; Warp storms sever contact for 800 years |
| M31.450 | "Crimson Reckoning" — an Alpha-level Psyker establishes theocratic rule over Tarr-Morag |
| M31.811 | Imperial fleet returns; the Inquisition arrives with the fleet. 3 million executed |
| M32.015 | The Inquisition establishes a permanent stronghold, the "Black Fortress," on Carcassonne |

| M35.100 | Adeptus Mechanicus output permanently drops to 40% |
| M36.500 | "Blight Rebellion" — the Sector Governor's secession movement is crushed; the Inquisition takes direct rule |
| M38.200 | Vergessen formally becomes an "Inquisitorial Mandate" |
| M41.200 | "Year of the Ravens" — Warp routes close three times, totaling 7 years of isolation |
| M41.800 | **Present day.** The Sector sits at the thinnest edge of Imperial control |

# Chapter Two: GM Operating Principles

## 2.1 The Seven GM Iron Laws

1. **The Emperor is not the answer — He is the question.** Do not tell players whether the Emperor exists or favors them. Let this question hang forever above the characters' heads.
2. **Victory is temporary; the cost is permanent.** Every success must carry a price — resource expenditure, growing Corruption, broken relationships.
3. **The environment is the strongest enemy.** The Hive City itself kills. Hunger, darkness, poison gas, collapse — more lethal than Chaos Cultists.
4. **Choice is not the answer.** Do not give players "right" vs. "wrong" choices, but "bad" vs. "worse."
5. **Hope exists — but must be struggled for.** Grant a sliver of light in the darkest moments. Enough to let characters move forward, but not enough to illuminate the whole world.
6. **Fear is real — but not cheap.** Do not roll Fear tests every round. Reserve them for truly important moments.
7. **Trust is the scarcest resource.** Temptation Notes keep trust never whole. Oath conflicts make trust complicated.

## 2.2 The 40k-Style "Yes, and..."

> **"Yes, and... it makes things more complicated."**

When players propose a creative solution, do not refuse outright. Say "yes" — then add a 40k turn: "but Imperial bureaucracy won't approve this," "but that thing may already be contaminated," "but your Inquisitor won't like this."

## 2.3 Narrative Pacing: The Breathing Cycle

```
Tension (combat / stealth / threat)
    │
    ▼
Release (brief safety / barracks time / companion interaction)
    │
    ▼
Deeper Despair (fewer resources / deeper Corruption / new truths)
    │
    ▼
Endgame (extraction / sacrifice / choice)
```

- One game session = 2–3 cycles
- One full mission = 3–5 cycles

---

## 2.4 Mortal Mode GM Notes

- **Keep Temptation Notes secret.** Never discuss note contents in the open. Pass them via private notes or messages.
- **Do not rush betrayal.** Let players decide when to act. Pressure comes from the environment, not from you.
- **Arrange "revelation moments."** In mid-to-late missions, let the effect of a Temptation Note surface — but do not name names.
- **Resources are the real pressure.** Strictly track ammunition, Medikits, and Flares.

## 2.5 Astartes Mode GM Notes

- **Do not provide "the correct answer."** Oath conflicts have no optimal solution. Let players bear the consequences of their choices.
- **Make survivors meaningful.** Civilians are not merely "objects to be saved" — they have opinions, needs, and may become allies or burdens.
- **Control the pacing of the extraction endgame.** Do not reveal extraction possibilities too early. Let despair fully ferment.
- **Respect all choices.** Warriors who stay behind should receive a dignified ending.

## 2.7 Investigation and Intelligence-Gathering System

The core of an Inquisition mission is investigation — before combat, Penitents must find the target, see through lies, and piece together the truth.

### Clue Chain Design

Each mission should contain **3–5 key clues**, forming a chain from "known" to "truth." Clues fall into three types:

| Clue Type | How Obtained | Always Obtainable? | Example |
|---------|---------|-------------|------|
| **Core Clue** | No test needed; discovered upon reaching the correct location | ✅ Yes | Cultist tattoo on a corpse; Chaos sigil on a chapel altar |
| **Hidden Clue** | Requires a successful test to discover | ❌ No | A diary hidden in a false compartment; an encrypted data-slate |
| **Bonus Clue** | Requires creativity or a high-difficulty test | ❌ No | Information an NPC lets slip unwittingly; an overlooked detail |

> **Three-Clue Principle:** Every core conclusion should have at least three different paths that can lead to it — ensure players do not get stuck because of a single failed test.

### Investigation Tests

| Action | Skill | Difficulty | On Success |
|------|------|------|---------|
| Search crime scene | Investigation | Standard | 1 Core Clue + possible Hidden Clue |
| Interrogate captive/witness | Oratory (friendly) / Deceive (trickery) / Intimidation (fear) | Standard–Hard | 1 piece of information |
| Analyze documents/data | Lore (Imperial / Chaos) | Standard–Hard | Decrypt or comprehend contents |
| Track traces | Awareness | Standard | Learn the target's whereabouts |
| Identify Chaos item | Psyniscience or Lore (Chaos) | Hard | Confirm contamination level and danger |
| Crack access/terminal | Tech-Use | Standard–Hard | Enter restricted area or obtain data |
| Infiltrate in disguise | Deceive | Hard | Enter enemy area without being detected |

### Intelligence Quality (DoS Impact)

| Degree of Success (DoS) | Intelligence Quality |
|-----|---------|
| 0 | Basic info — know where to go next |
| 1 | Useful detail — enemy weakness or environmental hazard |
| 2 | Key intel — enemy's true motive or a hidden trap |
| 3+ | Full truth — GM should grant a key plot reveal |

### Interrogation Rules

- The captive's initial attitude sets the base difficulty: Cooperative (Easy), Neutral (Standard), Hostile (Hard), Fanatical (Extreme)
- Attitude can be shifted (+1 level) through threats, incentives, or presenting evidence
- At most 3 test attempts per interrogation. After 3, the captive provides no new information
- Using violence on a captive: Mortal Corruption +1 (unnecessary violence); Astartes gain no increase

### Time Pressure

Investigation is not unlimited in time. Each investigation action consumes **1 Time Unit (TU)**. The GM tracks secretly:

| Time Unit (TU) Spent | Consequence |
|---------|------|
| 3 Time Unit (TU) | Enemies begin noticing anomalies — some enemies enter alert status |
| 5 Time Unit (TU) | Enemies prepare to respond — add 1–2 extra enemies |
| 7 Time Unit (TU) | Enemies retreat / destroy evidence — lose 1 Core Clue |
| 10 Time Unit (TU) | Mission's objective time limit reached — forced entry into final phase |

|------|----------|------|

| Intensity | Effect | Example |
|------|------|------|

### Radiation

| Level | Effect | Test Frequency |
|------|------|---------|
| Low radiation | Corruption +1 per hour (simulating cellular damage) | Per hour |
| Medium radiation | Hit Points (HP) −1d4 + Corruption +1 per 30 minutes | Per 30 minutes |
| High radiation | Hit Points (HP) −1d8 + Corruption +2 per 10 minutes | Per 10 minutes |
| Lethal radiation | Hit Points (HP) −2d6 per minute, death in 10 minutes unprotected | Per minute |

### Vacuum / Drowning

| State | Mortal | Astartes |
|------|------|---------|
| Vacuum exposure | Body (BOD) (Hard)/round, failure → Hit Points (HP) −1d6 + Unconscious 1d3 rounds | Power Armor fully sealed can sustain 7 days (intact state) |
| Drowning | Can hold breath Body (BOD)×2 rounds. After that, Body (BOD) (Hard) per round, failure → Hit Points (HP) −2d6 | Multi-lung system can hold breath Body (BOD)×10 rounds |

### Electric Shock

| Intensity | Damage | Effect |
|------|------|------|
| Light (frayed cable) | 1d8 Energy | Body (BOD) (Standard) avoids Stunned 1 round |
| Medium (industrial power) | 2d8 Energy | Body (BOD) (Hard) avoids Stunned 1d3 rounds |
| Heavy (plasma leak) | 4d8 Energy | Body (BOD) (Extreme) avoids cardiac arrest (instant death) |

# Chapter Four: Oath Conflicts and the Extraction Endgame

## 4.1 Oath Conflict Design

### Conflict One: Protect the Innocent vs. Obey Orders

| Choice | Effect | Long-Term Consequence |
|------|------|---------|
| Protect civilians | Spend resources, gain intel | Spatial crisis at endgame |
| Execute Exterminatus | Rapid advance | Corruption +2~5, Crisis of Faith |
| Persuade civilians to leave | Faith test (Hard) | Compromise, but with risk |

### Conflict Two: Never Retreat vs. Protect Comrades

| Choice | Effect | Long-Term Consequence |
|------|------|---------|
| Hold and fight to the death | 50% total wipe | If survive: a legend is born |
| Tactical retreat | Carry wounded comrade to retreat | Crack in the oath, but comrade survives |
| Sacrifice as cover | Hold the rear alone | Extremely low survival chance |

### Conflict Three: Reject the Alien vs. Survival Above All

| Choice | Effect | Long-Term Consequence |
|------|------|---------|
| Refuse help | Seek other solutions | Time spent, more danger |
| Accept cooperation | Corruption +3~5, gain "Debt to the Eldar" | Future xenos contact |
| Deceive and backstab | Higher Corruption +5~8 | Lose credibility forever |

## 4.2 The Five-Stage Extraction Endgame

```
1. Dawn of hope: discover the possibility of extraction
2. Rally: the whole team rushes to the extraction point
3. The reckoning: "The ship can't hold everyone."
4. Stay behind: warriors who stay receive a "Final Mission"
5. Liftoff: the epilogue. The fate of those who stayed
```

### Final Mission Types

| Mission | Description |
|------|------|
| **Rear Guard** | Hold the extraction point against pursuing enemies |
| **Seal** | Permanently close a Warp rift or contamination source |
| **Guard** | Stay to protect civilians who cannot be extracted |
| **Atonement** | Die honorably after completing an unfulfilled oath |
| **Message Relay** | Stay by the comms device and keep transmitting intel |

## 5.5 Non-Combat Warp Phenomena Table (d20)

Use this table when a Psyker uses psychic powers in a non-combat context (telepathy, Psyniscience, divination, etc.), or when a character remains in a corrupted environment for a long time:

| d20 | Phenomenon | Effect |
|-----|------|------|
| 1–6 | **Calm** | Nothing happens. The Warp is briefly at peace |
| 7–8 | **Whisper** | Everyone hears faint murmurs — perhaps a name, a word, or a fragment of broken sentence. The GM may choose to give a cryptic hint, or merely set the mood |
| 9–10 | **Chill** | Temperature drops sharply. Body (BOD) test (Standard) to avoid 1 point of Fatigue (Mortal). Astartes: armor's climate system briefly fails — Tech-Use test (Easy) to reboot |
| 11–12 | **Afterimage** | Each character sees a different image — Mortals see dead kin, Astartes see fallen battle-brothers. Mind (MND) test (Standard) to avoid Corruption +1. On success, the image may contain useful information |
| 13–14 | **Time Ripple** | Perceived time and objective time fall out of sync. All characters Confused 1d3 rounds (Mortal) / 1 round (Astartes) |
| 15–16 | **Tempting Whisper** | A voice whispers to each character — offering seemingly sound advice, but carrying subtle Chaos contamination. If the character accepts the advice, Corruption +1 but gains an Advantage |
| 17 | **Lesser Manifestation** | A small Warp entity coalesces nearby (not hostile — at least not at first). It may answer a question (not necessarily truthfully) or propose a deal |
| 18 | **Window of Truth** | A brief glimpse of the Warp's true face. Corruption +1d6 (Mortal) / +1d3 (Astartes). But the GM must grant a fragment of truth — possibly the key to hidden plot |
| 19 | **Time Rift** | The character briefly sees fragments of past or future (GM gives a prophetic scene). Corruption +2. Any Psyker present gains an extra consumable premonition (may reroll the next important test once) |
| 20 | **Eye of the Emperor** | Golden light flashes. All present: Corruption −2, Faith +3. Gain one free "Hand of the Emperor" (valid within this session). The Psyker's Warp risk drops to minimum for 24 hours |

# Chapter Six: Mission Design Templates

```
Mission Name: ________
├── Stated Objective (the purpose the Inquisitor announces publicly)
├── True Objective (what the Inquisitor truly wants — may differ)
├── Target Area (Hive layer, environment)
├── Known Threats (mentioned in the briefing)
├── Hidden Threats (Warp-related GM secrets)

└── Key NPCs (at least 3: ally / enemy / gray area)
```

```
Scenario Name: ________
├── Planet Background (what it was before Exterminatus destroyed it)

├── Survivor Factions (civilians, remnant rebels, others)
├── Available Resources (facilities, ships in the ruins)
├── Extraction Possibility (comms location, starship status)
├── Oath Conflict Point (the moral dilemma that must arise)
├── Endgame Conditions (extraction / total wipe / choose darkness)
└── GM Secrets (at least 3 hidden settings)
```

# Appendix: GM Quick Reference

## 10 Steps for Mortal Mission Design

1. Choose an Inquisitor (decides mission style)
2. Define the Stated Objective and True Objective
3. Choose the Hive layer and environment
4. Design main encounters (2–3 fights + 1–2 non-combat challenges)
5. Place hidden threats (Warp phenomena / secrets)
6. Allocate resource ration (always one less than you think needed)
7. Distribute Temptation Notes
8. Set extraction conditions and failure consequences
9. Create 3 NPCs
10. Prepare a backup plan for "when everything goes wrong"

## 10 Steps for Astartes Scenario Design

1. Define the planet and cause of Exterminatus
2. Design the initial awakening scene
3. Place 3–5 explorable areas
4. Design survivor factions (at least 2, in conflict with each other)
5. Lay out lingering threats
6. Set oath conflict points (at least 2)
7. Hide extraction possibility (don't reveal too early)
8. Prepare endgame conditions (at least 3 endings)
9. Create 3 GM secrets
10. Prepare a dignified ending for the warriors who stay behind
