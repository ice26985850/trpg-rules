# Exiles of the Empire TRPG

**In the corners the Emperor forgot, how much of your faith remains?**

**Reference Work Disclaimer:** The worldview, factions, and core atmosphere of these rules were inspired by *Warhammer 40,000* — drawing on its "grim dark future," "Emperor Worship," "Space Marine / Astartes," and "Chaos corruption" elements. This is an original, standalone TRPG design, not an officially licensed adaptation; all mechanics have been rebuilt from the ground up.

**What kind of world is this?**
The Imperium has abandoned you. Beneath the rust-red starlight of the Vergessen Sector, the Inquisition drafts criminals, heretics, and failures into Penitent Expeditions, assigning them near-suicidal missions. And the Astartes — the Space Marines forgotten after Exterminatus — alone face the ultimate torment on scorched wastelands: whether they should obey the order to exterminate. There are no heroic epics here, only the forgotten clinging to the last shred of dignity in despair.

**Who do you play, and what do you do?**
You are one of two kinds of "exile": a **Mortal Penitent** — carrying a crime and limited supplies, infiltrating, fighting, and struggling to survive in the darkness of the Hive City's lower depths; or an **Abandoned Astartes** — a transhuman soldier in battered Power Armor, searching the ruined planet for hope of evacuation. The core gameplay is survival, exploration, and moral choice: every action spends bullets, medkits, and — most importantly — your Faith.

**System Highlights**
1. **Faith & Corruption dual track:** Faith is a consumable, recoverable resource; Corruption silently accumulates upon contact with Chaos. As the two pull against each other, when their sum breaks the threshold you face the **Crisis of Faith** — a gamble over your soul.
2. **One ruleset, two scales:** Mortals and Astartes share the Five Attributes but have utterly different numerical baselines. A mortal facing a Space Marine is all but certain to lose — yet before the Emperor souls are equal; the contest of Faith and Mind ignores physical gaps.
3. **Tension of temptation and oaths:** In multiplayer mode, the GM privately hands you a **Temptation Note** — you may be asked to betray a companion, or may have received nothing at all, and you never know what others got. In solo mode it becomes the **Whisper of Chaos**, quietly knocking at your most vulnerable moments; an NPC companion's loyalty and wavering replace inter-player betrayal.

**Who is this for?**
If you enjoy scrimping and scheming when resources run dry, savor the weight of moral dilemmas, and crave stories of despair yet unyielding hope — this is made for you. It asks for some TRPG experience, as the mechanics are fairly rich; but the core die is just one: d100 Roll-Under, easier to pick up than you'd think.
