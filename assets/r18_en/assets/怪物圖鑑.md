# Monster Codex (Ghost Collector TRPG)

> This codex catalogues all spirits, zombies, and other entities a Ghost Collector may encounter, sixteen types in total.
> Statistics follow Section Seven of the Combat Design Draft (the combat-designer data block) and are internally consistent with the Core Design Baseline.
> Hard Format Rule: all game terms use the "Full Name (Abbreviation)" format, and every occurrence in the body text uses the full name.

---

## How to Use This Codex

Each entry is organized by the following fields for quick reference by the Game Master (GM) and players:

| Field | Description |
|---|---|
| Challenge Rating (CR) | The difficulty tier of the entity, used for encounter balance (see Balance Budget). |
| Health Points (HP) | Drops to zero means dispersal (temporary dispel); permanent resolution requires Deliverance / Subjugation / Sealing. |
| Armor Class (AC) | An attack must reach this value to count as a hit. |
| Initiative (INIT) | The order of action at the start of combat. |
| Attack | Hit bonus and damage dice, including appended status effects. |
| Special Ability | Passive or active powers other than attacks. |
| Yin Emission Value (YIN) | The intensity and range of Yin Energy the entity emits; it interferes with rites and raises environmental danger. |
| Weakness | The most effective means of resolution, or key information. |
| Correct Handling | How a Ghost Collector should wrap up and close the case. |
| Counter Tactics | The entity's behavioral patterns and how to respond. |
| Background | An atmospheric description drawing on Hong Kong folk legends. |

# Chapter One: Ghosts

> Ghosts are condensed from obsession, grievances, or unfinished business; they have no physical body. Physical attacks (unarmed) are ineffective against them; they can only be dissolved through spirit tools, talismans, or a Deliverance Rite.

## ① Bound Spirit

- **Challenge Rating (CR)**: 1
- **Health Points (HP)**: 14
- **Armor Class (AC)**: 8
- **Initiative (INIT)**: d20+0
- **Attack**: Cold Touch (hit bonus +2, damage 1d4 + Slowed 1 round)
- **Special Ability**: Fixed at the place of death, repeats behavior; appearances follow a pattern, and its route and timing can be predicted with Knowledge (KNW).
- **Yin Emission Value (YIN)**: 2 (radius 10 meters)
- **Weakness**: Deliverance (fulfilling its unfinished wish), Soul-Calming Talisman for communication
- **Correct Handling**: Deliverance Rite (send it to the afterlife)
- **Counter Tactics**: Passive, does not attack proactively; if players miss its appearance window, they leave empty-handed that night and must wait patiently on watch.

**Background**
Old buildings are demolished but not cleared; the dead with unfinished matters linger—in the stairwells of the Tong Lau where they once lit lamps in life, in the subdivided flats gutted by fire, or at Shui Mei before the land reclamation, looking back every three steps. Most Bound Spirits are not malicious ghosts; their days simply froze at the moment of death—sweeping the floor, feeding someone, waiting by the door, over and over. What a Ghost Collector must do is not fight, but listen. Discern who they wait for, what they owe, and a stick of incense, a stack of Hell Bank Notes, a word of "I'll tell them for you" is often more effective than a Peachwood Sword.

## ② Wandering Soul

- **Challenge Rating (CR)**: 1
- **Health Points (HP)**: 10
- **Armor Class (AC)**: 8
- **Initiative (INIT)**: d20+1
- **Attack**: Mischievous Shove (hit bonus +1, damage 1d3); no killing intent
- **Special Ability**: Wanders about; can be tracked to its origin with a Pathfinding Talisman
- **Yin Emission Value (YIN)**: 1
- **Weakness**: Settlement (set up a Spirit Tablet / burn Hell Bank Notes), Soul-Calming Talisman
- **Correct Handling**: Settlement (give it a place to return to; no need to force deliverance)
- **Counter Tactics**: Flees when startled; in a chase it may use fleeing Movement (MOVE) of 6 meters; best to trace its origin in reverse with a Pathfinding Talisman.

**Background**
Homeless wandering souls, mostly laborers and new immigrants who died far from home with no one to worship them. They are like drunkards in the depths of Mong Kok night—drifting into a store to steal food, lifting a girl's skirt, ringing the convenience-store bell until dawn. Not wicked, just lonely. A Spirit Tablet bearing a name, a few Hell Bank Notes burned at the roadside, and it settles down—which is why the street corners of Hong Kong always bear scattered ingot ashes.

## ③ Resentful Ghost

- **Challenge Rating (CR)**: 3
- **Health Points (HP)**: 26
- **Armor Class (AC)**: 10
- **Initiative (INIT)**: d20+1
- **Attack**: Resentment Shock (hit bonus +3, damage 1d6 + Fear 1 round)
- **Special Ability**: Against a specific target of hatred, gains an additional +2 attack bonus
- **Yin Emission Value (YIN)**: 4 (radius 15 meters)
- **Weakness**: Find the root of its resentment (successful negotiation), Exorcism Talisman
- **Correct Handling**: Talk first (negotiate); if that fails, consider subjugation or dispersal
- **Counter Tactics**: Locks onto its hated target for continuous revenge, ignoring others; prioritize Communication or investigation to reveal its cause of death and sever its obsession.

**Background**
Real-estate hegemons forcibly demolishing homes, swindlers stealing life savings, heartless lovers absconding with the money—Hong Kong's resentments are never short of material. A Resentful Ghost does not lash out at random; it remembers every old debt, singling out the faithless to sit on their chest at midnight, showing its bloated face in the mirror. A Ghost Collector who only knows how to scatter talismans will be haunted for life; only by finding out who owes it, and whether that debt can be repaid, can the resentment find release.

---

## ④ Fierce Ghost

- **Challenge Rating (CR)**: 5
- **Health Points (HP)**: 45
- **Armor Class (AC)**: 12
- **Initiative (INIT)**: d20+2
- **Attack**: Soul Devour (hit bonus +4, damage 2d6 +2); Possession Charge (on successful contact, possesses)
- **Special Ability**: Indiscriminate attack, immune to Fear feedback, Negotiation Will (WILL) = 0 (cannot negotiate)
- **Yin Emission Value (YIN)**: 7 (radius 20 meters)
- **Weakness**: Soul-Gathering Gourd, Demon-Sealing Array, Five-Thunder Palm to break its protection
- **Correct Handling**: Subdue or destroy (cannot negotiate)
- **Counter Tactics**: Prioritize the target with the lowest Health Points (HP) or the most isolated; teammates must cover each other and let no one be left alone.

**Background**
When a wrong reaches its extreme, and one dies a violent death with no one to collect the body, the resentment boils into a Fierce Ghost. It is no longer that person, only a mass of hatred that wants to drag everyone down with it. The vulnerable who vanished on the night the Kowloon Walled City was demolished, the dead of industrial accidents silenced by cover-ups—these are most prone to become such things. A Fierce Ghost has nothing to say—reason with it and it only smiles and slips into your body. In such moments, the Peachwood Sword must be swift and the Five-Thunder Palm merciless.

---

## ⑤ Infant Spirit

- **Challenge Rating (CR)**: 2
- **Health Points (HP)**: 16
- **Armor Class (AC)**: 9
- **Initiative (INIT)**: d20+2
- **Attack**: Drain Yang (hit bonus +2, damage 1d4 + Weakness 1 round)
- **Special Ability**: Attaches to host (hides inside the body; physical attacks easily wound the host by mistake)
- **Yin Emission Value (YIN)**: 3
- **Weakness**: Specialized Deliverance Rite (Difficulty Class (DC) 18), drive it out with Soul-Calming Talisman
- **Correct Handling**: Deliverance (threshold is Challenge Rating (CR) × 3, harder to send off than usual)
- **Counter Tactics**: Hides beside the mother/family to fight a war of attrition; must first drive it out with a Soul-Calming Talisman, then perform Deliverance—never force an attack that might wound the living by mistake.

**Background**
Behind Hong Kong's theaters and private clinics, there are always secrets no incense is burned for. An Infant Spirit is not evil; it is a child sent away before it could be loved. It clings to the birth mother, drawing a little Yang warmth, and the mother grows thin day by day, muttering in her sleep each night. A Ghost Collector's hardest cases are often here—you may be willing to drive it out, but not necessarily to send it off. A soul-calming Deliverance Rite frees both it and the mother.

# Chapter Two: Zombies

> Zombies retain their corpses, driven by corpse poison and Yin Energy. Unlike ghosts, physical attacks (unarmed / weapons) are effective against them, but higher-tier zombies are protected by "Hard-Skin Mitigation" and require spirit tools or talismans to break through.

## ⑥ Fresh Corpse

- **Challenge Rating (CR)**: 1
- **Health Points (HP)**: 12
- **Armor Class (AC)**: 9
- **Initiative (INIT)**: d20−1
- **Attack**: Claw Strike (hit bonus +2, damage 1d6)
- **Special Ability**: Fear of light (attack bonus −2 under light source); Hard-Skin Mitigation 0
- **Yin Emission Value (YIN)**: 1
- **Weakness**: Physical restraint + Corpse-Still Talisman (Paralysis)
- **Correct Handling**: Restrain then still the corpse (apply Corpse-Still Talisman to fix it)
- **Counter Tactics**: Clumsy direct charge, no dodging; shining a Flashlight on its face suppresses its offense; a Warrior pinning it up close is the most stable approach.

**Background**
A power failure in the mortuary, a dozing night shift at the funeral home—the just-dead body sits up on its own. A Fresh Corpse is the "freshest", still bearing the bewilderment of life, lunging straight at any warm living thing. It fears light—shine a Flashlight and its movements lag a beat. Such cases are mostly farces where the family refuses to accept "he's still here"; the Ghost Collector arrives, pins it, stills it, sends it off—done.

---

## ⑦ White Zombie

- **Challenge Rating (CR)**: 2
- **Health Points (HP)**: 22
- **Armor Class (AC)**: 11
- **Initiative (INIT)**: d20+0
- **Attack**: Heavy Strike (hit bonus +3, damage 1d8); Hard-Skin Mitigation −2
- **Special Ability**: Hopping movement (Movement (MOVE) 9 meters); white-hair protection
- **Yin Emission Value (YIN)**: 3
- **Weakness**: Exorcism Talisman burns, Corpse-Still Talisman paralyzes, Glutinous Rice
- **Correct Handling**: Combine talismans + physical force
- **Counter Tactics**: Leaps in to force close combat; scatter Glutinous Rice on the ground to limit its hopping paths, then finish with a Corpse-Still Talisman for Paralysis.

**Background**
Hillside village houses, old New Territories ancestral halls—the White Zombie is the entry-level model of zombie lore. Its body sprouts white hair, hopping after lights and human voices. The elders say "a corpse that changes first turns white, then black"; the White Zombie is still early-stage, and a handful of Glutinous Rice sprinkled at the threshold can hold it. Ghost Collectors love these "salvageable" cases—still it, open the altar, deliver it, and by the way share a cup of herbal tea with the village uncles.

---

## ⑧ Black Zombie

- **Challenge Rating (CR)**: 4
- **Health Points (HP)**: 36
- **Armor Class (AC)**: 12
- **Initiative (INIT)**: d20+1
- **Attack**: Tear (hit bonus +4, damage 1d10); Hard-Skin Mitigation −3 (only effective with a spirit tool)
- **Special Ability**: Dodges talismans (a talisman hit requires passing a Difficulty Class (DC) 12 save); increased agility
- **Yin Emission Value (YIN)**: 5
- **Weakness**: Peachwood Sword / Coin Sword + talisman combination
- **Correct Handling**: Spirit tools paired with talismans
- **Counter Tactics**: Flanks flexibly, kills the talisman-bearer first; protect your own talisman-drawer, break the hard skin with a spirit tool, then control with talismans.

**Background**
The Black Zombie is no longer "early-stage"; its body turns black, its strength boundless, and it knows to sidestep talismans. It singles out the best talisman-drawer in the team—instinctively knowing the talisman is more fearsome than the fist. The uninhabited villages of the New Territories, abandoned industrial buildings—its tracks are often found there. When a Ghost Collector meets a Black Zombie, never fight separately: the Warrior holds the front, the caster breaks the skin from range with the Peachwood Sword, and the talisman-hand hides behind a Warding Barrier to add control—that is the correct approach.

---

## ⑨ Hopping Corpse

- **Challenge Rating (CR)**: 6
- **Health Points (HP)**: 52
- **Armor Class (AC)**: 13
- **Initiative (INIT)**: d20+2
- **Attack**: Pounce Kill (hit bonus +5, damage 1d12); Hard-Skin Mitigation −5 (requires spirit tool)
- **Special Ability**: Appears human and can blend into crowds; ordinary spirit tools are ineffective
- **Yin Emission Value (YIN)**: 6
- **Weakness**: Five-Thunder Palm, century-old spirit tools, Celestial Master Talisman
- **Correct Handling**: High-tier spells (Five-Thunder Palm / Century Peachwood Sword / Celestial Master Talisman)
- **Counter Tactics**: Disguised sneak attack, ambushes after blending into crowds; first see through its disguise (Spirit Perception check), then focus fire with high-tier means.

**Background**
Long ago, corpse-drivers from western Hunan brought their trade into Guangdong, and the legend took root here. The Hopping Corpse's greatest terror is not its strength but that it is "like a person"—wearing the suit it wore in life, carrying a briefcase, quietly queuing in the MTR crowd, until you turn your back and it opens its mouth. An ordinary Peachwood Sword can no longer break its skin; it takes a Century Peachwood Sword, a Celestial Master Talisman, or a single Five-Thunder Palm. In such cases, several colleagues who "thought it was just an ordinary corpse change" have usually already died.

---

## ⑩ Flying Corpse

- **Challenge Rating (CR)**: 8
- **Health Points (HP)**: 70
- **Armor Class (AC)**: 15
- **Initiative (INIT)**: d20+3
- **Attack**: Dive (hit bonus +6, damage 2d8 +2); Hard-Skin Mitigation −8 (requires century-old spirit tool / Celestial Master Talisman)
- **Special Ability**: Brief flight (ignores terrain); retains the wisdom and memory of life
- **Yin Emission Value (YIN)**: 9
- **Weakness**: Its obsession in life (its only weakness, must be discovered through investigation)
- **Correct Handling**: Find its obsession in life and deliver it
- **Counter Tactics**: Aerial guerrilla, diving then pulling up; trap it to the ground with a Warding Barrier, or investigate its obsession then deliver it through a psychological strike—a head-on clash is extremely dangerous.

**Background**
The Flying Corpse is the pinnacle of zombies—able to soar, remembering everything from life, including who it hated and who it loved. It streaks between the villas atop Victoria Peak, gazes down from a Tsim Sha Tsui rooftop at those who wronged it years ago. Hard skin −8, peachwood and coin swords all fail; only century-old spirit tools or a Celestial Master Talisman can break it. But it ultimately retains the crack of "obsession": gather that whole story and the deliverance truly takes effect. Otherwise what comes crashing down is only temporary flesh.

## ⑪ Cultivated Zombie

- **Challenge Rating (CR)**: Base +1 (sample uses White Zombie tier = Challenge Rating (CR) 3)
- **Health Points (HP)**: 30
- **Armor Class (AC)**: 12
- **Initiative (INIT)**: d20+1
- **Attack**: Commanded Heavy Strike (hit bonus +4, damage 1d8); Hard-Skin Mitigation −2
- **Special Ability**: Obeys the Corpse-Refiner's commands (coordinated action)
- **Yin Emission Value (YIN)**: 4
- **Weakness**: Find and destroy the Corpse-Refiner → zombie loses control (mitigation fails, actions fall into Confusion)
- **Correct Handling**: Behead the Corpse-Refiner (resolve at the source)
- **Counter Tactics**: Executes commands, protects the Corpse-Refiner's position; prioritize locating and dealing with the mastermind Corpse-Refiner rather than grappling with the corpse.

**Background**
Not a natural corpse change, but one someone "cultivates". At Sheung Shui ancestral halls, Yuen Long abandoned farmhouses, someone uses wicked arts to refine the corpse of a relative or enemy into a hired thug, a guard dog. The Cultivated Zombie itself is not the strongest; what is terrifying is the living person behind it—the Corpse-Refiner points and it strikes, and will shift position to save itself. Veteran Ghost Collectors know: rather than wrestle the corpse, first feel out who is pulling the strings, and with one cut sever the thread's head—the corpse naturally falls into disorder.

# Chapter Three: Other Entities

> This category includes Spirit Beasts, foreign Evil Entities, possession phenomena, and environmental encounters such as Ghost Wall and Haunted House. Environmental types have no single-entity Health Points (HP); they are handled through domain effects and breaking checks.

## ⑫ Spirit Beast

- **Challenge Rating (CR)**: 2
- **Health Points (HP)**: 20
- **Armor Class (AC)**: 11
- **Initiative (INIT)**: d20+2
- **Attack**: Vine Whip / Claw (hit bonus +2, damage 1d6)
- **Special Ability**: Neutral, can serve as an information source; thrives in varied environments
- **Yin Emission Value (YIN)**: 2
- **Weakness**: Negotiation (Intuition (INS) + Communication / social)
- **Correct Handling**: Talk if you can (no pointless fighting)
- **Counter Tactics**: Defensive, retaliates only when threatened; proactively communicate with Intuition (INS), often yielding clues rather than combat.

**Background**
Behind banyan roots, Stone Guardians, and Earth God shrines dwell things that have gained spirit. Hong Kong's Spirit Beasts are mostly not very malicious—a banyan spirit guards a village, a snake spirit protects a well, at most stealing a few guavas. If a Ghost Collector meets a Spirit Beast, do not draw the sword first—it may know better than the temple keeper who died here and what was buried. A kind word, a stick of incense, and the case information gained is often worth ten talismans.

---

## ⑬ Evil Entity

- **Challenge Rating (CR)**: 6
- **Health Points (HP)**: 58
- **Armor Class (AC)**: 13
- **Initiative (INIT)**: d20+2
- **Attack**: Mental Shock (hit bonus +5, damage 2d6 + Confusion 1 round)
- **Special Ability**: Non-human entity, immune to negotiation (Negotiation Will (WILL) 0); corrupts the environment
- **Yin Emission Value (YIN)**: 8
- **Weakness**: Rite / banishment, Demon-Sealing Array
- **Correct Handling**: Ritual banishment (not deliverance, but exile)
- **Counter Tactics**: Uses mental attacks to break the team's will; a high Composure (CMP) member must hold the front while the Main Caster raises a Demon-Sealing Array or performs a large rite to banish it.

**Background**
Not a local wronged soul, but filth "threaded" in from elsewhere—the backlash of Nanyang sorcery, the leak of transit black arts, or something someone deliberately summoned. The Evil Entity is unreasonable, recognizes no kin, and specializes in attacking the human heart; struck once, a teammate may turn and cut down their own. Such beings cannot be delivered (they never belonged to this land and water); they can only be driven out, only sealed. Once the Demon-Sealing Array is laid, hold steady with a Dao Attainment (DOW) check to barely push it back to where it came from.

---

## ⑭ Possessed

- **Challenge Rating (CR)**: 3
- **Health Points (HP)**: 24 (host body)
- **Armor Class (AC)**: 11
- **Initiative (INIT)**: d20+1
- **Attack**: Host's fists/feet (hit bonus +3, damage 1d6) + Possession Wave (contact charges up possession)
- **Special Ability**: Cannot wound the host (mistaken injury triggers Fear / guilt); the spirit hides inside the body
- **Yin Emission Value (YIN)**: 4
- **Weakness**: Exorcism Talisman / Invite Deity Possession to drive out the spirit
- **Correct Handling**: Exorcism rite (drive out the spirit without harming the host)
- **Counter Tactics**: Uses the host's body as defense, forcing players to hesitate; never force an attack—use an Exorcism Talisman or Invite Deity Possession to drive the spirit out of the body before dealing with it.

**Background**
The scene that makes a Ghost Collector's hands go weak: across from you is someone you know, perhaps the client themselves, perhaps your apprentice. The spirit bores into his body, laughs through his mouth, chokes you with his hands. You throw a punch and the living person hurts. Now put away the Peachwood Sword; switch to an Exorcism Talisman on the forehead, switch to Invite Deity Possession to "squeeze" that thing out—what you save is the person inside the shell, not the shell. Strike wrong and it is a nightmare for life.

## ⑮ Ghost Wall (environmental)

- **Challenge Rating (CR)**: 3 (environmental encounter)
- **Health Points (HP)**: None
- **Armor Class (AC)**: —
- **Initiative (INIT)**: —
- **Attack**: No direct damage; traps victims so Movement (MOVE) fails
- **Special Ability**: Distorts space into a loop; each round Yin Value (YIN) +1
- **Yin Emission Value (YIN)**: 5 (global)
- **Weakness**: Composure (CMP) check to see through + Road-Opening Talisman / rite to break
- **Correct Handling**: See through + break (first Composure (CMP) + Will check Difficulty Class (DC) 15 to see through, then Knowledge (KNW) + Lineage Knowledge or Dao Attainment (DOW) + Warding Barrier check Difficulty Class (DC) 15 to break)
- **Counter Tactics**: Lures players in circles to drain resources; to break free, one person must see through it then "pull" nearby teammates (who must pass a Difficulty Class (DC) 12 Composure check) to escape.

**Background**
Late-night hill walks, deserted trails on outlying islands, or the endless staircases of old buildings—a straight path that always returns to the start, a watch hand spinning backward, a phone with no signal. The elders call it "ghosts covering the eyes". This is no monster; it is a Warding Barrier woven from the land's Yin Energy, preying on those who are guilty or restless at heart. The break lies not in strength but in Composure—calm the mind, spot the corner that should not exist, paste a Road-Opening Talisman, snap an Ink Line Reel thread, and the knot is broken.

## ⑯ Haunted House (environmental nest)

- **Challenge Rating (CR)**: 5 (environmental encounter)
- **Health Points (HP)**: No single-entity Health Points (HP)
- **Armor Class (AC)**: —
- **Initiative (INIT)**: —
- **Attack**: Yin trap (1d6 Burn each round indoors); structural assault
- **Special Ability**: The whole building is saturated; spirits within the domain gain +1 attack bonus / +1 Yin Emission Value (YIN)
- **Yin Emission Value (YIN)**: 10 (global, death-domain tier)
- **Weakness**: Whole-house great purification (team division of labor), break the Feng Shui layout
- **Correct Handling**: Large rite + team division of labor (break the situation, not a solo duel)
- **Counter Tactics**: A war of attrition, dragging players into the death domain for Assimilation; must act separately—some break the Feng Shui, some raise the altar, some guard the rite; none can be omitted.

**Background**
An apartment in a Mong Kok Tong Lau, an ancestral home in a Yuen Long walled village—those who move in fall ill, move out, or die, one after another. A Haunted House is not one ghost; the whole building has "come alive"—walls seep water like tears, lights extinguish on their own, the staircase gains an extra step. The Yin Value (YIN) piles to death-domain tier, and the living who stay long are Assimilated. For such a case, a single Peachwood Sword is not enough to watch: a Taoist must open the altar to suppress the center, a Feng Shui master must move objects to break the trap, a Warrior must hold the door—only with the team united can it be cleansed.
