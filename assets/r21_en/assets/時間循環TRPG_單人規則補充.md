# Time Loop TRPG — Single-Player Rule Supplement v1.0

> **For:** 1 player + 1 GM (Host)
> **Prerequisite reading:** Time Loop TRPG Core Rulebook v1.2
> **Design intent:** While keeping the core system intact, this supplement adds the NPC Companion mechanic, attitude adjustments, combat balancing, and an alternative fatigue solution, so that a single player can also experience the full loop narrative.

---

## I. Why This Supplement Is Needed

The original rulebook assumes 3–4 players acting cooperatively. In single-player mode, the following gaps arise:

| Problem | Impact | Solution |
|------|------|------|
| Teammate-directed abilities (e.g., the Optimist's Light of Hope) go to waste | Three attitudes' core abilities become ineffective in solo play | §II: Solo Attitude Adjustments |
| No teammates to assist in combat and exploration | Solo player faces extremely high risk of death against enemies; insufficient exploration coverage | §III: NPC Companion System |
| No teammates to provide Empathy Soothing | Fatigue recovery is limited to Will self-healing, too harsh | §IV: Alternative Fatigue Recovery |
| Team memory pool becomes meaningless | No teammates to share information with | §V: Simplified Memory Pool |
| Scenarios assume multi-player division of labor | Fragment distribution and event pacing become unbalanced | See the Single-Player Scenario Supplement document |

---

## II. Single-Player Adjustments to Time Attitudes

The following adjustments apply **only to single-player mode**. Multi-player mode still uses the original rules.

| Attitude | Original Ability | Solo Adjustment |
|------|------|------|
| **Optimist** | Light of Hope: grants an ally Advantage on Will checks | **Changed to "Self-Encouragement":** When recovering fatigue, additionally recover 1 extra point (from −1 becomes −2, from −2 becomes −3, and so on). Once per loop. |
| **Skeptic** | Eye for Flaws: forces the GM to reveal an NPC's contradiction | Unchanged — fully applicable to solo play. |
| **Guardian** | Guard Instinct: automatically senses when a Core Relationship NPC is in danger | **Enhanced:** Twice per loop (originally twice, but with this addition: you may also designate one NPC companion as a "secondary ward" — when that companion is in danger the sense also triggers, but moving to them costs 1 Action Point (AP)). |
| **Fatalist** | Gift of Fate: fatigue → temporary Anchor | Unchanged — fully applicable to solo play. |
| **Curious** | Detail Hunter: forces the GM to provide observational details | **Enhanced:** Twice per loop (because solo play needs to cover more investigation ground). |

> **Reference:** The original attitude table is in the Core Rulebook §2.1 Step 3 "Time Attitudes" table.

## III. NPC Companion System

### 3.1 Overview

In single-player mode, the player may recruit one **NPC Companion**. This NPC accompanies the player through the loops, assisting in combat, investigation, and providing social support. But the NPC **does not retain loop memories** — after each reset, the NPC companion returns to its starting state.

### 3.2 Companion Acquisition

- **Scenario presets:** Each scenario designates 1–2 candidate NPC companions (set by the GM during scenario preparation).
- **Independent recruitment:** After the player builds sufficient trust with an NPC during a loop, they may invite it to become a companion (Empathy check, Normal +0 → 7–9 Partial Success = temporarily follows, 10+ Full Success = follows for the entire loop).
- **Forced setup:** If the player chose the "Guardian" attitude during character creation, the GM should ensure a Core Relationship NPC or another NPC can serve as a companion — so the Guardian's ability does not go entirely to waste.

### 3.3 Companion Stats (Simplified)

NPC companions use a simplified four-attribute set; no full character sheet is needed:

| Attribute | Abbreviation | Meaning |
|------|:---:|------|
| **Physique** | Physique (BOD) | Physical ability / combat / Action (equivalent to Action (ACT)) |
| **Alertness** | Alertness (AWA) | Observation / sensing anomalies (equivalent to Insight (INS)) |
| **Will** | Will (WIL) | Mental resilience (equivalent to Will (WIL)) |
| **Affinity** | Affinity (AFF) | Social / soothing (equivalent to Empathy (EMP)) |

**Attribute range: 1–3 (companions are ordinary people and cannot exceed 3)**

| Attribute Value | Modifier |
|:---:|:---:|
| 1 | −1 |
| 2 | +0 |
| 3 | +1 |

### 3.4 Companion Actions

| Action | Description |
|------|------|
| **Assist in Combat** | The companion joins combat, rolling 2d6 + Physique (BOD) modifier. Degree of success is judged by the contest rules. When the companion takes damage, use the four-tier progression Minor → Moderate → Severe → Near-Death. |
| **Assist in Investigation** | The companion rolls 2d6 + Alertness (AWA) modifier. Full Success = the companion discovers a clue the player may have missed (GM hint); Partial Success = the companion feels "something is off" but is not specific. |
| **Empathy Soothing** | The companion can soothe the player (since the companion is one of the people closest to the player in this loop). Roll 2d6 + Affinity (AFF) modifier. Rules are the same as the original Empathy Soothing — Partial Success recovers 1 fatigue, Full Success recovers 2 fatigue. |
| **Divide-and-Conquer Action** | The companion can independently perform a simple task (e.g., "go to the library to look up information," "follow a certain NPC"). Costs the player 2 Action Points (AP). The GM makes one simplified check (2d6 + the companion's relevant modifier) to determine the result. |

### 3.5 Companions at Loop Reset

- The companion's memory is **fully reset** — they remember nothing across loops.
- The companion's trust and relationships are also reset — but their "personality" is retained. A friendly NPC is friendly at the start of every loop.
- However: **After Loop 7+**, the companion may trigger Déjà Vu (same as ordinary NPC rules) or memory residue (if tied to an Anchor).
- The companion's **physical state** is fully reset (wounds disappear, returns to starting position).

### 3.6 Companions and Anchored Information

A companion can act as the player's "extended sense" — if the companion discovers a piece of information during this loop, the player may spend Anchors at settlement to lock that information, just as if they had discovered it themselves. **But the companion cannot anchor information independently** — only the player has Anchors.

## IV. Alternative Fatigue Recovery Options

In single-player mode, the lack of a teammate's Empathy Soothing leads to insufficient fatigue recovery. The following adjustments are solo-exclusive:

| Recovery Method | Original Rule | Solo Adjustment |
|------|------|------|
| Empathy Soothing (teammate) | 2d6+Empathy (EMP) recovers 1–2 | **Replaced by NPC Companion Soothing** (see §3.4), or **Self-Soothing** (spend 1 Action Point (AP), roll 2d6 + Empathy (EMP), degree of success as per Empathy Soothing; but difficulty +1: the minimum result for Partial Success changes from 7 to 8, Full Success from 10 to 11) |
| Will Self-Healing | 2d6+Will (WIL) recovers 1–2, costs 2 Action Points (AP) | Unchanged |

---

## V. Simplified Memory Pool

The team memory pool is downgraded to a **personal tracking sheet** in single-player mode. Rules are simplified as follows:

- The player does not need to share information with anyone — the information-sharing rule is canceled.
- Hidden Information Cards still exist (draw one), but their dramatic tension of "whether to tell a teammate" becomes the inner struggle of "whether to tell the NPC companion."
- The GM may use the NPC companion as an information-verification tool — the companion may inadvertently say something that contradicts the player's anchored information, triggering a new investigation direction.

## VI. Combat Balance

In single-player mode, the player faces alone against enemies, with a much higher risk of death than in multiplayer. The following adjustments apply:

### 6.1 Lowered Threat Levels

| Original Threat | Adjustment |
|:---:|------|
| Threat 1 (Easy) | Unchanged |
| Threat 2 (Normal) | Unchanged |
| Threat 3 (Hard) | Lowered to Normal in solo mode (Situation Modifier −1 → +0) |
| Threat 4 (Critical) | Lowered to Hard in solo mode (Situation Modifier −2 → −1) |
| Threat 5 (Lethal) | Lowered to Very Hard (−2) in solo mode, but direct confrontation should still be avoided |

### 6.2 Companion Protection in Combat

- **Companion Takes the Hit:** The player may command the companion to absorb an incoming attack that would otherwise hit the player. The companion will not die — but enters a Severe Wound state and cannot assist in combat for the rest of the loop.
- **Companion Covers the Retreat:** When the player flees, the companion can delay the opponent for one round. After that round, the companion "falls" — and recovers at the start point after the next loop's reset.

### 6.3 Single-Player Escape Rules

In solo combat, the player may choose to "flee" in any round:
- Roll 2d6 + Action (ACT) modifier vs opponent's 2d6 + Action (ACT) modifier
- Success → break away from combat (the opponent may give chase, entering chase rules)
- Partial Success → break away but leave behind an item / lose Action Points (AP)
- Failure → intercepted

## VIII. Additional Solo Character Creation Steps

On top of the standard six-step character creation, add:

**Step 7: Choose (or discover) your NPC Companion**

- The GM presets 1–2 candidate companions during scenario preparation.
- After interacting with a candidate NPC in the first loop, the player may "invite" it to become a companion.
- If the player chose the "Guardian" attitude: the GM should ensure a Core Relationship NPC can serve as a companion, or provide an additional companion candidate beyond the Core Relationship NPC.
