# Part One: Worldview

## 1.1 Rift Theory

The time loop is not magic, nor is it divine punishment. It is the spontaneous fracture of reality's structure under extreme emotional pressure.

**Core Principle:** When a single moment carries too much emotional energy—an irrecoverable death, a truth that cannot be spoken, an ending that cannot be accepted—reality fractures at that point. The rift tears that moment out of the flow of time, forming an enclosed loop interval.

The loop is not punishment, but reality's attempt to "heal" itself. Each loop is reality making a tiny adjustment, trying to find a path that can heal the rift.

### Loop Dynamics

- The loop is sustained by the "Anchor Event"—the source of the rift, and the reason the loop cannot end
- On each Reset, all NPC memories reset completely except for the "Anchor Person"
- Player memories can be carried across loops—a signal from the rift seeking outside help
- Loop boundaries are fixed: there may be a geographic range (a small town), a time range (three days), or a spatial range (an elevator)

### Key Insight: No One Controls the Loop

The loop is a natural phenomenon. This means:
- There is no "final boss" to kill
- The only way to end the loop = heal the rift
- The opponent is not a concrete enemy, but "truth" itself

## 1.2 Unified Setting Elements

| Concept | Definition | GM Usage |
|------|------|------|
| **Anchor** | The original "unbearable moment" | The starting point of scenario design—first decide what event is enough to tear reality |
| **Degradation** | Loss of reality's structural stability after each loop | The core tool for adjusting difficulty—physical shifts, causal breaks, NPC distortion |
| **Déjà Vu** | NPCs' unconscious residue of previous loops | An information-release tool—give players hints through Déjà Vu |
| **Awakened One** | A person who can retain memory in the loop (= player character) | Awakening condition: emotional resonance with the rift + making a different choice |
| **Loop Fatigue** | The cumulative psychological pressure borne by the Awakened One | Different from physical fatigue (which resets), fatigue is cumulative |

## 2.2 Information Control: Handling "Metagaming"

### Three Principles

**Principle One: Character actions need a "reasonable trigger"**

| Loop | Cross-Loop Knowledge Use | GM Handling |
|:--:|:---|:---|
| 1-3 | Need this-loop clues | "Your character doesn't know this yet" |
| 4-6 | "Intuitive action" may be requested | "You have a strange premonition..." |
| 7-9 | Mostly accessible via Déjà Vu | "Your body remembers before your mind does" |
| 10+ | Fully open | "You have become one with the loop" |

**Principle Two: Information is in three layers**
- Layer 1: Known by the character (obtained this loop) → freely usable
- Layer 2: Known by the player, character can reasonably infer → GM adjudication
- Layer 3: Known by the player, character cannot know → needs a "Déjà Vu exemption"

**Principle Three: Don't punish a good memory**
If a player remembers details of NPC dialogue—that is worth encouraging. Handle it through Déjà Vu/intuition rather than saying "you couldn't possibly know that."

### GM Phrasing (turning metagaming into a worldview element)

| Player Says | GM Response |
|--------|---------|
| "I know he'll appear at the dock at that time" | "You have a strange feeling that the dock might be an important place. Will you go?" |
| "Don't talk to him, he lied to us last time" | "You look at this person's face and feel an inexplicable unease. What do you want to do?" |
| "Just go to the basement, the code is 1945" | "You stand before the door. Your fingers move involuntarily toward the code lock—as if your body remembers something your mind does not." |

## 2.3 Pacing Control: The Four-Stage Narrative Arc

| Stage | Loop | Player Mindset | GM Goal | Pacing |
|------|:--:|------|------|:--:|
| Exploration | 1-3 | "What happened here?" | Build the world, plant seeds | Slower |
| Optimization | 4-6 | "Let me optimize every step" | Allow attempts, deepen relationships | Accelerating |
| Truth | 7-10 | "We're about to know" | Puzzle takes shape, emotional pressure | Slow then sudden |
| Endgame | 11-13+ | "This is the last chance" | Endgame pressure, emotional release | Extremely tense |

### Pacing Transition Techniques

- **Exploration → Optimization:** When the player first says "we should try a different route"
- **Optimization → Truth:** When the player has obtained 4-5 fragments
- **Truth → Endgame:** When degradation reaches level 4, or the player realizes "only a few chances remain"

## 2.4 Loop Degradation Roadmap: The Five-Stage Model

| Stage | Loop | Degradation | Environment | NPC | Causality | GM Focus |
|------|:--:|:--:|------|------|------|------|
| **Mirror** | 1-3 | 1 | 0% change | Strictly by baseline | Normal | Establish the baseline so later degradation has impact |
| **Ripple** | 4-6 | 2 | Items slightly adjusted, light/shadow shifts | 1-2 NPCs slightly shifted | Occasionally "A before B but feels like B first" | Changes subtle enough that players can't tell if it's a clue or a bug |
| **Crack** | 7-9 | 3 | Buildings show nonexistent cracks, abnormal color saturation | Multiple NPCs deviate, one NPC may speak a previous-loop line | Event order scrambled | First "NPC memory residue" |
| **Fracture** | 10-12 | 4 | Spatial jumps, items move on their own, areas inaccessible | NPC briefly "conscious" of the loop | Severe causal breaks | Endgame pressure + release of final key clue |
| **Collapse** | 13+ | 5 | Spatial fragmentation, "no longer exists" but afterimages visible | Stagnant or abnormally clear | Nearly completely failed | Final tenderness + emotional climax |

## 2.5 Handling "Suicide Restart"

**Core stance: Not forbidden, but there must be a cost.**

| Loop | Cost |
|:--:|------|
| 1-3 | Information lost |
| 4-6 | Above + degradation accelerates by 1 step |
| 7-9 | Above + random NPC permanently degrades by 2 |
| 10-12 | Above + loop cap −1 |
| 13 | Suicide restart not allowed |

**Every suicide is a narrative moment.** Reveal something new in the suicide scene—not to reward suicide, but to make it part of the story.

## 2.6 Opening Narration Template

**Loop 1:** "You wake up. Sunlight streams through the hotel's old curtains. The air smells of autumn—dry leaves, distant cooking smoke. From downstairs comes the sound of the hotel proprietress preparing breakfast. Everything is normal. Everything is peaceful. Except... you don't quite remember how you came to this small town."

**Loop 5+:** "Sunlight streams through that curtain again. This time you notice the pattern on the curtain—in Loop 1 it was blue flowers, now those flowers look slightly purple. You're not sure if it's the light, or if it's truly changing. The sound from downstairs comes on time. You close your eyes and can perfectly predict the next 15 seconds."

**Loop 10+:** "Waking up has become hard. Not physically—the body always resets perfectly. But mentally. You know exactly what will happen every minute. You know who will say what. You know who will die. But you still open your eyes."

## 2.7 Team Memory Pool

Place it on the table (a whiteboard or large sheet of paper) and have the players maintain it together.

Use colors:
- 🟡 Yellow = Confirmed
- 🔵 Blue = Hypothesis
- 🔴 Red = Urgent/critical
- 🟢 Green = Resolved

**Core function: Separate "facts" from "hypotheses"**—let players clearly see "what we know" vs "what we guess."

---

## 2.8 Loop Echoes

Never say "same as last time." Even if the scene is 90% identical, find the 10% difference:

| Time | Echo Type | Focus |
|:--:|------|------|
| 1 | First experience | Describe the scene in full |
| 2 | Detail discovery | "Last time you didn't notice the vase in the corner" |
| 3 | Emotional layer | "This time you notice his right hand trembles slightly after he finishes speaking" |
| 4 | Meaning shift | "Same conversation, but now you know what he did last night" |
| 5+ | Beyond the scene | "What you hear is not the words—but the apology unspoken for thirty years behind them" |

# Part Three: NPC Design System

## 3.1 Behavior Baseline Card Template

```
NPC Name: ______  Role Position: ______

[Surface Persona]Occupation/age/appearance/public personality/hidden personality

[First-Loop Baseline]
  Fixed action schedule (with time + description)
  Key dialogue baseline (the 3 most important lines)
  Emotional baseline: ___/10 (5 = calm)
  Trust baseline: toward players ___/10
  Secret baseline: the secret the NPC hides in the first loop

[Relationship to the Anchor]
  Direct relationship / indirect relationship / how much they know / why they deny it

[Degradation Sensitivity]
  □ Low (+0) — unrelated to the Anchor
  □ Medium (+10) — indirectly related
  □ High (+20) — direct participant
  □ Extreme (+30) — the Anchor Person itself

[Cross-Loop Record] (loop# / shift value / shift direction / special behavior)
```

### Baseline Design Principles

1. **The baseline must be predictable:** Players need to grasp NPC patterns within 3 loops
2. **Every NPC has at least one secret:** Not necessarily the core truth, can be personal
3. **Fixed actions > free actions:** At least 60% of behavior is fixed
4. **Plant one clue in the baseline:** Each NPC's daily routine contains at least one piece of information pointing to the truth

---

## 3.2 d100 NPC Behavior Shift Table

At the start of each new loop (or at a specific trigger point), roll a shift check for each key NPC:

```
Shift = d100 + degradation level modifier + NPC sensitivity modifier

Degradation level modifier: Lv1 −10 / Lv2 +0 / Lv3 +10 / Lv4 +20 / Lv5 +30
NPC sensitivity modifier: Low +0 / Medium +10 / High +20 / Extreme +30
```

| d100 (after modifier) | Shift Type | Effect | GM Narration Guide |
|:---:|:---:|------|------|
| ≤10 | No shift | Exactly by baseline | "Everything as usual" |
| 11-20 | Subtle shift | A habitual action disappears | Let players discover it themselves |
| 21-30 | Wording shift | Fixed line's wording changes | "His words sound... a little different?" |
| 31-40 | Emotional shift | Emotional baseline ±2 | The shift players notice most easily |
| 41-50 | Time shift | Appears at baseline time ±5 minutes | May cause players to miss the interaction |
| 51-60 | **Minor Déjà Vu fit** | Stops 2-3 seconds, looks confused, says "I think I... never mind" | ★ GM information-release tool |
| 61-65 | Memory fragment | Says a word/phrase from a previous loop then immediately forgets | "What did you just say?" "What?" |
| 66-70 | Role inversion | Does something contrary to personality | Reveals hidden personality |
| 71-75 | **Cross-loop dialogue** | Says to the player a line the player said in a previous loop | The most unsettling shift |
| 76-80 | **Truth leak** | Unintentionally says a truth fragment related to the Anchor | ★ Rewards players with an unexpected gain |
| 81-85 | Temporal confusion | Behaves as if at another point in the loop | "What is he doing? It's only nine o'clock..." |
| 86-90 | Freeze | Stagnant 5-30 seconds, completely still | Creates unease; returns to normal afterward |
| 91-95 | **Edge of awakening** | Briefly "conscious" of the loop—forgets after 1 minute | ★ Major plot beat |
| 96-98 | Reversal | Behavior completely reversed within 1 minute | Extremely rare—degradation has reached dangerous levels |
| 99-100 | Double presence | Appears in two places at once | Extremely rare—the loop structure is tearing apart |
| 101+ | **Truth collapse** | Directly states the full truth—then the NPC "disappears" | Ultimate shift. Use at loop 10+. Players must act immediately |

### Shift Stacking Rules
- 3 consecutive shifts of the same type → solidifies into a new baseline
- Single NPC total shift ≤ +10; exceeding → enters Collapse state (behavior completely unpredictable, may become a half-Awakened One)

---

## 3.3 NPC Memory Residue (Loop 10+)

Trigger condition: Loop count ≥ 10 + NPC directly associated with the Anchor + had "meaningful interaction" with players in previous loops (sustained 5+ minute conversation / physical contact / shared emotional moment / revealed a secret).

| d6 | Type | Example |
|:--:|------|------|
| 1 | **Echo** | Repeats a line they spoke in a previous loop, word for word |
| 2 | **Emotional residue** | Unexplained fondness or hostility toward the player |
| 3 | **Scene flashback** | Describes a previous-loop scene, but believes it was a dream they had |
| 4 | **Trigger-word reaction** | Strong reaction to a specific word/name |
| 5 | **Precognitive residue** | Reacts before the event happens |
| 6 | **Half-awakening** | Realizes the loop exists—lasts one minute, then forgets |

**GM Tips:**
- At most one memory residue per NPC every 2-3 loops
- Should make players feel "remembered"—a relief from loneliness
- Should also make players feel sad—the NPC will forget the next second

---

## 3.4 Hidden Relationship Depth (one-sided bond between NPC and player)

| Depth | Name | Condition | Narrative Effect |
|:--:|------|------|------|
| 0 | Stranger | <2 interactions | Normal interaction |
| 1 | Familiar Stranger | Interacted across 3-5 loops | Players know the NPC's routine. GM may skip repeated dialogue |
| 2 | Invisible Friend | Deep interaction across 6-9 loops | NPC begins to show memory residue. Player can predict reaction without a check |
| 3 | One-sided Bond | 10+ loops and once tried to save them | Memory residue frequency doubles. NPC unconsciously repeats the player's kindness (loop echo) |
| 4 | Rift Resonance | Shared the Anchor's key scene | NPC may become a half-Awakened One—briefly recognizes the player at a key moment |

# Part Four: Narrative Guide

## 4.1 Emotional Beats

| When | What to do |
|------|------|
| Every 3-4 loops | Arrange a "stillness moment"—pure atmosphere, no clues |
| 2 consecutive high-pressure loops | Hint at a "coffee and sunset" opportunity |
| After NPC memory residue | Give the group private reflection space |
| Approaching the truth | NPC shows unexpected tenderness |
| Before the final loop | A "last ordinary day" scene |

### "Coffee and Sunset" Scene Example

> "You sit atop the clock tower. The sunset dyes the whole small town gold. From afar comes the music of the school festival—the same song you've heard eight times. But this time, no one rushes to the next location. No one records clues. You just look at each other, then at the sunset.
>
> The café owner—the middle-aged man who says 'Welcome' to you every loop—today he suddenly falls silent. He sets the coffee before you, sits down. 'What a beautiful sunset,' he says. 'A pity tomorrow won't be the same sunset.'
>
> You know he's right. The sunset degrades a little each loop. This may be the last such sunset."

---

## 4.2 Information Reveal Pacing

| Layer | Loop | Fragments Released | Goal |
|:--:|:--:|:--:|------|
| First | 1-2 | 1-2 | Establish the question, give no answer |
| Second | 3-5 | 2-3 | Reveal NPC hidden relationships, introduce the first red herring |
| Third | 6-8 | 2-3 | Overturn or confirm hypotheses, red herring disproven |
| Fourth | 9-12 | 1-2 | Fill puzzle gaps, may be released via NPC memory residue |
| Fifth | 13+ | All obtainable | Shift focus from "discovering the truth" to "how to face the truth" |

---

## 4.3 Red Herring Design

A good red herring must: seem reasonable, have an explanation, not waste time, be disprovable.

Template:
```
Misleading direction: Players may thus believe ______
Actual fact: ______
Why players would believe it: ______
How it's disproven: ______
Truth fragment revealed after disproof: ______
Value of the red herring itself: ______
```

1-2 red herrings per scenario is enough (no more than 3). They should be disproven between loops 4-6.

---

## 4.4 Narrative Meaning of Loop Numbers

| Loop | Player Psychology | NPC Psychology | GM Narrative Style |
|:--:|------|------|------|
| 1-3 | Explorer—curious, confused | Normal | Establish a sense of real texture |
| 4-6 | Optimizer—efficiency thinking | Few subtle Déjà Vu | Introduce tiny anomalies |
| 7-9 | Investigator—approaching truth | Some behavior shifts | Turn dark, truth surfaces |
| 10-12 | Witness—loneliness and responsibility | Obvious shifts, partial memory residue | Every interaction carries weight |
| 13+ | Terminator—calm or despair | At the edge of collapse | Poetry and sorrow intertwined |

## Part Five: "Town Judgment Day" Example Scenario Framework

### Scenario Summary

- **Loop Length:** 3 days
- **Loop Endpoint:** Midnight of the third day, all townsfolk collectively vanish
- **Starting Point:** 6:00 AM on the first day, players wake in the town's hotel
- **Tone:** Collective guilt / silent complicity / delayed justice

### Core Truth

15 years ago, the townsfolk of the small town lynched an outsider teacher named "Chen Yan." He was accused of "corrupting the town's youth"—in reality his only "crime" was telling students about the outside world. The true reason: Chen Yan discovered the mayor's financial scandal.

The loop is reality giving the small town a chance to face its crime. Rift-Healing Conditions: at least one townsperson publicly admits Chen Yan was wrongly accused + players find Chen Yan's diary.

### Eight Truth Fragments

1. **Last Lesson (document/old schoolhouse/Medium):** A letter Chen Yan wrote to his students the day before he died—"Tomorrow I'm going to tell you something important"
2. **The Absent Mayor (document/town hall archive room/Hard):** That night's phone records—the mayor was not at the scene, but covered up the truth afterward
3. **The Old Tailor's Dream (dialogue/Grandma Lin's tailor shop/Easy):** The 76-year-old tailor will say on the afternoon of the second day: "I dreamed that young teacher came back again"
4. **Traces Beneath the Square (scene/town square/Medium):** At sunset on the third day, blood-colored stains show in the stone seams under specific light
5. **The Merchant's Ledger (item/grocery back room/Medium):** The grocery owner received "special compensation" after Chen Yan's death
6. **The Student's Silence (dialogue/town high school/Hard):** Chen Yan's student from back then—now a high school teacher—kept Chen Yan's diary
7. **The Basement Diary (item/old schoolhouse basement/Hard):** Chen Yan's diary, last page: "They know. Something may happen tonight."
8. **The Truth of the Ritual (memory/town square/Special):** The annual silent ritual—actually a ritual forcing the whole town into silence

### Three Key NPCs

**Mayor Zhou Zhengyi (56):** Emotional baseline 4/10 (repressed anxiety), sensitivity "High," secret: he knows Chen Yan was innocent but believes speaking the truth would destroy the small town.

**High School Teacher Lin Xiao (30):** Emotional baseline 5/10 (calm surface, sad inside), sensitivity "Extreme," secret: kept Chen Yan's diary.

**Grocery Owner Wang Fugui (62):** Emotional baseline 6/10 (forced cheerfulness), sensitivity "High," secret: participated in the lynching and took compensation money.

### Degradation Roadmap (Brief)

| Loop | Key Degradation |
|:--:|------|
| 1-2 | Normal. Let players feel the "nice small town." First full-wipe at midnight of the third day. |
| 3 | Last normal loop. Fragments 3+4 obtainable. |
| 4-5 | Subtle anomaly: hotel clock 3 minutes slow, old schoolhouse a bit darker. Mayor shifts for the first time (forgets to order coffee at lunch). |
| 6 | Lin Xiao's first memory residue—unconsciously writes Chen Yan's name on the blackboard. Fragment 6 obtainable. |
| 7-9 | Obvious anomaly: old schoolhouse window glass shows a reflection from 15 years ago, fountain water still. Fragments 2+5+7 obtainable. |
| 10-12 | Severe degradation. Lin Xiao may half-awaken. Fragments 1+8 obtainable. |
| 13 | Collapse. Last chance. |

### Three Endings

**A - Justice Delayed (Perfect):** All 8 fragments + persuade the mayor to publicly reveal the truth at the ritual + Chen Yan's diary made public. No townsperson vanishes—the rift heals.

**B - One Person's Farewell (Ordinary):** At least 6 fragments + Lin Xiao reads the diary aloud at the end. The town vanishes, but Lin Xiao stays. Rift half-healed.

**C - The Fifteenth Autumn (Bad):** Loops exhausted. The residents turn to statues, forever surrounding the square. The only one who remembers everything is the player standing at the hotel entrance.
