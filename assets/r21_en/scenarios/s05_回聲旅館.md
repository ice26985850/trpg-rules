# Scenario 5: Echo Hotel (Solo · Progressive ★→★★★★★)

**Design Note:** This is a scenario built entirely for a single player. It is not an adaptation of an existing scenario, but is constructed from the loop structure to the fragment collection entirely around "one person investigating." The player takes the role of a traveler staying at a remote mountain hotel, trapped by a blizzard, and discovers that the hotel resets to the previous evening at midnight every night—and that every room hides the secret of a former guest.

The difficulty curve covers: Loops 1-3 (novice exploration) → 4-6 (standard investigation) → 7-9 (advanced puzzle) → 10-12 (expert truth) → 13+ (ultimate choice).

## Scenario Overview

| Item | Content |
|------|---------|
| **Difficulty** | ★→★★★★★ Progressive (novice-friendly entry, expert-level truth) |
| **Loop Length** | 1 day (the blizzard night: 6:00 PM → midnight 00:00 the next day) |
| **Daily Action Points (AP)** | 9 + Action (ACT) Modifier (6-hour effective window) |
| **Suggested Loop Cap** | 15 |
| **Tone Tags** | Obsession in loneliness / Every room holds a story / The past is never truly past |

## Loop Setting

- **Loop Endpoint:** Midnight 00:00; the bell in the hotel lobby rings, all guest-room doors lock simultaneously—then reset.
- **Starting Moment:** 6:00 PM; you wake up by the fireplace in the hotel lobby. A blizzard howls outside. The hotel keeper is wiping a glass behind the bar.
- **Loop Boundary:** The hotel building + 50 meters around it (beyond 50 meters you cannot survive the blizzard—automatic reset).
- **Opening Narration:** "The fireplace crackles. You open your eyes and find yourself curled up on the old sofa in the lobby. Outside the window is a blizzard—a white void, no road in sight. Behind the bar, an old man is wiping a glass. 'Awake?' he says without looking up. 'The blizzard will last a while yet. The rooms are upstairs—pick any one. No other guests anyway.' As he says 'no other guests,' your gaze inadvertently sweeps across the register—it is densely covered with a dozen-odd names. But every one of them is crossed out."

## Core Truth

**Anchor Event:** This hotel belongs to no era. It is a "seam in time"—every person who gets lost in the blizzard and dies in these mountains, their final consciousness upon death comes to this hotel. They stay one night, then dissipate at midnight. The hotel keeper is the only one who is here forever—he is not a living person, he is the Keeper of this seam. He cannot remember any guest, because in the seam of time, "people" are only passersby.

Ten years ago, one guest did not dissipate. Her name was **Lin Suwen**, an archaeologist who fell off a cliff while saving a companion in the blizzard. Her regret was—she never told her daughter "Mom loves you." This regret was too heavy, too heavy for the seam to digest—and a Rift formed. Since then, every guest who comes to the hotel has been trapped in a six-hour loop. Lin Suwen herself lives in Room 204, but she does not remember who she is—she only remembers "I am waiting for someone."

**Rift-Healing Conditions:**
- Required: The player finds Lin Suwen's keepsake in Room 204 (an unsent letter to her daughter)
- Required: The player tells Lin Suwen the truth—"You are no longer among the living, but your daughter is still alive. She needs to know you died to save someone. She needs this letter."
- Optional: The player visits the other guest rooms and understands the other guests' stories—these stories are not necessary, but they let the player understand the nature of the loop (and may provide Cognition Points (CP) and clues).

## Eight Truth Fragments

**Fragment 1: The Secret of the Register (Scene / lobby bar / Easy / Loop 1)**
- The crossed-out names in the register, looked at closely—each name has a line of small text beside it, written in a different hand: "Died on Month X, Day X, Year XXXX." The last entry is from ten years ago. After that the register is blank—until you appear. Your name is not crossed out. Beside it are only three words: "Still alive."

**Fragment 2: Weeping from Room 204 (Scene / second-floor corridor / Medium / Loop 2)**
- At 10:00 PM, if you pass Room 204, you hear someone weeping inside. The door is locked—but if you wait until 11:00 PM, the door opens briefly for 5 minutes. In the room is a woman, sitting by the window, watching the blizzard. She will not speak to you on her own. But if you ask her "Who are you waiting for?", she will answer: "My daughter. She said she'd come for me. But the snow is too heavy. She may not make it back."

**Fragment 3: The Other Guests' Stories (Conversation / the various rooms / Medium / Loop 3+)**
- The following 3 short stories are scattered across the other rooms of the hotel. You need to understand at least 2 of them to piece together the nature of the loop:
- **Room 201 - The Old Hunter (73):** Froze to death after getting lost in the blizzard five years ago. Every night he wipes a hunting rifle—but the gun is not actually his. It is a relic he found in a cave before he died, and to his last moment he wondered "whose gun is this."
- **Room 203 - The Young Climber (24):** Died three years ago. Every night he writes a letter—but each time he writes half of it he forgets what he was writing. The letter begins: "Dear Mom, I'm sorry, I shouldn't have come alone. The mountain is beautiful, but—" and then the handwriting breaks off.
- **Room 205 - The Trucker (41):** Died in an avalanche eight years ago. Every night he studies a map, trying to find a road that does not exist. If you look at his map—you'll find that road once existed. But it was abandoned ten years ago.

**Fragment 4: The Keeper's Diary (Item / behind the bar / Medium / Loop 4)**
- The hotel keeper's diary. The first page is dated "date unknown." The last page is from ten years ago: "Today a woman came. She was unlike the others—she remembered who she was. She asked me a question: 'Who are you waiting for?' I answered: 'I wait for no one. I am simply here.' She said: 'So am I.' Then she went upstairs. Since that day, time here has been broken. The snow no longer stops. The guests no longer leave. I cannot remember their faces—but tonight, I wrote down this sentence: 'So am I.'"

**Fragment 5: Lin Suwen's Archaeological Notes (Item / under the bed in Room 204 / Hard / Loop 5)**
- An old backpack, containing an archaeological notebook. It records an archaeological project from ten years ago. The last page is a photo torn from a newspaper—a little girl, with the headline "Mountain Rescue Team Searches for Missing Archaeologist." On the back is written: "My daughter asked when I'm coming home. I said next Wednesday. Was that a Wednesday? I no longer know."

**Fragment 6: The Unsent Letter (Item / under the pillow in Room 204 / Hard / Loop 6)**
- A letter Lin Suwen wrote to her daughter, never sent. Full text:

> "Dear Duoduo:
> Mom is trapped in a place. Not a cave—a hotel. There's a fireplace, a warm bed, and snow that never stops. But Mom is not afraid—because it's safe here. The only thing Mom fears is that you don't know Mom loves you. That morning before I left we quarreled. You said 'You never cared about me'—that was the wrongest thing you ever said in your life. Mom cares about you. So much that in this blizzard, the only thing Mom thinks about is that next Wednesday is your birthday. I'm sorry Mom can't come back. If one day someone brings you this letter—please remember: Mom died to save someone. That matters more than any archaeological discovery."

**Fragment 7: The Keeper's True Name (Conversation / bar / Special / Loop 8+)**
- The hotel keeper is not really a hotel keeper. He is the mountain itself—or rather, the mountain's apology to all the dead. If you ask him directly "Who are you, really?", he falls silent. But after the 8th+ loop, if you have already collected at least 4 of Fragments 1-6—he will answer: "What my name is doesn't matter. What matters—is that woman called Lin Suwen. She should not be here. You should help her." Then he gives you the key to Room 204 (before this, the door only opened from 23:00-23:05).

**Fragment 8: Delivering the Letter (Action / triggered / Loop 9+)**
- At the end of this loop, you make a choice: whether to "anchor" that letter. Anchoring the letter costs **3 Anchors** (full-reproduction-level anchoring). If you anchor it—when you wake in the next loop, the letter is clutched in your hand. The paper is warm. The ink is not yet dry. Then you realize: this letter is not for you to read. You must leave this hotel. You must find Lin Suwen's daughter. And when the loop ends—you find the hotel door, for the first time, opening outward.

## Five Key NPCs

**Lin Suwen "The Waiting Mother" (42):** Emotion baseline 3/10 (peaceful within sorrow), sensitivity "Extremely High"—she is the Anchor character. Every night she watches the window in Room 204. If you don't speak to her, she won't open her mouth on her own. If you do, she will ask you: "Have you seen a girl called Duoduo?"

**The Hotel Keeper "The Mountain" (appears 60, actually ∞):** Emotion baseline 5/10 (eternal calm), sensitivity N/A (he is not human—he will not degrade or shift). At the start of every loop he says the same thing: "Awake? The blizzard will last a while yet. The rooms are upstairs." He will not shift, will not have Déjà Vu throughout the scenario—he is the only unchanging Anchor.

**The Old Hunter (Room 201):** Emotion baseline 4/10 (peaceful sorrow). He does not speak—his throat was damaged when he froze to death. He only nods, shakes his head, or points at something. His hunting rifle points at a map—marking where Lin Suwen fell off the cliff. If the player can understand what he is pointing at: gain an extra 1 Cognition Point (CP).

**The Young Climber (Room 203):** Emotion baseline 2/10 (regret). Every night he writes a letter. If you help him finish the ending before he forgets, he will smile before he vanishes—and at the start of the next loop, an extra key appears on his door, which can open Room 204 (an alternative way to obtain the key from Fragment 7). This is the reward for "completing for others what was left undone."

**The Trucker (Room 205):** Emotion baseline 6/10 (stubborn optimism). He believes he can find a way out. Every loop he rushes out of the hotel with his map—then is scared back by the blizzard at the door. If you tell him "this road was abandoned ten years ago," he falls silent, then hands you a slip of paper: "Lin Suwen. She was a passenger ten years ago. I drove her. Where she was going was at that cliff. The coordinates are—"

## Recommended NPC Companion

**Candidate Companion: The Young Climber (Room 203)—** Attributes: Physique 3 / Alertness 2 / Will 2 / Affinity 1. He can become a companion after the 3rd+ loop (after you help him finish his letter). He is young and action-oriented; Physique 3 is high among companions—suited for combat and action support. The cost: he has a deep fear of "leaving home"; if the player mentions "going home," he falls into dejection—all checks this loop suffer Disadvantage.

## Degradation Roadmap (Solo Only)

| Loop | Degradation Stage | Key Event |
|:--:|:--:|------|
| 1-2 | Mirror | Normal hotel. The player explores the lobby, guest rooms, and meets the NPCs. Fragments 1+2 can be obtained. |
| 3-4 | Mirror→Ripple | Room 203's door begins to open on its own (the climber's half-written letter). The stories in Fragment 3 begin to "play" on loop—the NPCs' repeated behaviors become obvious. |
| 5-6 | Ripple | The fireplace no longer crackles—it becomes a soundless flame. The weeping from Room 204 is clearer than before—then suddenly stops (Lin Suwen nearly remembers something at that moment). Fragments 4+5+6 can be obtained. |
| 7-8 | Crack | Faces appear in the blizzard—flickering past the window. They are all the faces of those who died in these mountains ten years ago. The Keeper says something different for the first time: "Today? What day is it?" Fragment 7 can be obtained. |
| 9-10 | Fracture | Room 204's door no longer locks automatically. Lin Suwen begins to speak complete sentences—but she is not speaking of now. She is speaking—but the content is what she said to her daughter at the moment she fell off the cliff ten years ago. The trigger condition for Fragment 8 is met. |
| 11-12 | Fracture→Collapse | The hotel walls begin to turn transparent—you can see the mountain in the blizzard outside the corridor. But it is not the mountain of now. It is the mountain of ten years ago—no abandoned roads, no collapsed bridges. |
| 13-15 | Collapse | The final loop. All the hotel room doors open simultaneously. All the "guests" stand at the doorways—not waiting for you, but waiting for Lin Suwen. They know who she is. They are waiting for her to make a choice. |

## Three Endings

**A - The Letter Is Delivered (Perfect · Anchored Ending)**
Trigger: All of Fragments 1-7 obtained + Fragment 8 anchored (spend 3 Anchors to anchor the letter) + persuade Lin Suwen to accept the truth.
Narrative: When you wake in the next loop, the letter is in your hand—warm. The hotel door opens. Beyond it is not the blizzard—but a mountain path, sunny. You know where you're going: the town at the foot of the mountain, to find someone called "Duoduo." The hotel vanishes behind you—but it is not erased. It simply no longer needs the loop. The woman called Lin Suwen has finally waited for the one she was waiting for. Though that person is not her daughter—but it is enough.

**B - You Told Her (Normal · Unanchored Ending)**
Trigger: Fragments 1-6 obtained + tell Lin Suwen the truth but do not anchor the letter.
Narrative: After hearing the truth, Lin Suwen is silent for a long time. Then she smiles. "Tell him—or her. I don't need to send that letter. I know what it says." She walks toward the window. The blizzard stops. She pushes the window open—beyond the window is not the mountain. It is her daughter's room. She walks in. The window closes. The loop stops.

**C - Left in the Blizzard (Bad Ending)**
Trigger: Loops exhausted (15 times) without letting Lin Suwen face the truth.
Narrative: The hotel no longer resets. The blizzard stops—but all the guest rooms are empty. In Lin Suwen's room only a letter remains, pressed under the pillow. The letter has yellowed—as if placed there ten years. You pick up the letter. Walk out of the hotel. There are no footprints on the mountain path—only your own. You must walk to the foot of the mountain. You must find the one called Duoduo. But you don't know how old she is now. You don't even know if she is still waiting.
