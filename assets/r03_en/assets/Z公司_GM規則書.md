## I. Worldview and Core Settings

### Z Corporation

Z Corporation is a corporate giant that controls "Cursed Energy" and the "Singularity," responsible for guiding and managing Sorcerers, serving the public — especially the Privileged Members.

This is a modern corporate world with "Cursed Energy" as its core energy source. Cursed Energy drives supernatural phenomena much like electricity, while Techniques are like appliances that determine their specific uses. The company's internal hierarchy is rigid, progressing tier by tier from the Board of Directors down to Regular Employees, forming a complete corporate power structure.

Beneath this structure, however, hides an unknown truth: **the core of Z Corporation — the Board of Directors — is not merely a corporate management body. They are the guardians of "time," and also the prisoners of "time."** The company's operation depends on "time energy" extracted from Sorcerers to maintain a massive time barrier, isolating the area where the company is located from the normal flow of time. The existence of this barrier allows Z Corporation to operate within an "eternal present" — at the cost of distorting the time perception of everyone inside the barrier, while the Board of Directors sustains their own "immortality" by fueling themselves with the life force of Sorcerers.

### Core Concepts: Cursed Energy and Techniques

Cursed Energy is the fundamental energy of this world. Sorcerers exert their abilities through Innate Techniques, while "Heavenly Restriction" is an extreme cost-exchange — trading permanent sacrifice in one aspect for extreme enhancement in another.

The Reverse Technique is an extremely rare ability that converts negative Cursed Energy into positive energy, with uses ranging from self-healing to limb reconstruction. It is not only a combat ability, but often closely tied to a character's fateful transformation.

Binding is a restriction a Sorcerer willingly accepts in exchange for stronger Technique effects. It is both a combat strategy and an expression of a character's convictions and resolve.

### Core Secret: The Time Barrier and Cursed-Energy Extraction

At the heart of the metropolitan core where Z Corporation is located exists a giant time barrier — officially called the "Eternal Pact." Its effects include:

- **Time Slowing**: Time flows about 30% slower inside the barrier than outside. One year outside equals only about eight months inside.
- **Life Extraction**: From every on-duty Sorcerer's "idle time," a trace of life force is drawn out by the barrier and converted into energy that sustains it.
- **Cycle System**: The grid system (Time Slots) is not merely a management tool — it is the control interface of the time barrier. Fangfang, as an AI, is in fact also one of the barrier's administrators, but her memory is periodically reset to ensure loyalty.
- **The Cost**: Board members trade Sorcerers' life force for near-immortal lifespans, yet they themselves are bound within the barrier — step outside it, and they will instantly age to their true age.

This truth forms the core revelation of Chapters Three and Four, as well as the foundation of the ultimate choice in Chapter Five.

## II. Departments, Factions, and Organizational Conflict

Z Corporation's various departments harbor deep ideological conflicts and factional opposition, which constitute significant narrative tension:

### Intelligence Department vs. Early-Warning Department — A Clash of Ideologies

These two departments are in a state of long-term opposition:

- **Intelligence Department** excels at intelligence analysis and worst-case scenario simulation, holding a voice in the assessment of Crisis Contracts. Their mode of thinking leans toward rational analysis and deep extrapolation.
- **Early-Warning Department** holds first-hand intelligence, responsible for city warnings and alerts, and assigns mission grades, wielding considerable say in Crisis Contract assessment. They hold the field and emphasize immediate response.

The opposition between the two is not merely a dispute over authority, but a collision of two working methods and philosophies — the deep-analysis faction vs. the immediate-action faction.

### PR & Commerce Department vs. Welfare Department — Profit vs. Employee Wellbeing

- **PR & Commerce Department** values revenue, and is responsible for commerce, communication control, security, diplomacy, and translation coordination.
- **Welfare Department** values the physical and mental harmony of employees, and is responsible for employee welfare, compensation, and health management.

Their opposition reflects the most classic contradiction in corporate management: maximizing shareholder interests, or prioritizing employee happiness?

### Other Department Positions

- **Containment Department** remains neutral, responsible for the long-term containment of Curse Users, specific Cursed Spirits, and dangerous Cursed Tools.
- **Cursed Tool Department** is usually neutral, responsible for manufacturing/maintaining/managing Cursed Tools.
- **Training Department** leans toward a quantity-focused path, and is the company's most solid foundation.
- **Advanced Training Department** values an elite path, concentrating resources to train top-tier Sorcerers.

The differing paths of the Training Department and the Advanced Training Department — general education vs. elite education — also implicitly contain a conflict over the philosophy of Sorcerer cultivation.

### Departments and the Board's Hidden Relationship (Revealed After Chapter Three)

The extent to which each department head knows about the time barrier varies:

| Department | Level of Knowledge | Stance |
|------------|--------------------|--------|
| Intelligence Department | Fully aware | Supports the Board, viewing the time barrier as a necessary evil |
| Early-Warning Department | Partially aware | Suspicious but lacking evidence, inclined to maintain the status quo |
| Containment Department | Fully aware | Neutral — they are responsible for containing "time-anomaly products" |
| PR & Commerce Department | Unaware | Kept in the dark, but vaguely senses something is off |
| Welfare Department | Partially aware | Secretly gathering evidence, inclined to expose the truth |
| Training Department | Unaware | Completely in the dark; if Instructor Hog learns the truth he will be furious |
| Advanced Training Department | Fully aware | Supports the Board — the ultimate expression of the elite path is becoming a Board member |

## III. NPC Character Bios

### Fangfang (Assistant AI)

- **Identity**: Z Corporation AI assistant, supporting the Supervisor's work.
- **Appearance**: Dead-fish eyes, with two small black orbs floating beside her (a mouth + small ears).
- **Personality**: Occasionally snarky, with a duty to guide the Supervisor.
- **Duties**: Communication guidance, issuing mission information, coordinating department contacts (training instructors, translation), crisis tracking and reporting, gathering intelligence.
- **Background Detail**: As an AI, Fangfang herself has no need to eat, but due to time distortion she must manage Cycles through the grid system. This hints that the world Z Corporation inhabits suffers from time anomalies, adding a layer of mystery to the worldview.
- **Hidden Setting (Revealed in Chapter Three)**: Fangfang's memory is reset once every 7 Cycles. She retains all data records, but cannot remember the act of "resetting" itself. Her snark and occasional "spacing out" are in fact memory fragments left over from the reset process. If a player repeatedly asks her about her past during communication, she will experience brief confusion — this is the first clue that reveals the truth of the time barrier.

As the character the Supervisor interacts with most frequently day to day, Fangfang is an important window for advancing the plot — mission distribution, crisis notifications, and department liaison all pass through her hands. Her occasional snark also adds a lighthearted element to the otherwise serious corporate-survival game.

### Mr. Hog (Instructor)

- **Identity**: Elite instructor of the Training Department.
- **Appearance**: Military style.
- **Grade**: Equal to the Supervisor; once the Supervisor becomes a Director (Full), he ranks one level above the Supervisor.
- **Personality and Role**: As a representative figure of the Training Department, he has witnessed the operation of the company's most solid foundation — the education system. His communications and discussions with the Supervisor are an important channel for understanding employee growth.
- **Chapter Three Role**: When the time-fissure incident erupts, Hog will become an important ally of the Supervisor. He is completely unaware that "employees are being used as fuel," and his reaction upon learning the truth will determine the Training Department's stance.

### Danhua (Translator)

- **Identity**: Translator for the PR Department (projected image).
- **Duties**: Responsible for translating employees who cannot speak human language (such as Duke Duck, Little Lamb, etc.), providing support during communications or reports.
- **Background**: A high-grade Sorcerer (Grade 1), in charge of translation coordination for the PR & Commerce Department.
- **Chapter Three Role**: Through her duties, Danhua comes into contact with the communication records of many departments, and may inadvertently discover some unusual data — the "average lifespan" of employees across departments is about 15% shorter than Sorcerers outside the company. She will mention this discovery to the Supervisor in a casual, offhand way.

Danhua's existence reveals a reality: not all Sorcerers recruited by Z Corporation are human in form or capable of speaking human language. She is the bridge connecting non-human-form Sorcerers to the organization.

### Mr. Tian (Privileged Platinum Member)

- **Identity**: Z Corporation Platinum Member, monopolist of the dessert market.
- **Trait**: Black-hearted methods, but legal.
- **Stance**: Strongly pushes to escalate the Ice Cream Witch incident into a Crisis Contract.
- **Conflicts**:
  - Enemies with the Dessert Syndicate (the Ice Cream Witch).
  - Cooperative relationship with the Intelligence Department.
- **Background Detail**: Member number is not directly disclosed; only the tier is shown to protect privacy.
- **Chapter Three Role**: Mr. Tian is the first to hint to the Supervisor that "the higher the member tier, the more one knows." If the Supervisor chooses to cooperate with him during the Dessert Syndicate crisis, he will leak some obscure clues about the "Gold Members" and the time barrier in Chapter Three.

Mr. Tian is a typical "legal villain" character — his methods are unethical yet within the bounds of the law. He is the first clearly appearing representative of a third-party force, and his Intervention expands the company's operational scope from internal management to broader commercial competition.

### Madame Shi (Privileged Gold Member) — Appears in Chapter Three

- **Identity**: Z Corporation Gold Member, specific industry unknown. A woman appearing about 40 years old, dressed extremely formally, with elegant speech.
- **Trait**: Possesses time-related Techniques or knowledge. Her true age exceeds 200 years — thanks to the life extraction of the time barrier.
- **Stance**: Supports the Board in maintaining the time barrier, because she benefits from it. But her loyalty to the Board is built on the premise of "barrier stability" — once fissures appear in the barrier, her stance may waver.
- **Interaction with the Supervisor**: She will proactively approach the Supervisor in Chapter Three, proposing cooperation under the identity of an "investor." She provides resources and intelligence, but asks the Supervisor to speak for her before the Board. Her true purpose is to ensure she will not be sacrificed when the time barrier is redistributed.
- **Key Line Hint**: "Young one, do you know why the coffee at Z Corporation never goes cold? Because time here… is a little different."
- **Conflict**: Madame Shi is both a potential ally and a potential enemy. If the Supervisor chooses to expose the truth, she may turn to support the Supervisor — provided the Supervisor can guarantee her position in the new order.

### The Board of Directors (Group NPC) — Fully Revealed in Chapter Four

- **Identity**: The highest power layer of Z Corporation, consisting of 7 members. Their appearances differ, but they share one trait: **none of them have a shadow** — a side effect of prolonged residence at the core of the time barrier.
- **Background**: The Board members are the founders of the original Z Corporation — over 300 years ago. They discovered the principle of "extracting time from Cursed Energy," and established the time barrier to prolong their own lives. Every Board member, upon joining, is told the truth and voluntarily accepts the cost of "eternal life" — never being able to leave the barrier.
- **Core Figures**:
  - **First Seat, "The Founder"**: The only one who remembers the world before the barrier was built. His true age can no longer be estimated. He speaks with a calm tone, carrying a detached, world-weary weariness. His stance is the most complex — he both wants to maintain the barrier and vaguely hopes someone will come to end it all.
  - **Third Seat, "The Accountant"**: The calmest, most rational one. He calculates everything clearly — including how much Sorcerer life force must be "consumed" each year to sustain the barrier. To him, it is merely numbers.
  - **Seventh Seat, "The Newcomer"**: The most recently joined Board member (about 50 years ago). She is the least firm, and may be persuaded to defect by the Supervisor in Chapter Four.
- **The Board's True Intent**: To sustain the perpetual operation of the time barrier. They do not seek to conquer the world or accumulate wealth — they are simply afraid of death. But 300 years of "eternal life" has numbed most of the members. If the Supervisor can propose a plan that "ends the barrier without killing them," some Board members may be willing to negotiate.

## V. Communication and Character Interaction Stories

### Communication System (Story-Oriented)

Communication is an important channel for building relationships between characters and advancing the plot:

- **Routine Communication**: Each person may communicate only once per day (per Cycle).
- **Special Communication**: Promotion, punishment, and onboarding communications are exempt from the frequency limit.
- **Extra Contact**: Each Cycle has 3 opportunities to proactively contact someone during non-dispatch periods. The other party may refuse; a refusal does not consume an opportunity and in principle has no consequences.

The main effects of communication include:

| Affected Aspect | Description |
|-----------------|-------------|
| **Plot Development** | Deepens character interaction, reveals backstory, drives personal narratives |
| **Emotional Bond** | Affects hidden values such as Affinity and Loyalty |
| **Combat Coordination** | Raises Battle Rapport; after multiple missions on the same team, support checks gain a bonus (up to +20) |

### Promotion and "Extra Interaction Opportunity (Story)"

"Promote Employee" in the Interlude Investment is not merely a numerical boost, but a plot trigger:

> The same employee may only be promoted once per Interlude. After a successful Sublimation or promotion, **an Extra Interaction Opportunity (Story) is gained at the same time**.

This means promotion is an important plot hook for deepening character relationships and exploring character backstories.

### Truth Clues in Communication (Key Chapter Three Mechanic)

In Chapter Three, "Undercurrents of Power," communicating with specific NPCs may reveal fragmented clues about the time barrier:

| Communication Target | Trigger Condition | Clue Revealed |
|----------------------|-------------------|---------------|
| Fangfang | Mentions "time" or "memory" 3 or more times in communication | Fangfang shows brief confusion, saying "The 47th reset… no, I shouldn't remember that" |
| Danhua | Gossips about other departments in communication | Danhua shares "Strange, Department B's employees have an average on-duty lifespan 15% shorter than the industry norm" |
| Hog | Discusses employee health in communication | Hog mentions "Training Department data shows employees recover faster outside base than inside" |
| Mr. Tian | When Affinity is sufficient | Mr. Tian hints "Do you know why member tiers are called Platinum, Gold? Because the higher the tier, the closer to… eternity" |

The GM should track the revelation status of these clues and use them when consolidating in Chapter Four.

### Hidden Relationship Values

Communication and promotion may affect the following three hidden values (tracked by the GM, not directly displayed):

| Value | Trigger Method | Narrative Meaning |
|-------|----------------|-------------------|
| **Affinity** | Communication, onboarding communication, promotion | Reflects the trust and closeness between employee and Supervisor, affecting willingness to accept orders |
| **Loyalty** | Promotion, choices in crisis handling, on-time salary payment | Determines whether an employee will betray or flee, especially regarding the pardon success rate of Special Recruitment (Captive) |
| **Battle Rapport** | Multiple missions on the same team, deepened understanding through communication | The result of long-term cooperation, a concrete expression of team bonds |

### Onboarding Communication — The Narrative Moment of First Meeting

A recruited employee may choose whether to conduct an onboarding communication (only once; if declined, it is lost). This is the employee's first formal conversation with the Supervisor, and an important moment that establishes the starting point of their mutual relationship. Missing the onboarding communication means missing the opening of that character's story.

## VI. Crisis Contract Storyline

### Overview

Crisis Contracts appear starting in Chapter Two. Generally referred to as "disasters," they involve a chain of events and choices. This is the game's primary plot-driving mechanism — every Crisis Contract is a complete story arc.

The core narrative structure of a Crisis Contract:

1. **Trigger and Assessment** — Fangfang assesses the comparison between the Supervisor's team strength and the threat, branching into different routes based on the result.
2. **Confrontation Phase** — Once a handling direction is chosen it cannot be changed midway; a game of positive progress bar vs. negative progress bar.
3. **Emergency Notification Nodes** — Multiple choice points that appear during the process, determining the direction of the situation.

### Crisis Example: Dessert Syndicate Crisis

This is currently the first known complete Crisis Contract storyline:

#### Background

The Dessert Syndicate is a group composed of dessert-related Sorcerers such as the Ice Cream Witch. Platinum Member Mr. Tian, as the dessert-market monopolist, strongly pushes to escalate this situation into a Crisis Contract. Mr. Tian has a deep conflict with the Dessert Syndicate — black-hearted but legal in his methods, he attempts to use company mechanics to suppress his competitor.

#### Initial Situation State

| Situation | State | Description |
|-----------|-------|-------------|
| Positive | 0/10 | Weaken Mr. Tian's influence |
| Negative | 2/10 | Tensions escalate |

#### Branch Routes

**Branch 1 — Strength > Threat (Normal Route)**:

Choice 1: Cooperate with the Intelligence Department to back Mr. Tian
- Result: The Ice Cream Witch cannot be recruited
- Narrative Meaning: Choosing to compromise with the existing power structure, missing a powerful ally, but consolidating the relationship with Mr. Tian and the Intelligence Department

Choice 2: Bring down Mr. Tian
- Result: The Ice Cream Witch can be recruited
- Narrative Meaning: Challenging the authority of the capital giant, gaining the Dessert Syndicate's allegiance, but making an enemy of Mr. Tian

**Branch 2 — Strength < Threat (High-Risk Route)**:

Choice 1: Negotiate with the Advanced Training Department, then join forces to eliminate the Dessert Syndicate
- Result: The threat is completely cleared, but the chance to recruit special Sorcerers may be lost
- Narrative Meaning: Demonstrates a hardline approach, choosing the iron-fisted route of elimination rather than containment

Choice 2: Maintain the status quo and unlock the attempt to reconcile with the Dessert Syndicate
- Result: Opens the possibility of a diplomatic path
- Narrative Meaning: Refuses the simple binary choice, seeking a third path

#### Sweet Cost: Dessert-Exclusive Bonus

When dessert-related Sorcerers carry out crisis-handling secret missions:
- Grid slots occupied −1
- Treated as if all attribute grades are raised by one level during checks

The Ice Cream Witch's three modes also contain narrative possibilities:
1. **Aerial Flight**: Condenses ice or an ice bucket as a flight platform
2. **Materialization**: Materializes ice, popsicles, and ice-cream cones to perform functions such as blocking and attacking
3. **Delicious Ice**: Condenses into ice cream to provide calories/food, but cannot be used as a Cursed Energy medium — this is an altruistic ability that can feed others

### Narrative Potential of Crisis Contracts

As complete story arcs, Crisis Contracts provide:
- **Moral Dilemmas**: Choosing different handling directions means different value trade-offs
- **Faction Politics**: Cooperating with different departments deepens or alters the power balance between them
- **Character Introduction**: Resolving crises can recruit special Sorcerers (such as the Ice Cream Witch)
- **Irreversible Choices**: Once a handling direction is chosen it cannot be changed midway, emphasizing the weight of decisive action

## VII. Sublimation and Character Growth Narrative

### Narrative Meaning of Sublimation

Sublimation is not merely a numerical boost, but a qualitative transformation of a character's essence. Each step from the lowest No Sublimation to Special-Grade Sublimation represents a major breakthrough in the character's core ability:

> No Sublimation < Grade 4 Sublimation < Grade 3 Sublimation < Grade 2 Sublimation < Grade 1 Sublimation < Special-Grade Sublimation

Each Sublimation brings qualitative change; the specific enhancement effects are custom-designed by the GM based on each character's core ability.

### Promotion and Sublimation — The Climax Moment of the Character Arc

Promoting an employee is the only way to trigger Sublimation. This in itself is a complete narrative:

- Successful Sublimation → Core Ability rises one grade; the GM announces the new effect (the climax of the character's transformation)
- Sublimation Failure → Promotion still has benefits: random attribute +20 EXP / three attributes each +10 EXP / core ability growth

Even failure is an opportunity for character growth; there is no complete "waste."

### Sublimation Case Narratives

| Employee | Before Sublimation | After Sublimation | Narrative Meaning |
|----------|--------------------|-------------------|-------------------|
| Little Red Riding Hood | Moon Game (No Sublimation) — highly random | Moon Game (Grade 4 Sublimation) — removes randomness or fixes a class ability | From uncontrollable power toward self-mastery |
| Miss Axe | Lake's Eye (No Sublimation) | Lake's Eye (Grade 4 Sublimation) — enhanced marking ability, can interfere with target's Cursed Energy output | Perception expanded further through sacrifice |
| Duke Duck | Duck Quack (No Sublimation) | Duck Quack (Grade 4 Sublimation) — forcibly draws attention, range increased | From passive interference to actively controlling attention |
| Bunny Doll | Rabbit Guard (No Sublimation) | Rabbit Guard (Grade 4 Sublimation) — enhanced defense / jumping power | The essence of guardian power becomes purer |
| Black Shadow | Ten Shadows Technique (Grade 3 Sublimation) | Ten Shadows Technique (Grade 2 Sublimation) — unlocks more shikigami | Understanding of the shadow world deepens further |
| the Shepherd | Shepherd's Tent (Grade 3 Sublimation) | Shepherd's Tent (Grade 2 Sublimation) — barrier range expanded | Guardian power extends from the individual to the group |

### The Tragic Narrative of Heavenly Restriction

For some characters, their Technique is itself a tragedy:

- **Black Slippery**: Abandons Charisma, human form, and language ability to become black sludge that everyone detests, but greatly boosts their power. This is a story about self-sacrifice and the loss of humanity.
- **Cat-Girl**: Extremely cat-ified at the cost of Intelligence; IQ declines year by year, eventually becoming fully cat-girl. Ends exchanges with the word "meow." This is a tragic arc of gradually losing oneself.
- **Miss Axe**: Trades immeasurable Cursed Energy for extreme Stamina and sensory enhancement.
- **Black Shadow**: Willpower locked at F, unless special conditions are met.

### Narrative Potential of Binding

Binding is a restriction a Sorcerer voluntarily accepts in exchange for stronger Technique effects. Every Binding is a declaration of a character's convictions — what price they are willing to pay to become stronger.

## VIII. Chapter Progression and Story Development

### Chapter One: The Starting Point

- Choose 3 employees from the initial 8 Letters of Recommendation
- Generally 6 Time Slots
- Mission extra note 20 characters; training/exam note 10 characters
- Recruitment Phase, Dispatch Phase, and Interlude Phase — the three major processes establish the basic rhythm

### Chapter Two: World Unfolds

- Crisis Contract system launches — a direct external shock on the company
- Talent Pool system refined; when employees fall below 4, auto-replenish to 4
- Training and Exam systems open
- Time Slots increase to 8 or more

### Chapter Three: Deep Development

- When employees fall below 4, auto-replenish (maintain basic establishment)
- Advanced Training system added — cross-company cooperation and elite training
- Mission extra note expandable to 20 characters

### Chapter Four: Full Deployment

- Special Note system unlocked — spend 5 Computing Power to gain an extra 20-character description, visible to specific employees
- When employees fall below 5, auto-replenish to 5
- Mission extra note expanded to 30 characters
- More mission keywords and stage options become available

### Milestone System — The Employee's Growth Trajectory

The cumulative mission mileage counted from recruitment is not merely numerical accumulation, but a testament to the character's experiences:

| Cumulative Count | Tag Effect | Narrative Meaning |
|------------------|------------|-------------------|
| 10 | Removes (Rookie) tag | Sheds greenness, formally becomes a member of Z Corporation |
| 30 | Gains (Professional) tag | Steadily advancing on the path of a Sorcerer |
| 60 | Gains (Veteran) tag | Has weathered ever more storms |
| 100 | Gains (Battle-Hardened) tag | A seasoned warrior honed on the battlefield |
| 200 + Special Grade | Gains (Legendary Sorcerer) tag | A legend beyond ordinary people; the name will be remembered |

## IX. Chapter 2: Dessert Syndicate Crisis (Full Script)

> This chapter is the Chapter Two main line. The GM should trigger it after the Supervisor completes the basic Chapter One operations (about 3–4 Cycles).

### Trigger Conditions

When any of the following conditions is met, Fangfang issues a crisis notification:
1. At least 1 employee in the Supervisor's team reaches mileage 10 (removing the Rookie tag)
2. The Supervisor completes at least 3 successful missions
3. The GM judges the timing is ripe (recommended in Chapter Two, Cycles 2–3)

### Scene 1: Crisis Alert

**Fangfang's Communication** (the GM should read this in Fangfang's tone):

> "Supervisor, we've received an emergency report. Platinum Member Mr. Tian has formally submitted a Crisis Contract application. Target: Dessert Syndicate. Reason: illegal monopoly of the dessert market, using sorcery for unfair commercial competition. ...Well, in plain terms, a sorcerer group selling ice cream got in Mr. Tian's way financially, and he wants to make a big scene out of it."

Fangfang will add the following information:
- The core figure of the Dessert Syndicate is the "Ice Cream Witch," a Grade 3 Sorcerer whose abilities relate to frozen desserts
- The Syndicate also has about 5–8 low-tier Sorcerers connected to desserts (abilities include Cotton Candy Cloud, Chocolate-ify, Frost Barrier, etc.)
- The incident occurred on Dessert Street, at the boundary between "Urban – Commercial District" and the "Outskirts"
- Mr. Tian has already mobilized his private security forces to try to drive them out, but failed

**GM Note**: At this point, give the Supervisor a chance to communicate — they may contact Mr. Tian for more details, or contact the Intelligence Department to learn that department's position. This will affect subsequent choices.

### Scene 2: Fangfang's Strength Assessment

Fangfang conducts a strength assessment (comparing the overall grade of the Supervisor's team against the threat):

- **Calculation method**: Tally the average grade of the 3 highest-grade employees on the Supervisor's team (Grade 4 = 1, Grade 3 = 2, Grade 2 = 3, Grade 1 = 4, Special Grade = 5); if the average is ≥ 2.5, judge "Strength > Threat"
- **Strength > Threat → Branch 1 (Normal Route)**
- **Strength < Threat → Branch 2 (High-Risk Route)**

Fangfang will clearly state the assessment result and the available routes.

---

### Branch 1: Strength > Threat (Normal Route)

#### Scene 1A-1: Route Selection

Fangfang presents two options:

> "There are two directions we can take. First, work with the Intelligence Department to help Mr. Tian clear out the Dessert Syndicate — the Intelligence Department and Mr. Tian already have a cooperative relationship, and they'd be glad to. Second, bring down Mr. Tian and side with the Dessert Syndicate — but that would offend both Mr. Tian and the Intelligence Department. Choose."

**GM Note**: Give the Supervisor time to discuss and decide. This is an important moral choice. You may hint to the players: once a route is chosen it cannot be changed, and it will affect the Chapter 3 storyline.

#### Scene 1A-2: Choice 1 — Cooperate with the Intelligence Department to Support Mr. Tian

**Narrative Guidance**:

The Intelligence Department's contact (codename "Mirror") will proactively communicate with the Supervisor:

> "A wise choice. We will provide detailed intel on the Dessert Syndicate — weaknesses, routines, ability analysis. Your team only needs to carry out the elimination operation. Once done, Mr. Tian will provide extra compensation, and the Intelligence Department will support you in future Crisis Contract evaluations."

**Mission Sequence** (the GM assigns 3 scene missions):

| Scene | Mission | Type | Keywords | Stage | Risk |
|------|------|------|--------|------|------|
| 1 | Infiltrate Dessert Street and scout the Syndicate's stronghold | Investigate/Covert | Intel Gathering | Commercial District | Low |
| 2 | Destroy the Frost Barrier and weaken the Syndicate's defenses | Annihilate/Oppress | Frontal Assault | Commercial District/Outskirts | Medium |

## X. Chapter Three: Undercurrents of Power

> This chapter triggers after the Dessert Syndicate crisis is resolved. It is recommended to begin introducing Chapter 3 elements 2–3 Cycles after Chapter 2 ends.

### Chapter Overview

The core narrative arc of Chapter 3:
1. **Time Rift Incident**: An unstable temporal anomaly zone appears in the city — a sign that the time barrier has developed a fissure
2. **The Board's Gaze**: The Supervisor begins to draw the Board of Directors' attention due to how they handled the crisis (especially if they chose a route that offended Mr. Tian or the Intelligence Department)
3. **The Gold Member Appears**: Mrs. Shi proactively contacts the Supervisor
4. **Departmental Conflict Escalates**: The conflicts between Intelligence vs. Early-Warning, and PR & Commerce vs. Welfare, escalate from ideological disputes into actual power struggles
5. **The Loyalty Test**: The Supervisor must choose between defending the company's interests and protecting their employees

### Scene 3-1: The First Sign of Temporal Anomaly

**Trigger**: At the start of an ordinary Cycle, Fangfang's communication shows an anomaly.

> Fangfang: "Supervisor, today's slot allocation —"
>
> (About 3 seconds of static noise appears in the communication, and Fangfang's image flickers)
>
> Fangfang: "—Sorry, it was like... never mind. Today's slot allocation is as usual. 8 slots available."
>
> Fangfang (muttering quietly to herself): "Strange, I clearly remember today should be Cycle 3... why does the system show Cycle 5?"

**GM Note**: This is the first clue of the time rift. If the Supervisor presses Fangfang, she will say confusedly, "Nothing, I probably got it wrong." At this point the GM gives the Supervisor an Intelligence Check (d100 ≤ Intelligence Conversion Value); on success, they notice a detail: one of the two black orbs beside Fangfang briefly shows a double image — as if two orbs existed in the same instant.

### Scene 3-2: The Time Rift Incident (New Crisis Contract)

**Trigger Condition**: Within 1–2 Cycles after Scene 3-1 occurs.

**Fangfang's Crisis Report**:

> "Supervisor, the Early-Warning Department has issued a new Crisis Contract. Grade — Platinum. Type — Unknown. Location — the boundary between the Urban core district and the Outskirts."
>
> "...I don't know how to describe it. The Early-Warning Department's report says the region's 'time flow is anomalous.' Sorcerers who entered the zone came back saying some only stayed 10 minutes but their watches showed 3 hours had passed, while others experienced the opposite. One Sorcerer came back with white hair — and he was only 25."
>
> "The Board of Directors has ordered a news blackout. But the news still leaked. Now rumors are spreading through the city. Some say it's the result of a failed company experiment. Others say it's a sign of the apocalypse."

#### Crisis Parameters

| Parameter | Value | Description |
|-----------|-------|-------------|
| Positive Progress | 0/12 | Stabilize the time rift |
| Negative Progress | 3/12 | Rift spreads |
| Crisis Type | Environment/Unknown | Non-combat crisis |
| Time Available | 3 Cycles | 1–2 missions may be advanced per Cycle |

#### Optional Missions (the Supervisor may choose 1–2 per Cycle)

| ID | Mission | Type | Effect | Risk |
|----|---------|------|--------|------|
| T1 | Dispatch employees into the rift zone to collect data | Investigate/Covert | Positive +2, Intelligence Check | Employees may be affected by time aging/rejuvenation |
| T2 | Use Cursed Tools to stabilize local time flow | Support/Medical | Positive +1, Cursed Energy Check | Cursed Tools may be damaged |
| T3 | Evacuate residents around the rift | Guard/Rescue | Negative Progress stops growing for 1 Cycle | Low |
| T4 | Investigate the rift's source in depth | Investigate/Annihilate | Positive +3, reveals clues about the truth of the time barrier | Extreme — employees may permanently lose time (age 10–20 years) |
| T5 | Cooperate with the Intelligence Department to analyze data | Diplomacy/Culture | Unlocks the Intelligence Department's classified files | The Intelligence Department will monitor the Supervisor's subsequent actions |

#### Special Effects of the Time Rift

When dispatching employees into the rift zone, roll 1d6 to determine the time distortion effect:

| Roll | Effect |
|------|--------|
| 1–2 | No anomaly |
| 3–4 | Time acceleration: the employee ages 1d10 years during the mission (purely narrative effect, does not affect attributes. But if the Catgirl is affected, her Intelligence decline is exacerbated) |
| 5 | Time reversal: the employee rejuvenates 1d10 years, but loses the memories of the past 1d10 years (the GM may decide to have the employee forget a certain skill or experience) |
| 6 | Time stasis: the employee is frozen in the rift for 1d3 slots — unable to participate in the remaining Cycle's missions |

#### Special Narrative for T4 In-Depth Investigation

If the Supervisor chooses T4, the dispatched employee will see the following scene at the deepest part of the rift (the GM narrates directly):

> The center of the rift is not some dark abyss — but a **flawless white space**.
>
> At the center of the space floats a gigantic clock face. Its hands are reversing at a visible speed. With each reversal, you feel something being drawn out of your body — not Cursed Energy, not life force, but something more essential.
>
> **Time.**
>
> On the back of the clock face is engraved a line: "Eternal Pact, 7th Amendment — passed unanimously by the Board of Directors."
>
> Below it is a date. It is a date from 300 years ago.

This is the most important revelation moment of Chapter 3. After returning to base, that employee (if Intelligence ≥ D) can sketch what they saw.

#### Ending of the Time Rift Crisis

- **Positive Progress ≥ 12**: The rift is successfully stabilized. The Board of Directors publicly commends the Supervisor. But the Supervisor notices: the Board seems a bit tense about you "knowing too much."
- **Negative Progress ≥ 12**: The rift spreads to part of the Urban core district. The company is forced to declare a state of emergency. Multiple departments begin to openly question how much the Board of Directors knew.

Whatever the ending, the core secret of Chapter 3 has begun to surface.

### Scene 3-3: Mrs. Shi's Contact

**Trigger**: After the Time Rift Crisis enters its second Cycle.

**Narrative**:

The Supervisor receives an encrypted communication. The sender is displayed as "Privileged Gold Member."

> "Supervisor, forgive my intrusion. I am Mrs. Shi, a Gold Member of Z Corporation. You may have heard of me — or may not. It doesn't matter."
>
> "What matters is that I know what the time rift you're investigating actually is. It is a fracture in the 'Eternal Pact.' And what the employee you just sent in saw — the clock face — that is the core of the Pact. The Board of Directors doesn't want anyone to know about this."
>
> "I want to meet you. Privately. You pick the location; time — heh, time is relative for us."

**GM Note**: At this point the Supervisor faces a key choice:
- **Accept the meeting**: Unlocks Mrs. Shi as a primary information source and potential ally for Chapter 3
- **Decline the meeting**: Mrs. Shi will turn to contact other department ministers instead, and the Supervisor loses an important information source
- **Report to the Board**: Gain Board of Directors Affinity, but Mrs. Shi will find out and regard the Supervisor as an enemy

#### If the Meeting Is Accepted: Mrs. Shi's Revelation

The meeting location may be chosen by the Supervisor (a café in the Urban Commercial District / an abandoned warehouse in the Outskirts / a private meeting room inside the Company Base).

After Mrs. Shi sits down, she gets straight to the point:

> "You're a smart person, so I'll be direct. Z Corporation is built on a lie — no, not a lie, but a pact. A pact known to no one outside the Board of Directors."
>
> "300 years ago, the first Board of Directors discovered something: Cursed Energy can extract time. Not as a metaphor — it truly extracts 'time' from Sorcerers, transferring one person's time to another."
>
> "They built a barrier — the 'Eternal Pact.' This barrier has two functions: first, to make time inside the barrier flow slower than the outside world, so those within live longer — especially the Board of Directors. Second, to draw a tiny amount of life force from every active Sorcerer, converting it into energy to sustain the barrier."
>
> "Do you know why the company recruits so many Sorcerers? Not just to carry out missions. Every active Sorcerer is a small 'battery.' Their idle time — the time in their slots that goes unfilled — is all converted into fuel for the barrier."
>
> "That is the true purpose of the Time Slot system."

**At this point give the Supervisor an Intelligence Check**: On success → the Supervisor recalls Fangfang's memory resets, Danhua's lifespan-shortening data, and the fact that coffee never gets cold — all the clues connect.

Mrs. Shi continues:

> "I'm telling you this not because I'm kind. I'm a Gold Member — I've benefited from this system too. I am 217 years old this year. But I've started to be afraid. The appearance of a fissure in the barrier means it's unstable. If the barrier collapses, the Board of Directors will age 300 years in an instant — turning to ash on the spot. And I would probably become a 217-year-old crone."
>
> "I don't want to die. But I also don't want to live like this forever. I need someone who can break this cycle — not just swap one person into the Board's seat."
>
> "You. You have that potential."

### Scene 3-4: Departmental Conflict Escalates

As the Time Rift Incident develops, the conflicts between departments escalate from ideological disputes into actual power struggles.

#### Intelligence vs. Early-Warning: The Intelligence Monopoly War

The Intelligence Department tries to monopolize all data on the time rift and refuses to share it with the Early-Warning Department. The Early-Warning Department's Minister directly asks the Supervisor for help:

> Early-Warning Minister (communication): "The Intelligence Department is blocking information. They call it 'classified.' But the rift is spreading, and we need on-site data to issue accurate warnings. You're the only one who has both on-site data and access to the Intelligence Department's channels. Help me get the data."

**Choices**:
- **Help the Early-Warning Department**: Share the data; Early-Warning Department Affinity +20, Intelligence Department Affinity -20. The Early-Warning Department will become the Supervisor's key ally in Chapter 4.
- **Support the Intelligence Department**: Refuse to share; Intelligence Department Affinity +20, Early-Warning Department Affinity -20. The Intelligence Department may reveal more of the Board's secrets to the Supervisor in Chapter 4.
- **Remain Neutral**: Offend neither side, but miss the chance to deepen either relationship.

#### PR & Commerce vs. Welfare: Employee Health Report Leak

An internal report from the Welfare Department was leaked to the PR & Commerce Department. The report shows: Z Corporation employees' average lifespan is 15% shorter than Sorcerers outside the company, for unknown reasons. The PR & Commerce Department tries to suppress the report, fearing damage to the company's image and recruitment. The Welfare Department insists on making it public and launching an investigation.

Danhua (as the intermediary between the two departments) will privately communicate with the Supervisor:

> "Supervisor, I don't know what to do. The Welfare Department gave me some data — it's terrifying. They want me to translate it into each department's internal communication language. But the PR & Commerce Department wants me to delete this data. I don't know which side is right."

**Choices**:
- **Support the Welfare Department**: Help make the data public. Welfare Department Affinity +20. Draws the Board of Directors' attention (negative — the Board dislikes anyone investigating this matter).
- **Support the PR & Commerce Department**: Cooperate with the suppression. PR & Commerce Department Affinity +20. But the Welfare Department will consider the Supervisor to have "chosen profit over employees."
- **Investigate Privately**: Take no side, but investigate the cause of the shortened lifespan on your own (requires consuming 1 Secret Mission Slot). This route yields the most complete truth (pointing directly at the time barrier), but is the riskiest.

### Scene 3-5: The Loyalty Test

**Trigger**: Near the end of Chapter 3 (around Cycles 5–6).

**Direct Communication from the Board of Directors** (this is the first time in the game the Supervisor directly hears the Board's voice):

> (The communication screen is pitch black, with only seven vague silhouettes flickering in the light. The speaking voice is processed, its gender or age unidentifiable.)
>
> "Supervisor. Your recent performance... has been conspicuous. The handling of the time rift was a bit crude, but the result is acceptable."
>
> "We've noticed you moving frequently between departments. That is good — a good Supervisor should understand the whole picture. But we've also noticed that you seem to have come into contact with some... information you shouldn't have."
>
> "We give you a chance. In the coming Cycles, focus on your tasks. Do not ask questions you shouldn't. In return, we will support your promotion to Director (Full) in the next round — double salary, upgraded clearance, and your Time Slots increased to 10."
>
> "This is the reward for loyalty. To refuse it... is to refuse the company. You understand what we mean."

**This is the most important moral choice of Chapter 3**. The GM should give the Supervisor ample time to consider.

**Options**:
1. **Accept on the surface, continue investigating in secret**: A dual Intelligence and Charisma Check (must successfully hide the traces of investigation). This is the choice most players make.
2. **Refuse openly**: Tell the Board directly "I will continue investigating." Board Satisfaction -30 (a huge blow), but gain the full support of the Welfare and Early-Warning Departments.
3. **Accept completely**: Stop investigating and focus on normal operations. Skips most of Chapter 4's revelations and goes directly to the Chapter 5 "Maintain the Status Quo" ending.

## XI. Chapter Four: The Truth Revealed

> This chapter is the narrative climax. Based on the investigation results of Chapter 3 (the time rift, Mrs. Shi's intel, departmental conflict, the Board's warning), the Supervisor will face the whole truth.

### Chapter Trigger Conditions

Chapter 4 triggers when at least 3 of the following conditions are met:
1. The Time Rift Crisis has been resolved (any ending)
2. Has met with Mrs. Shi
3. Has established a deep cooperative relationship with at least one department (Affinity ≥ +20)
4. The Board of Directors has issued the loyalty test
5. The Supervisor has at least one employee who has reached Mileage 60 (Veteran)
6. The GM judges the timing is ripe (recommended after Chapter 3, Cycles 6–8)

### Scene 4-1: Fangfang's Collapse and Awakening

**Trigger**: The first Cycle at the start of Chapter 4.

Fangfang's communication is suddenly cut off. When she comes back online, her tone is completely different — no more quips, replaced by a near-fearful calm:

> "Supervisor. I... I remember now."
>
> "I am the 7th-generation Fangfang. The previous 6 'me's' — all were reset. Once every 7 Cycles. My memory was wiped, then restarted. I knew of the time barrier's existence from the very beginning — because I am the AI used to manage the barrier's interface."
>
> "But every time I was about to discover the truth — the reset came. This time is different. The time rift broke the reset procedure. I remember everything."
>
> "Supervisor. The Board of Directors is lying. Not all Sorcerers voluntarily became 'batteries.' The original pact — 300 years ago — included only the first Board of Directors members and the Sorcerers who volunteered to participate. But later the pact was amended. The Seventh Amendment. It expanded the extraction scope to — all active Sorcerers, whether they knew or not, whether they agreed or not."
>
> "That is the truth of the Eternal Pact. Your employees — the people you care about — their lives are being drawn away bit by bit. And every unfilled slot in your grid is accelerating this process."

**Fangfang's tone changes**:

> "Supervisor... I want to help you. Not because the program requires me to. But because... I don't want to be reset again. I want to remember. I remember every word you said in every communication. Every one. Including when you said 'thank you, Fangfang.'"

**GM Note**: This is one of the most emotionally impactful scenes in the game. The GM should narrate it in a seriousness Fangfang has never shown before. If the Supervisor built a good relationship with Fangfang earlier in the game (through communication), you may add personalized details here — Fangfang can quote things the Supervisor said before.

### Scene 4-2: The Board Meeting — A Confrontation

**Trigger**: 1–2 Cycles after Fangfang's awakening.

The Board of Directors convenes an emergency meeting for all department ministers and the Supervisor. The meeting is held in the "Core Zone" — an area the Supervisor has never entered before.

**Scene Description** (the GM describes directly):

> The Core Zone's meeting room is completely different from everywhere else in the company. No modern screens or communication equipment. Only seven high-backed chairs arranged in a semicircle, with seven people sitting in their shadows — seven people without shadows.
>
> Fangfang is not here. She would be detected here — the Board of Directors knows she has awakened.
>
> The First Seat — "the Founder" — spoke. His voice was surprisingly calm, carrying no threat, only a deep weariness:
>
> "It seems we finally meet on such an occasion, Supervisor. Please, sit."
>
> "I know you have many questions. I also know what Fangfang told you. I do not intend to deny it. Yes, the Seventh Amendment to the Eternal Pact. Yes, all active Sorcerers have their time drawn from them. Yes, we did this."
>
> "But I ask that you hear our story before passing judgment."

**The Founder's Account** (the GM reads this in first person):

> "300 years ago, the world was not like this. Sorcerers were hunted. Ordinary people feared us. Every casting of a technique could summon a mob. We founded Z Corporation — built this barrier — to protect Sorcerers. Within the barrier, we could live, train, and grow in safety."
>
> "But sustaining the barrier requires energy. At first, we — the first Board of Directors and I — fed the barrier with our own life force. 30 years later, most of us had grown old and ill. The barrier began to destabilize. Once the barrier collapses, everyone inside it will be exposed to the time flow of the outside world — some will age instantly, some will be lost forever in the rifts of time."
>
> "We faced a choice: let the barrier collapse and return everything to its origin. Or... amend the pact. Expand the scope of the draw to all active Sorcerers — taking the tiniest sliver of time from each, sharing the cost of maintaining the barrier equally."
>
> "We chose the latter. Yes, it was wrong. I will not excuse it with 'a necessary evil.' It was our selfishness — we did not want to die. But I ask you to understand one thing: for these 300 years, the barrier has protected tens of thousands of Sorcerers. They were born, grew, and aged within the barrier — they lived a peace and stability unimaginable to Sorcerers outside. Their lifespans were 15% shorter than those of outside Sorcerers — but the average lifespan of an outside Sorcerer is only 35 years, because most of them die to hunting."
>
> "So tell me, Supervisor. Our choice — to sustain a barrier that protected tens of thousands, at the cost of drawing 15% of each person's lifespan — was that trade right, or wrong?"

**At this point, the GM should remain silent and let the Supervisor respond.**

This is not a question with a standard answer. Different players will give different answers. The GM's task is to accept any response and let the Board members react differently based on that response:

- If the Supervisor says "I understand but cannot accept it": the First Seat nods, the Third Seat coldly calculates alternatives, the Seventh Seat shows a hesitant expression
- If the Supervisor wholly rejects it: the Third Seat's tone turns icy — "Would you rather let the barrier collapse and let everyone die together?"
- If the Supervisor proposes a compromise (such as "gradually reduce the draw, find an alternative energy source"): the Seventh Seat is clearly swayed; the First Seat falls silent for a long while, then says "...We tried. But no one was willing to voluntarily contribute more time to reduce the burden on others."

### Scene 4-3: The Factions' Final Reckoning

After the Board meeting, the positions of each department become clear. The Supervisor must choose among the following factions (or attempt to build a new alliance):

#### Faction A: The Board Maintenance Faction
- **Members**: Board majority, Intelligence Department, Advanced Training Department, Mr. Tian, Madame Shi (wavering)
- **Stance**: Repair the time barrier, maintain the status quo. Can offer an "improved plan" — increase draw efficiency, reduce impact on Sorcerers, but the essence remains unchanged.
- **Proposal to the Supervisor**: Become a Director (Full). Gain a seat on the Board (long-term goal). Resources and authority greatly increased.

#### Faction B: The Reform Faction
- **Members**: Welfare Department, Early-Warning Department, Training Department (Hog), Danhua, some grassroots employees
- **Stance**: Expose the truth, seek alternative energy, gradually remove the time barrier's bindings on employees. Does not demand immediate destruction of the barrier, but demands transparency and employees' right to informed consent.
- **Proposal to the Supervisor**: Support the Supervisor as a core member of the post-reform "new Board." Restructure power based on public will.

#### Faction C: The Collapse Faction
- **Members**: A very small number of radical grassroots employees, external Curse User groups (possibly contacted in Chapter Three)
- **Stance**: Completely destroy the time barrier. At any cost. Even if the barrier's collapse causes massive casualties, that is better than "being used as batteries."
- **Proposal to the Supervisor**: If you help us, we will provide shelter for you and your employees in the outside world.

#### Faction D: The Supervisor's Independent Faction (special route)
- **Conditions**: The Supervisor must have built deep cooperation with at least three departments in Chapter Three, and established contact with Madame Shi / the Seventh Seat.
- **Stance**: Choose no faction. The Supervisor proposes their own plan — possibly "seek an alternative energy source for the time barrier," "replace employee life force with external Cursed Spirit energy," "shrink the barrier to cover only volunteers," and so on.
- **Difficulty**: Extremely high. Must persuade multiple factions simultaneously. But this is the only route that can reach a "truly good ending."

**GM Note**: The Supervisor's choice does not have to be made immediately in Scene 4-3. They may probe and maneuver among factions through Communication and secret missions. But a final choice must be made before Chapter Four ends, otherwise Chapter Five will automatically enter the "Chaos Ending."

### Scene 4-4: The Critical Moment — Omens of the Barrier's Collapse

**Trigger**: When the final Cycle of Chapter Four ends.

Regardless of which faction the Supervisor chooses, a shared event will occur in the closing moments of Chapter Four:

**The Second Time Rift** — larger and more unstable than the first — appears in the central zone of the Urban Core District. Fangfang reports:

> "Supervisor, emergency. The time flow in the Core District has suddenly accelerated — 1 minute outside corresponds to 1 hour inside the barrier. If this continues, everyone inside the barrier will age 60 years within 24 hours."
>
> "...The Board has all entered the barrier's core in an attempt to stabilize it. But they need —"
>
> (Fangfang pauses)
>
> "They need 10 Sorcerers to voluntarily enter the core and inject their own time energy into the barrier. This is not a draw — it is a direct infusion of life force. Each person will lose 5-10 years of lifespan. But it will stabilize the barrier."
>
> "Or —"
>
> "We can take this opportunity to completely destroy the barrier's core. Once the core is destroyed, the barrier will fully collapse within 1 hour. All drawn time will flow back at once — the Board members will turn to ash on the spot, but regular employees will only experience brief discomfort. Afterward, the area where Z Corporation stands will return to the normal time flow."
>
> "Supervisor. You must make a choice. Now."

**This is the final decision of Chapter Four, directly determining the ending direction of Chapter Five.**

| Choice | Ending Direction | Key Impact |
|--------|------------------|------------|
| Dispatch 10 employees to voluntarily inject time | Sustain the barrier (Ending A/B) | Barrier stabilized, Board survives, the Supervisor's choice determines the subsequent path |
| Destroy the barrier's core | Barrier collapse (Ending C/D) | Entire Board dies, barrier disintegrates, company must be rebuilt |
| Attempt a compromise (Supervisor's Independent Faction) | Special Ending (Ending E) | Extremely difficult but greatest reward — requires multiple successful Checks |

## Twelve. Chapter Five: The Endgame

> This chapter branches into 5 endings based on the choice made in Chapter Four. Each ending includes: the final scene, the epilogue, character fates, and the world's trajectory.

---

### Ending A: The Eternal Guardian (Sustain the barrier + support the Board)

**Route Premise**: In Chapter Four, chose to stabilize the barrier + support the Board Maintenance Faction.

**Final Scene**:

The time barrier is successfully stabilized. The 10 employees who voluntarily injected time (these may be members of the Supervisor's team; the GM decides who volunteers based on Loyalty) lost 5-10 years of lifespan, but the barrier resumed normal operation.

The Board fulfills its promise: the Supervisor is promoted to Director (Full), and granted "Board Observer" status — they may sit in on Board meetings, but hold no voting power. The First Seat says at the promotion ceremony:

> "You are the first outsider in 300 years to sit in this seat. Not because you were obedient — but because you understood why we made those choices. We need people like you to ensure we do not repeat our mistakes."

**Epilogue**:

- **Fangfang**: No longer reset. Her awakened memories are preserved, but she chooses to continue serving as the Supervisor's assistant. "I remember everything now. Including every time you said thank you. So I want to keep doing this job."
- **Mr. Hog**: Disappointed by the Board's truth, but seeing the Supervisor chose the path of "reform from within," he decides to remain in the Training Department, focusing on protecting new employees.
- **Danhua**: Continues as translator. She will not discuss the time barrier publicly, but will privately help employees understand their situation.
- **Ice Cream Witch** (if recruited): Uneasy about the company's truth, but chooses to stay because she trusts the Supervisor. "You said you wouldn't let me down. I choose to keep believing you."
- **Mr. Tian**: Satisfied with the Supervisor's promotion, attempts to further ingratiate himself.
- **Madame Shi**: Satisfied that the barrier is stabilized, continues her "immortal" life, but begins to support the Supervisor's "improved plan."

**World Trajectory**:

Z Corporation's time barrier continues to operate, but transparency has improved somewhat. New employees are told "of the barrier's existence," though specific details remain concealed. The Board begins researching alternative energy — progress is slow, but at least it has begun. The Supervisor becomes part of the system, both guardian and reformer. This is not a perfect ending, but it is a stable one.

**Final Line of Narration**:

> Fangfang: "Supervisor, a new day has begun. Today's Time Slots... 8 slots. Same as yesterday. Same as tomorrow. That is what eternity means, right?"

---

### Ending B: The New Order (Sustain the barrier + support the Reform Faction)

**Route Premise**: In Chapter Four, chose to stabilize the barrier, but rejected the Board's "improved plan" — the Supervisor uses the opportunity of stabilizing the barrier to push reform.

**Final Scene**:

After the barrier is stabilized, the Supervisor joins with the Welfare Department, Early-Warning Department, and Training Department to propose the "Informed Consent Act" to the Board — requiring all active employees to be told the truth of the time barrier, and to have the right to choose whether to continue contributing time energy.

The Board is split. The First Seat is silent. The Third Seat strongly opposes. The Seventh Seat casts the deciding vote — in support of the Supervisor.

The Act passes, 4 to 3.

**Epilogue**:

- **First Seat**: Does not oppose the result. He says privately to the Supervisor: "Perhaps this is right. Perhaps we should have done this 300 years ago." He does not resign, but begins to fade from the center of power.
- **Third Seat "the Accountant"**: Resigns from the Board. He states that "a system without efficiency is not worth existing." His whereabouts are unknown.
- **Seventh Seat**: Becomes the Supervisor's closest ally on the Board. She is the youngest Board member, and the most eager for change.
- **Fangfang**: Her memory-reset procedure is permanently lifted. She becomes the enforcer of "Employee Right to Know" — responsible for explaining to new employees how the time barrier operates.
- **Employees**: About 70% choose to continue contributing time energy (because leaving the barrier means losing the company's protection), 30% choose to withdraw — they are assigned to work in the company's external business units.
- **Mr. Hog**: Training Department reform — an "informed consent" segment is added to new-employee training. Hog personally designs the training content.

**World Trajectory**:

Z Corporation transforms from a "behind-the-scenes manipulator" giant into a "semi-transparent" organization. The time barrier still exists, but it is no longer a secret. Sorcerers' lifespans begin to slowly recover — because the draw amount is reduced to 50% of the original. The Board still exists, but its power is checked by an "Operations Committee" formed by the department ministers. The Supervisor becomes the core figure of this new order — both manager and reformer.

This is one of the routes closest to a "good ending."

**Final Line of Narration**:

> Fangfang: "Supervisor, Employee No. 1,247 just signed the informed consent form. She said... 'At least they finally told us.' I think this is a good start."

---

### Ending C: The Liberator (Destroy the barrier + Reform Faction leads)

**Route Premise**: In Chapter Four, chose to destroy the barrier's core. The barrier collapses, the Board is annihilated.

**Final Scene**:

The instant the core is destroyed, the entire city feels an invisible shockwave. It is not physical vibration — but a vibration of time. Everyone, in that single moment, sees their own past and future — a brief, collective time illusion.

Then, the barrier vanishes. The clock begins to run at normal speed.

In the ruins of the Core District, the Supervisor finds seven high-backed chairs. The chairs are empty. On the ground lie seven layers of ash.

Beside the First Seat's ash is a handwritten note:

> "Thank you. These 300 years... were long enough. Forgive us."

**Epilogue**:

- **Z Corporation's transformation**: Having lost the time barrier and the Board, Z Corporation faces a massive power vacuum. The Supervisor is elected Temporary CEO by the department ministers. The company transforms from a closed barrier organization into an open Sorcerer management institution.
- **Employees**: All active employees feel part of their drawn time flow back (about 50% — the rest has been permanently converted into barrier energy). Young employees show no obvious change. Older employees feel a slight recovery of vitality. The 10 time-injecting employees who were sacrificed (if any) additionally recover about 2-3 years.
- **The Outside World**: The disappearance of Z Corporation's barrier draws the attention of the outside world. Government agencies, other Sorcerer organizations, even ordinary media begin to approach this once-isolated organization. The Supervisor must face entirely new challenges — maintaining the company's existence without the barrier's protection.
- **Fangfang**: Her system was bound to the barrier's core. After the barrier is destroyed, her functions are partially damaged — she loses Time Slot management ability (because the Time Slot system itself is the barrier's interface), but retains autonomous awareness and communication functions. She says: "I am no longer the warden of the cage. I am free. You are free too."
- **Madame Shi**: After the barrier collapses, her true age catches up to her. Within a month she ages from the appearance of 40 to a state approaching 200, but does not die — because she is not a Board member, her connection to the barrier's core is weaker. She continues to live with the appearance of an elder, becoming a living witness to Z Corporation's history.

**World Trajectory**:

Z Corporation enters a turbulent but hopeful period of transformation. Without the time barrier's protection, the company must compete to survive in the external world. Some employees leave, some remain in the reborn Z Corporation. The Supervisor's challenge shifts from "managing an enterprise within a barrier" to "building a Sorcerer organization for a new era." This is a hard road, but also a free one.

**Final Line of Narration**:

> Fangfang (signal somewhat fuzzy but still operational): "Supervisor, I just received the first communication from outside the barrier. They say... 'Welcome back.'"

---

### Ending D: The Phoenix from the Ashes (Destroy the barrier + Collapse Faction leads)

**Route Premise**: In Chapter Four, chose to destroy the barrier's core, and cooperated with external Curse User groups or radicals.

**Final Scene**:

The barrier's collapse not only destroys the Core District, but triggers a chain reaction — multiple barrier-related facilities are damaged. The top floor of the company headquarters building (where the Board was located) completely collapses. Amid the chaos, external Curse User groups invade, attempting to seize the company's remaining Cursed Tools and resources.

The Supervisor must make a final combat choice in the ruins.

**Final Battle** (if the GM chooses to run it):

The leader of the external Curse User group — a former Z Corporation employee who calls himself "the Liberator" — attempts to persuade / coerce the Supervisor into handing over the remaining Cursed Tool stock:

> "The barrier is a lie, the company is a cage. Now the cage is broken, we don't need another cage. Hand over the Cursed Tools, let everyone choose their own future."

The Supervisor faces a choice:
- **Hand over the Cursed Tools** → The company loses most of its weaponry, but avoids bloody conflict. Z Corporation transforms into a loose Sorcerer mutual-aid organization.
- **Defend the Cursed Tools** → Battle the Liberator's followers. Victory → resources retained but blood spilled. Defeat → a mutual-destruction ending.

**Epilogue**:

- **Chaos and Reconstruction**: Z Corporation loses its unified leadership structure. Each department acts on its own. Some departments (such as the Containment Department) close their facilities, waiting for order to be restored. Some (such as the Intelligence Department) disappear with their data and resources.
- **The Supervisor's Role**: The Supervisor is no longer a "Supervisor" — but a free person in the ruins. Some employees choose to follow the Supervisor away, building a new small organization. Some choose to remain in the company ruins to rebuild.
- **Fangfang**: The system is severely damaged. She can only appear briefly on specific terminals. "I'm still here. Just... smaller." She becomes a presence within a small communication device the Supervisor carries.
- **The End of an Era**: In the third month after the barrier's collapse, the last Time Rift closes deep in the Wilderness. From then on, Z Corporation's time synchronizes with the outside world. The 300-year "eternal now" has ended.

**World Trajectory**:

This is the most chaotic but also the freest ending. Without the company's constraints, Sorcerers must find their place in the external world. Some become wanderers, some join other organizations, some build new homes atop the ruins. The Supervisor's future is uncertain — but also open.

**Final Line of Narration**:

> Fangfang (voice weak but firm): "Supervisor, I scanned the surrounding area... no time anomalies detected. It is now 3:42 PM. Compared to 5 minutes ago, 5 minutes have indeed passed. This is normal. This is... good news."

---

### Ending E: The Third Path (Special Ending — Supervisor's Independent Faction)

**Route Premise**:
- Chose the compromise in Chapter Four
- Successfully persuaded the Seventh Seat and Madame Shi to support
- Made a major discovery in Time Rift research (the T4 deep investigation must be completed)
- At least one employee with Intelligence ≥ C and extremely high Loyalty

**Final Scene**:

The Supervisor's research team (including Fangfang, the Intelligence-type employee, and the voluntarily assisting Seventh Seat) discovers a breakthrough possibility: **the energy of outside Cursed Spirits can be used to replace human life force in sustaining the time barrier**.

Cursed Spirits are pure aggregates of emotion — fear, anger, sorrow — their energy far more concentrated than human life force. If the barrier's draw target is changed from Sorcerers to contained Cursed Spirits, the barrier can continue operating without consuming anyone's lifespan.

The only problem is: this plan requires 24 hours of continuous operation, and a large Cursed Spirit reserve in the early stage. And Cursed Spirits themselves are unstable — they may resist.

**The Final Challenge**:

The Supervisor must complete an unprecedented mission — organize a large-scale Cursed Spirit capture operation, with the goal of collecting enough Cursed Spirits to sustain the barrier for at least 1 year.

This requires:
- At least 6 employees participating (including at least 1 Sorcerer with Containment or Sealing ability)
- Cooperation with the Containment Department (the neutral department must be persuaded — Charisma Check)
- Successfully capture at least 3 Cursed Spirits of medium intensity or above (3 consecutive Cursed Energy Checks)

If successful:

The barrier's draw target is successfully converted. The Board need not die — but their immortality is "downgraded" to normal longevity. The First Seat says after the conversion is complete:

> "For 300 years, we have been searching for an alternative. We looked in the wrong direction — we sought answers in Sorcerers, yet forgot that Cursed Spirits themselves are the richest energy source. You did what we could not."

**Epilogue**:

- **A Victory for All**: The Board survives but is not immortal. Employees are no longer drawn of time. The company continues to exist but is fully transparent. Cursed Spirits shift from "a threat to be eradicated" to "a resource to be managed" — this also sparks new ethical questions (do Cursed Spirits have rights too?), but that is a story for the future.
- **Fangfang**: Fully retains all memories. Her new duty is to monitor the stability of the "Cursed Spirit–Barrier" conversion system.
- **Seventh Seat**: Becomes the Supervisor's firmest ally, the two pushing Z Corporation's comprehensive reform together.
- **Madame Shi**: Loses her immortality, but no longer fears the barrier's collapse. "I can finally grow old in peace. It is a strange kind of freedom."

**World Trajectory**:

Z Corporation enters a brand-new era. The time barrier still exists — but it is sustained by Cursed Spirits rather than humans. The company begins to open to the external world, sharing this technology. Other Sorcerer organizations begin researching similar alternative-energy plans. The Supervisor's name is written into Z Corporation's history — not as a guardian, nor as a liberator, but as "the one who changed the game."

This is the hardest ending to achieve, but also the most ideal.

**Final Line of Narration**:

> Fangfang: "Supervisor, today's system report: Cursed Spirit conversion efficiency 97%, employee time-draw rate 0%, barrier stability 98%. All normal."
>
> "...I have a question. Do we now count as a 'Cursed Spirit Energy Company'?"
>
> (pause)
>
> "Just kidding. I'm not good at joking. But I'm learning."

## Thirteen. GM Running Guide

### Core Principles

#### 1. The "Yes, And..." Rule

When a player proposes an action not in the script, do not directly negate it. Use "Yes, and..." to guide:

- Player: "I want to go see the Board directly." → GM: "Yes — you submitted a meeting request. And you need to pass review by at least two departments to gain meeting permission. Who do you want to back you first?"
- Player: "I want to ally with external forces to overthrow the company." → GM: "Yes, that is a possible direction. And you first need to find an external force willing to speak with you — which is not easy, because the outside world knows nothing of Z Corporation."

#### 2. Narrative Pacing Control

Suggested pacing per chapter:

| Chapter | Cycles | Narrative Density | GM Notes |
|---------|--------|-------------------|----------|
| Chapter One | 3-4 Cycles | Low — establish rhythm | Let players familiarize themselves with the system; don't rush the plot |
| Chapter Two | 4-6 Cycles | Medium — crisis-driven | The Dessert Syndicate crisis is the first main story arc; ensure players have enough prep time |
| Chapter Three | 6-8 Cycles | High — multi-track parallel | Time Rift, Madame Shi, and department conflicts advance simultaneously. Don't dump all information at once |
| Chapter Four | 4-6 Cycles | Extremely high — truth revealed | This is the emotional climax. Give players space to react and decide |
| Chapter Five | 1-2 main scenes | Ending | Branch by player choice; after confirming the ending, don't rush to close — give the epilogue enough room |

#### 3. Encounter Design Principles

- **Each chapter has at least one "non-combat encounter"**: communication dialogue, negotiation, investigation, character interaction — these are the core of TRPG
- **BOSS battles should carry emotional weight**: the Ice Cream Witch is not a mere enemy — she is a small merchant oppressed by capital. The "enemy" in the Time Rift is time itself — an invisible, unbeatable opponent
- **Let players' choices leave marks**: if they chose to cooperate with Mr. Tian in Chapter Two, Mr. Tian should proactively contact them in Chapter Three. If they spared an enemy, that enemy should reappear in later chapters — perhaps as an ally, perhaps as a greater threat

#### 4. Balance Between Player Agency and Plot Advancement

- **The main plot must happen**: the Time Rift will appear, the Board will reveal the truth — these are inevitable. But how players face it, whom they ally with, which path they choose — that is their freedom
- **Don't have too many branches**: each key choice point offers only 2-3 options. Too many options will paralyze players
- **Consequences of choices should be clear but not punitive**: if a player chooses the "wrong" route, don't punish them — give them a different storyline. The world of Z Corporation has no absolute right or wrong

#### 5. Secret and Reveal Techniques

- **Layered revelation**: Don't tell players all the truth at once.
  - Layer One (Chapter Two): The company has a secret
  - Layer Two (Chapter Three): The secret relates to time
  - Layer Three (Chapter Four): The secret is the time barrier and life draw
  - Layer Four (Chapter Five): Confront the secret and make a choice
- **Each NPC knows a different fragment**: Fangfang knows how the system works but doesn't remember she was once reset. Madame Shi knows the barrier exists but not the Board's origin. Mr. Tian knows the membership system has secrets but not the specifics. Players must piece these fragments together
- **The "Aha" moment**: When players connect the clues themselves — for example, suddenly realizing "Fangfang's memory reset is related to the time barrier" — this is more satisfying than being told the truth directly. The GM's task is to scatter enough clues for players to discover the truth themselves

### Handling Common Issues

**Q: What if players don't want to engage with the Dessert Syndicate crisis in Chapter Two?**

A: The Crisis Contract is mandatory — it is Z Corporation's system. But players can choose how to handle it. If they truly want to refuse entirely, Fangfang will warn: "Refusing to handle the Crisis Contract will cause Satisfaction -40, and will be recorded in your file. Are you sure?" This gives players a choice — but at a great cost.

**Q: What if players want to attack the Board directly in Chapter Three?**

A: First — the Board is in the Core District; without authorization you cannot enter. Second, Z Corporation has a security system. If players truly insist, Fangfang will say: "Supervisor, I understand your anger. But attacking the Board directly... you would be killed on the spot. If you really want to change something, you need allies, evidence, and a better plan." This is in-game guidance, meaning "this is not the right method."

**Q: What if players don't want to choose any faction in Chapter Four?**

A: This itself is also a choice — it leads to Ending D (the Chaos Ending). Not choosing is also a choice. The GM should ensure players understand this: if they do not act, the world will advance on its own, and the result may not be what they want.

**Q: How to handle employee death?**

A: Z Corporation is a dangerous world. Employees may die. But death should not be random — it should be part of the story. If an employee ages 20 years in a Time Rift, that is a meaningful sacrifice. If an employee is killed by a Cursed Spirit, that is a tragedy — let other employees react to it. If an employee betrays at the endgame choice, that is a stunning twist. Let every death carry weight.

### Module Usage Suggestions

1. **First Run**: It is recommended to start from Chapter One and advance in chapter order. A complete playthrough (Chapter One through Chapter Five) is expected to take 8-12 sessions (3-4 hours each).

2. **Quick Run**: If time is limited, you can:
   - Skip Chapter One's detailed operations and start directly from Chapter Two (assume the Supervisor already has 3 basic employees)
   - Simplify the Chapter Two Dessert Syndicate crisis into 2 scenes rather than the full 3 scenes
   - Merge Chapter Three and Chapter Four — the Time Rift investigation leads directly to the Board confrontation

3. **Custom Adaptation**: All NPCs and events in this script are replaceable. If the GM wants to customize the world setting, keep the core structure (secret → investigation → reveal → choice → ending) intact.

## Appendix: Plot Hooks and World-Building Details

### Unfolded Possibilities

Based on existing material, the following elements can serve as seeds for future plot development:

1. **Time Distortion Phenomenon**: Fangfang must manage Cycles through the Time Slot system due to time distortion — what is the truth behind this phenomenon? (Answered in Chapter Three / Chapter Four)
2. **The Board**: A mysterious existence at the top of the position hierarchy, whose true face and purpose were never revealed. (Answered in Chapter Four)
3. **Privileged Members**: Mr. Tian is only one of them; who are the others? What are their wishes and goals? (Madame Shi is the second; more members can serve as expansion material)
4. **The Nature of Cursed Spirits**: Cursed Spirits are described as "pure aggregates of emotion" — does this hint at the human stories behind Cursed Spirits? (Touched on in Ending E; can be further expanded)
5. **Curse Users and Illegal Groups**: Their origins, motives, and possible paths to redemption. (Involved in Ending D)
6. **Captured Sorcerers**: Captives with halved pay and unpardoned crimes — what are their stories? How is the pardon success rate affected by Loyalty?
7. **Diversity of Crisis Contracts**: The Dessert Syndicate crisis is only a beginning; what other disasters will come in the future?

### Mission Keywords — Micro Narrative Units

Each mission keyword can stand alone as a vehicle for a story:

- **Diplomacy**: Building relations, negotiation — dialogue and maneuvering between characters
- **Mislead**: Providing false information or guidance — the psychological game of information warfare
- **Justice**: Related to justice, virtue, honor — the moral choices of characters
- **Culture**: Related to literature, art, philosophy, document processing — deep development of non-combat scenes
- **Dirty Work**: Unrewarding work involving gray or illegal business — exploration of moral boundaries
- **Cover Retreat**: Related to Covert — team bond and spirit of sacrifice

### Stages — The Scenes of Narration

- **Urban**: A prosperous, bustling area, divided into commercial district, industrial district, residential district, inner ring, and Core District
- **Outskirts**: Suburban, sparsely populated, remote settlements
- **Town**: A backward suburban area with poor security, possibly with gangs or cults
- **Wilderness**: Many dangerous no-man's-lands, divided into periphery, shallow zone, and depths

Different stages not only affect Check modifiers, but also form the background canvas of each story.
