## Five Core Building Principles

1. **Monster count diversity**: Mooks/Elite/Boss reasonable mix
2. **Elemental weakness coverage**: at least 1–2 weaknesses exploitable
3. **Action economy balance**: enemy action count ≈ player action count (Common difficulty)
4. **Toughness balance**: total Toughness ÷ per-round Toughness reduction ≈ 2–4 rounds
5. **Phases and turning points**: Boss fights must have phase transitions

## Quick Encounter Building Flow

> Determine difficulty → check Experience Points (XP) budget → select monster combination → check Toughness balance → check elemental weaknesses → add environment/terrain → design Boss phases → calculate expected rounds

# Chapter 3: Original Planet Creation Toolkit

## Five-Step Guide

### Step 1: Determine Planet Theme
Choose 1–2 theme tags: Steampunk / Cyberpunk / Fantasy Magic / Post-Apocalyptic Wasteland / Ocean World / Desert Civilization / Biochemical Horror / Utopia / Dystopia / Primitive Jungle

### Step 2: Stellaron / Aeon Influence
- What force influences this planet?
- Is the Stellaron hidden or public?
- Does it involve a specific Aeon / Path?

### Step 3: Civilization Stage
- Primitive Tribe → Feudal Kingdom → Industrial Revolution → Modern Technology → Interstellar Civilization
- Is the civilization distorted/accelerated by the Stellaron?

### Step 4: Core Conflict
- Political Struggle / Resource Crisis / Stellaron Pollution Spread / External Invasion / Internal Revolution / Religious Schism

### Step 5: Key NPC Creation
- 3–5 key NPCs, each with a clear goal, secret, and relationship with the players

## Example Planet A: "Rustcrown"

**Theme**: Steampunk + Apocalypse
**Background**: A planet whose industrialization was accelerated by a Stellaron, gear-cities shrouded in eternal smoke. Nobles oppress the working class with steam technology, while an underground resistance discovers that the Stellaron may be the only force capable of shutting down the factories—but using it might make everything worse.

**Core Conflict**: Nobles vs Working Class vs Stellaron — a triangular moral dilemma
**Key NPCs**: Steam Duke (ruler), Daughter of Gears (resistance leader), Rust Prophet (Stellaron Resonator)

## Example Planet B: "Azure Abyss Song"

**Theme**: Ocean World + Memory Civilization
**Background**: The entire planet is an endless ocean, with civilization existing in deep-sea cities. The Garden of Recollection collects "ocean memories" here—the echoes of ancient civilizations contained within the water. But recently the memories have begun to fracture, and the inhabitants gradually lose their knowledge of the past.

**Core Conflict**: Preserving memories vs Forgetting the past, Deep-sea Monsters vs City Safety
**Key NPCs**: Recollection Emissary (guardian of memories), Deep-Sea Prophet (knows the truth but won't speak), Amnesiac City Lord (forgetting their own identity)

# Chapter 4: GM Running Guide

## Three-Phase Pacing Model

> Exploration Phase ──→ Social Phase ──→ Combat Phase ──→ Exploration Phase ──→ ...
>     │                 │               │
>     ▼                 ▼               ▼
>   Skill Checks     Roleplay        Turn-based Combat
>   Clue Gathering   NPC Interaction  Tactical Confrontation
>   Environment Narration  Info Exchange  Resource Drain

- Each phase lasts about 20–40 minutes (combat may be longer)
- The Astral Express is the most natural transition tool between chapters
- On the train, character development, information organization, and equipment upgrades can take place

## Three-Layer Information Revelation Method

Divide each clue into three layers:
1. **Surface layer** (always discovered): the minimum to keep the story moving forward
2. **Middle layer** (discovered on successful check): rewards engaged players
3. **Deep layer** (discovered at high Margin of Success (MoS) / Tier 2+): secrets that subvert understanding

## Fail-Forward Principle

A failed check should not cause the story to stall. Instead:
- Fail but advance: achieve the goal but at a cost (lose resources / attract enemies / damage equipment)
- Succeed but at a cost: only a critical success achieves it completely without loss
- Forced clue flow: key clues are never locked behind a check

## Team Check Rules

- **Assist**: one character assists another, granting advantage
- **Team Challenge**: if more than half the party succeeds, it counts as a team success
- **Relay Check**: consecutive challenges are completed in relay by different characters

# Chapter 5: Adventure Module Template

## Template Structure

1. **Module Overview**: theme, recommended level, estimated duration, Path suggestions
2. **Story Background and Hooks**: core conflict, 4 types of hooks (corresponding to different identities)
3. **Chapter Structure**: 3–5 acts, each act containing scene description, NPCs, check Difficulty Class (DC), rewards
4. **Key NPC and Monster Data Slots**: reserve space for monster data cards
5. **Key Scene Description Template**: including environment, sensory details, interaction points
6. **Reward Suggestions**: Experience Points (XP), Credit Points (CP), equipment, Stellar Jade

## Demonstration Module Outline: "The Steam Heart of Rustcrown"

**Recommended Level**: 3–4 | **Estimated Duration**: 2–3 sessions

**Hook**: The Steam Duke issues a bounty—the central power furnace of Rustcrown, the "Steam Heart," has stopped functioning, and the entire city faces a freezing crisis. But the truth is: it's not a malfunction; someone deliberately shut it down.

**Chapter Structure**:
1. **Arriving at Rustcrown**: explore the steam city, meet the Duke
2. **Underground Investigation**: infiltrate the workers' district, discover the resistance's secret
3. **Heart's Truth**: the Steam Heart is actually a sealed Stellaron—it was shut down to prevent the Duke from exploiting it
4. **The Choice**: restart the Heart (save the city but aid the villain) vs destroy the Heart (free the workers but doom the city) vs find a third path

# Chapter 6: Optional Rules

## Social Combat

When a social situation requires detailed handling, enable turn-based Social Combat:

| NPC Type | Social Toughness | Weakness | Resistance |
|---------|---------|------|------|
| Cautious Merchant | 30 | Intimidation | Persuasion |
| Arrogant Noble | 40 | Deception (flattery) | Intimidation |
| Fanatical Believer | 50 | Lore (theological debate) | logical persuasion |

Social attacks: Persuasion / Deception / Intimidation / Performance checks → reduce the opponent's Social Toughness → when Toughness reaches zero, the NPC concedes.

## Interstellar Travel Random Event Table

Roll d20 to trigger an event during interstellar travel: abnormal signal / Stellaron fluctuation / pirate interception / meteor storm / distress signal / train malfunction / mysterious passenger / Aeon's Gaze / time anomaly / supply crisis / crew conflict / unexpected discovery…

## Simulated Universe

Based on the Genius Society's Simulated Universe framework, supports roguelike gameplay:

- **Three-layer structure**: room selection → blessing collection → Boss challenge
- **Blessing system**: collect Path blessing combinations (e.g.: 3 Destruction + 2 Preservation → half-tank build)
- **Difficulty ladder**: Common / Hard / Chaos, affecting monster stats and blessing rarity
