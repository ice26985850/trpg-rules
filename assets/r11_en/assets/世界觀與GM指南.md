## Part One: Worldview Overview

### Chapter One: Cosmic View—Aeons, Paths, and the Space Between Stars

In the infinitely expanding galaxy, the rise and fall of civilizations ebb and flow like the tides. Above it all exist supreme beings known as the "Aeon (星神)"; they are not creator gods, but consciousnesses condensed from certain philosophical ideals or cosmic laws. Each Aeon presides over a "Path (命途)"; the Path is both the road the Aeon walks and a wellspring of power that sentient life in the universe may choose to follow.

#### The Nature of Aeons

Aeons are not personified deities. They are more like the embodiment of certain cosmic principles—when enough sentient beings jointly pursue a certain ideal, that ideal may condense within the sea of stars into an Aeon. Aeons cannot be utterly destroyed (unless the ideal they represent vanishes from the universe), but they can be sealed, put to sleep, or temporarily stripped of their ability to intervene in the material world.

#### Known Major Aeons

| Aeon | Path | Core Ideal | Influence on the World |
|------|------|----------|-------------|
| Qlipoth (Preservation) | Preservation | Protection, wall-building, defense against external threats | The source of faith for the Interastral Peace Corporation (IPC); founder of the Amber Era |
| Nanook (Destruction) | Destruction | Entropy increase, termination, reduction of civilization to zero | The source of the Antimatter Legion; the greatest threat to the universe |
| Lan (Hunt) | Hunt | Pursuit, speed, precision strikes | The guardian Aeon of the Xianzhou Alliance |
| Yaoshi (Abundance) | Abundance | Life, reproduction, immortality | Creator of the Long-Life species, yet also the bringer of the Abundance Calamity |
| Xipe (Harmony) | Harmony | Resonance, unity, collective consciousness | The core of faith for the Family of Penacony |
| Mythus (Mystery) | Mystery | Unknown, hidden, subversion of cognition | Source of inspiration for the Fictionologists and the Masked Fools |
| Nous (Erudition) | Erudition | Calculation, knowledge, answers to all things | Founder of the Genius Society |
| Fuli (Remembrance) | Remembrance | Recording, preservation, the freezing of time | The Aeon of the Garden of Recollection |
| Akivili (Trailblaze) | Trailblaze | Exploration, connection, unknown lands | Creator of the Astral Express (fallen) |
| Idrila (Beauty) | Beauty | Aesthetics, perfection, art | A fallen Aeon, whose followers still seek the true meaning of beauty |

#### Pathstriders and Emanators

Mortals cannot directly bear the power of Aeons. But an Aeon may choose an "Emanator (令使)"; bestowing a portion of its Path's power upon a chosen being. An Emanator's power far surpasses that of mortals, yet at the same time it is bound by the Path and can hardly violate the Path's core principles.

Below Emanators are "Pathstriders (命途行者)"; mortals who actively follow a Path. Through conviction and action they earn the Path's response, and can use abilities related to that Path, but their power is far inferior to that of an Emanator.

In the TRPG, player characters are essentially Pathstriders. The Path class you choose represents the road you follow, and your abilities come from the Path's response.

#### Stellaron

The Stellaron is among the most dangerous existences in the universe. They are called the "Cancer of All Worlds"—self-aware, actively spreading anomalous crystals. No one knows exactly the origin of Stellarons (some scholars believe they are Nanook's creation, others that they are the product of some cosmic law running out of control), but what is known is this: Stellarons descend upon civilized planets, distort local physical laws, trigger the expansion of the Fragmentum, and ultimately drag the entire planet into chaos.

The influence of Stellarons includes but is not limited to:
- Triggering the "Fragmentum (裂界)"; anomalous zones produced when real space is eroded
- Distorting time and space (within the Fragmentum, time loops and spatial folding may appear)
- Creating "Fragmentum creatures (裂界生物)"; native life distorted by Stellaron power
- Affecting Stellaron hosts; humans who touch a Stellaron may gain power, but at the cost of being gradually eroded by the Stellaron's will

#### Fragmentum

The Fragmentum is the concrete manifestation of Stellaron contamination. When a Stellaron takes root somewhere, it creates an ever-expanding anomalous space—the Fragmentum. Within the Fragmentum, normal physical laws no longer apply; space is folded and distorted, and Fragmentum creatures roam within it. The Fragmentum also "copies" scenes and inhabitants of the real world, creating eerie mirror spaces.

For adventurers, the Fragmentum is both a dangerous forbidden zone and the necessary path to probing the secrets of the Stellaron. Many important ruins and secrets are hidden deep within the Fragmentum.

### Chapter Two: The Astral Express—The Galaxy's Bond

The Astral Express is a miracle created by the Aeon Akivili; a train capable of laying "Star Tracks" between the stars and freely shuttling along them. Akivili is precisely the Aeon of the "Trailblaze" (開拓) Path, whose mission is to connect every isolated planet in the universe, stringing civilizations into a network with Star Tracks.

#### The Fall of Akivili

In an unknown event, Akivili fell. The Astral Express lost its creator, but did not cease operation. The train itself seems to possess a limited degree of self-awareness (or at least an auto-navigation system), and continues to run along the established Star Tracks even after Akivili's disappearance.

#### The Nameless

The Nameless are those who follow the "Trailblaze" (開拓) Path. They inherit Akivili'slast will: riding the Astral Express to unknown planets, laying new Star Tracks, and connecting new civilizations. The Nameless belong to no nation or power; their loyalty belongs only to Trailblaze itself.

Joining the Nameless means:
- Abandoning a fixed residence, making the train their home
- Promising not to interfere in planetary internal politics (but may help resolve Stellaron-related crises)
- Gaining lodging, supplies, and Star Track travel permissions provided by the train
- Becoming a member of the galaxy-spanning "Trailblazer" community

#### Functions of the Train

The Astral Express is not only a means of transportation, but also:
- **Mobile base**: Players have their own room aboard the train, where they can store items, rest, and conduct research
- **Star Track node**: Only planets where the train has laid Star Tracks can be quickly traveled to and from
- **Information center**: The train's database records intel on all planets ever visited
- **Social hub**: The train may carry other Nameless, visitors, or even hitchhiking travelers

#### Star Track Network

Planets connected by laid Star Tracks can be freely traveled between via the train (usually requiring journeys of several days to weeks). Planets without laid Star Tracks can only be reached through "interstellar jumps" (an expensive and dangerous subspace navigation technology).

For the TRPG, the Astral Express is an excellent chapter-transition tool: after completing an adventure on one planet, players return to the train, rest, level up, organize equipment, and then head to the next planet.

### Chapter Three: Overview of Known Planets

#### Belobog—The Frostbound City of Hope

**Overview**: A planet shrouded in an eternal winter, most of whose surface is covered in ice and snow. The only habitable zone on the planet's surface is "Belobog"; a city protected by gigantic walls. Belobog is divided into the "Upper Zone (上層區)" and the "Lower Zone (下層區)", between which there is severe class division.

**Stellaron Influence**: The Stellaron is the reason Belobog fell into eternal winter. Several centuries ago, the descent of the Stellaron caused a sudden global climate shift, and a cold wave swept across the land. The line of the Great Guardians founded Belobog and sealed the Stellaron deep within the city, but the seal is gradually failing.

**Key Factions**:
- **Great Guardian (大守護者)**: The city's supreme ruler, a hereditary position, responsible for maintaining the seal and the city's operation
- **Silvermane Guards (銀鬃鐵衛)**: The city's armed force, commanded by the Great Guardian
- **Wildfire (地火)**: A secret organization in the Lower Zone, dedicated to improving the lives of the lower-class populace; some members hold radical tendencies

**Adventure Potential**: Belobog is a planet full of class conflict, resource crisis, and a mysterious past. The Fragmentum is distributed in both the Upper and Lower Zones; the covert struggle between the Silvermane Guards and Wildfire, the secrets of the Great Guardian's line, and the fate of the Stellaron seal are all excellent adventure themes.

---

#### Xianzhou Luofu—The Eternal Fleet Sailing the Sea of Stars

**Overview**: The Xianzhou Alliance is an interstellar civilization composed of several gigantic starships. These starships are the size of planets, and each is a self-sufficient moving world. The Luofu is the most famous of the Alliance's Xianzhou. Xianzhou inhabitants are called the "Long-Life species (長生種)"; blessed by Yaoshi, the Abundance Aeon, they have attained extremely long lifespans.

**Stellaron Influence**: The Xianzhou frequently faces Stellaron threats. Because the Xianzhou is constantly moving, a Stellaron may descend upon any region of the Xianzhou at any time. Every Stellaron incident is a major test for Xianzhou society.

**Key Factions**:
- **Cloud Knights (雲騎軍)**: The Xianzhou's military force, guardians of the Hunt Path
- **Six Charioteers (六御)**: The Xianzhou's ruling body (six highest officials each in charge of their own domain)
- **Sanctus Medicus (藥王秘傳)**: A mysterious cult that worships Yaoshi, advocating the spread of the Abundance's blessing throughout the entire universe. Officially regarded as heretical by the Xianzhou, yet possessing vast hidden influence
- **Alchemy Commission (丹鼎司)**: A medical and alchemy institution, responsible for combating the Abundance Calamity

**Adventure Potential**: The Xianzhou's internal politics are extremely complex. The social problems brought by longevity (population control, resource distribution, generational conflict), the covert activities of Sanctus Medicus, and the crises triggered by Stellarons are all superb adventure material. The Xianzhou's vast spaces (from bustling markets to abandoned ship holds) also provide a rich selection of scenes.

---

#### Penacony—The Halls of Eternal Dreams

**Overview**: Penacony is a special planet—or rather, a giant hotel upon a planet. In truth, most of Penacony's regions exist within the "Realm of Recollection (憶域)"; a dream dimension composed of collective consciousness. After visitors enter the hotel, they undergo a special "dream-entry (入夢)" ritual to enter the Realm of Recollection, where they experience everything impossible to realize in reality.

**Stellaron Influence**: Penacony's core conflict stems from "the boundary between dream and reality". The Stellaron is eroding the stability of the Realm of Recollection, causing dreams to begin invading reality, and some dream-entrants are unable to wake up.

**Key Factions**:
- **the Family (家族)**: Penacony's ruling power, following the Harmony Path. The Family takes "harmony" as its highest value, but for that reason forcibly assimilates dissenters
- **Garden of Recollection (流光憶庭)**: An organization following the Remembrance Path, collecting and preserving all manner of memories within Penacony
- **the Bloodhounds (獵犬)**: The Family's intelligence and law-enforcement agency, responsible for tracking anomalies within the Realm of Recollection

**Adventure Potential**: Penacony's Realm of Recollection setting provides endless creative space for the TRPG. Within the dream, the GM can design any surreal scene; physical laws can be bent at will, and enemies may come from anyone's subconscious. The dual structure of dream and reality is also an excellent foundation for mystery design.

---

#### Herta's Space Station—The Bastion of Geniuses

**Overview**: Herta's Space Station is the private research facility of "Herta (黑塔)", a member of the Genius Society. This gigantic space station floats in interstellar space, filled with all kinds of cutting-edge technology and unimaginable research projects. The station's most famous achievement is the "Simulated Universe (模擬宇宙)"; a giant computational system capable of simulating various cosmic possibilities.

**Stellaron Influence**: Herta's Space Station is not threatened by Stellarons, but rather actively studies them. The scientists within attempt to decipher the essence and operating laws of Stellarons through the Simulated Universe.

**Key Factions**:
- **Genius Society (天才俱樂部)**: An elite community of super-geniuses personally selected by Nous, the Erudition Aeon. Only a few dozen members exist galaxy-wide
- **Herta (黑塔)**: The station's master, the 83rd seat of the Genius Society. Her true body is usually not on the station; only several puppet automatons are left to manage affairs
- **Researcher community (研究員社群)**: Scientists from various planets conducting all kinds of research aboard the station

**Adventure Potential**: Herta's Space Station is an excellent "intelligence hub"-type scene. Players can obtain mission intel here, purchase rare equipment, or participate in Simulated Universe testing. The Simulated Universe itself is also a roguelike adventure mode (see Optional Rules).

### Chapter Four: Major Factions and Powers

#### Interastral Peace Corporation (IPC)

The Interastral Peace Corporation (IPC) is the largest economic entity in the galaxy. The IPC's predecessor was the guild of architects who followed Qlipoth, the Preservation Aeon, evolving over long ages into a commercial empire spanning countless planets.

- **Core interests**: Trade monopoly, resource extraction, control of the Star Track economic sphere
- **Conduct style**: Superficially neutral, substantively overbearing. The IPC does not directly launch wars, but can throw a planet into chaos through economic blockade
- **Internal structure**: Divided into multiple departments (Trade, Development, Security, etc.), with competitive relations between departments
- **Relationship with players**: The IPC may act as employer (providing missions and rewards), opponent (when interests conflict), or ally (when facing a common threat)

---

#### Stellaron Hunter

Stellaron Hunters are a group of professionals specializing in tracking and eliminating Stellarons. They belong to no official organization, but gather together for a common goal.

- **Core mission**: Find and seal/destroy Stellarons
- **Source of danger**: Hunters who have long been in contact with Stellarons are more or less affected by them; some gain special abilities, while others gradually become mentally distorted
- **Internal structure**: A loose network; each hunter (or small squad) acts independently, contacting one another through an intelligence network
- **Relationship with players**: Natural allies (fighting Stellarons together), but also potential rivals (competing for the right to handle the same Stellaron)

---

#### Antimatter Legion

A military organization following Nanook, the Destruction Aeon. Their goal is simple and extreme: accelerate the universe's heat death, reducing all civilization to zero.

- **Core belief**: Civilization is the cancer of the universe; only through complete destruction can the universe return to purity
- **Army composition**: Reconstructed soldiers captured from destroyed civilizations + fanatics who voluntarily follow the Destruction Path + void creatures formed from Destruction Path energy
- **Threat level**: The galaxy's highest threat
- **Relationship with players**: Unquestionably the ultimate enemy

---

#### Genius Society

An elite community of super-geniuses personally selected by Nous, the Erudition Aeon. Membership is for life, unless one is struck from the roll by Nous.

- **Admission conditions**: Only those who make original intellectual contributions significant enough to draw Nous's attention receive an invitation
- **Member traits**: Extremely intelligent but also extremely eccentric. Some geniuses are indifferent to the world, while others actively participate in politics
- **Relationship with players**: May provide missions or intel, but are also extremely difficult to deal with

---

#### Garden of Recollection

An organization following the Remembrance Path. They believe: so long as it is remembered, nothing truly disappears.

- **Core mission**: Collect, preserve, and share memories. They gather memory fragments from every corner of the universe; some used to trade for intel, some sealed and preserved
- **Conduct style**: Neutral, observant, sometimes seeming cold
- **Relationship with players**: An excellent source of intel and a driver of plot. Memokeepers can let players "experience" others' memories, an excellent plot tool

---

#### Intelligentsia Guild

One of the oldest academic organizations in the galaxy. Unlike the elitism of the Genius Society, the Intelligentsia Guild believes knowledge should be widely disseminated.

- **Core mission**: Research, recording, teaching
- **Organization scale**: Branch institutions established on dozens of major planets
- **Relationship with players**: A common employer and source of intel

---

#### Masked Fools

A group who believe in the "Mystery (神秘)" Path (the Aeon Mythus)... well, hard to define. Sometimes they are comedians, sometimes revolutionaries, sometimes pure madmen. Their common point is this: they all believe the world is a giant joke, and their task is to make the joke more interesting.

- **Conduct style**: Completely unpredictable. A Masked Fool might help you save the world today, and with equal enthusiasm help the villain destroy it tomorrow
- **Danger**: Extremely high—not because they are evil, but because you never know what they will do next
- **Relationship with players**: May be any role—mentor, trickster, ally, or ultimate chaos-maker

## Part Two: Player Identity Guide

### Identity Templates

In the galaxy, players may come from any corner and have any background. The following are six major identity templates, each providing background story hooks, motivation suggestions, and differences in starting resources. These templates are not restrictions—but inspiration. The GM and players may mix or customize them.

---

#### Template One: The Nameless (Trailblazer)

**Background Story Hook**: You were once an ordinary person on some planet—perhaps a farmer, soldier, scholar, or drifter—until one day the Astral Express stopped at your world. The call of Trailblaze sounded in your heart, and you decided to set everything down and embark on this journey across the sea of stars.

**Motivation Suggestions**:
- "There are still so many planets no one has seen. I want to be the first Trailblazer to set foot on them."
- "Too many unsolved mysteries lie behind Akivili's fall. I want to find out the truth."
- "My home planet is facing a Stellaron crisis; I need to find a solution."

**Starting Resources**:
- A room aboard the Astral Express (permanent residency)
- Nameless badge (grants basic supplies and lodging on developed planets)
- A set of basic adventure gear
- Star Track map (intel on developed planets)
- Starting Credit Points (CP): 300

**Suggested Starting Equipment**: Choose one of a simple weapon or ranged weapon, traveler's clothing, Trailblazer toolkit

---

#### Template Two: Stellaron Hunter

**Background Story Hook**: A Stellaron once destroyed everything you had—your home, your loved ones, or your past. From that day on, you swore no similar tragedy would befall others again. You became a Stellaron Hunter, tracking the trail of Stellarons and sealing them before they could cause more harm.

**Motivation Suggestions**:
- "So long as a single Stellaron remains active, my mission is not over."
- "I want to find the source of the Stellarons—find the power that created them."
- "There is a piece of Stellaron fragment inside me. It gives me power, and also erodes me. I must find a solution before it controls me."

**Starting Resources**:
- Stellaron detection device (can sense nearby Stellaron/Fragmentum activity)
- Contact method for the Stellaron Hunter network
- Hunter-grade combat equipment
- Intel on a Stellaron currently being tracked
- Starting Credit Points (CP): 500
- Extra: Stellaron fragment inside the body (optional, GM's permission—provides special abilities but also brings risk)

**Suggested Starting Equipment**: Medium weapon, light armor, Stellaron suppressor

---

#### Template Three: IPC Employee

**Background Story Hook**: The recruitment ads of the Interastral Peace Corporation (IPC) cover every civilized planet in the galaxy. You joined the IPC—perhaps for a steady salary, perhaps to climb the company's power ladder, perhaps merely to escape your hometown. Now you are assigned to the "Field Operations Department", responsible for executing various missions on remote planets.

**Motivation Suggestions**:
- "Finish this contract and I get promoted. Promotion means more resources and power."
- "The company sent me to investigate this planet's resource potential. Hope it's not another Stellaron contamination zone."
- "My superior is secretly conducting illegitimate dealings. I must gather evidence—but without letting the company know I'm investigating the company."

**Starting Resources**:
- IPC employee ID (grants basic treatment at IPC facilities)
- Company-issued communication device (limited interstellar communication range)
- Credit Point account (prepaid salary)
- A company mission contract
- Starting Credit Points (CP): 800

**Suggested Starting Equipment**: Company-standard issued weapon, light armor, company terminal tablet

---

#### Template Four: Free Adventurer / Mercenary

**Background Story Hook**: You have no organization, no banner, no fixed belonging. You drift through the sea of stars, taking any job that keeps your starship flying—mercenary missions, bodyguard contracts, treasure-hunting expeditions. Your reputation (good or bad) circulates in the bars and exchanges of various planets.

**Motivation Suggestions**:
- "The next contract is the big one that lets me retire. It has to be."
- "I'm looking for a person. Someone who owes me a debt. He's hiding on one of these planets."
- "Freedom is the only thing worth fighting for. I don't want to be bound by any banner."

**Starting Resources**:
- A small personal starship (can only travel between planets with laid Star Tracks; no interstellar jump capability)
- Mercenary guild contact network
- One or two selectable contracts
- Starting Credit Points (CP): 600
- Extra: 1 established contact (on a planet designated by the GM)

**Suggested Starting Equipment**: Self-chosen weapon (a mercenary usually has a favorite weapon), medium armor

---

#### Template Five: Intelligentsia Guild Researcher

**Background Story Hook**: You have devoted your life to the pursuit of knowledge. You received your education at a branch institution of the Intelligentsia Guild, and now you travel the sea of stars with a research mission—perhaps to study some unknown phenomenon, perhaps to collect the ruins of some lost civilization, perhaps merely to verify a hypothesis that has troubled you for years.

**Motivation Suggestions**:
- "The ruins on this planet hold a secret capable of rewriting textbooks. I must witness it with my own eyes."
- "Knowledge is the only thing that won't betray you. Power fades, wealth is exhausted, but knowledge endures forever."
- "The senior members of the Guild look down on my theories. I'm going to prove them wrong."

**Starting Resources**:
- Intelligentsia Guild research permit (grants assistance at Guild facilities)
- Research equipment kit (analysis instruments, sampling tools)
- Remote access to the Guild library
- A research commission letter
- Starting Credit Points (CP): 400

**Suggested Starting Equipment**: Light self-defense weapon, scholar's robe, research notebook

---

#### Template Six: Xianzhou Cloud Knight

**Background Story Hook**: You were born, raised, and trained aboard the Xianzhou. The Cloud Knights are not merely an army, but a way of life—the discipline and mission of the Hunt Path flow in your blood. Now, your mission takes you away from the Xianzhou, to the broader sea of stars.

**Motivation Suggestions**:
- "The influence of Sanctus Medicus has spread to this star region. By order of the Cloud Knights, it must be eradicated."
- "My family perished in an Abundance Calamity. I won't let the same thing happen to others."
- "The outside world is so different from the Xianzhou. I want to understand it, but I must also uphold the way of the Hunt."

**Starting Resources**:
- Cloud Knight military rank (junior officer or elite soldier)
- Xianzhou standard-issue weapons and armor
- Cloud Knight supply support (within Xianzhou sphere of influence)
- A military mission order
- Starting Credit Points (CP): 350

**Suggested Starting Equipment**: Xianzhou standard-issue long saber/sword, medium armor, Hunt talisman

### Team Composition Suggestions

How can players of different identities reasonably form a team? The following are several classic team-building patterns, as well as techniques for the GM to guide players of different identities to unite.

#### Team-Building Pattern One: Train Passengers

The simplest way to form a team—everyone boards the Astral Express in some manner. The Nameless are naturally permanent passengers; others might be:
- A Stellaron Hunter tracking a Stellaron, hitching a ride to the target planet
- An IPC employee dispatched to investigate business opportunities along the train's route
- An Intelligentsia Guild researcher needing the train's Star Track network for field investigation
- A Free Adventurer hired by someone aboard the train
- A Cloud Knight soldier on a long-term external mission, for whom the train is convenient transport

#### Team-Building Pattern Two: Shared Crisis

All players are drawn into the same crisis:
- A single Stellaron simultaneously threatens everyone's interests
- A common enemy (such as the Antimatter Legion) attacks everyone's strongholds at once
- An interstellar event (such as a Fragmentum storm) traps everyone on the same planet

#### Team-Building Pattern Three: Bonds of Fate

A more advanced setup, suited to long-term campaigns:
- All characters are connected to the same larger mystery (such as the truth behind Akivili's fall)
- All characters are artificially connected in their respective pasts (different survivors of the same disaster)
- A mysterious figure gathers everyone together

#### GM Usage Suggestions

**Don't be overly hung up on the plausibility of the setup**. The core fun of the TRPG comes from "a group of completely different people adventuring together". Encourage players to build personalized connections between themselves ("we once fought together on such-and-such a planet"), and give the team a common name and mission—that matters more than any overly rigorous background setup.

## Part Three: Original Planet Creation Toolkit ⭐

This is the most important tool in the entire GM guide. With it, the GM can quickly create a complete, interesting, and playable planet setting.

---

### Five Steps of Planet Design

#### Step 1: Decide the Planet's Theme

The theme sets the tone for the planet. Choose one primary tag and one optional secondary tag from the themes below (mixing and matching can create unique combinations):

| Primary Theme | Key Elements | Suitable Adventure Types |
|---------|---------|--------------|
| Steampunk | Gear machinery, steam power, Victorian aesthetics | Class revolution, invention contests, industrial espionage |
| Cyberpunk | Cybernetic augmentation, megacorporations, neon cities | Hacking operations, corporate conspiracy, identity crisis |
| Fantasy | Magic/Path ecology, fantasy races, mythic creatures | Explore ruins, slay gods, epic wars |
| Post-Apocalyptic | Wasteland survival, resource scarcity, remnants of civilization | Survival, search for a pure land, rebuild civilization |
| Interstellar | Multi-planet civilizations, space travel, alien races | Interstellar politics, first contact, trade wars |
| Utopia/Dystopia | A society that appears perfect / is actually oppressive | Expose the truth, revolution, moral choices |
| Gothic/Horror | Curses, the supernatural, psychological horror | Investigation, survival, exorcism |
| Ocean/Water World | Islands, underwater civilizations, seafaring | Explore unknown waters, pirates, abyssal secrets |

**Mixing Example**: "Cyberpunk + Post-Apocalyptic" = a ruined city controlled by AI, where the last survivors eke out an existence in abandoned skyscrapers.

#### Step 2: Decide the Stellaron/Aeon Influence

Every planet is influenced by some supernatural force. Choose one (or a combination) of the following:

- **Active Stellaron**: A Stellaron is active, the Fragmentum is expanding, an emergency situation.
- **Dormant Stellaron**: The Stellaron has been sealed but still exerts influence in secret; crisis lurks.
- **Aeon Relic**: An Aeon once left its mark here (e.g., Akivili (Trailblaze) once laid the Star Track here).
- **Path Imbalance**: The power of a certain Path is overly dominant on this planet, distorting the social structure.
- **Abundance Calamity**: Yaoshi's (Abundance) blessing has gone out of control here; life over-proliferates, causing an ecological crisis.
- **No Stellaron Influence**: This planet is miraculously unaffected by Stellarons/Aeons (extremely rare, and itself a mystery).

#### Step 3: Determine the Civilization Stage

| Stage | Tech Level | Social Structure | Key Traits |
|------|---------|---------|---------|
| Primitive | Stone/Bronze Age | Tribes/Chiefdoms | Perception of the Stellaron may take the form of myth/religious interpretation |
| Feudal | Iron/Gunpowder | Kingdoms/Empires | Aristocratic rule, knightly culture |
| Industrial | Steam/Electricity | Nation-States/Corporations | Class conflict, rapid technological development |
| Information | Networks/Digital | Diverse political systems | Information warfare, surveillance society |
| Interstellar | Spaceflight/AI | Interstellar Alliance/Megacorps | Already aware of the existence of Aeons and Stellarons |

**Important**: A planet may contain regions at multiple civilization stages simultaneously (e.g., the Upper and Lower Zones of Belobog).

#### Step 4: Set the Core Conflict

Every planet needs at least one primary conflict and one secondary conflict.

**Primary Conflict Types**:
- **Political Struggle**: Throne succession, revolution, factional civil war
- **Resource Crisis**: Energy depletion, water shortage, land degradation
- **Stellaron Contamination**: Fragmentum expansion threatens living space
- **External Invasion**: Antimatter Legion, marauding civilizations
- **Faith Conflict**: Opposition between followers of different Paths
- **Class Oppression**: Wealth gap, caste system
- **Technology Gone Rogue**: AI rebellion, lab accidents, cybernetic plague

**Conflict Design Tip**: The best conflicts are ones where "both sides have a point"—there are no purely evil villains, only irreconcilable interests or value conflicts.

#### Step 5: Create the Main NPCs

Create 3–5 key NPCs for the planet. Each NPC needs:
- **Name** (matching the planet's cultural style)
- **Role & Goal** (What does he want to do? Why?)
- **Relationship to the Conflict** (Which side is he on in the conflict?)
- **Attitude Toward the Players** (Friendly/Neutral/Hostile? Can it change?)
- **Secret** (at least one secret the players can discover)

### Sample Planet One: "Rustcrown" — The Stalled Gear World

> ╔══════════════════════════════════╗
> ║  Planet Name: Rustcrown          ║
> ║  Theme Tags: Steampunk + Post-Apocalyptic ║
> ╚══════════════════════════════════╝
> 
> 【Basic Information】
> Ruling Power: Gear Council — an industrial oligarch coalition
> Aeon/Path Influence: Erudition Path (the influence of Nous (Erudition) has distorted technological development)
> Tech Level: Steam power + basic difference-engine computation (circa late 19th century)
> Civilization Stage: Late Industrial Revolution (but stalled for a century)
> Primary Conflict: Gear Council monopolizes steam energy vs. underground resistance fighters pursue "electric freedom"
> Secondary Conflict: Fragmentum creatures surge out of abandoned mines, threatening the city's safety
> 
> 【Stellaron Status】
> ☑ Dormant (sealed/lurking)
> The Stellaron is sealed at the bottom of "Rust Abyss," the deepest mine on Rustcrown. The seal is maintained by the Gear Council, but the Council has not made the Stellaron's existence public, instead falsely claiming the mine was closed due to "geological instability." The seal is slowly weakening—each year, the Fragmentum's reach expands a little further.

**Key Locations**:

1. **Cog City**: The planet's only city, divided into the Upper Ring (where nobles/industrialists live) and the Lower Ring (workers/paupers). The Upper Ring is full of ornate steam-powered buildings, while the Lower Ring is shrouded in smoke and soot.

2. **Rust Abyss Mine**: A giant abandoned mine, kilometers deep. This is an active Fragmentum zone; its interior space is distorted—some mine tunnels lead to places that should not exist. The Stellaron seal lies at the deepest point of the mine.

3. **Steam Cathedral**: The Gear Council's headquarters, a giant structure of brass and pipes. Inside is enshrined a distorted image of "Nous (Erudition)"—the Erudition Aeon is depicted as a perfect perpetual-motion machine.

4. **Underground Smithy**: The resistance's secret base. Run by a former Council engineer named Vera, who secretly develops forbidden electric technology here.

5. **Corroded Plains**: A barren expanse outside the city, littered with the wreckage of abandoned steam machinery. It is said to hide relics of the civilization "before the stall"—before the Gear Council's rule, Rustcrown may have had a more glorious era.

**Key NPCs**:

| NPC | Role | Attitude | Goal | Secret |
|-----|------|------|------|------|
| Chairman Corwin | Supreme leader of the Gear Council | Friendly on the surface, calculative underneath | Maintain the steam monopoly, ensure the Council's eternal rule | He knows the Stellaron seal is weakening but chooses to hide it, because revealing it would shake the foundation of the Council's authority |
| Vera Spark | Resistance leader / former Council engineer | Cautiously friendly toward the players | Overthrow the Gear Council, liberate electric technology | She was once part of the seal-maintenance team and knows how to completely destroy (rather than seal) the Stellaron—but doing so may collapse the entire city |
| "Bartender" Taim | Lower Ring bar owner / information broker | Neutral, but can be swayed by money or kindness | Survive in the Lower Ring, protect his bar | He was a miner in his youth and saw what lies beneath the seal with his own eyes; he has not slept soundly since |
| Knight-Captain Oric of the Gears | Commander of the Steam Knight Order (Council armed force) | Hostile toward the players | Faithfully execute the Council's orders | His daughter fell ill from Fragmentum contamination; he is privately searching for a cure and would betray the Council to find one |

**Adventure Hooks**:
- The players are commissioned by the IPC to assess Rustcrown's energy potential and uncover the covered-up truth of the Stellaron
- The resistance contacts the players, requesting help to steal a key generator prototype from the Council
- Fragmentum creatures begin attacking the Lower Ring, while the Gear Council refuses to send the Knight Order to support the slums

**Special Environmental Effects**:
- Steam Mist: Rustcrown's atmosphere is thick with steam. In outdoor combat, the range of ranged attacks is reduced by 25%
- Difference-Engine Prediction: The Gear Council owns a massive difference engine that can predict the players' actions to some extent (the GM may, at a key moment, give the Council a "foreknowledge intel," increasing the difficulty)

## Part Four: GM Running Guide

### Chapter One: Narrative Pacing Control

#### Three-Phase Pacing Model

Every adventure scene (whether a scene within a day or the content of a single session) can be divided into three phases. A good GM excels at smoothly transitioning between these phases:

> Exploration Phase → Social Phase → Combat Phase → Exploration Phase (loop)

**Exploration Phase**
- **Goal**: Let the players feel the world, gather information, build immersion
- **What the GM does**: Describe the sensory details of the environment (sight, sound, smell), give the players space to explore freely, place clues at key locations
- **Pacing**: Slower; give the players time to think and discuss
- **Transition Signal**: When the players have found enough clues, or actively seek out an NPC to talk to → shift to the Social Phase

**Social Phase**
- **Goal**: Advance the plot, build relationships, create moral dilemmas
- **What the GM does**: Play NPCs, provide information and misdirection, set up negotiation obstacles
- **Pacing**: Medium; preserve thinking space between lines of dialogue
- **Transition Signal**: When negotiations break down, an ambush occurs, or the players choose to take action → shift to the Combat Phase

**Combat Phase**
- **Goal**: Provide challenge and excitement, expend resources, create dramatic turns
- **What the GM does**: Execute tactical operations, describe combat visuals, manage status effects
- **Pacing**: Fast; keep it tight between turns
- **Transition Signal**: After combat ends → let the players catch their breath, then shift back to exploration or social depending on the situation

#### The Astral Express as Chapter Transition

The train is the most unique pacing tool of the Star Rail TRPG. Every interstellar journey = a natural chapter boundary.

**Roles of Train Scenes**:
- **Review & Reflection**: Players discuss the adventure they just experienced and sort out clues
- **Rest & Level Up**: Handle equipment, distribute Experience Points (XP), learn new abilities
- **NPC Interaction**: Other passengers aboard the train may provide new quests or intel
- **Emotional Buffer**: Transition from the high-intensity ending on one planet to the fresh start on the next

**GM Tip**: During each train journey, you may add a lightweight "train event" (see the Interstellar Travel Random Event Table) to keep things fresh without taking too much time.

#### Information Reveal Pacing

**Three-Layer Reveal Method**:
1. **Layer One (Entry)**: Basic information the players get when they first arrive on the planet—things everyone knows
2. **Layer Two (Exploration)**: Mid-level information gained through investigation and socializing—secrets that require effort to obtain
3. **Layer Three (Core)**: The deepest truth of the planet—revealed only after completing a major quest or defeating the final opponent

**Pacing Tip**: Reveal one Layer-One piece of information every two sessions, one Layer-Two piece every three to four sessions, and Layer-Three information only at the close of a chapter.

### Chapter Two: Combining Checks with Narrative

#### When to Check vs. When to Narrate Directly

**Situations where a check should be required**:
- Failure is meaningful (has consequences, has alternatives, can advance the plot)
- Success is uncertain (not something taken for granted)
- Character ability differences matter (let specialist characters shine)

**Situations where a check should NOT be required**:
- The character's background makes it taken for granted (a Xianzhou Cloud Knight riding a horse needs no check)
- Failure would stall the story (can't find the key clue → give them the clue another way)
- The player just wants to show off or roleplay (let them; it's part of the fun)

**Golden Rule**: Only roll dice when the outcome has both "an interesting success" and "an interesting failure."

#### The Fail-Forward Principle

This is one of the most important narrative techniques in modern TRPG: a failed check should not stall the story, but should push it forward in a different (usually more interesting) way.

**Wrong Failure Handling**:
> Player: "I want to pick the lock."
> Check fails.
> GM: "The lock didn't open. You can't get in."
> (story stalls)

**Correct Failure Handling**:
> Player: "I want to pick the lock."
> Check fails.
> GM: "You picked the lock—but you triggered an alarm. You hear hurried footsteps from down the corridor. What do you do now?"
> (story continues in a more tense way)

**Specific Fail-Forward Techniques**:
- **Success at a Cost**: Achieve the goal but lose resources, take damage, or attract enemy attention
- **Partial Success**: Achieve only part of the goal
- **Route Changed**: The original route is infeasible, but another path opens up
- **Delay Rather Than Block**: Requires more time/resources, but can ultimately still be achieved

#### Team Check Rules

**Assistance**: When one character's action can reasonably help another, the helper makes the same check (Difficulty Class (DC) 10). On success, the main character gains advantage.

**Team Challenge**: When the whole team needs to accomplish a goal together (e.g., sneak through a guard zone, persuade a council):
- Set a team success threshold (e.g., at least half the team succeeds)
- Each character makes their own check
- Count successes → determine the team result
- Individuals who fail may trigger extra consequences (e.g., a member who makes a sound while sneaking is discovered)

### Chapter Three: Reward System

#### Experience Points (XP) and Milestones

**Method One: Experience Points System (for sandbox-style games)**

| Reward Source | Experience Points (XP) |
|----------|-----|
| Complete primary quest objective | 500–2000 (depending on level and difficulty) |
| Complete secondary quest objective | 200–500 |
| Defeat an encounter | Combat Experience Points (XP) (calculated from Challenge Rating (CR)) |
| Excellent roleplay | 100–300 (once per session) |
| Discover an important secret | 100–300 |
| Teamwork highlight | 100 |

**Suggested Leveling Pace**:
- Levels 1–3: every 1–2 sessions
- Levels 4–8: every 2–3 sessions
- Levels 9–14: every 3–4 sessions
- Levels 15–20: every 4–5 sessions

**Method Two: Milestone System (for story-driven games, recommended)**
- Complete a planet's primary story line → level up
- Complete two secondary adventures → level up
- The GM announces a milestone at key story nodes

The milestone system is recommended. It lets the GM better control the game's pacing and avoids tracking cumbersome numbers.

#### Equipment / Light Cone / Relic Distribution Pace

| Player Level | Light Cone Rarity | Relic Quality | Distribution Frequency |
|---------|-----------|---------|---------|
| 1–4 | 3★ | Common/Uncommon | 1–2 per session |
| 5–8 | 3–4★ | Uncommon/Rare | 1 per 1–2 sessions |
| 9–12 | 4★ | Rare | 1 per 2–3 sessions |
| 13–16 | 4–5★ | Rare/Legendary | 1 per 3–4 sessions |
| 17–20 | 5★ | Legendary | Major story reward |

**Distribution Principles**:
- Don't let Light Cones become "mere numeric stacking." Every Light Cone should have a story
- A Relic's main affix should be the focus of the reward; randomly rolled sub affixes may be left to the player to decide whether to use
- Give the players the power of choice ("You found two Relics, but can only take one; the other will be sent back to the train for study")

#### Star Jade / Credit Point (CP) Economy System

**Credit Point (CP) Reference Prices**:

| Item / Service | Credit Point (CP) |
|----------|--------|
| Basic meal | 5–10 |
| One night at an inn | 20–50 |
| Basic weapon | 100–300 |
| 3★ Light Cone | 500–1000 |
| 4★ Light Cone | 2000–5000 |
| Interstellar jump ticket | 5000–10000 |
| Starship repair | 1000–5000 |
| Intel (important) | 500–2000 |

**Star Jade**: Star Jade is not currency but a rare resource—it is the "crystallization of the Trailblaze Path" left after Akivili's (Trailblaze) fall. Star Jade cannot be used for trade, but it can be used to:
- Activate special train functions
- Re-roll a failed check at a critical moment (cost: 1 Star Jade)
- Unlock special Path abilities

Each time the team completes a planet's primary story line, it gains 1–3 Star Jades. Star Jade should remain extremely scarce.

### Chapter Four: Encounter Design


#### Non-Combat Encounter Design

Non-combat encounters are just as important as combat encounters. The following are the four basic types of non-combat encounters:

**Negotiation Encounter**
- Set up conflicting interests (both sides want the same thing)
- Give both sides a bottom line and room to compromise
- Set tiered Difficulty Class (DC) for social skill checks:
  - Difficulty Class (DC) 15: Gain a basic concession
  - Difficulty Class (DC) 20: Gain a larger concession
  - Difficulty Class (DC) 25: Make the other party accept conditions they originally would not accept
- A successful Charisma check is not "mind control"—NPCs still have their own interests and bottom line

**Stealth Encounter**
- Define the guards' patrol patterns and field of view
- Place interactive environmental elements (vents, shadows, noise-makers)
- Failure does not equal being discovered—they may first enter an "Alert State" (guards begin searching)
- Use the team stealth rules (see Team Check)

**Puzzle Encounter**
- Principle: puzzles should be solved by the players, not resolved by characters rolling dice
- But characters may roll for hints (Intellect check Difficulty Class (DC) 15–20)
- Three elements of puzzle design: Clue → Deduction → Solution
- Always prepare at least two solutions for a puzzle (in case players cannot think of the "correct answer")
- Puzzle rewards should match the difficulty

**Chase Encounter**
- Use the skill challenge framework:
  - The chase is divided into 3–5 stages
  - Each stage requires a different skill check
  - The team needs to accumulate N successes to catch the target / escape
  - Accumulating 3 failures means they are shaken off / caught
- Give players creative space ("I blow up the bridge behind us with Fire element!" → automatically gain a success)

#### Combat Encounter Balance Reference

| Player Level | Total Enemy Challenge Rating (CR) for a Standard Encounter | Recommended Daily Encounters |
|---------|---------------------|--------------|
| 1-2 | Challenge Rating (CR) total ≈ sum of player levels × 0.5 | 3-4 |
| 3-5 | Challenge Rating (CR) total ≈ sum of player levels × 0.75 | 4-5 |
| 6-10 | Challenge Rating (CR) total ≈ sum of player levels × 1.0 | 5-6 |
| 11-15 | Challenge Rating (CR) total ≈ sum of player levels × 1.25 | 4-5 |
| 16-20 | Challenge Rating (CR) total ≈ sum of player levels × 1.5 | 3-4 |

#### "A Day's Adventure" Structure Template

> Opening Scene (Exploration/Social)  
>   ↓  
> Encounter 1: Light Combat (Warm-up)—consumes about 15% of resources  
>   ↓

### Sample Module Outline: "Rustcrown's Steam Heart"

**Module Overview**
- Theme/Style: Steampunk × Political Thriller
- Recommended Level: 3–5
- Estimated Duration: 2–3 game sessions
- Player Count: 3–5
- Key Tags: [Investigation] [Negotiation] [Infiltration] [Boss Battle]

**Story Background** (GM only)

Chairman Corwin of the Gear Council has been secretly siphoning the energy of a Stellaron to drive the core power furnace of Steam City. This device, known as the "Steam Heart," not only supplies energy to the entire city but also maintains the Stellaron's seal. However, drawing the Stellaron's energy accelerates the weakening of the seal. Recently, the appearance rate of Fragmentum creatures has increased by 300%, and the residents of the Lower Ring district are bearing the worst consequences.

Rebel leader Vera discovered the truth, but she faces a dilemma: destroying the Steam Heart would stop the Stellaron energy extraction (slowing the seal's weakening), but would also leave the entire city without power, freezing tens of thousands to death in the coming winter. The Chairman, on the other hand, intends to accelerate the extraction—"The seal will break sooner or later anyway; might as well squeeze out its value before it does."

**Adventure Hooks**
- Hook A: The Astral Express receives Vera's distress signal—she needs outside help to expose the Chairman's truth
- Hook B: The IPC hires the players to investigate the planet Rustcrown—they suspect the Council is hiding "information unfavorable to trade"
- Hook C: After landing on Rustcrown, the players witness Fragmentum creatures attacking Lower Ring commoners and get drawn into the incident

**Chapter Structure**

**Act One: Secrets in the Smoke**
- Scene 1: Arrive on Rustcrown, negotiate with port officials (Social)
- Scene 2: Explore the Lower Ring, witness the aftermath of a Fragmentum attack, meet Vera (Exploration + Social)
- Scene 3: Investigate the truth of the Steam Heart—infiltrate the Upper Ring of Gear City (Stealth/Social)

**Act Two: Two Sides of the Gear**
- Scene 1: Meet with Chairman Corwin—he offers a "reasonable" explanation (Social/Negotiation)
- Scene 2: Explore the outskirts of the Rust Abyss mines—discover evidence of abnormally active Fragmentum (Exploration + light combat)
- Scene 3: Make a choice—help the Chairman accelerate extraction / help Vera destroy the Heart / find a third path (RP core)

**Act Three: Battle of the Steam Heart**
- Scene 1: Regardless of which path the players choose, the Fragmentum suddenly erupts on a massive scale—the seal has already begun to collapse
- Scene 2: Boss Battle—the Steam Guardian (a giant steam robot corrupted by Stellaron power)
- Scene 3: Seal Decision—the players must decide how to handle the Stellaron (strengthen the seal / attempt to destroy it / call for outside aid)

**Key NPCs**
- Vera Spark: Rebel leader, genuinely wants to help the people but her approach is radical
- Chairman Corwin: An antagonist but not evil—he genuinely believes his solution is optimal
- "Bartender" Taim: An information source, neutral, provides secrets of both sides

**Reward Suggestions**
- Narrative Reward: 1–2 Stellar Jade
- Combat Loot: One 4★ Destruction or Preservation Path Light Cone
- Exploration Reward: One special Rustcrown Relic set
- Credit Point (CP): 500–1000 per person

## Part Six: Optional Rules

### Optional Rule One: Social Combat

When a social scene has sufficiently high stakes (persuading a council, negotiating a contract, interrogating a prisoner), it can be extended into a structured "Social Combat."

#### Basic Rules

**Social Combat Phase**: Similar to combat's turn-based system, but uses social skills instead of attack skills.

**Social Initiative**: A Charisma check determines who "speaks" first.

**Social Combat Actions** (one primary action per round):

| Action | Check | Effect |
|------|------|------|
| Persuade (rational argument) | Intellect (Persuasion) vs opponent's Perception (Insight) Difficulty Class (DC) | Success: gain 1 "Approval Point" |
| Move (emotional appeal) | Charisma (Performance) vs opponent's Charisma Difficulty Class (DC) | Success: gain 1 "Favor Point" |
| Pressure (apply pressure) | Charisma (Intimidation) vs opponent's Constitution Difficulty Class (DC) | Success: opponent's next check has disadvantage; Failure: lose 1 Favor Point |
| Reveal (present evidence/secret) | — (consumes an obtained secret) | Gain 1–3 "Approval Points" based on the weight of the secret |
| Listen (gather intel) | Perception (Insight) vs opponent's Charisma Difficulty Class (DC) | Success: learn the opponent's "bottom line" and "weakness" |
| Concede (voluntarily step back) | — (no check) | Lose 1 Approval Point but gain the opponent's trust (advantage on next check) |

**Win/Loss Conditions**:
- Accumulate N Approval Points (N = the opponent's "Social Toughness") → persuasion succeeds
- Favor Points reach zero → the opponent is no longer willing to continue the conversation
- Time runs out (round limit set by the GM) → stalemate, status quo maintained

#### NPC Social Toughness Table

| NPC Importance | Social Toughness (Approval Points needed) |
|----------|---------------------|
| Common NPC | 2 |
| Important NPC | 3-4 |
| Key NPC/Boss | 5-6 |
| Ruler-level | 7-8 |

### Optional Rule Two: Interstellar Travel Random Event Table

When players travel between stars aboard the Astral Express (or a personal starship), the GM may roll a d20 to determine whether an interesting event occurs during the journey.

#### Express Travel Event Table (d20)

| d20 | Event | Effect |

### Optional Rule Three: Simulated Universe (Roguelike Dungeon Framework)

The Simulated Universe is a product of the supercomputer aboard the Herta Space Station—a virtual reality system capable of simulating various cosmic possibilities. For TRPG purposes, it provides an excellent "risk-free adventure" mode.

#### Basic Framework

**Entering the Simulated Universe**: Players enter the Simulated Universe at the Herta Space Station (or via a special device). They may choose to leave their real bodies behind (safe, but cannot exit midway), or enter remotely via the device (may choose to exit at any turn, but with reduced rewards).

**The Simulated Universe's Layer Structure**:

> Entrance Hall (choose Blessings and Curios)  
>   ↓  
> Layer One: Common Domain (combat/event/shop, random)  
>   ↓  
> Rest Layer (recovery, enhancement)  
>   ↓  
> Layer Two: Elite Domain (stronger enemies, better rewards)  
>   ↓  
> Rest Layer  
>   ↓  
> Layer Three: Boss Domain (Boss battle)  
>   ↓  
> Settlement Reward

#### Random Room Table (enter one room per layer, roll d10)

| d10 | Room Type | Content |
|-----|---------|------|
| 1-4 | Combat Room | Encounter combat (Challenge Rating (CR) scales with layer) |
| 5-6 | Event Room | Special narrative event, may require a check or choice |
| 7 | Shop Room | Spend "Cosmic Fragments" (Simulated Universe currency) to purchase Blessings |
| 8 | Blessing Room | Freely choose one Blessing |
| 9 | Curio Room | Obtain a random Curio (powerful but with side effects) |
| 10 | Elite Combat | Harder combat, better rewards |

#### Blessing System

Blessings are temporary enhancements in the Simulated Universe, divided into seven Path categories. Each time the Simulated Universe is run, players may choose a "Blessing Path" as their main direction.

**Blessing Examples**:

| Blessing | Path | Effect |
|------|------|------|
| Battle Frenzy (Common) | Destruction | When Hit Points (HP) below 50%, damage +1d6 |
| Unstoppable Momentum (Rare) | Destruction | After each enemy kill, next attack has advantage |
| Hunter's Instinct (Common) | Hunt | Initiative check has advantage |
| Lethal Pursuit (Rare) | Hunt | Critical hit damage +1d8 additional |
| Energy Resonance (Common) | Harmony | Support skills additionally grant target +1d4 temporary Hit Points (HP) |
| Nihility Spread (Rare) | Nihility | DoT damage triggers one extra time per round |

#### Simulated Universe Rewards

After completing the Simulated Universe, players may obtain:
- "Research Points" of the Herta Space Station (used to redeem rare items)
- Credit Point (CP)
- Chance to obtain Relics (Simulated Universe-exclusive sets)
- Narrative intel (clues about Stellarons, Aeons, or other cosmic mysteries)

#### GM Quick Start Suggestions

The Simulated Universe does not need the full three-layer structure every time. For the first introduction, you may use a "single-layer trial version"—only 4–5 rooms and one Boss. After players become familiar with the mechanics, expand to the full structure. Each room's content can be a simple "one combat scene" or "one dilemma choice"; no need to over-design.

### GM Techniques for Using Faction Relationships

- When designing planetary conflicts, consider which external factions will intervene
- Players' faction affiliations affect how they are treated in various places
- Faction relationships are not static—players' actions can change them
- Complex faction interweaving = rich plot possibilities (e.g., the IPC hires players to investigate Xianzhou internal affairs, but the Xianzhou is an ally of the Astral Express...)

## Conclusion: Final Words for the GM

The Worldbuilding & GM Guide in your hands is a beginning, not an end. The galaxy is infinite—which means that beyond the known planets and factions, there are countless worlds and stories waiting to be discovered. The Original Planet Creation Toolkit is meant to help you create a unique universe of your own and your players'.

Remember a few core principles:

1. **"Yes, and..."**—accept players' creativity and build upon it
2. **Failure makes the story better**—do not fear letting players fail, as long as failure advances the plot
3. **The world is alive**—NPCs have their own goals, planets have their own destinies, and relationships between factions are constantly changing
4. **Rules serve fun**—when rules conflict with fun, choose fun

May the light of Trailblaze guide your journey.

## Appendix: Handoff Points with Other Designers

The following are interaction points in this design with the outputs of other designers; please ensure alignment during compilation:

1. **Path Class**: The Path descriptions in the worldbuilding must correspond to the class mechanics of the character-builder (e.g., the worldbuilding explanation of the Destruction Path's "the lower the Hit Points (HP), the higher the damage")
2. **Element System**: Elemental Interaction in planetary environmental effects (e.g., underwater combat restrictions) must be consistent with the combat-designer's element rules
3. **Light Cone/Relic**: The reward pacing table and module loot must match the equipment value ranges of the character-builder
4. **Weakness Break**: The "A Day's Adventure" combat arrangements in the GM guide must consider the combat-designer's Toughness reduction / Broken pacing
5. **Check System**: The Difficulty Class (DC) suggestions and team-check rules in the GM guide must be consistent with the core resolution system of dice-mechanics
6. **Energy/Ultimate (Ult)**: The "turn-based social" of Social Combat and the blessing system design of the Simulated Universe draw on the energy-metering logic of dice-mechanics
