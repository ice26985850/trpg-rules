## Background

Three months ago, a young mage named **Cole (科爾)** vanished while exploring the Grey Mist Marsh. His mentor — the Academy mage **Victor Paige (維克託·佩吉)** — sent out a plea for help to the Independent Mage community. Cole's last message came through a scrying pool, a single sentence:

> *"There's something in the mist. Not alive. Not dead. It is — recording."*

The Grey Mist Marsh lies about three days' travel east of your tower. It is a natural magical anomaly zone — the local heavy fog carries a rare suspended magical particulate, extremely valuable for research into "Frost" and "Dark" elements. But lately the mist has grown thicker than ever — and has begun spreading beyond the marsh.

## Act One: Departure Preparations

### Scene 1-1: Victor's Commission

**Narration:**

> On a cold, damp morning, the visitor signal sounds at your tower's observatory. A thin, middle-aged man stands at the door, his robes caked with trail dust. He introduces himself as **Victor Paige (維克託·佩吉)**, a Bibliographic-style mage of the Academy.
>
> "Forgive me for interrupting your research. My — one of my students vanished in the Grey Mist Marsh. The Academy sent no search-and-rescue team — they say Cole 'ventured out on his own,' and the Academy is not responsible. But… I cannot just give up on him."

**NPC: Victor Paige (維克託·佩吉)** (refers to the same character from the original Scenario One — if the player has completed the original Scenario One or chose to work with him, he remembers you; otherwise this is your first meeting)
- Research style: Bibliographic
- Attributes: Perception 4, Theory 6, Craft 3, Will 4, Erudition 7
- Specialty: Light element
- Stance: A desperate mentor — he is willing to pay, but would rather you genuinely help

**Rewards Victor offers:**
- **Gold:** 200 Gold Pieces (GP) (100 GP prepaid)
- **Knowledge:** A rare manuscript on the Grey Mist Marsh's magical ecology (research material granting Erudition +1d3 Reputation)
- **Favor:** One future free library borrowing right (Victor's Academy Library Lv3)

**If you already have a working relationship with Victor** (from the original Scenario One): he additionally shares a secret — "Cole was studying an ancient structure in the mist. He believes it was **the first necromancer's laboratory**."

### Scene 1-2: A Companion Opportunity on the Journey

**Travel event** (on the road to the Grey Mist Marsh, at the end of the first day):

> You rest at a roadside inn. The only other guest is a woman in traveling leather armor, a deep claw mark gouged across the pauldron on her shoulder. A longsword and a crossbow that looks self-modified rest at her hand. She is carefully cleaning the crossbow's trigger — but her eyes — she is watching you.

**NPC: Lena Ironwood (莉娜·鐵林)**
- Identity: Mercenary, specializing in wilderness tracking and dangerous-beast hunting
- Attributes: Perception 6, Theory 2, Craft 7, Will 5, Erudition 3 (attribute total 23, expert-grade Companion)
- Personality: Silent, pragmatic, distrustful of mages ("I've seen too many mages blow someone else sky-high and then run") — but will cooperate when gold and justice are at stake
- Why she is at the inn: She is also heading to the Grey Mist Marsh — a merchant caravan hired her to escort cargo past the marsh's edge. But after hearing of the marsh's anomaly, she is weighing whether to take the job.

**Player Choices:**
1. **Hire Lena as a Companion:** Persuade her to drop the caravan contract and instead protect you deep into the marsh. DC 15 Persuasion (gold can lower DC: –1 per 50 GP, max –4). On success Lena joins (Loyalty 40 — a contractual relationship). Cost: 200 GP one-time + 30 GP/month. She may continue as your resident Companion in the future.
2. **Propose a division of labor:** You pay her 100 GP as a "marsh guide"; she leads you to the marsh's edge but does not enter the depths. You gain marsh intelligence and DC –2 on wilderness exploration checks.
3. **Go alone:** Save money, but you lose your only combat Companion. The GM should lower difficulty in later encounters accordingly.

## Act Two: Into the Grey Mist

### Scene 2-1: Clues in the Mist

**Environmental effect:** The Grey Mist Marsh's fog has faint magical activity — all Perception checks have Disadvantage (unless a Light-element spell is used for illumination, which cancels the Disadvantage). For every 4 hours spent in this area, make a Will DC 10 or gain one level of "Mist Fatigue" (equivalent to Fatigue Lv1, but it does not stack into higher Fatigue — it only affects checks inside the grey mist).

**Search for Cole's trail (Perception DC 14, Disadvantage):**

- **Success:**
  > You find footprints in the soft mud at the marsh's edge — fresh, from about two or three days ago. But the stride is strange — as if someone were running, then stopped abruptly. Before the stopping footprints there is no trace at all — as if the person had been lifted away on the spot.
  >
  > Beside the footprints, a small patch of frozen mud on the ground — impossible to form naturally at this temperature. Residue of Frost magic.

  Gain: Cole's route direction + a Frost Shard residue sample (usable to infer the last spell he cast — Theory DC 12).

- **Failure:**
  > You search the marsh's edge all afternoon; the only thing you find is a deer sunk in the mire — its eyes are white, but not the white of death. The white of magical residue. The mist is changing living things.

### Scene 2-2: The First Battle in the Marsh

**Environment:** An open, muddy area. The terrain is difficult terrain (speed halved). The mist is thick — all ranged attacks (beyond 30 ft) have Disadvantage.

#### Enemy: Mist Elemental ×2 (★ threat, solo-adjusted — see [Solo Supplement §1.2] Hesitation rule)

| Attribute | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| HP | 15 (originally 20, solo –20%) |
| Armor Class (AC) | 13 |
| Speed | 30 ft (flight) |
| Attack (Mist Touch) | 2d10+2 Frost (spell attack), range 20 ft, on hit target Perception DC 10 or Disadvantage on main action next round |
| Special Ability | Mistform — after taking a physical attack, reactively becomes mist (damage halved); Invisible in mist — all attacks against it in heavy fog have Disadvantage |
| Weakness | Fire damage ×1.5; Storm damage breaks Mistform (after taking Storm damage it cannot Mistform next round) |
| **Hesitation (solo rule)** | One of the mist elementals randomly takes no attack action each round (drifting blankly in the mist) |

**Tactical Hints:**
- If Lena is present (mercenary Companion): Lena can pin down one mist elemental — her crossbow is unaffected by the mist's Disadvantage at close range (within 20 ft). The player uses Fire spells to quickly destroy the other.
- If alone: Use Fire + area spells — mist elementals are hard to lock onto in the mist, and area spells need not worry about hit chance. Prioritize destroying one, then deal with the other.
- **Suggested companion command:** Round 1 free "Coordinated Attack" (Lena crossbow +2), main action Fire spell — quickly resolve one.

**Defeat rewards:**
- Mist Essence (Uncommon material) ×1 — usable for Frost Shard experiments (material cost –10 GP)
- If Lena is present: she acknowledges your tactical coordination — Loyalty +1d6

### Scene 2-3: The Ancient Laboratory

**Narration** (after following Cole's trail deep into the marsh):

> The mist grows ever thicker — you can barely see five feet ahead. Then, suddenly, the mist clears. You find yourself standing before a building — no, not a building. A ruin.
>
> A low, circular structure of black stone, half-sunk into the marsh. The stone's surface is carved with ancient runes, emanating a faint dark-purple glow. The entrance — a doorless arch — is dark as a mouth.
>
> This is not a necromancer's laboratory. This is older than that — from the weathering of the stone, this structure existed before the first mage, Selena.

**Discoveries after entering the laboratory (Erudition DC 14 to interpret the runes):**

> The runes use the primal language of the Starweave (星辰之織) — a script forgotten at the world's birth. You can only make out a few words: "the Recorder," "the Ward," "the Seventh Hole."
>
> This building is not a laboratory — it is an **observation station**. It was not built by humans — it was built by the servants of the Weavers (守織者). They observed here the influence of "the Seventh Hole" — the Void — upon the world.

**Core area:**

The laboratory's interior is a single circular room. At its center stands a stone pedestal bearing a translucent sphere. Inside the sphere — **Cole**. He is sealed in a time-stasis field, eyes open, lips moving — but his words cannot be heard until the time field is lifted.

**Methods to lift the time-stasis field:**

| Method | Check | Risk |
|--------|-------|------|
| **Stable Release** | Theory DC 15 + Craft DC 13 (consecutive successes) | Failure: DC +2 to retry |
| **Force Break** | Craft DC 18 (one-time) | Failure: time field destabilizes — Cole takes 2d6 force damage + the player takes equal damage |
| **Use a Light-element spell** | Spell attack vs DC 15 | Light element can neutralize the time-stasis — automatic release on success |

**After Cole is rescued:**

> The instant the time field lifts, Cole lurches forward a step — his last sentence finally finishes, three weeks late:
>
> "— recording. It is recording all magic. All spells. All shard combinations. It is learning."

**Cole's intelligence:**
- The "thing" in the mist is not a creature — it is this observation station's **automated recording system**, designed by the ancient servants of the Weavers. It has recorded every spell cast in the marsh for thousands of years.
- The core of the recording system — the **Recording Crystal** — is still operating. It holds records of thousands of spells — including some lost combinations.
- But the recording system has a cost: it absorbs the caster's Spirit as fuel. The magical particulate in the mist is the "waste" it expels. Cole was trapped in the time field while trying to study the Recording Crystal.

**Key item:** Before being trapped, Cole successfully pried a small shard from the Recording Crystal — the **Memory Chip**.

> Cole pulls a small glass vial from his pocket. Inside is a shard of crystal the size of a fingernail, glimmering with faint blue-white light.
>
> "For you. This flaked off the Recording Crystal — before I was trapped. I meant to study it, but now… I just want to get back to the Academy. This chip records the last spell the observation station captured. I don't know what it is — but whatever made this thing record it, it cannot have been an ordinary spell."

## Act Three: The Secret of the Recording Crystal

### Scene 3-1: Return to the Tower

**Cole's ending:**
- If escorted back to a safe location: Victor pays the full reward + an extra 100 GP gratitude bonus + Academic Reputation +1d6
- If Cole dies en route (e.g., Force Break fails): Victor is grieved but understanding — still pays the original reward, but the relationship sours
- If Lena is in the party: she suggests Cole return to the inn with her ("You're in no state to walk back to the Academy") — a good roleplay moment

**First thing after returning to the tower:** Analyze the Memory Chip.

### Scene 3-2: Studying the Memory Chip

**Analysis (Theory DC 14 + Erudition DC 12, the two checks may be made separately):**

- **Theory success:**
  > You connect the chip to the analyzer. Data streams across the screen — then you gasp. This spell is like nothing you have seen. It is a three-element combination: "Frost + Dark + Void." A three-element combination requires extremely high Theory and legendary shards to create — yet this spell predates the systematization of shard-combination theory.
  >
  > Whoever created this spell did not use combination formulas — they **extracted it directly from the Starweave**. This is a lost method of casting — not combining shards, but drawing directly from the raw fabric of magic.

  Gain: research insight — deepened understanding of the "Frost + Dark" dual-element crossover. Future use of this combination gains DC –2. You also gain a brand-new research goal: **reproduce the "Direct Extraction Method"** (requires obtaining a Void Shard in Scenario Three or Four).

- **Theory failure:**
  > The chip's encryption is too complex. You spend a whole night and crack less than a tenth. But in cracking it you stumble onto a pattern — a pattern of how Frost and Dark shards interact at the subatomic level. This is not a Void spell — it is a **Concealment spell**. Its effect wraps the caster in a bubble of "non-existence" — completely invisible to all detection (magical and physical alike).
  >
  > Whoever created this spell — did so to hide from something. Some seer that sees everything.

  Even on failure you still gain: deepened understanding of Frost shards — future Frost experiments DC –2.

- **Erudition success** (may be done alongside Theory):
  > You spend a whole day poring over every historical record you can find. About this observation station — nothing. About this spell — nothing. But you find one record, from the year 2800 of the Era — Dragon Elder **Valastra (瓦拉斯特拉)** once mentioned "the eye in the marsh."
  >
  > The "eye" — not the observation station. A surveillance system. The servants of the Weavers built it to watch — not humans. To watch **the lingering consciousness of the Void God (虛無之神)**. They feared the Void God would find a way to influence the world from the seal.
  >
  > This means: beneath the Grey Mist Marsh's observation station — there may be a Void Rift.

  This is a long-term thread pointing to Scenario Three and Scenario Five.

### Scene 3-3: Lena's Stay or Departure

If Lena is still a hired Companion:

> Back at the tower, Lena stands in your laboratory doorway, arms folded, watching the flickering data on your analyzer screen.
>
> "I've fought monsters half my life," she says, "but I've never seen — how a person reads history out of something the size of a fingernail."
>
> She is silent a moment.
>
> "Your tower. Needs someone who isn't a mage watching your back. Because honestly — you mages get so focused sometimes you don't notice you're on fire."

**Player Choices:**
1. **Formally recruit Lena as a resident Companion:** Salary 30 GP/month, Loyalty +2d6 (initial 40 → about 47–52). Lena begins to adapt to tower life — she stays in a spare room of the apprentice dorm, helping with watch duty and outside gathering.
2. **Thank her and settle the bill:** Lena leaves, but leaves contact information — rehireable any time in the future for 150 GP (Loyalty 40).
3. **Dismiss her:** Lena takes her pay and goes. You lose your most direct combat Companion.
